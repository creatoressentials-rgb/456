<h2>YouTube Shopping</h2>
<p><strong>YouTube Shopping</strong> is the set of features that let eligible creators sell and promote products directly through their YouTube content — either by connecting their own store to their channel, or by tagging other brands' products through the YouTube Shopping affiliate program and earning a commission on resulting sales.</p>
<p>It shows up across the platform in a few places: a Store tab on your channel, a product shelf under your videos, shopping tags inside videos, Shorts, and live streams, and links inside video descriptions. For viewers, it turns a recommendation into a one-tap path to purchase without leaving the app.</p>
<p>YouTube Shopping is not the same as <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">ad revenue</a> — it's a separate income stream you opt into, on top of whatever you're already earning through the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a>.</p>

<h3>The two ways to use YouTube Shopping</h3>
<p>YouTube Shopping actually covers two different setups, with different eligibility rules and different ways you get paid. Mixing them up is the most common source of confusion for creators researching this feature.</p>

<h4>1. Connect your own store (sell your own products)</h4>
<p>This is for creators with their own merch or product line — think branded apparel, mugs, or other goods sold through a store you already run.</p>
<p><strong>Eligibility to connect your own store:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>You're in the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a>.</li>
<li>Your channel meets the subscriber threshold for the YPP (there are multiple qualifying paths now, including a lower-subscriber early-access tier), or you're an Official Artist Channel.</li>
<li>Your channel audience isn't set to <a href="/glossary/made-for-kids/">Made for Kids</a>, and you don't have a significant number of made-for-kids videos.</li>
<li>You haven't received a Hate Speech Community Guideline strike.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Supported store platforms include:</strong> Shopify, Wix, SHOPLINE, Spreadshop, Spring (formerly Teespring), FourthWall, TeePublic, plus region-specific platforms like BASE, Cafe24, Marpple Shop, and Suzuri (Japan/South Korea only). YouTube also works with a long list of merch retailers such as Fanjoy, Bonfire, Cotton Bureau, Represent, and Threadless.</p>
<p><strong>How you get paid:</strong> Entirely through your retailer or platform. YouTube and AdSense are not involved in this revenue stream at all — for actual sales figures, you check your merch platform's dashboard, not YouTube Studio.</p>

<h4>2. Join the affiliate program (promote other brands' products)</h4>
<p>This is for creators who want to earn a commission by recommending products they don't sell themselves — everything from tech gear to beauty products to home goods, sourced from participating brands and retailers.</p>
<p><strong>Eligibility for the affiliate program:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>You're in the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a> and meet its subscriber threshold. YouTube lowered this bar in March 2026, opening the affiliate program to all YPP creators with at least 500 subscribers who meet the other guidelines below — a meaningfully lower bar than in the program's earlier years, so don't trust older guides citing 10,000 or 15,000 subscribers.</li>
<li>You're based in one of these locations: United States, South Korea, Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia, Philippines, India, Singapore, Brazil, Taiwan, or Japan.</li>
<li>Your channel is not a music channel, Official Artist Channel, or associated with music partners (labels, distributors, publishers, VEVO).</li>
<li>Your audience isn't set to <a href="/glossary/made-for-kids/">Made for Kids</a>.</li>
<li>You have no active Community Guideline strikes.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>How to join:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Sign in to YouTube Studio and go to Earn.</li>
<li>Under Programs, click Join Now in the Shopping section.</li>
<li>Review and accept the YouTube Shopping Affiliate Program Terms of Service.</li>
<li>Start tagging products in your content, following the tagging guidelines below.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>How commissions work:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Each brand or retailer sets its own commission rate and attribution window per product — YouTube doesn't set a universal rate. Third-party analysis of the program puts the typical (median) commission around 15%, with lower-margin categories like consumer electronics often at 5–8% and higher-margin categories like beauty and fashion often at 12–20%+, though these are independent estimates rather than an official published figure.</li>
<li>You can browse rates in the Affiliate hub inside YouTube Studio (Earn → Shopping → Explore affiliate offers), which also surfaces custom, higher commission rates some brands offer for limited campaigns ("Special offer promotions").</li>
<li>The official "YouTube Shopping" Chrome extension lets you save products to tag later and preview commission rates while you browse the web.</li>
<li>Commissions are paid through AdSense for YouTube, typically 60–120 days after purchase, to account for the return window. If a customer returns the item, the commission is reversed.</li>
<li>Some creators can also tag Amazon products directly by linking an Amazon Influencer Program or Amazon Associates Program account — currently rolled out to a limited set of eligible U.S. affiliate-program creators, with earnings paid through the same AdSense cycle on a monthly lock schedule.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to tag products</h3>
<p>Once you're eligible for either track, tagging works largely the same way in YouTube Studio:</p>
<ol>
<li>Sign in to YouTube Studio.</li>
<li>When uploading a new video or Short (or editing an existing one), open the Details page and click Products under the description.</li>
<li>Search for the product and drag it into place. You can tag up to 60 products per video or Short.</li>
<li>For long-form videos, you can add timestamps so a product surfaces on-screen at the right moment (timestamps must run 15–60 seconds each, and videos need to be at least 1 minute long).</li>
<li>For Shorts, the first product in your list becomes the visible Shopping product sticker; reorder your list or move/resize the sticker in the mobile app to control what shows.</li>
<li>For live streams, you can pin specific tagged products during the stream to highlight them in real time.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Tagging guidelines to follow:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>The product should be easily identifiable and verbally or textually mentioned, not just visible in the background.</li>
<li>The product should be meaningfully related to your content (reviewed, demonstrated, used, or clearly relevant to the activity shown).</li>
<li>Use products as the manufacturer intends — no unsafe or off-label demonstrations.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Why a tag might get blocked or removed:</strong> the video is set to <a href="/glossary/made-for-kids/">Made for Kids</a>, the video has an active <a href="/glossary/copyright-claim/">copyright claim</a> against it (see <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a>), the content is limited or ineligible for monetization, you're using a non-Shopping-eligible sound on a Short with a product sticker, the viewer is outside an eligible country, or the viewer is on a mobile browser, smart TV, or game console. Repeated non-compliant tagging can put your access to the affiliate program at risk, so treat the guidelines the same way you'd treat any other YouTube monetization policy.</p>

<h3>Where Shopping features show up for viewers</h3>
<p>Tagged and store products can surface in several places beyond the video itself: a product shelf below or beside a video or live stream, an automatically generated product section in the watch feed or home feed (in a subset of countries), search results, and via <a href="/glossary/browse-features/">browse features</a> like the home feed or <a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">suggested videos</a> that recommend related content. Viewer purchasing availability spans a much longer list of countries and regions than creator eligibility does — so a creator who isn't yet eligible to join the affiliate program may still see shopping features active on similar content from bigger channels in their niche.</p>

<h3>Measuring your Shopping performance</h3>
<p>For affiliate tags, use expanded reports in YouTube Analytics (found via <a href="/glossary/youtube-advanced-mode/">YouTube Advanced Mode</a> in YouTube Studio) to see engagement with tagged products and how much of your <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">traffic sources</a> come specifically from product pages. For your own store, sales and order totals are visible in the Shopping tab of Studio for supported platforms, but the authoritative numbers live on your retailer's own dashboard — YouTube's figures may not exactly match due to attribution differences, ad blockers, or multi-device purchases.</p>

<h3>How YouTube Shopping fits into your monetization mix</h3>
<p>YouTube Shopping is one of several ways creators diversify income beyond straight ad revenue. It sits alongside features like <a href="/glossary/channel-memberships/">channel memberships</a>, <a href="/glossary/super-chat/">Super Chat</a>, <a href="/glossary/super-stickers/">Super Stickers</a>, <a href="/glossary/super-thanks/">Super Thanks</a>, and other forms of <a href="/glossary/fan-funding/">fan funding</a> — and it's distinct from brand deals arranged through <a href="/glossary/youtube-creator-partnerships/">YouTube Creator Partnerships</a>, since Shopping commissions and merch sales are self-serve and don't require a brand to reach out to you first.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Do I need to be in the YouTube Partner Program to use YouTube Shopping?</h4>
<p>Yes, for both tracks. Connecting your own store and joining the affiliate program both require active <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a> membership plus the specific eligibility rules for each (subscriber threshold, country, content type, and strike history).</p>
<h4>How much can I actually earn from the YouTube Shopping affiliate program?</h4>
<p>It depends entirely on which brands you tag and the commission rate each one sets — YouTube doesn't publish a fixed rate. Independent estimates put the typical commission around 15% of the sale, with electronics and gaming gear often lower (5–8%) and beauty, fashion, and home goods often higher (12–20%+), plus occasional short-term promotional boosts from individual brands.</p>
<h4>Why haven't I been paid for a sale I know happened?</h4>
<p>Affiliate commissions are paid through AdSense 60–120 days after the purchase date, to account for the return window — it's not instant. If you're selling through your own connected store instead, that payment comes from your retailer/platform directly on their own schedule, not from YouTube at all.</p>
