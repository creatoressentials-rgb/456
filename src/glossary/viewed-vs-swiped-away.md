<h2>Viewed vs. Swiped Away</h2>
<p><strong>Viewed vs. Swiped Away</strong> is a YouTube Shorts Analytics comparison showing the percentage of feed presentations where viewers continued watching versus moving past the Short.</p>
<p>YouTube commonly presents this report as <strong>How many chose to view</strong>. In current metric definitions, the viewed side is also described as <strong>Stayed to watch</strong>: the percentage of times viewers stayed past the initial seconds of a Short.</p>

<h3>What Do the Two Sides Mean?</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Outcome</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Viewed or Stayed to watch</td><td>The viewer remained past the initial seconds rather than immediately moving on</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Swiped away</td><td>The viewer moved past the Short before qualifying as having stayed to watch</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>YouTube does not publicly specify one exact number of seconds that universally separates the two outcomes.</p>

<h3>Where Can You Find the Report?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Analytics</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open the <strong>Content</strong> tab.</li>
  <li>Select the <strong>Shorts</strong> chip.</li>
  <li>Find <strong>How many chose to view</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>At the individual-Short level, open the Short's Analytics and review the Reach or engagement-related Shorts reports.</p>

<h3>Viewed vs. Swiped Away vs. Stayed to Watch</h3>
<p><a href="/glossary/stayed-to-watch/">Stayed to Watch</a> is the current metric name used in YouTube's broader Analytics definitions. Viewed vs. Swiped Away is the comparison displayed in the Shorts report.</p>
<p>In practical use:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Viewed percentage corresponds to the viewers who stayed.</li>
  <li>Swiped Away percentage represents the opposite initial choice.</li>
  <li>The two percentages should approximately total 100%, with possible display rounding.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Viewed vs. Swiped Away vs. Shorts Views</h3>
<p>Since March 31, 2025, YouTube counts a Shorts view whenever a Short starts to play or replay, with no minimum watch-time requirement.</p>
<p>This means a playback can contribute to the public Shorts Views count even when the viewer swipes away quickly. The Viewed vs. Swiped Away report provides context that the broader view count no longer supplies by itself.</p>

<h3>Viewed vs. Swiped Away vs. Engaged Views</h3>
<p><a href="/glossary/engaged-views/">Engaged Views</a> preserve YouTube's former, stricter Shorts view methodology. They count viewers who stayed past the initial seconds and exclude loops.</p>
<p>Use the metrics together:</p>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Metric</th><th>What It Helps Explain</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Shorts Views</td><td>How many legitimate starts and replays occurred</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Viewed vs. Swiped Away</td><td>How often viewers initially chose to stay</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Engaged Views</td><td>How many meaningful first-pass views passed the initial threshold</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Average View Duration</td><td>How long engaged viewers watched</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Average Percentage Viewed</td><td>What percentage engaged viewers watched on average</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>Is It a Click-Through Rate?</h3>
<p>No. Ordinary impressions click-through rate measures whether viewers clicked a thumbnail after a registered impression. Shorts Feed viewing is a vertical, auto-playing experience where the viewer decides whether to stay or swipe.</p>
<p>Viewed vs. Swiped Away is the closer feed equivalent to an initial selection measure, but it should not be called thumbnail CTR.</p>

<h3>What Is a Good Viewed Percentage?</h3>
<p>YouTube does not publish one universal good, bad, or algorithmic cutoff for Viewed vs. Swiped Away.</p>
<p>Benchmarks vary by:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Topic</li>
  <li>Audience</li>
  <li>Short length</li>
  <li>Traffic mix</li>
  <li>Channel familiarity</li>
  <li>Language and geography</li>
  <li>Whether the subject requires context</li>
  <li>How broadly YouTube is testing the Short</li>
</ul>
<p>Compare against your own Shorts with similar topics, lengths, and audience conditions.</p>

<h3>Does a Low Viewed Rate Stop Distribution?</h3>
<p>YouTube does not publish a rule stating that distribution automatically stops at 40%, 50%, 70%, or any other exact viewed or swipe percentage.</p>
<p>The report is useful because initial viewer choice matters, but recommendations also consider deeper watch behavior, satisfaction, feedback, audience fit, and other personalized signals.</p>

<h3>Why Might Viewers Swipe Away?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The first frame is unclear.</li>
  <li>The opening takes too long to explain the subject.</li>
  <li>The viewer has already seen similar content.</li>
  <li>The topic is irrelevant to that viewer.</li>
  <li>Text is difficult to read.</li>
  <li>Audio begins abruptly or is unclear.</li>
  <li>The Short appears misleading or low quality.</li>
  <li>The viewer is simply moving quickly through the feed.</li>
  <li>The Short was shown to a broader experimental audience.</li>
</ul>
<p>A swipe does not always mean the content is objectively bad. It can reflect audience mismatch or viewing context.</p>

<h3>Why Might a Viewer Stay?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The subject is instantly recognizable.</li>
  <li>The opening raises a relevant question.</li>
  <li>The creator shows the result or conflict early.</li>
  <li>The first frame has clear movement or visual information.</li>
  <li>The audio and captions are understandable.</li>
  <li>The creator or series is familiar.</li>
  <li>The topic closely matches the viewer's current interests.</li>
</ul>

<h3>High Viewed Rate but Low Retention</h3>
<p>A strong initial choice does not guarantee that viewers complete the Short. They may stay for the opening but leave because:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The payoff is delayed.</li>
  <li>The middle becomes repetitive.</li>
  <li>The promised answer is weak.</li>
  <li>The pacing changes.</li>
  <li>The video is longer than necessary.</li>
  <li>The conclusion does not satisfy the setup.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Low Viewed Rate but Strong Engaged Retention</h3>
<p>This pattern can mean the Short strongly serves a narrower audience but the opening or targeting does not appeal broadly.</p>
<p>Possible responses include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Clarify the first frame.</li>
  <li>Identify the intended viewer sooner.</li>
  <li>Use more specific on-screen text.</li>
  <li>Keep the niche topic but improve immediate context.</li>
  <li>Avoid changing the whole idea if engaged viewers are highly satisfied.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to Improve the Viewed Side</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Show the subject immediately.</li>
  <li>Remove greetings and unnecessary setup.</li>
  <li>Use one clear opening idea.</li>
  <li>Make captions readable on a phone.</li>
  <li>Begin with a result, question, demonstration, or conflict when appropriate.</li>
  <li>Match the title and opening.</li>
  <li>Test different opening structures across new Shorts.</li>
  <li>Compare similar videos rather than copying a viral benchmark.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Should You Use Clickbait Hooks?</h3>
<p>A dramatic opening can increase initial attention, but it can reduce deeper retention and <a href="/glossary/viewer-satisfaction/">viewer satisfaction</a> when the promised payoff is misleading.</p>
<p>The strongest opening makes the actual value clear and earns the next few seconds honestly.</p>

<h3>How to Analyze the Metric Properly</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Check Shown in feed.</li>
  <li>Review Viewed vs. Swiped Away.</li>
  <li>Check Engaged Views.</li>
  <li>Review retention among those who stayed.</li>
  <li>Check likes, shares, comments, and subscribers.</li>
  <li>Compare the traffic sources.</li>
  <li>Compare several similar Shorts.</li>
  <li>Change one opening principle at a time.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Common Viewed-vs.-Swiped-Away Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Calling it impressions CTR.</li>
  <li>Assuming Swiped Away produces no public view after the 2025 change.</li>
  <li>Using one universal benchmark.</li>
  <li>Believing an exact percentage triggers a distribution cutoff.</li>
  <li>Ignoring deeper retention.</li>
  <li>Comparing unrelated topics and lengths.</li>
  <li>Changing successful niche content solely to appeal broadly.</li>
  <li>Using misleading hooks to raise the viewed percentage.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>What does Viewed vs. Swiped Away measure?</h4>
<p>It shows the percentage of Shorts Feed opportunities where viewers stayed to watch versus moving past the Short.</p>

<h4>Is Viewed the same as a public Shorts view?</h4>
<p>No. Since March 31, 2025, a public Shorts view can count when playback starts, while Viewed or Stayed to Watch requires remaining past the initial seconds.</p>

<h4>What percentage is good?</h4>
<p>YouTube does not publish one universal benchmark. Compare similar Shorts on your own channel.</p>

<h4>Does a high viewed rate guarantee growth?</h4>
<p>No. Deeper retention, satisfaction, audience fit, and other signals also matter.</p>
