<h2>YouTube Outro</h2>
<p>A <strong>YouTube outro</strong> is the closing portion of a video that resolves the main promise, gives the viewer a final takeaway, and directs attention toward an appropriate next action.</p>
<p>An outro can include spoken conclusions, a next-video recommendation, credits, a call to subscribe, sponsor disclosures, music, and visual space for YouTube end-screen elements. The outro is part of the video itself; the end screen is an interactive YouTube overlay added through Studio.</p>

<h3>What an Outro Can Do</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Resolve the main question</li>
  <li>Summarize the decision or result</li>
  <li>Explain the next practical step</li>
  <li>Recommend a related video or playlist</li>
  <li>Invite a relevant comment</li>
  <li>Thank contributors or viewers</li>
  <li>Display credits or disclosures</li>
  <li>Create space for end-screen elements</li>
</ul>

<h3>Outro vs. End Screen</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>YouTube Outro</th><th>End Screen</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Edited into the video file</td><td>Added and positioned in YouTube Studio</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Can contain speech, graphics, credits, and music</td><td>Contains interactive elements such as videos, playlists, Subscribe, channels, or eligible links</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Appears wherever the video file plays</td><td>May not appear on every device or context</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Cannot be hidden separately from the video</td><td>Viewers can hide the end-screen overlays for the current viewing session</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>Outro vs. End Card</h3>
<p>Creators often use "end card" to describe either the designed background at the end of the video or the interactive end-screen feature. The terms are used inconsistently.</p>
<p>For clarity:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Outro:</strong> The complete closing sequence.</li>
  <li><strong>Outro background or end card:</strong> The visual design created in the edit.</li>
  <li><strong>End screen:</strong> The interactive YouTube elements placed over the closing footage.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Outro vs. Credits</h3>
<p>Credits identify contributors, music, sources, locations, and production roles. They can be part of an outro, but an outro does not require formal credits.</p>
<p>Required disclosures should not be hidden in unreadably fast credits.</p>

<h3>Outro vs. Call to Action</h3>
<p>A call to action asks the viewer to do something. An outro can contain one or more calls to action, but a strong ending also completes the video's value.</p>
<p>Do not allow the final minute to become a list of unrelated requests.</p>

<h3>End Screen Requirements</h3>
<p>YouTube's current Help documentation says:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The video must be at least 25 seconds long.</li>
  <li>End screens can appear during the video's final 5–20 seconds.</li>
  <li>Standard 16:9 videos can use up to four elements.</li>
  <li>Other aspect ratios may support fewer elements.</li>
  <li>End screens are unavailable on Made for Kids videos.</li>
  <li>They are unavailable in certain apps, mobile-web contexts, Flash videos, and 360 videos.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Available End-Screen Elements</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Video:</strong> Most recent upload, Best for viewer, or a selected video</li>
  <li><strong>Playlist:</strong> A Public or eligible playlist</li>
  <li><strong>Subscribe:</strong> The channel subscription element</li>
  <li><strong>Channel:</strong> Another channel with a custom message</li>
  <li><strong>External link:</strong> Available under current YouTube Partner Program requirements and policies</li>
</ul>

<h3>End Screens May Display Differently</h3>
<p>YouTube says an end screen may not always appear or may appear differently from the creator's design because the system can adjust it based on performance, viewer behavior, device, and context.</p>
<p>Do not place essential information behind an overlay and assume every viewer sees the same layout.</p>

<h3>Viewers Can Hide End Screens</h3>
<p>YouTube introduced a Hide control that lets viewers dismiss end-screen overlays for the current video and restore them with Show.</p>
<p>The control does not permanently disable end screens across YouTube. It reinforces the need to keep overlays away from important visuals.</p>

<h3>Design the Outro Around the End Screen</h3>
<p>YouTube recommends considering the final 20 seconds during editing and leaving sufficient space and time for the interactive elements.</p>
<p>Plan:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Where the host or main subject appears</li>
  <li>Where video rectangles will sit</li>
  <li>Where the Subscribe circle will appear</li>
  <li>When each element begins</li>
  <li>Whether final text remains readable</li>
  <li>Whether the viewer can understand the recommendation</li>
</ul>

<h3>Do Not Cover the Payoff</h3>
<p>The final result, score, transformation, recipe, artwork, or emotional resolution should not be hidden by end-screen cards.</p>
<p>Deliver the essential payoff first, then move the composition or create clean visual space for the overlays.</p>

<h3>A Strong Outro Structure</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Deliver the final result or conclusion.</li>
  <li>Explain what the viewer should remember.</li>
  <li>Identify one logical next question.</li>
  <li>Recommend one closely matched video or playlist.</li>
  <li>Allow enough time for the viewer to understand and click.</li>
</ol>

<h3>The Next-Video Bridge</h3>
<p>A next-video bridge explains why another video is the natural continuation.</p>
<p>Weak:</p>
<blockquote>Click one of these videos and subscribe.</blockquote>
<p>Stronger:</p>
<blockquote>Now that your microphone is positioned correctly, the next problem is room echo. This guide shows the cheapest fixes first.</blockquote>

<h3>Specific Video vs. Best for Viewer</h3>
<p>YouTube lets the creator select:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The most recent upload</li>
  <li>A specific video</li>
  <li>A video selected by YouTube as Best for viewer</li>
</ul>
<p>Use a specific video when the current video has one clear next step. Test Best for viewer when the audience has several valid paths.</p>

<h3>Video vs. Playlist Recommendation</h3>
<p>A single video is useful for one precise next question. A playlist is useful when the viewer needs an ordered course, topic collection, or continuing series.</p>
<p>Use <a href="/glossary/playlist-traffic/">Playlist Traffic</a> and <a href="/glossary/end-screens-traffic/">End Screens Traffic</a> to evaluate the destination.</p>

<h3>How Long Should the Outro Be?</h3>
<p>There is no required outro length. End screens can use the final 5–20 seconds, but the spoken and edited outro should be only as long as needed to close the video and make the next action clear.</p>
<p>A static 20-second screen is not required merely because the end-screen window supports it.</p>

<h3>The Early-Exit Problem</h3>
<p>Viewers often recognize signals that a video is ending:</p>
<ul>
  <li>"Thanks for watching"</li>
  <li>Long credits</li>
  <li>Music changes</li>
  <li>A static end card</li>
  <li>Several calls to action</li>
  <li>A recap that repeats information</li>
</ul>
<p>When the viewer perceives no remaining value, they may leave before the interactive elements appear.</p>

<h3>Finish the Value While Elements Appear</h3>
<p>One approach is to deliver a useful final sentence, demonstration, or next-step explanation while the end-screen elements become visible.</p>
<p>Do not manufacture filler solely to keep the overlays on screen.</p>

<h3>Outro for Tutorials</h3>
<p>A tutorial outro can:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Show the finished result</li>
  <li>Repeat the most important caution</li>
  <li>Recommend the next skill</li>
  <li>Invite a specific troubleshooting question</li>
</ul>

<h3>Outro for Reviews</h3>
<p>A review ending should clearly state:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Who should buy the product</li>
  <li>Who should avoid it</li>
  <li>The most important tradeoff</li>
  <li>The best alternative or related comparison</li>
</ul>

<h3>Outro for Story Videos</h3>
<p>A story needs emotional and narrative closure before promotional requests.</p>
<p>The ending can then point to:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The next chapter</li>
  <li>A behind-the-scenes video</li>
  <li>A related story</li>
  <li>A playlist containing the full series</li>
</ul>

<h3>Outro for a Video Series</h3>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/video-series/">Video Series</a> can use a recognizable ending structure:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Resolve the episode</li>
  <li>Preview the next confirmed episode</li>
  <li>Link to the previous or next installment</li>
  <li>Preserve consistent end-screen placement</li>
</ul>

<h3>Outro for Shorts</h3>
<p>Shorts do not use the same traditional end-screen workflow as standard long-form videos. A Short ending can still:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Deliver the payoff</li>
  <li>Create a smooth loop back to the first frame</li>
  <li>Use a brief verbal next step</li>
  <li>Point viewers toward an eligible related video</li>
</ul>
<p>A long closing screen can encourage a swipe.</p>

<h3>Subscribe Call to Action</h3>
<p>Explain why subscribing matters:</p>
<blockquote>Subscribe for one practical creator-equipment test every month.</blockquote>
<p>This is clearer than asking for a subscription with no future promise.</p>

<h3>Comment Call to Action</h3>
<p>Ask one relevant question:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Which product should be tested next?</li>
  <li>What step caused the most trouble?</li>
  <li>Which result surprised you?</li>
</ul>
<p>A vague request for "engagement" may produce lower-quality discussion.</p>

<h3>Multiple Calls to Action</h3>
<p>Asking viewers to like, subscribe, comment, buy, join, follow, share, download, and watch another video creates competing decisions.</p>
<p>Choose the action most valuable to the viewer's current intent.</p>

<h3>Credits and Music</h3>
<p>Credits can identify:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Cast and crew</li>
  <li>Music</li>
  <li>Footage and image sources</li>
  <li>Locations</li>
  <li>Research contributors</li>
  <li>Sponsors</li>
</ul>
<p>Keep required information readable and preserve the rights needed for outro music.</p>

<h3>Made for Kids Limitation</h3>
<p>End screens are not available when a video is set as <a href="/glossary/made-for-kids/">Made for Kids</a>. The edited outro remains in the file, but interactive end-screen elements will not appear.</p>

<h3>Suppressed Interactive Elements</h3>
<p>YouTube says card teasers and video watermarks are suppressed while the end screen is displayed.</p>
<p>Do not depend on those elements during the same final window.</p>

<h3>How to Add an End Screen</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Content</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open the video.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Editor</strong>.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>End screen</strong>.</li>
  <li>Add a template or individual elements.</li>
  <li>Adjust timing and placement.</li>
  <li>Preview the video.</li>
  <li>Save the changes.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Measure End-Screen Performance</h3>
<p>YouTube provides:</p>
<ul>
  <li>End screen element click rate for a specific video</li>
  <li>Top end screen element types</li>
  <li>Top videos by end screen</li>
</ul>
<p>Review the Engagement tab and <a href="/glossary/end-screens-traffic/">End Screens Traffic</a>.</p>

<h3>Measure the Outro, Not Only the Click</h3>
<p>Also review:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Retention during the final 30 seconds</li>
  <li>Where the audience begins leaving</li>
  <li>Whether the payoff is covered</li>
  <li>Clicks by destination</li>
  <li>Watch time on the recommended next video</li>
  <li>Viewer comments about the ending</li>
</ul>

<h3>Outro Testing</h3>
<p>Test across comparable future videos:</p>
<ul>
  <li>One recommended video vs. several elements</li>
  <li>Specific video vs. Best for viewer</li>
  <li>Spoken recommendation vs. silent card</li>
  <li>Short outro vs. longer explanation</li>
  <li>Playlist vs. single-video destination</li>
</ul>

<h3>Common YouTube-Outro Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Confusing the outro with the end screen.</li>
  <li>Covering the final payoff with overlays.</li>
  <li>Using a long static screen.</li>
  <li>Recommending an unrelated video.</li>
  <li>Adding too many calls to action.</li>
  <li>Assuming every viewer sees the same end screen.</li>
  <li>Ignoring the viewer's Hide control.</li>
  <li>Using end screens on Made for Kids content.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Is a YouTube outro the same as an end screen?</h4>
<p>No. The outro is edited into the video; the end screen is an interactive YouTube overlay.</p>

<h4>How long can end screens appear?</h4>
<p>YouTube allows them in the final 5–20 seconds of eligible videos.</p>

<h4>How many end-screen elements can I add?</h4>
<p>Standard 16:9 videos can currently use up to four, while other aspect ratios may allow fewer.</p>

<h4>Can viewers hide end screens?</h4>
<p>Yes. Viewers can dismiss the overlays for the current video and restore them with Show.</p>
