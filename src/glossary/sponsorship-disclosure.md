<h2>Sponsorship Disclosure</h2>
<p>A <strong>sponsorship disclosure</strong> is a clear statement telling viewers that a creator has a commercial or other material relationship with a brand connected to the content. It helps the audience understand that money, products, travel, services, commissions, employment, family ties, or another benefit may have influenced the message.</p>
<p>A disclosure is not an apology or a warning that the content is dishonest. It provides context viewers may use when deciding how much weight to give a recommendation, demonstration, review, or endorsement.</p>
<h3>When is a sponsorship disclosure needed?</h3>
<p>A disclosure may be needed when a creator:</p>
<ul>
<li>Is paid to mention, demonstrate, review, or promote a brand</li>
<li>Receives a free or discounted product or service</li>
<li>Receives travel, lodging, event admission, meals, or other perks</li>
<li>Earns affiliate commissions from links or codes</li>
<li>Has an employment, ownership, family, or personal relationship with the brand</li>
<li>Receives a licensing fee or payment for brand use of the content</li>
<li>Participates in an ambassador or long-term <a href="/glossary/sponsorship/">sponsorship</a></li>
<li>Creates a <a href="/glossary/sponsored-video/">sponsored video</a> or sponsored segment</li>
<li>Posts about a product received through a gifting campaign</li>
</ul>
<p>The need for disclosure depends on the actual connection and applicable law, not only the label written on the contract. A campaign described as a “collaboration,” “partnership,” or “gift” can still require disclosure.</p>
<h3>What should a good disclosure communicate?</h3>
<p>A useful sponsorship disclosure answers two questions:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Who is connected to the creator?</strong></li>
<li><strong>What is the nature of that connection?</strong></li>
</ol>
<p>Clear examples can include:</p>
<ul>
<li>“This video is sponsored by Acme.”</li>
<li>“Acme paid me to create this video.”</li>
<li>“Thanks to Acme for providing the product for free.”</li>
<li>“I earn a commission if you purchase through these links.”</li>
<li>“This trip was paid for by Acme.”</li>
<li>“I work for Acme.”</li>
<li>“Acme is my company.”</li>
</ul>
<p>The exact wording can vary. The goal is for an ordinary viewer to understand the relevant relationship without decoding abbreviations or clicking another page.</p>
<h3>Where should the disclosure appear?</h3>
<h4>Long-form video</h4>
<p>Place the disclosure in the video close to the sponsored message. If the paid integration begins later, disclose immediately before or as the integration begins rather than relying only on an announcement viewers may have heard much earlier.</p>
<p>An on-screen disclosure plus a spoken disclosure is generally more noticeable than either format alone. The description can repeat the information but should not be the only place viewers can find it.</p>
<h4>YouTube Shorts and other short video</h4>
<p>Make the disclosure visible and understandable within the content. Avoid placing tiny text where interface buttons or captions can cover it. Give viewers enough time to read any on-screen wording.</p>
<h4>Livestream</h4>
<p>A one-time disclosure at the beginning can be missed by viewers who join later. Repeat the disclosure periodically and when the sponsored segment or product is discussed. A persistent on-screen label can provide additional context when it remains readable.</p>
<h4>Description, caption, or community post</h4>
<p>Place the disclosure before viewers must click “more” and close to the endorsement or link it explains. Do not bury it after a long block of hashtags, affiliate links, or unrelated text.</p>
<h4>Affiliate links</h4>
<p>Explain that the creator may earn money or a commission. “Affiliate link” alone may not communicate the financial result to every viewer as clearly as “I earn a commission from qualifying purchases.”</p>

<p>Creators may need all three layers: contractual instructions, a platform declaration, and a clear viewer-facing statement.</p>
<h3>Weak or unclear disclosure wording</h3>
<p>The meaning may be unclear when a creator uses only:</p>
<ul>
<li>“Thanks Acme”</li>
<li>“Partner”</li>
<li>“Collab”</li>
<li>“Ambassador”</li>
<li>“Spon” or “SP”</li>
<li>A brand tag with no explanation</li>
<li>A discount code with no commission statement</li>
<li>A disclosure hidden at the bottom of the description</li>
<li>A disclosure shown after the endorsement is already complete</li>
<li>Tiny on-screen text that disappears too quickly</li>
</ul>
<p>Terms such as “ad,” “advertisement,” “sponsored,” or a direct sentence explaining the payment or free product are usually easier to understand than insider language.</p>
<h3>Sponsorship disclosure on YouTube</h3>
<p>YouTube requires creators to select the paid-promotion box when their content contains an applicable paid product placement, sponsorship, endorsement, or other commercial relationship. YouTube then displays its own disclosure message near the beginning of the video.</p>
<p>That label does not remove the need to evaluate the creator's own disclosure. YouTube states that creators and brands are responsible for understanding when, how, and to whom their local laws require disclosure.</p>
<p>The <a href="/glossary/approval-process/">approval process</a> should include a disclosure check before publication. The brand should confirm required factual information, while the creator should confirm that the final placement remains noticeable in the actual platform experience.</p>
<h3>A practical disclosure checklist</h3>
<p>Before publishing, confirm that the disclosure:</p>
<ul>
<li>Identifies the sponsor or relevant brand when needed</li>
<li>Explains the relationship in ordinary language</li>
<li>Appears before or with the endorsement</li>
<li>Is easy to see, hear, and understand</li>
<li>Is not hidden behind a “more” button</li>
<li>Uses the same language as the sponsored message</li>
<li>Remains visible long enough to read</li>
<li>Is repeated when a livestream or long video requires it</li>
<li>Is included in each standalone sponsored post</li>
<li>Is supplemented by the platform's required paid-promotion tool</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is #ad always enough?</h4>
<p>Not automatically. The wording must be noticeable and understandable in context. A clear #ad near the beginning of a text post may work, while the same tag buried among many hashtags, hidden below “more,” or displayed too briefly in a video may be easy to miss.</p>
<h4>Is a disclosure needed for a free product?</h4>
<p>It can be. A free or discounted product is a material benefit. If the creator mentions or endorses the product, viewers may need to know it was provided by the brand.</p>
<h4>Is putting “sponsored” in the description enough for a video?</h4>
<p>A description disclosure is useful, but it may not be enough by itself because viewers can watch without opening the description. Put the disclosure in the video near the sponsored message as well.</p>
<h4>Does every post in a campaign need its own disclosure?</h4>
<p>Each sponsored post should be understandable when viewed by itself. Do not assume viewers saw an earlier post or already know about the brand relationship.</p>
