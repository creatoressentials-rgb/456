<h2>Digital Product</h2>
<p>A <strong>digital product</strong> is a product delivered, downloaded, streamed, accessed, or used electronically rather than shipped as a physical item.</p>
<p>Examples include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ebook</li>
<li>Template</li>
<li>Preset</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/lut/">LUT</a></li>
<li>Spreadsheet</li>
<li>Printable</li>
<li>Digital art</li>
<li>Stock photograph</li>
<li>Sound effect</li>
<li>Music track</li>
<li>Video course</li>
<li>Software</li>
<li>Plugin</li>
<li>Mobile app</li>
<li>Membership resource</li>
<li>Paid newsletter archive</li>
<li>Online guide</li>
<li>Downloadable pattern</li>
<li>Virtual ticket</li>
<li>License key</li>
</ul>
<p>A digital product can be sold once, licensed repeatedly, bundled with services, or offered through a subscription.</p>
<h3>Downloadable vs. access-based digital products</h3>
<h4>Downloadable product</h4>
<p>The customer receives a file.</p>
<p>Examples:</p>
<ul>
<li>PDF</li>
<li>ZIP archive</li>
<li>Video file</li>
<li>Audio file</li>
<li>Image</li>
<li>Spreadsheet</li>
<li>Template</li>
<li>Preset</li>
<li>Software installer</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator should define how many downloads are allowed and whether future updates are included.</p>
<h4>Access-based product</h4>
<p>The customer receives access through:</p>
<ul>
<li>Account</li>
<li>App</li>
<li>Website</li>
<li>Course platform</li>
<li>Membership portal</li>
<li>Private feed</li>
<li>Cloud software</li>
<li>Streaming service</li>
</ul>
<p>The product may stop being available when the subscription ends or the platform closes.</p>
<h4>Hybrid product</h4>
<p>The customer receives both files and ongoing access, support, community, or updates.</p>

<p>A course can be a digital product when customers receive the same recorded material. One-on-one coaching is primarily a service. A course with live coaching is a hybrid.</p>
<h3>Digital product vs. physical product</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>

  <th>Physical product</th>
</tr>
</thead>

<p>Digital products still have costs, including software, hosting, support, marketing, payment fees, updates, and refunds.</p>
<h3>Does the customer own a digital product?</h3>
<p>The customer may own the downloaded copy or device on which it is stored, but the creator normally retains copyright unless the agreement transfers it.</p>
<p>The customer usually receives a license defining allowed use.</p>
<p>A template license might allow:</p>
<ul>
<li>Personal use</li>
<li>One business use</li>
<li>Commercial client work</li>
<li>Unlimited end products</li>
<li>Team use</li>
<li>Modification</li>
</ul>
<p>It might prohibit:</p>
<ul>
<li>Resale of the source file</li>
<li>Sharing login credentials</li>
<li>Redistribution</li>
<li>Uploading to a public library</li>
<li>Claiming authorship</li>
<li>Using the asset for artificial-intelligence training</li>
<li>Creating a competing template product</li>
</ul>
<p>The product page and license should match.</p>
<h3>Common digital product business models</h3>
<h4>One-time purchase</h4>
<p>The customer pays once for a file or access package.</p>
<h4>Subscription</h4>
<p>The customer pays regularly for software, content, updates, community, or a resource library.</p>
<h4>Membership</h4>
<p>The customer receives continuing access to a collection, community, events, or benefits.</p>
<h4>Freemium</h4>
<p>The creator offers a free version and charges for advanced features or content.</p>
<h4>Tiered license</h4>
<p>Different prices apply to personal, commercial, team, or extended use.</p>
<h4>Bundle</h4>
<p>Several products are sold together.</p>
<h4>Pay what you want</h4>
<p>The creator sets a minimum price or lets buyers choose the amount.</p>
<h4>Lifetime access</h4>
<p>The customer receives access with no ordinary expiration. The creator should define whether this means the customer's lifetime, product lifetime, business lifetime, or platform availability.</p>
<h3>Digital product fulfillment</h3>
<p>A creator can deliver products through:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ecommerce platform</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/creator-storefront">Creator storefront</a></li>
<li>Course platform</li>
<li>Membership platform</li>
<li>Email</li>
<li>Download application</li>
<li>Cloud storage</li>
<li>Software license server</li>
<li>App store</li>
<li>Marketplace</li>
<li>Private RSS feed</li>
</ul>
<p>Shopify notes that digital products are often delivered immediately after purchase and can require a digital-download application or other fulfillment setup.</p>

<p>Consider:</p>
<ul>
<li>Customer outcome</li>
<li>Specificity</li>
<li>Time saved</li>
<li>Difficulty of creating an alternative</li>
<li>Quality</li>
<li>Audience purchasing power</li>
<li>Support burden</li>
<li>Updates</li>

<li>Competition</li>

<li>Bundling</li>
<li>Customer acquisition cost</li>

<p>Low duplication cost does not mean the product has no cost or should be cheap.</p>
<h3>Digital product refunds</h3>
<p>Digital products create special refund questions because the customer may access or copy the product immediately.</p>
<p>A clear policy can address:</p>
<ul>
<li>Accidental duplicate purchase</li>
<li>Broken or corrupted file</li>
<li>Misleading description</li>
<li>Technical incompatibility</li>
<li>Downloaded versus undownloaded order</li>
<li>Subscription cancellation</li>
<li>Refund deadline</li>
<li>Fraud</li>
<li>Chargeback</li>
<li>Revocation of access</li>
<li>Consumer-law requirements</li>
</ul>
<p>A “no refunds under any circumstances” statement may not override applicable consumer law or a failure to deliver the promised product.</p>
<h3>Digital product taxes</h3>
<p>Digital-product tax treatment varies substantially.</p>
<p>Stripe's tax documentation distinguishes digital goods and electronically supplied services and uses product classifications to determine applicable tax rules.</p>
<p>In the United States:</p>
<ul>
<li>There is no single federal sales-tax rule for every digital product.</li>
<li>States define taxable digital goods differently.</li>
<li>Customer location can matter.</li>
<li>Software, downloads, subscriptions, and electronically supplied services can be classified differently.</li>
</ul>
<p>International sales can involve VAT, GST, or other consumption taxes based on customer location.</p>
<p>Creators should classify each product accurately and obtain tax advice for their business.</p>
<h3>Digital product intellectual property</h3>
<p>A digital product can contain:</p>
<ul>
<li>Copyrighted text</li>
<li>Artwork</li>
<li>Photographs</li>
<li>Music</li>
<li>Video</li>
<li>Software code</li>
<li>Fonts</li>
<li>Trademarks</li>
<li>Licensed stock</li>
<li>Guest contributions</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator should confirm ownership or permission for every included element.</p>
<p>Buying an asset does not automatically permit the creator to repackage and resell the source file.</p>
<h3>Piracy and access control</h3>
<p>Creators can reduce unauthorized distribution through:</p>
<ul>
<li>License keys</li>
<li>Account access</li>
<li>Watermarking</li>
<li>Download limits</li>
<li>Customer names on files</li>
<li>Terms of use</li>
<li>Monitoring</li>
<li>Takedown requests</li>
<li>Unique links</li>
<li>Software activation</li>
</ul>
<p>No method eliminates copying. A practical strategy balances protection with a good customer experience.</p>
<h3>Maintaining a digital product</h3>
<p>Ongoing work can include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Compatibility updates</li>
<li>Broken-link repair</li>
<li>Customer support</li>
<li>Platform migration</li>
<li>Security patches</li>
<li>Version updates</li>
<li>Corrected instructions</li>
<li>New examples</li>
<li>Accessibility improvements</li>
<li>License clarification</li>
<li>Tax and checkout maintenance</li>
</ul>
<p>A digital product can become outdated even when the underlying file never changes.</p>
<h3>Digital product vs. creator content</h3>
<p>Free content attracts, educates, and builds trust. A digital product provides a packaged outcome or resource that customers purchase.</p>
<p>A creator can turn recurring audience needs into products such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>Editing checklist</li>
<li>Tax spreadsheet</li>
<li>Thumbnail template</li>
<li>Camera settings guide</li>
<li>Course</li>
<li>Preset pack</li>
<li>Script framework</li>
<li>Notion workspace</li>
<li>Printable planner</li>
<li>Contract template prepared with appropriate professional review</li>
</ul>
<p>The product should provide value beyond merely copying material already available for free.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is an online course a digital product?</h4>
<p>A self-paced recorded course is generally a digital product. Live coaching or custom feedback adds a service component.</p>
<h4>Are digital products automatically delivered immediately?</h4>
<p>Many are, but delivery depends on the platform and product. Some require manual approval, scheduled access, or account setup.</p>
<h4>Does buying a digital product transfer copyright?</h4>
<p>Usually no. The customer generally receives a license or access right unless the agreement expressly transfers copyright.</p>
<h4>Are digital products subject to sales tax?</h4>
<p>Sometimes. Rules vary by jurisdiction, product classification, customer location, and delivery method.</p>
