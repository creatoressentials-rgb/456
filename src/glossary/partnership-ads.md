<h2>Partnership Ads</h2>
<p><strong>Partnership ads</strong> are a Meta advertising format that lets an advertiser run paid ads on Facebook or Instagram using the identity, content, or visible partnership of a creator, brand, or other business partner.</p>
<p>A creator may authorize the brand to promote an existing post, Story, or Reel, or to use approved partnership-ad permissions for eligible advertising. The resulting ad can display the partnership rather than appearing only as an ordinary ad from the brand's account.</p>
<p>Partnership ads are a Meta-specific tool. They are not another name for every <a href="/glossary/sponsored-video">sponsored video</a>, collaboration post, or paid creator campaign.</p>
<h3>How partnership ads work</h3>
<p>A typical workflow is:</p>
<ol>
<li>The creator and brand negotiate the campaign and <a href="/glossary/paid-usage">paid usage</a>.</li>
<li>The creator publishes eligible branded content or approves another supported ad setup.</li>
<li>The creator grants the advertiser permission through Meta.</li>
<li>The advertiser builds and runs the ad in Ads Manager.</li>
<li>The ad uses the permitted partnership identity or content.</li>
<li>The creator and brand monitor the campaign.</li>
<li>Permission is revoked or expires according to the agreement.</li>
</ol>
<p>The exact interface and eligibility can change. Meta's Help Center should be treated as the source of truth for the current setup steps.</p>
<h3>Partnership ad permission types</h3>
<h4>Content-level permission</h4>
<p>The creator allows an advertiser to promote a specific piece of content, such as one post, Story, or Reel.</p>
<p>This is narrower than general account access and can be easier to align with one campaign's licensed asset.</p>
<h4>Account-level permission</h4>
<p>The creator approves a broader relationship at the account level. Depending on Meta's available features and the permission granted, the advertiser may be able to create or run eligible partnership ads involving that partner identity.</p>
<p>Because the permission is broader, the contract should carefully limit what the advertiser is authorized to do.</p>
<h4>Partnership ad code</h4>
<p>The creator generates a code that the advertiser can enter when building an ad. The code authorizes use of specified creator content under Meta's system.</p>
<p>Meta notes that creators can generate codes for content, including certain archived Instagram content. Archiving a post does not necessarily revoke code access, so creators should manage the permission directly.</p>

<h3>What creators should negotiate</h3>
<p>Before granting permission, define:</p>
<ul>
<li>The exact advertiser and ad account</li>
<li>The permitted content</li>
<li>Account-level or content-level access</li>
<li>Platforms covered</li>
<li>Campaign start and end dates</li>
<li>Territory and audience</li>
<li>Advertising objectives</li>
<li>Maximum or expected ad spend</li>
<li>Whether the brand can change copy, headlines, links, or calls to action</li>
<li>Whether the brand can create new ad versions</li>
<li>Whether the creator's profile image, name, and handle can appear</li>
<li>Restricted claims or product categories</li>
<li>Creator review of final ad creative</li>
<li>Reporting access</li>
<li>Compensation and renewal fees</li>
<li>Permission revocation date</li>
<li>Treatment of ads after the license ends</li>
<li>Responsibility for policy violations or rejected ads</li>
</ul>
<p>A technical permission should not be broader or longer than the contract.</p>
<h3>Can creators revoke partnership-ad permissions?</h3>
<p>Meta says partners can revoke partnership-ad permissions. The creator can manage permissions through the available Instagram, Facebook, or Meta business tools.</p>
<p>Revocation has two separate dimensions:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Technical control:</strong> Whether Meta still lets the advertiser run the ad</li>
<li><strong>Contractual obligation:</strong> Whether the creator promised to maintain permission for a defined term</li>
</ul>
<p>A creator who revokes permission early may breach the contract, while a brand that continues running ads after the licensed term may exceed its contractual <a href="/glossary/paid-usage">paid usage</a>.</p>
<h3>Partnership ads and disclosure</h3>
<p>Partnership ads are paid advertisements and are subject to Meta's advertising rules. The underlying creator content may also need a paid partnership label or other <a href="/glossary/sponsorship-disclosure">sponsorship disclosure</a>, depending on the content and applicable rules.</p>
<p>The platform label and ad format do not excuse false or unsupported product claims.</p>
<h3>Partnership-ad risks for creators</h3>
<p>Creators should consider:</p>
<ul>
<li>The brand controls targeting and spend</li>
<li>Ads can reach audiences outside the creator's normal followers</li>
<li>The content may run longer than the organic post remains relevant</li>
<li>New headlines or landing pages can change the message</li>
<li>Negative comments may become associated with the creator</li>
<li>Ad performance can influence future campaign negotiations</li>
<li>Music or third-party content cleared for organic posting may not be cleared for advertising</li>
<li>Regulated products can create additional compliance risk</li>
<li>Account-level permission may be broader than one post</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Are partnership ads the same as branded-content posts?</h4>
<p>No. A branded-content post can remain organic. A partnership ad uses paid advertising distribution and requires the appropriate Meta permissions.</p>
<h4>Does a paid partnership label let the brand run ads?</h4>
<p>Not automatically. The advertiser needs partnership-ad permission through Meta and should also have the required contractual paid-usage rights.</p>
<h4>Can a creator revoke partnership-ad access?</h4>
<p>Meta says partners can revoke permissions. The creator should still follow any contract requiring access for a specified campaign term.</p>
<h4>Does archiving an Instagram post stop an ad code?</h4>
<p>Meta warns that archiving content does not necessarily remove the advertiser's ad-code access. Revoke or manage the permission directly.</p>
