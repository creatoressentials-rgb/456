<h2>Shorts Monetization Module</h2>
<p>The <strong>Shorts Monetization Module</strong> is an optional YouTube Partner Program agreement that lets eligible creators share revenue from ads shown between videos in the Shorts Feed and from YouTube Premium members watching eligible Shorts.</p>
<p>Revenue sharing begins on the date the creator accepts the module. Eligible Shorts views accumulated before acceptance are not paid retroactively through the Shorts revenue-sharing system.</p>

<h3>What Does the Module Unlock?</h3>
<ul>
  <li><a href="/glossary/shorts-feed-ads/">Shorts Feed ad revenue</a></li>
  <li><a href="/glossary/youtube-premium-revenue/">YouTube Premium revenue</a> from eligible Shorts viewing</li>
  <li>Estimated daily Shorts revenue reporting in YouTube Analytics</li>
  <li>Participation in the Creator Pool allocation system</li>
</ul>
<p>It does not unlock Watch Page advertising on long-form videos or fan-funding products. Those use separate modules.</p>

<h3>Who Can Accept the Shorts Module?</h3>
<p>The creator must qualify for the full advertising and Premium revenue level of the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a>, accept YouTube's Base Terms, and receive approval into YPP.</p>
<p>Meeting the lower expanded-YPP threshold for memberships and Supers does not automatically provide Shorts Feed ad revenue.</p>

<h3>How Do You Accept It?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Earn</strong>.</li>
  <li>Find the Shorts Feed Ads option.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Get started</strong>.</li>
  <li>Review the Shorts Monetization Module.</li>
  <li>Accept the terms.</li>
</ol>
<p>Revenue sharing begins from the acceptance date for eligible engaged views. No separate ad switch is required for every individual eligible Short in the same way long-form Watch Page ads are turned on per video.</p>

<h3>Does the Module Pay for Earlier Views?</h3>
<p>No. YouTube explicitly says Shorts views accrued before acceptance are not eligible for Shorts ad revenue sharing.</p>
<p>After acceptance, eligible future engaged views on older Shorts can be considered. The key date is when the eligible view occurs, not simply when the Short was uploaded.</p>

<h3>How Shorts Ad Sharing Works</h3>
<ol>
  <li>YouTube pools monthly revenue from ads shown between Shorts.</li>
  <li>The system calculates the Creator Pool after accounting for music licensing.</li>
  <li>The pool is allocated to monetizing creators according to their share of eligible engaged views within each country.</li>
  <li>Creators receive 45% of the revenue allocated to them.</li>
</ol>
<p>The module does not give a creator the revenue from one specific ad shown after one specific Short. It provides participation in a pooled allocation model.</p>

<h3>What Is the Creator Revenue Share?</h3>
<p>YouTube states that creators receive <strong>45% of the revenue allocated to them</strong> from the Creator Pool.</p>
<p>The 45% is applied after:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Eligible Shorts Feed ad revenue is pooled</li>
  <li>Music-licensing allocation is calculated</li>
  <li>The creator's country-level eligible engaged-view share is determined</li>
</ul>
<p>It is not 45% of all worldwide Shorts ad revenue or the gross price paid by an advertiser.</p>

<h3>How Does Music Affect Revenue?</h3>
<p>Music usage affects the amount of associated revenue entering the Creator Pool. Under YouTube's published examples:</p>
<ul>
  <li>No music: all associated revenue enters the Creator Pool.</li>
  <li>One music track: half enters the pool and half covers music licensing.</li>
  <li>Two music tracks: one third enters the pool and two thirds cover music licensing.</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube says the creator still receives the same 45% share of their final allocation whether or not the creator's individual Short used music.</p>

<h3>What Are Eligible Engaged Views?</h3>
<p>YouTube uses eligible <a href="/glossary/engaged-views/">engaged views</a> to calculate payments. Views can be excluded when they involve:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Non-original Shorts</li>
  <li>Reuploaded or minimally changed third-party content</li>
  <li>Artificial or fake viewing activity</li>
  <li>Content inconsistent with advertiser-friendly guidelines</li>
  <li>Creators who have not accepted the module</li>
  <li>Other content or rights conditions that make the views ineligible</li>
</ul>

<h3>Shorts Premium Revenue</h3>
<p>The module also covers Premium revenue from eligible Shorts. YouTube allocates Premium revenue using the creator's share of subscription Shorts views within each country and uses a portion to cover music-licensing costs.</p>
<p>YouTube states that creators receive 45% of the net Premium revenue allocated to their Shorts.</p>

<h3>Shorts Module vs. Watch Page Module</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Shorts Module</th><th>Watch Page Module</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Primary content</td><td>Eligible Shorts in the Shorts Feed</td><td>Eligible long-form and live videos</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Ad model</td><td>Pooled ads shown between Shorts</td><td>Ads displayed or streamed on Watch Pages</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Creator share</td><td>45% of allocated Creator Pool revenue</td><td>55% of net qualifying Watch Page ad revenue</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Premium</td><td>Eligible Premium Shorts views</td><td>Eligible Premium long-form and live views</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Start date</td><td>Eligible views from module-acceptance date</td><td>According to active module and monetization settings</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>Does Every Short Earn After Acceptance?</h3>
<p>No. Acceptance creates the contractual right to participate, but each Short and view must remain eligible.</p>
<p>Possible reasons for no revenue include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The Short received no eligible engaged views.</li>
  <li>The content is non-original or reused.</li>
  <li>The views were artificial or invalid.</li>
  <li>The Short does not follow advertiser-friendly guidelines.</li>
  <li>Copyright or music-rights conditions block eligibility.</li>
  <li>The views occurred before module acceptance.</li>
  <li>Revenue data has not finished processing.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Three-Minute Shorts and Claimed Content</h3>
<p>YouTube's current policy says Shorts over one minute that contain claimed content are blocked and ineligible for monetization. Longer Shorts must meet the platform's current upload-date, music, rights, and format rules.</p>
<p>Accepting the module does not override a claim or make an otherwise blocked Short monetizable.</p>

<h3>How Earnings Appear in Analytics</h3>
<p>From the date the creator begins Shorts monetization, YouTube Analytics can display estimated daily Shorts Feed ad revenue and other Shorts performance metrics.</p>
<p>Shorts Feed ad revenue can include estimated Shorts Feed ad and YouTube Premium revenue for the selected date range. Finalized earnings later appear in AdSense for YouTube.</p>

<h3>Can Creators Opt Out?</h3>
<p>Yes. YouTube says creators can opt out of the Shorts Monetization Module by contacting Creator Support.</p>
<p>Opting out stops future related revenue sharing. It does not create a selective per-Short monetization switch or retroactively erase finalized earnings.</p>

<h3>What Happens If a Creator Leaves YPP?</h3>
<p>A creator outside YPP is not entitled to Shorts Feed ad or Premium revenue under the module. YouTube may still display ads under its general Terms of Service without paying a former or non-partner uploader.</p>
<p>Rejoining requires meeting the current eligibility and policy requirements and accepting the applicable terms again when directed.</p>

<h3>Shorts Module vs. Shorts Fund</h3>
<p>The Shorts revenue-sharing model replaced the former YouTube Shorts Fund as the primary platform ad-sharing system. The module is not an application for a discretionary monthly bonus.</p>
<p>Creator earnings now depend on eligible engaged-view allocation and the pooled revenue-sharing model.</p>

<h3>Common Shorts Module Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Expecting retroactive revenue for pre-acceptance views.</li>
  <li>Assuming every public Short is eligible.</li>
  <li>Applying 45% before Creator Pool allocation.</li>
  <li>Believing music lowers the creator's final 45% rate.</li>
  <li>Using public view count instead of eligible engaged views.</li>
  <li>Confusing the Shorts module with the Watch Page or Commerce modules.</li>
  <li>Assuming acceptance removes copyright or reused-content restrictions.</li>
  <li>Treating the module as the old Shorts Fund.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>When does Shorts revenue sharing begin?</h4>
<p>It begins for eligible views on the date the creator accepts the Shorts Monetization Module.</p>

<h4>Can earlier Shorts views be paid retroactively?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube says views before module acceptance are not eligible for Shorts ad revenue sharing.</p>

<h4>What percentage do creators keep?</h4>
<p>Creators keep 45% of the revenue allocated to them from the Creator Pool.</p>

<h4>Does the module cover Premium revenue?</h4>
<p>Yes. It covers eligible Shorts Feed advertising and YouTube Premium revenue.</p>
