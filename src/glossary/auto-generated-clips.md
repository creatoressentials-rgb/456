<h2>Auto-Generated Clips</h2>
<p><strong>Auto-Generated Clips</strong> refers to YouTube's AI-assisted systems for surfacing short, "clippable" moments from longer videos and livestreams — most notably the <strong>AI-powered suggested clips</strong> inside the Video Clips tool in <a href="/glossary/youtube-studio/">YouTube Studio</a>, which scans a creator's own long-form video and recommends highlight segments a creator can publish as standalone videos. It's distinct from the older, manual, viewer-created "Clip" button, and distinct from <a href="/glossary/auto-generated-shorts/">Auto-Generated Shorts</a>, a separate AI feature that turns livestream highlights into vertical Shorts.</p>
<p>Because YouTube has shipped several overlapping "clip" features over the past few years, the terminology gets muddled fast. This entry untangles what's actually automated, what's manual, what changed in 2026, and what it means for creators trying to grow a channel.</p>

<h3>How Auto-Generated Clips actually work</h3>
<ol>
<li>A creator opens an existing long-form video (or an archived livestream) in <strong>YouTube Studio → Content → Videos</strong>, then selects the <strong>Clips</strong> tab and <strong>Create video clip</strong>.</li>
<li>If the video qualifies for AI suggestions, YouTube surfaces a list of <strong>AI-powered suggested clips</strong> — highlight moments it has already identified — along with an AI-generated outline of the transcript to help the creator navigate the source video.</li>
<li>The creator can accept a suggested clip as-is, or adjust its start/end point using the transcript or <a href="/glossary/timeline/">timeline</a>. Manual clipping (selecting transcript text, or setting start/end points by word or timestamp) is also available for every video, whether or not AI suggestions are offered.</li>
<li>The result is uploaded like any other video, complete with its own title, description, and upload dialog — including the option to add an intro/outro clip.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Eligibility note:</strong> AI-powered suggested clips are currently limited to <strong>English-language videos in a podcast playlist</strong>, and only in the US, Canada, Brazil, Mexico, the UK, Spain, Germany, France, India, and Korea. Manual clipping via the same tool, however, is available globally, for all videos, to all creators.</p>
<p>YouTube has also announced plans to expand this system — rolling out <strong>Video Clips to Shorts</strong> and broader <strong>auto-suggestions for "clippable" moments</strong> (beyond podcasts) later in 2026 — though no firm date has been confirmed.</p>

<h3>Does clipping hurt the original creator, or count against their metrics?</h3>
<p><strong>Video Clips tool:</strong> No penalty. YouTube's own Help Center confirms creators are not penalized for re-using or re-uploading their own footage as a clip — its <a href="/glossary/reused-content/">reused content</a> and <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a> policies aren't triggered by clipping your own video. Because a clip becomes a genuine new video, it generates its own <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">ad revenue</a>, can appear in <a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">suggested videos</a>, and contributes its own <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">traffic sources</a> and <a href="/glossary/unique-viewers/">unique viewers</a> — it does not "borrow" or dilute the original video's numbers.</p>
<p><strong>Legacy manual Clips:</strong> Since a Clip was never a separate video — just a link that plays a segment from the original's watch page — it was not an independently monetized asset, and there's no public documentation of it being tracked as a distinct line item in <a href="/glossary/youtube-advanced-mode/">YouTube Analytics</a> separate from the source video. Treat any specific claim about "how Clip views get counted" with caution, as YouTube has not published exact mechanics for that legacy feature — and it's now largely moot, since new Clips can no longer be created.</p>

<h3>What changed in April 2026 (and why it matters if you're reading older guides)</h3>
<p>If you've seen instructions describing a "Clip" button below a video's Subscribe button — tap it, drag a slider, hit Share — that flow is being phased out. On April 16–17, 2026, YouTube confirmed it is discontinuing the ability to create <strong>new</strong> viewer-made Clips, in favor of <strong>Share at Timestamp</strong>, a simpler deep link to a specific moment in a video, now rolling out to mobile in addition to desktop. Existing Clips remain viewable, but the creation flow itself is gone.</p>
<p>In its place, YouTube is investing in creator-facing tools: the Video Clips tool already covered above, plus a stated plan to extend it to Shorts and widen AI auto-suggestions beyond podcasts later in 2026. For everyday "clip this moment and send it to a friend" sharing, YouTube points people to Share at Timestamp or to the broader ecosystem of third-party clipping tools that has grown up around YouTube content.</p>

<h3>Why this matters for channel growth</h3>
<p>For creators, the practical upside of Auto-Generated Clips is repurposing: a single long-form upload — a podcast episode, an interview, a livestream VOD — can be mined for several standalone clip-videos without manual editing, each one a new discovery surface with its own thumbnail, title, and shot at <a href="/glossary/browse-features/">browse features</a> or search traffic. Because each clip is monetized like any other video, a good clip can meaningfully add to <a href="/glossary/monetized-playbacks/">monetized playbacks</a> and shift a channel's blended <a href="/glossary/youtube-rpm/">YouTube RPM</a> and <a href="/glossary/playback-based-cpm/">playback-based CPM</a>, especially if clips convert new viewers into <a href="/glossary/regular-viewers/">regular viewers</a> or <a href="/glossary/returning-viewers/">returning viewers</a> of the full-length source video.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Is "Auto-Generated Clips" the same as <a href="/glossary/youtube-shorts/">YouTube Shorts</a>?</h4>
<p>No. The Video Clips tool (with AI-powered suggested clips) publishes standard 16:9 videos, not Shorts. YouTube's separate Auto-Generated Shorts feature is what turns livestream highlights into vertical Shorts — a different tool, a different output format, and a different source (livestreams specifically).</p>
<h4>Can viewers still clip my videos?</h4>
<p>Not through the old Clip button — YouTube discontinued the ability to create new viewer-made Clips starting in April 2026. Existing Clips made before the change remain viewable. Viewers can still share a specific moment using Share at Timestamp, and creators can publish their own clips via the Video Clips tool in Studio.</p>
<h4>Do I get penalized for publishing a clip of my own video?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube explicitly states there's no copyright or reused-content penalty for re-using your own footage as a clip through the Video Clips tool.</p>
<h4>Why don't I see AI-suggested clips on my videos?</h4>
<p>AI-powered suggested clips are currently limited to English-language videos placed in a podcast playlist, and only for creators in the US, Canada, Brazil, Mexico, the UK, Spain, Germany, France, India, and Korea. Outside those conditions, you can still clip manually using the same tool — you just won't see AI suggestions yet.</p>
