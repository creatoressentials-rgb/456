<h2>Channel Permissions</h2>
<p><strong>Channel permissions</strong> are YouTube Studio's built-in system for giving other people — editors, managers, translators, agencies, family members, business partners — a defined level of access to your channel without ever handing over your Google Account password. Instead of one shared login, each person signs in with their own Google Account and gets a role that determines exactly what they can see and do.</p>

<h3>What channel permissions actually control</h3>
<p>When you grant someone channel permissions, you're deciding what they can do in three places: YouTube Studio (desktop), the YouTube Studio app, and YouTube itself. Depending on their role, that can include uploading and publishing videos, replying to comments, going live, viewing <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">ad revenue</a> and analytics, or just looking at performance data without touching anything.</p>
<p>This matters because YouTube channels increasingly run like small businesses: a creator edits, someone else uploads and schedules, a third person handles comments and community posts, and a translator adds captions. Channel permissions let all of that happen without a single shared password floating around in a group chat.</p>
<p><strong>You do not need a Brand Account to use channel permissions.</strong> This is an important correction to a lot of older guidance still circulating: for several years now, channel permissions have worked directly on personal YouTube channels through YouTube Studio &gt; Settings &gt; Permissions. If your channel still runs on legacy Brand Account roles, YouTube will prompt you to "Move Permissions" over to the current system — but converting to a Brand Account first is not a requirement.</p>

<p>A few details that don't fit neatly in the table:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Manager</strong> can do almost everything an Owner can, including inviting or removing other people's access, but cannot delete the channel itself.</li>
<li><strong>Editor</strong> can edit and publish nearly everything but cannot delete already-published content or the channel, cannot manage permissions, and cannot enter into contracts or delete completed/live streams and stream keys.</li>
<li><strong>Editor (Limited)</strong> and <strong>Viewer (Limited)</strong> are identical to their unrestricted counterparts except for one thing: they're blocked from all revenue data, including <a href="/glossary/super-chat/">Super Chat and Super Stickers</a> revenue and the Viewer activity tab in Analytics. These are the roles to use for freelancers, interns, or short-term collaborators who don't need to see how much the channel earns.</li>
<li><strong>Subtitle Editor</strong> exists specifically for translators and captioners: it opens only the subtitles section and nothing else, making it useful for channels running <a href="/glossary/multi-language-audio/">multi-language audio</a> or <a href="/glossary/auto-dubbing/">auto-dubbing</a> workflows that need outside language help without any other access.</li>
<li>Only the <strong>Owner</strong> and <strong>Manager</strong> roles can even see the list of who else has access to the channel.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to add or remove someone's access</h3>
<ol>
<li>Sign in to YouTube Studio as the Owner or a Manager.</li>
<li>Click <strong>Settings</strong> &gt; <strong>Permissions</strong>.</li>
<li>Click <strong>Invite</strong>, enter the person's Google Account email address, and choose their role from the dropdown.</li>
<li>Click <strong>Done</strong>, then <strong>Save</strong>. The invite is sent by email and expires after 30 days if unanswered.</li>
</ol>
<p>To remove or change someone's access, go back to <strong>Settings &gt; Permissions</strong>, click their current role, and either select a new role or choose <strong>Remove access</strong>, then <strong>Save</strong>. Access changes take effect immediately.</p>
<p>Two practical notes that trip people up:</p>
<ul>
<li>The person you're inviting must have a Google Account. If they don't, they'll be prompted to create one (they can still use a work email address to do it).</li>
<li>Permissions can only be edited from a computer, not the mobile Studio app, even though invited users can accept and use most roles from mobile.</li>
</ul>

<h3>What channel permissions don't cover</h3>
<p>A few things stay outside channel permissions no matter which role someone has:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>YouTube Music, the YouTube Kids app, and YouTube APIs</strong> are never accessible to delegates — only the account signed in as the actual Owner can reach these.</li>
<li><strong>Ownership transfer</strong> doesn't happen through the Permissions page; it still runs through the separate Brand Account channel-transfer process.</li>
<li>If you grant access to a <strong>supervised teen account</strong> (roughly ages 13–17, depending on region), that teen's parent or guardian gets a dashboard showing public information about the channel they now help manage.</li>
<li>Actions a delegate takes are split into "public" and "private": replying to comments, posting updates, or going live as the channel is attributed publicly to the channel, while the delegate's own searches, watch history, and purchases stay tied to their personal account, never the channel.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Channel permissions vs. "Advanced features" — not the same thing</h3>
<p>A common mix-up: there is no "Advanced features controller" role in YouTube's permission system, despite some lower-quality guides implying otherwise. <strong>Advanced features</strong> (like pinned comments, longer uploads, and higher daily upload limits — see <a href="/glossary/youtube-advanced-mode/">YouTube Advanced Mode</a>) are unlocked through a separate identity-verification process — phone verification plus either sufficient channel history or ID/video verification — and only the <strong>primary channel owner</strong> can complete that verification. Managers, Editors, and everyone else with channel permissions simply inherit whatever feature tier the owner has unlocked; they can't run the verification themselves or unlock advanced features on the owner's behalf.</p>

<h3>Channel permissions vs. Content Manager roles</h3>
<p>Don't confuse channel permissions with <strong>Content Manager roles</strong>. Channel permissions are the 7 fixed roles above, available to any individual creator on their own channel. <strong>Content Manager</strong> is a separate tool inside YouTube Studio, used almost exclusively by <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a> partners such as multi-channel networks, labels, and studios that manage many channels or assets at once. Inside Content Manager, an admin builds fully custom roles from a checklist of individual features and restrictions — a much more granular system than the fixed roles individual creators use. If you're a solo creator or small team, you almost certainly want channel permissions, not Content Manager.</p>

<h3>Channel permissions, monetization, and AdSense</h3>
<p>Channel permissions decide who can <em>see</em> revenue numbers inside YouTube Studio and Analytics — not who can touch the money. The linked AdSense account (which holds banking details, tax forms, and payout controls) is a completely separate system tied to one payee. Giving someone the Manager or Editor role does <strong>not</strong> give them AdSense access; there's no channel-permission role that reaches into AdSense at all. This is worth understanding alongside how YouTube monetization and policies work generally, and how perks tied to <a href="/glossary/channel-memberships/">channel memberships</a> get managed — those settings are visible to Managers and full Editors, but hidden from anyone in a "(Limited)" role.</p>

<h3>Google Ads channel linking is a different system too</h3>
<p>If an advertiser or agency needs data from your channel for ad campaigns, that happens through Google Ads account linking, not channel permissions. Linking shares organic view counts, remarketing "data segments," and earned-action metrics with a linked Google Ads account — but it never grants Studio access and never lets the advertiser edit, delete, or manage your videos. One YouTube channel can link to up to 300 Google Ads accounts, and one Google Ads account can link to up to 10,000 YouTube channels — useful context if you're weighing whether a channel permission or a Google Ads link is the right way to work with an outside partner.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Do I need a Brand Account to add an editor or manager to my channel?</h4>
<p>No. Channel permissions work directly on personal YouTube channels through YouTube Studio &gt; Settings &gt; Permissions. Brand Accounts are only relevant now if your channel hasn't migrated to the current permissions system yet, or for other, unrelated Google-service reasons.</p>
<h4>Can an Editor or Manager access my AdSense account?</h4>
<p>No. Channel permissions control what someone can see and do inside YouTube Studio, including whether they can view revenue <em>numbers</em> — but the linked AdSense account itself (payment info, tax details, withdrawals) is a separate system tied to one payee and isn't reachable through any channel-permission role.</p>
<h4>What's the difference between Editor and Editor (Limited)?</h4>
<p>They're identical except Editor (Limited) is blocked from all revenue data, including chat revenue and the Viewer activity tab in Analytics. Use the Limited version for anyone who needs upload/edit access but shouldn't see how much the channel earns.</p>
<h4>How many people can I give access to?</h4>
<p>YouTube's current documentation doesn't state a hard numeric limit for channel permissions — you can grant multiple people any of the seven roles as needed.</p>
<h4>Can someone with channel permissions unlock "Advanced features" for my channel?</h4>
<p>No. Advanced feature access (higher upload limits, pinned comments, etc.) is unlocked through identity verification that only the primary channel owner can complete. Delegates inherit whatever tier the owner has, but can't perform the verification themselves.</p>
