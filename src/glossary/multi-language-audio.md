<h2>Multi-Language Audio</h2>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Multi-Language Audio is the system in YouTube Studio's Languages tab that lets a video carry more than one audio track — one per language. It covers two different ways a track gets there: YouTube's free <a href="/glossary/auto-dubbing/">Auto-Dubbing</a> (AI generates the track for you automatically) and <strong>manually uploaded audio tracks</strong> (you, a translator, or a voice actor record the dub and upload it yourself). Google's own documentation technically uses "multi-language audio" to mean the second, creator-uploaded half specifically — but both live in the same tab, and viewers can't tell them apart in the player, so most creators use the term to mean the whole multi-track system. This entry covers the parts Auto-Dubbing doesn't: uploading your own track, and how viewers actually pick a language.</p>

<h3>Multi-Language Audio vs. Auto-Dubbing: the actual difference</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th></th><th>Auto-Dubbing</th><th>Multi-Language Audio (manual upload)</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Who creates the audio</td><td>YouTube's AI</td><td>You — recorded, hired out, or produced with a third-party tool</td></tr>
<tr><td>Cost</td><td>Free</td><td>Whatever it costs you to produce the dub</td></tr>
<tr><td>Setup</td><td>Toggle on in Studio settings</td><td>Upload an audio file per video, per language</td></tr>

</tbody>
</table>
<p>The two are linked by one hard rule: <strong>if YouTube has already auto-dubbed a language for your video, you have to delete that auto-dub before you can upload your own track for the same language.</strong> You can't run both at once for a single language. For everything about how the AI side works — supported languages, quality, lip-sync, revenue impact — see <a href="/glossary/auto-dubbing/">Auto-Dubbing</a>. Everything below is specific to doing it yourself.</p>

<h3>How to manually upload your own audio track</h3>
<ol>
<li>Produce the dub first — record it yourself, hire a translator/voice actor, or generate it with a dubbing tool. Export it as an audio-only file roughly the same length as your video.</li>
<li>In YouTube Studio (desktop only — this isn't available on mobile), open the Languages tab from the left menu.</li>
<li>Select the video you want to add a track to.</li>
<li>Click Add Language, pick the language.</li>
<li>Next to "Dub," click Add → Select file → choose your audio file.</li>
<li>Click Publish.</li>
</ol>
<p>A few things that trip people up:</p>
<ul>
<li>If an auto-dub already exists for that language, delete it first (Languages → Audio → Delete) — you can't upload over it.</li>
<li>The secondary track's copyrighted content (music, sound effects) needs to match the original track. A mismatch can get the file pulled.</li>
<li>To swap in a new version later, delete the old track the same way and re-upload.</li>
<li>Pairing your uploaded track with a translated title and description is what unlocks foreign-language search — auto-dubbed tracks don't get this benefit, since there's no translated metadata attached.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How viewers experience multiple audio tracks</h3>
<p>Viewers don't have to hunt for a language switch — YouTube handles it by default:</p>
<ul>
<li>The track that plays automatically matches the viewer's preferred language, inferred from their watch history.</li>
<li>Viewers can set an explicit preferred language in their own YouTube account settings.</li>
<li>At any point, a viewer can manually switch tracks using the Settings (gear icon) → Audio menu in the video player itself.</li>
</ul>
<p>This means a single upload can serve an English-speaking viewer the original track and a Spanish-speaking viewer a dubbed track automatically, without either of them doing anything.</p>

<h3>Why it matters for growth</h3>
<p>Multi-language audio (in either form) is a way to reach viewers outside your primary language without spinning up a second channel or re-uploading content. It shows up in how your video performs across <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">traffic sources</a> and who counts as your <a href="/glossary/unique-viewers/">unique viewers</a> or <a href="/glossary/returning-viewers/">returning viewers</a> — a track in a new language can open up a genuinely new audience segment rather than just re-slicing your existing one. Because it changes how (and to whom) a video gets recommended, it's worth watching in <a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">Suggested Videos</a> performance by language once you've added tracks.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Do I need Auto-Dubbing turned on to use Multi-Language Audio?</h4>
<p>No. The two are independent. You can upload your own tracks without ever enabling auto-dubbing, and vice versa. They only interact in one way: you can't have an auto-dub and a manual track active for the same language on the same video at once.</p>
<h4>Is manual multi-language audio available to every channel?</h4>
<p>No — as of this writing, manual upload access is limited to a subset of creators with Advanced features access and is expanding gradually. Auto-Dubbing has a separate, generally broader rollout. Check the Languages tab in your own Studio to see what you have access to.</p>
<h4>Will a manually uploaded track hurt my monetization or count against Content ID?</h4>
<p>YouTube checks that the copyrighted material (music, sound effects) in your secondary track matches the original. If it doesn't match, the upload can be removed — so keep music/SFX consistent between your original and dubbed tracks to avoid a <a href="/glossary/copyright-claim/">copyright claim</a>.</p>
