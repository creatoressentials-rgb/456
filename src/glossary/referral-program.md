<h2>Referral Program</h2>
<p>A <strong>referral program</strong> is a structured system that rewards a customer, user, employee, creator, or other participant for introducing a new eligible person to a business, product, service, or platform.</p>
<p>A referral is usually not complete when the participant merely shares a link or code. The invited person may need to:</p>
<ul>
<li>Create a new account</li>
<li>Make a first purchase</li>
<li>Complete a trip or delivery</li>
<li>Start a paid subscription</li>
<li>Install and activate software</li>
<li>Remain a customer for a minimum period</li>
<li>Complete another program-defined action</li>
</ul>
<p>The reward is issued only when the referral satisfies the applicable conditions.</p>
<h3>How a referral program works</h3>
<p>A typical customer referral program follows this process:</p>
<ol>
<li>An existing customer receives a referral link or code.</li>
<li>The customer shares it with a friend or colleague.</li>
<li>The invited person uses the link or code.</li>
<li>The program verifies that the person is new and eligible.</li>
<li>The invited person completes the required action.</li>
<li>The program reviews the referral for fraud or duplication.</li>
<li>The referrer, referred person, or both receive the reward.</li>
</ol>
<p>Dropbox, for example, awards eligible storage benefits for qualifying referrals. Uber referral offers can require the invited person to complete a first trip or specified activity before a reward is earned.</p>
<h3>Common referral program rewards</h3>
<h4>Referrer-only reward</h4>
<p>The person making the introduction receives:</p>
<ul>
<li>Cash</li>
<li>Store credit</li>
<li>Discount</li>
<li>Points</li>
<li>Free service</li>
<li>Extra storage</li>
<li>Product</li>
<li>Gift card</li>
<li>Account upgrade</li>
</ul>
<h4>Referred-customer reward</h4>
<p>The new customer receives an introductory discount, free trial, bonus, or credit.</p>
<h4>Two-sided reward</h4>
<p>Both people receive something.</p>
<p>A two-sided structure can make the invitation more useful to the new customer, but the conditions for both rewards should be clear.</p>
<h4>Tiered reward</h4>
<p>The participant receives a larger or different reward after reaching several successful referrals.</p>
<h4>Milestone or ambassador reward</h4>
<p>High-performing referrers can unlock status, early access, products, events, or a transition into a formal <a href="/glossary/affiliate-program">affiliate program</a>.</p>
<h3>Referral program vs. affiliate program</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>Referral program</th>
  <th><a href="/glossary/affiliate-program">Affiliate program</a></th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Often targets current customers or users</td>
  <td>Often targets creators, publishers, and marketers</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Based on personal recommendation or invitation</td>
  <td>Based on ongoing commercial promotion</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Reward may be credit, product, discount, or cash</td>
  <td>Compensation is commonly cash commission</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Referred person may receive a reward</td>
  <td>Customer reward is optional</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Promotion is often limited to personal channels</td>

</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Program can be simple and short-term</td>
  <td>Detailed attribution and publisher rules are common</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The boundary is not universal. Shopify's affiliate program describes commissions for merchant referrals, and some companies call creator commission arrangements referral partnerships.</p>
<p>The correct category depends on the participant, reward, promotional expectations, and written rules.</p>
<h3>Referral program vs. referral code</h3>
<p>A <strong>referral program</strong> is the complete arrangement.</p>
<p>A <strong>referral code</strong> is one tracking tool inside it. Programs can also use:</p>
<ul>
<li>Referral links</li>
<li>Email invitations</li>
<li>QR codes</li>
<li>Phone numbers</li>
<li>Account matching</li>
<li>Manual sales attribution</li>
<li>Unique landing pages</li>
</ul>
<p>The code identifies the referrer but does not by itself explain eligibility or reward terms.</p>
<h3>What makes a successful referral?</h3>
<p>The program should define:</p>
<ul>
<li>Who qualifies as a new customer</li>
<li>Whether previous trial users are excluded</li>
<li>Required purchase or activity</li>
<li>Minimum order or subscription value</li>
<li>Geographic eligibility</li>
<li>Completion deadline</li>
<li>Whether the new customer can cancel or return</li>
<li>Whether household members qualify</li>
<li>Whether self-referrals are prohibited</li>
<li>Which link or code must be used</li>
<li>Whether another referrer can receive credit</li>
<li>When the reward becomes available</li>
</ul>
<p>An invited person who merely registers may not satisfy a program that requires a completed purchase.</p>
<h3>Referral tracking and attribution</h3>
<p>A program may use:</p>
<ul>
<li>Unique URL</li>
<li>Referral code</li>
<li>Account ID</li>
<li>Email invitation</li>
<li>Cookie</li>
<li>Server-side event</li>
<li>Customer relationship management record</li>
<li>Sales representative</li>
<li>Product activation</li>
</ul>
<p>Attribution questions include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Which referrer receives credit when multiple people invite the same customer?</li>
<li>How long does the referral remain valid?</li>
<li>Can support add credit manually?</li>
<li>Does a code override a link?</li>
<li>What happens when the customer changes devices?</li>
<li>Are offline purchases eligible?</li>
<li>Can an existing lead become a referral?</li>
</ul>
<h3>Referral rewards and disclosure</h3>
<p>A reward can create a material connection when a participant publicly recommends the product.</p>
<p>Creators and businesses should consider clear wording such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>“I receive a credit if you sign up with this link.”</li>
<li>“We both get a discount if you use my referral code.”</li>
<li>“This is a referral link, and I may receive a reward.”</li>
</ul>
<p>A vague “use my code” statement may not tell viewers that the person benefits.</p>
<p>The disclosure should be close to the recommendation and invitation rather than hidden on a profile or separate legal page.</p>
<h3>Referral programs and incentivized reviews</h3>
<p>A referral reward should not be confused with payment for a positive review.</p>
<p>The FTC says businesses offering an incentive for a review should not condition the incentive—explicitly or implicitly—on the review being positive. Some review platforms prohibit incentivized reviews entirely, even when the incentive is disclosed.</p>
<p>A referral program can reward a new customer introduction without requiring:</p>
<ul>
<li>A five-star review</li>
<li>Positive sentiment</li>
<li>A testimonial</li>
<li>A public endorsement</li>
</ul>
<p>If the program does request promotional content, endorsement and disclosure rules become more important.</p>
<h3>Referral fraud and abuse</h3>
<p>Common issues include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Self-referrals</li>
<li>Fake accounts</li>
<li>Duplicate identities</li>
<li>Disposable email addresses</li>
<li>Repeated household referrals</li>
<li>Automated sign-ups</li>
<li>Coupon-site distribution</li>
<li>Paid advertising using the brand's trademark</li>
<li>Referrals to existing customers</li>
<li>Returns after rewards are paid</li>
<li>Geographic manipulation</li>
<li>Misleading claims</li>
</ul>
<p>Programs can use identity checks, delayed rewards, limits, reversals, and manual review.</p>
<h3>Terms a referral program should define</h3>
<p>Include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Eligible referrers</li>
<li>Eligible new customers</li>
<li>Required action</li>
<li>Reward for each party</li>
<li>Reward value</li>
<li>Reward limit</li>
<li>Expiration</li>
<li>Geographic availability</li>
<li>Referral window</li>
<li>Attribution method</li>
<li>Fraud and self-referral rules</li>
<li>Returns and cancellations</li>
<li>Tax treatment</li>
<li>Program changes</li>
<li>Termination</li>
<li>Privacy and data use</li>
<li>Promotional restrictions</li>
<li>Disclosure requirements</li>
<li>Customer support and disputes</li>
</ul>
<h3>Creator-owned referral programs</h3>
<p>Creators can use referral programs for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Memberships</li>
<li>Newsletters</li>
<li>Courses</li>
<li>Communities</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/digital-product">Digital products</a></li>
<li>Events</li>
<li>Consulting</li>
<li>Merchandise</li>
<li>Software</li>
<li>Subscription services</li>
</ul>
<p>A creator might offer existing customers a store credit for referring a new buyer. The creator should monitor economics so the combined discount and reward do not exceed the value of the acquired customer.</p>
<h3>Referral program red flags</h3>
<p>Be cautious when a program:</p>
<ul>
<li>Focuses primarily on recruiting more participants rather than selling a real product</li>
<li>Requires payment for the right to refer others</li>
<li>Promises guaranteed income</li>
<li>Hides the qualifying action</li>
<li>Changes rewards after the referral occurs</li>
<li>Provides no way to check status</li>
<li>Requires positive reviews</li>
<li>Encourages spam or deceptive claims</li>
<li>Collects unnecessary personal data</li>
<li>Delays rewards indefinitely</li>
<li>Makes geographic or customer restrictions difficult to find</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is a referral program the same as affiliate marketing?</h4>
<p>Not always. Referral programs commonly reward existing customers for personal introductions. Affiliate marketing commonly pays creators or publishers for ongoing commercial promotion. Some programs combine both models.</p>
<h4>Does sharing a referral link guarantee a reward?</h4>
<p>No. The referred person normally must satisfy eligibility, activation, purchase, or retention requirements.</p>
<h4>Do referral rewards need disclosure?</h4>
<p>They can when someone publicly recommends a product and receives a benefit for successful referrals. Clear disclosure helps viewers understand the incentive.</p>
<h4>Can creators run their own referral program?</h4>
<p>Yes. Creators can reward customers or members for qualifying referrals, but the program should define economics, eligibility, tracking, fraud controls, disclosure, privacy, and tax treatment.</p>
