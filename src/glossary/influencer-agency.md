<h2>Influencer Agency</h2>
<p>An <strong>influencer agency</strong> is a company that provides services related to influencer and creator marketing.</p>
<p>An agency may work for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Brands</li>
<li>Influencers</li>
<li>Content creators</li>
<li>Advertising agencies</li>
<li>Platforms</li>
<li>Retailers</li>
<li>Several parties in the same campaign</li>
</ul>
<p>The phrase does not identify whose interests the agency represents. That must be determined from the contract and compensation structure.</p>
<h3>Common influencer agency models</h3>
<h4>Brand-side influencer agency</h4>
<p>Helps brands plan and run campaigns.</p>
<p>Services can include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Strategy</li>
<li>Creator discovery</li>
<li>Audience analysis</li>
<li>Outreach</li>
<li>Briefing</li>
<li>Contracting</li>
<li>Content approval</li>
<li>Payment</li>
<li>Measurement</li>
<li>Reporting</li>
</ul>
<h4>Talent representation agency</h4>
<p>Represents influencers in commercial negotiations and career opportunities.</p>
<p>It may handle:</p>
<ul>
<li>Brand outreach</li>
<li>Rate negotiation</li>
<li>Contract coordination</li>
<li>Licensing</li>
<li>Appearances</li>
<li>Long-term positioning</li>
</ul>
<h4>Campaign execution agency</h4>
<p>Provides operational support for a specific campaign without serving as the creator's long-term representative.</p>
<h4>Creative and production agency</h4>
<p>Produces creator-style content, manages shoots, or develops campaign assets.</p>
<h4>Full-service influencer agency</h4>
<p>Combines brand strategy, creator sourcing, representation, production, paid amplification, and analytics.</p>

</tr>

</table>
<p>In everyday use, the terms overlap substantially.</p>
<h3>Influencer agency vs. talent manager</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>Influencer agency</th>
  <th><a href="/glossary/talent-manager">Talent manager</a></th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Company or campaign organization</td>
  <td>Individual or management firm representing talent</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>May represent the brand</td>
  <td>Usually represents the creator</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Can provide project-based services</td>
  <td>Often has an ongoing career relationship</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>May manage many campaign participants</td>

</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Fees may come from several parties</td>
  <td>Often paid through creator commission</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>A talent manager can work within an influencer agency.</p>
<h3>Agency responsibilities in a campaign</h3>
<p>A brand-side agency may coordinate:</p>
<ol>
<li>Campaign objective</li>
<li>Target audience</li>
<li>Creator shortlist</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/campaign-brief">Campaign brief</a></li>
<li>Compensation</li>
<li>Contract</li>
<li>Deliverables</li>
<li>Approval</li>
<li>Disclosure</li>
<li>Usage rights</li>
<li>Paid media</li>
<li>Reporting</li>
</ol>
<p>The creator should know which instructions come from the brand and which are agency recommendations.</p>
<h3>Fees and markups</h3>
<p>An agency can earn:</p>
<ul>
<li>Brand retainer</li>
<li>Project fee</li>
<li>Percentage of campaign spend</li>
<li>Creator commission</li>
<li>Production fee</li>
<li>Media-buying fee</li>
<li>Technology fee</li>
<li>Campaign markup</li>
<li>Success fee</li>
<li>Revenue share</li>
</ul>
<p>The same campaign can include several layers of fees.</p>
<p>A creator should clarify whether the amount offered is:</p>
<ul>
<li>Gross brand budget</li>
<li>Creator fee before management commission</li>
<li>Net creator payment</li>
<li>Inclusive of usage</li>
<li>Inclusive of expenses</li>
<li>Inclusive of agency markup</li>
</ul>
<h3>Representation conflicts</h3>
<p>Conflicts can arise when an agency:</p>
<ul>
<li>Represents the brand and creator</li>
<li>Receives money from both parties</li>
<li>Recommends agency-owned talent</li>
<li>Controls campaign reporting</li>
<li>Sets the creator's rate while retaining the difference</li>
<li>Owns the production vendor</li>
<li>Receives a media-spend commission</li>
<li>Uses confidential data for another client</li>
</ul>
<p>Disclosure does not eliminate every conflict, but it lets the parties evaluate it.</p>
<h3>Contract terms</h3>
<p>Review:</p>
<ul>
<li>Client represented</li>
<li>Services</li>
<li>Term</li>
<li>Exclusivity</li>
<li>Territory</li>
<li>Platforms</li>
<li>Fee</li>
<li>Payment flow</li>
<li>Approval authority</li>
<li>Signing authority</li>
<li>Usage rights</li>
<li>Data access</li>
<li>Reporting</li>
<li>Confidentiality</li>
<li>Conflicts</li>
<li>Termination</li>
<li>Post-campaign obligations</li>
</ul>
<p>A brand-side agency usually does not become the creator's manager merely because it negotiates one campaign.</p>
<h3>Influencer agency and FTC compliance</h3>
<p>The FTC's Endorsement Guides apply to endorsements and material connections.</p>
<p>Agencies should help ensure:</p>
<ul>
<li>Clear disclosure</li>
<li>Truthful claims</li>
<li>Actual product experience</li>
<li>Proper instructions</li>
<li>Monitoring</li>
<li>Correction of noncompliant posts</li>
<li>Honest reviews and testimonials</li>
</ul>
<p>An agency cannot solve disclosure by placing one sentence deep in a contract.</p>
<h3>Choosing an influencer agency</h3>
<p>Creators should ask:</p>
<ul>
<li>Who is your client?</li>
<li>Who pays you?</li>
<li>Are you taking a commission from my fee?</li>
<li>Is my relationship exclusive?</li>
<li>Can you sign on my behalf?</li>
<li>Who owns campaign data?</li>
<li>Who pays me if the brand does not?</li>
<li>Are usage rights included?</li>
<li>What happens after the campaign?</li>
<li>Can I communicate directly with the brand?</li>
</ul>
<p>Brands should ask:</p>
<ul>
<li>How are creators vetted?</li>
<li>Are audience metrics verified?</li>
<li>How are conflicts disclosed?</li>
<li>Who owns the relationship?</li>
<li>How are payments handled?</li>
<li>What is the disclosure process?</li>
<li>How is performance measured?</li>
</ul>
<h3>Warning signs</h3>
<ul>
<li>Guaranteed viral results</li>
<li>Hidden compensation</li>
<li>Unclear client identity</li>
<li>Pressure to hide sponsorship</li>
<li>No written scope</li>
<li>Broad account access</li>
<li>Unlimited usage buried in campaign terms</li>
<li>No payment timeline</li>
<li>Inflated audience data</li>
<li>Creator fees withheld without explanation</li>
<li>No conflict policy</li>
<li>No termination process</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Does an influencer agency represent the influencer?</h4>
<p>Sometimes. Many influencer agencies work for brands, so creators should confirm the agency's client.</p>
<h4>How do influencer agencies get paid?</h4>
<p>Common structures include retainers, project fees, creator commissions, campaign markups, production fees, and percentages of spend.</p>
<h4>Is an influencer agency the same as an MCN?</h4>
<p>No. An MCN is a YouTube-specific network relationship. An influencer agency can operate across platforms without linking channels.</p>
<h4>Who is responsible for disclosure?</h4>
<p>Brands, creators, and agencies all have roles in creating compliant endorsements. The creator should not rely solely on agency instructions.</p>
