<h2>Mono vs. Stereo Audio</h2>
<p><strong>Mono audio</strong> uses one audio signal.</p>
<p><strong>Stereo audio</strong> uses two distinct channels—left and right—to create width and spatial placement.</p>
<p>Stereo is not automatically higher quality than mono. The correct format depends on what is being recorded.</p>

<h3>What is mono audio?</h3>
<p>A mono microphone produces one signal.</p>
<p>Examples include many:</p>
<ul>
<li>Podcast microphones</li>
<li>Lavalier microphones</li>
<li>Shotgun microphones</li>
<li>Wireless transmitters</li>
<li>Voice-over microphones</li>
<li>USB microphones</li>
</ul>
<p>That signal can be played equally through both speakers while remaining mono.</p>
<h3>What is stereo audio?</h3>
<p>True stereo uses different information in the left and right channels.</p>
<p>A stereo recording can capture:</p>
<ul>
<li>Location from left to right</li>
<li>Width</li>
<li>Movement</li>
<li>Room ambience</li>
<li>Music ensemble placement</li>
<li>Environmental space</li>
</ul>
<p>Stereo normally requires:</p>
<ul>
<li>Stereo microphone</li>
<li>Two microphones in a stereo arrangement</li>
<li>Multiple channels mixed spatially</li>
<li>Software-generated stereo elements</li>
</ul>
<h3>Mono signal in a stereo file</h3>
<p>A file can contain two channels without containing true stereo.</p>
<p>Examples:</p>
<h4>Dual mono</h4>
<p>The same mono signal appears on both left and right.</p>
<h4>One-sided mono</h4>
<p>The mono signal appears only on the left or right channel.</p>
<h4>Split mono</h4>
<p>One microphone is recorded on the left and another on the right.</p>
<p>These are two-channel files, but they are not necessarily stereo recordings of one spatial scene.</p>
<h3>Voice recording</h3>
<p>Spoken voice is usually recorded in mono and placed in the center.</p>
<p>This is common for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Talking-head videos</li>
<li>Podcasts</li>
<li>Tutorials</li>
<li>Livestreams</li>
<li>Voice-over</li>
<li>Interviews</li>
</ul>
<p>Mono keeps the voice stable for viewers using:</p>
<ul>
<li>Headphones</li>
<li>Phone speakers</li>
<li>One earbud</li>
<li>Smart speakers</li>
<li>Accessibility devices</li>
</ul>
<h3>When stereo is useful</h3>
<p>Stereo works well for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Music performances</li>
<li>Nature ambience</li>
<li>Crowd sound</li>
<li>Travel environments</li>
<li>ASMR</li>
<li>Room tone</li>
<li>Events</li>
<li>Sound effects with movement</li>
<li>Immersive demonstrations</li>
</ul>
<h3>Wireless microphone routing</h3>
<p>A two-transmitter system may offer:</p>
<h4>Merged mode</h4>
<p>Both microphones are combined into one mono mix.</p>
<h4>Split mode</h4>
<p>One microphone goes to the left channel and the other to the right.</p>
<h4>Stereo mode</h4>
<p>Used with a true stereo source or supported input configuration.</p>
<p>Split mode gives editors separate control over each speaker but sounds wrong if uploaded without centering or mixing the channels.</p>
<h3>Why audio plays in only one ear</h3>
<p>Common causes include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Mono mic recorded only to left channel</li>
<li>Split wireless mode</li>
<li>Incorrect cable</li>
<li>Stereo track interpreted incorrectly</li>
<li>Channel routing mistake</li>
<li>One channel muted</li>
</ul>
<p>The fix may involve converting the clip to mono, copying the active channel, or adjusting channel mapping.</p>
<h3>Mono compatibility</h3>
<p>Mono is often safer for primary dialogue.</p>
<p>A centered mono voice remains clear when:</p>
<ul>
<li>Playback is collapsed to one speaker</li>
<li>A viewer uses one earbud</li>
<li>A phone has limited stereo separation</li>
<li>Platforms recompress the audio</li>
</ul>
<h3>Stereo width and phase</h3>
<p>Stereo recordings can suffer from phase problems when left and right channels combine to mono.</p>
<p>Possible effects include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Thin sound</li>
<li>Missing frequencies</li>
<li>Reduced center content</li>
<li>Unstable ambience</li>
</ul>
<p>Check important stereo audio in mono before publishing.</p>

<h3>Editing workflow</h3>
<p>A common YouTube mix contains:</p>
<ul>
<li>Mono dialogue</li>
<li>Stereo music</li>
<li>Stereo game or computer audio</li>
<li>Mono or stereo sound effects</li>
<li>Stereo master output</li>
</ul>
<p>A mono source can sit inside a stereo final mix without becoming a stereo recording itself.</p>
<h3>Common mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Assuming two channels means true stereo</li>
<li>Uploading split voices hard left and right</li>
<li>Recording dialogue in stereo unnecessarily</li>
<li>Duplicating mono and calling it stereo</li>
<li>Ignoring one-ear playback</li>
<li>Failing to check phase</li>
<li>Using the wrong TRS or TRRS cable</li>
<li>Converting ambience to mono without listening</li>
<li>Confusing stereo width with audio quality</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is mono or stereo better for YouTube?</h4>
<p>Neither is universally better. Mono is usually best for dialogue, while stereo is useful for music, ambience, and spatial sound.</p>
<h4>Is a mono microphone heard in both headphones?</h4>
<p>Yes, when it is routed or centered correctly to both output channels.</p>
<h4>Why is my wireless microphone only in one ear?</h4>
<p>The receiver may be in split mode or the mono input may be recorded to only one side of a stereo track.</p>
<h4>Does recording one microphone twice create stereo?</h4>
<p>No. Duplicating the same signal creates dual mono, not true stereo.</p>
