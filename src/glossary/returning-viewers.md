<h2>Returning Viewers</h2>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Returning Viewers was a YouTube Analytics metric that counted anyone who'd watched your channel before and came back to watch again within a selected date range. YouTube retired it in <strong>July 2025</strong>, replacing the old New vs. Returning split with three segments: <strong>New, Casual, and Regular viewers</strong>. If you're looking for "Returning Viewers" in YouTube Studio today and can't find it, that's why — it's not a bug, it's a discontinued metric. Go to <strong>Analytics → Audience</strong> and you'll see Casual and Regular viewers in its place.</p>

<h3>What Returning Viewers used to mean</h3>
<p>In the old system, YouTube Analytics split your audience into two buckets: <strong>new viewers</strong> (first-time watchers in the date range) and <strong>returning viewers</strong> (anyone who'd watched before and came back). There was no minimum threshold — one repeat visit within your selected window was enough to count as "returning." You could see both lines on a graph under Analytics → Audience, and a rising returning-viewer line was generally read as a sign of audience loyalty and content-market fit.</p>

<h3>Why you can't find it in Studio anymore</h3>
<p>YouTube announced the change in <strong>July 2025</strong>, rolling it out globally over the following weeks. The company's stated reason: a binary new/returning split couldn't distinguish someone who watched once six months ago from someone who watches every single upload — both got lumped into "returning viewers." The new system replaces that single metric with three tiers based on how many separate months a viewer showed up over the past year. YouTube has been explicit that the new segments are <strong>"not a direct equivalent of the previous returning viewers metric,"</strong> so don't expect your old returning-viewer number and your new regular-viewer count to line up.</p>

<p>Because the new bar is much higher — especially for Regular viewers — most creators see a smaller Regular-viewer count than their old returning-viewer number ever showed. That's expected, not a sign your channel got worse. For the deeper breakdown of what qualifies as Regular and how to grow that segment, see our <a href="/glossary/regular-viewers/">Regular Viewers</a> entry.</p>

<h3>Where to find this data now</h3>
<p>In YouTube Studio: <strong>Analytics → Audience tab.</strong> Your monthly audience is broken into New, Casual, and Regular. There's no toggle to bring back the old Returning Viewers graph — it's gone for good.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Is "Returning Viewers" still a real YouTube metric?</h4>
<p>No. It was discontinued starting July 2025 and replaced by the New/Casual/Regular viewer segments. You may still see the term used in older tutorials, third-party tools, or cached screenshots, but it no longer exists in YouTube Studio.</p>
<h4>Do Casual + Regular viewers equal my old Returning Viewers number?</h4>
<p>Roughly, directionally — both are people who came back. But YouTube has stated the new segments aren't a direct equivalent, and the Regular viewer bar (6+ months of activity) is much stricter than the old "any repeat visit" threshold. Don't expect the numbers to match.</p>
<h4>Why is my Regular viewer count so low compared to my old returning viewer count?</h4>
<p>Because the bar moved. Regular now requires consistent monthly activity across at least 6 of the past 12 months — a high standard by design. A tiny Regular-viewer percentage is normal, even for healthy, growing channels.</p>
