<h2>Members-Only Video</h2>
<p>A <strong>members-only video</strong> is a YouTube video whose playback is restricted to active channel members at an eligible membership level.</p>
<p>The video can still be discoverable to non-members. YouTube says anyone may find a members-only video, but only members at the required level can watch it.</p>

<h3>Who Can Watch a Members-Only Video?</h3>
<p>The creator can make the video available to:</p>
<ul>
  <li>All paid channel members</li>
  <li>A selected membership level and every higher level</li>
</ul>
<p>Membership perks stack upward. A higher-priced level receives the perks assigned to lower levels in addition to its own perks.</p>

<h3>Can Non-Members See the Video?</h3>
<p>Yes. Non-members may see:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The thumbnail</li>
  <li>The title</li>
  <li>The description or other metadata</li>
  <li>A members-only label</li>
  <li>A prompt to join the channel</li>
</ul>
<p>They cannot play the full video unless they join at a qualifying level or receive a qualifying gift membership.</p>

<h3>Where Can Members-Only Videos Appear?</h3>
<p>YouTube says they can appear on:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The channel's Memberships tab</li>
  <li>The channel's Content tabs</li>
  <li>The channel's Posts tab</li>
  <li>The member's Home feed</li>
  <li>The member's Subscriptions feed</li>
</ul>
<p>Discovery outside the paywall can help show non-members what kinds of exclusive content are available.</p>

<h3>How to Publish a Members-Only Video</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Upload or select the video in YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Complete the title, description, thumbnail, audience, checks, and other settings.</li>
  <li>Open the visibility options.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Members only</strong>.</li>
  <li>Choose all paid members or the minimum membership level that receives access.</li>
  <li>Save or publish the video.</li>
</ol>
<p>The exact interface can vary by device and rollout, but members-only is a visibility and access setting rather than an ordinary Public, Private, or Unlisted option.</p>

<h3>Members-Only vs. Private Video</h3>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/private-video/">Private Video</a> is shared with specifically authorized Google Accounts. A members-only video is shared with a changing group of channel members who meet the selected tier requirement.</p>
<p>Private access is not a substitute for a paid membership perk, and membership access is not a private invitation list.</p>

<h3>Members-Only vs. Unlisted Video</h3>
<p>An <a href="/glossary/unlisted-video/">Unlisted Video</a> can generally be watched by anyone who has the link. A members-only link does not bypass the membership check.</p>
<p>Sharing the URL with a non-member lets them see the locked page, not watch the exclusive video.</p>

<h3>Members-Only vs. Early Access</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Permanent Members-Only</th><th>Members-Only to Public</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Initial access</td><td>Qualifying members</td><td>Qualifying members</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Later access</td><td>Remains restricted until the creator changes it</td><td>Automatically or intentionally becomes public after the early-access period</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Main purpose</td><td>Exclusive library</td><td>Reward members without permanently limiting reach</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>Early access is often easier to sustain because the same video can later serve the full audience.</p>

<h3>How Membership Levels Affect Access</h3>
<p>If a channel has several membership levels, a video assigned to one level is available to that level and all more expensive levels.</p>
<p>For example:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Level 1 video: available to Levels 1, 2, and 3</li>
  <li>Level 2 video: available to Levels 2 and 3</li>
  <li>Level 3 video: available only to Level 3</li>
</ul>

<h3>What Happens When Membership Ends?</h3>
<p>A member who cancels normally retains access through the remaining paid billing period. When access expires, the viewer can no longer play the members-only video unless they rejoin.</p>
<p>A viewer with a <a href="/glossary/gift-membership/">Gift Membership</a> receives access for the gift period and loses it when the non-recurring gift expires.</p>

<h3>Members-Only Shorts</h3>
<p>YouTube also supports members-only Shorts. Current viewer guidance says these can appear in the member's Shorts feed, Home feed, and Watch Next.</p>
<p>A members-only Short follows short-form discovery and viewing behavior, while a standard members-only video uses ordinary video playback.</p>

<h3>Can Members Download the Video?</h3>
<p>Channel membership does not give members a downloadable copy of YouTube-hosted content. YouTube's membership policies prohibit offering downloads of content, including music, that is available on YouTube as a members-only perk.</p>
<p>Members may have ordinary YouTube Premium offline-viewing features when separately eligible, but that is not a creator-provided file download.</p>

<h3>Copyright and Policy Requirements</h3>
<p>Members-only videos must follow the same core YouTube rules as public content:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Community Guidelines</li>
  <li>Copyright policies</li>
  <li>Channel monetization policies</li>
  <li>Membership content and perk policies</li>
  <li>Applicable advertising and fan-funding terms</li>
</ul>
<p>A paywall does not make prohibited content acceptable.</p>

<h3>Can Members-Only Videos Earn Ad or Premium Revenue?</h3>
<p>Membership revenue and video monetization are separate systems. The video and channel must independently meet the applicable advertising, Premium, rights, and policy requirements.</p>
<p>Creators should not assume that a monthly membership payment guarantees a particular amount of video advertising revenue.</p>

<h3>Analytics for Members-Only Videos</h3>
<p>Members-only videos appear in YouTube Studio and can be analyzed using ordinary video metrics such as:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Views</li>
  <li>Watch time</li>
  <li>Average view duration</li>
  <li>Audience retention</li>
  <li>Traffic sources</li>
  <li>Likes and comments</li>
  <li>Revenue where applicable</li>
</ul>
<p>Interpret reach relative to the number of members who had access rather than comparing every exclusive video directly with a public upload.</p>

<h3>What Content Works Well?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Behind-the-scenes videos</li>
  <li>Extended versions</li>
  <li>Monthly updates</li>
  <li>Member Q&amp;A recordings</li>
  <li>Project files explained on screen without distributing prohibited downloads</li>
  <li>Production breakdowns</li>
  <li>Early-access uploads</li>
  <li>Small-community discussions</li>
</ul>

<h3>Permanent Exclusivity vs. Early Access</h3>
<p>Permanent exclusive content can increase perceived membership value but requires maintaining a separate content library. Early access can reward members while preserving public reach.</p>
<p>A sustainable program can combine:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Occasional permanent exclusives</li>
  <li>Regular early-access videos</li>
  <li>Members-only posts and polls</li>
  <li>Live streams and chat benefits</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to Promote a Members-Only Video</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Use an accurate title and thumbnail that non-members can understand.</li>
  <li>Explain which level receives access.</li>
  <li>Describe the exclusive value without revealing the entire video.</li>
  <li>Link to the channel's Join flow.</li>
  <li>Promote it through public videos, posts, and live streams without overwhelming viewers.</li>
  <li>Keep a clear membership content schedule.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Common Members-Only-Video Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Assuming non-members cannot discover the page.</li>
  <li>Sharing the URL as though it bypasses access.</li>
  <li>Confusing members-only with Private or Unlisted.</li>
  <li>Forgetting that higher levels inherit lower-level access.</li>
  <li>Promising a publishing schedule that cannot be sustained.</li>
  <li>Offering downloadable YouTube content as a perk.</li>
  <li>Assuming a paywall exempts the video from policy.</li>
  <li>Comparing exclusive reach directly with public reach.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Can non-members find a members-only video?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube says anyone can find it, but only members at the correct level can watch it.</p>

<h4>Can I send the link to a non-member?</h4>
<p>You can send the link, but it does not bypass the membership requirement.</p>

<h4>Can I limit a video to one membership tier?</h4>
<p>You can select a minimum level. That level and all higher levels receive access.</p>

<h4>Is early access the same as members-only?</h4>
<p>Early access begins as members-only but later opens to the public; permanent exclusive content remains restricted until changed.</p>
