<h2>Monthly Audience</h2>
<p><strong>Monthly audience</strong> is YouTube's estimate of the total unique viewers who watched a channel's content during the previous 28 days. YouTube calculates the metric every day using a rolling 28-day window.</p>
<p>It is designed to help creators estimate the size of their active audience rather than relying only on total subscriber count.</p>

<h3>How Is Monthly Audience Calculated?</h3>
<p>For each date shown, YouTube looks backward over the preceding 28 days and estimates the number of distinct viewers who watched channel content during that window.</p>
<p>This means monthly audience is:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Rolling:</strong> The window moves forward every day.</li>
<li><strong>Twenty-eight days:</strong> It is not a calendar month and does not always match 30 or 31 days.</li>
<li><strong>Estimated:</strong> YouTube uses available data to estimate individual viewers.</li>
<li><strong>Cross-format:</strong> Viewer metrics can include videos, Shorts, live content, and uploaded channel videos.</li>
</ul>

<h3>What Happens When You Change the Date Range?</h3>
<p>YouTube notes that monthly audience still uses the previous 28 days from the most recent day in the selected range. Choosing a 90-day Analytics range does not turn monthly audience into a 90-day unique-viewer total.</p>
<p>For example, a monthly-audience point dated August 1 represents the estimated active audience from the 28-day period ending on August 1, not every viewer from an arbitrary longer range.</p>

<h3>Monthly Audience vs. Unique Viewers</h3>
<table><thead><tr><th>Metric</th><th>Time Window</th><th>Primary Use</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Monthly audience</td><td>Rolling previous 28 days from each reported date</td><td>Track active audience size consistently over time</td></tr>
<tr><td><a href="/glossary/unique-viewers/">Unique viewers</a></td><td>The selected Analytics date range</td><td>Estimate distinct viewers for a chosen campaign or period</td></tr>
<tr><td>Views</td><td>The selected date range</td><td>Count legitimate plays, including multiple views by viewers</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>Monthly audience and unique viewers can be related, but they should not be assumed to match when their date windows differ.</p>

<h3>Monthly Audience vs. Subscribers</h3>
<p>Subscribers are accounts that have chosen to follow the channel. Monthly audience measures people who actually watched during the rolling 28-day period.</p>
<p>Monthly audience can be lower than subscriber count because many subscribers do not watch every month. It can also be higher because a large portion of viewers may watch without subscribing.</p>
<p>YouTube describes monthly audience as a more accurate indicator of active audience size than subscriber count alone.</p>

<h3>How Is Monthly Audience Segmented?</h3>
<p>YouTube divides monthly audience into three watch-behavior segments:</p>
<table><thead><tr><th>Segment</th><th>Definition</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>New viewers</td><td>Watched the channel for the first time in the applicable period, including certain viewers without usable recent history</td></tr>
<tr><td>Casual viewers</td><td>Watched at least monthly for one to five months in the past year</td></tr>
<tr><td><a href="/glossary/regular-viewers/">Regular viewers</a></td><td>Watched at least monthly for more than six months in the past year</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>The segment mix helps separate discovery from emerging and established loyalty.</p>

<h3>Where Can You Find Monthly Audience?</h3>
<ol>
<li>Sign in to YouTube Studio.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Analytics</strong>.</li>
<li>Open the <strong>Audience</strong> tab.</li>
<li>Review the monthly-audience metric and its watch-behavior breakdown.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube may display the metric in key-metric cards or other audience reports depending on platform and account availability.</p>

<h3>How to Interpret Monthly Audience Growth</h3>
<p>Growing monthly audience means more distinct people watched during the latest rolling window than during earlier windows. Possible contributors include:</p>
<ul>
<li>New uploads bringing discovery traffic</li>
<li>A breakout or evergreen video continuing to attract viewers</li>
<li>More casual and regular viewers returning</li>
<li>Stronger performance across Shorts, live streams, and long-form video</li>
<li>Seasonal audience demand</li>
<li>Collaborations, external exposure, and promotions</li>
</ul>
<p>Use the segment breakdown and traffic-source reports to understand whether growth came primarily from discovery, returning behavior, or both.</p>

<h3>Why Can Monthly Audience Drop?</h3>
<ul>
<li>A high-performing video's strongest days left the rolling 28-day window.</li>
<li>The channel uploaded less frequently.</li>
<li>Seasonal demand declined.</li>
<li>Fewer new viewers discovered the channel.</li>
<li>Casual or regular viewers returned less often.</li>
<li>A temporary event or trend stopped producing traffic.</li>
</ul>
<p>A drop does not necessarily mean subscribers were removed or that YouTube applied a penalty. Rolling-window metrics can decline when older strong days are replaced by newer weaker days.</p>

<h3>How to Use Monthly Audience Strategically</h3>
<ul>
<li>Track the direction over several rolling windows rather than reacting to one day.</li>
<li>Compare changes with publication dates and major videos.</li>
<li>Review the new, casual, and regular viewer mix.</li>
<li>Compare active audience with subscriber count.</li>
<li>Study which content is popular with different audience segments.</li>
<li>Separate content formats because viewing behavior differs across videos, Shorts, and live streams.</li>
<li>Use <a href="/glossary/youtube-advanced-mode/">YouTube Advanced Mode</a> for deeper selected-range analysis when needed.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Common Monthly-Audience Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Calling it a calendar-month metric.</li>
<li>Assuming a selected 90-day range produces 90-day monthly audience.</li>
<li>Treating monthly audience as the same as subscriber count.</li>
<li>Adding daily monthly-audience values together.</li>
<li>Assuming every format contributes equally.</li>
<li>Interpreting a rolling-window decline as proof of an algorithm penalty.</li>
<li>Comparing monthly audience directly with a unique-viewer number from a different date range.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Is monthly audience based on the last 30 days?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube defines it using the previous 28 days.</p>
<h4>Does monthly audience reset at the start of the month?</h4>
<p>No. It is calculated daily using a rolling window.</p>
<h4>Can monthly audience be higher than subscribers?</h4>
<p>Yes. Many people watch without subscribing, so the active audience can exceed subscriber count.</p>
<h4>Does monthly audience count repeat views?</h4>
<p>It estimates distinct viewers rather than adding every playback as another audience member.</p>
