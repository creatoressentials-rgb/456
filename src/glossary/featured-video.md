<h2>Featured Video</h2>
<p>A <strong>YouTube featured video</strong> is a video selected in the channel Home tab's Spotlight area for returning subscribers. It lets the creator highlight a priority video when subscribed viewers revisit the channel.</p>
<p>The term can be used loosely elsewhere, but this glossary entry focuses on YouTube's current Home-tab feature—not a video selected by the recommendation system, placed in a featured section, or labeled as trending.</p>

<h3>Who Sees the Featured Video?</h3>
<p>YouTube describes the Spotlight featured video as content for returning subscribers who visit the channel Home tab.</p>
<p>It is different from the channel trailer, which is intended for people who have not subscribed.</p>

<h3>What Happens After a Viewer Watches It?</h3>
<p>YouTube says that once a viewer watches the featured video, it will not be shown to that viewer again in this Spotlight role.</p>
<p>The creator can continue featuring the same video for other eligible returning subscribers who have not watched it.</p>

<h3>Featured Video vs. Channel Trailer</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Featured Video</th><th>Channel Trailer</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>For returning subscribers</td><td>For people who have not subscribed</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Highlights a current or priority viewing choice</td><td>Explains the overall channel promise</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Added through Spotlight</td><td>Added through Channel trailer</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Can be any selected YouTube video</td><td>Usually selected to introduce the creator's channel</td></tr>

<h3>Featured Video vs. Featured Section</h3>
<p>The Home tab can contain up to 12 custom sections. Sections can highlight videos, Shorts, live streams, playlists, memberships, channels, posts, and other eligible content.</p>
<p>The Spotlight featured video is a dedicated single-video placement rather than a row of content.</p>

<h3>Featured Video vs. For You Section</h3>
<p>The For You section is personalized by YouTube based on the individual viewer's watch history and can be limited to selected content types or recent content.</p>
<p>The featured video is chosen directly by the creator as one highlighted video for the returning-subscriber audience.</p>

<h3>Featured Video vs. YouTube Recommendation</h3>
<p>Selecting a featured video does not mean YouTube has endorsed it or will recommend it broadly across Home, Suggested Videos, Search, or the Shorts Feed.</p>
<p>It controls a channel-layout placement for eligible channel visitors.</p>

<h3>Featured Video vs. Pinned Video</h3>
<p>YouTube does not currently describe this Home-tab control as "pinning a video." Creators may use that phrase informally, but the official layout language is Spotlight and featured video.</p>

<h3>How to Add a Featured Video</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Sign in to YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Customization</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open the <strong>Home tab</strong>.</li>
  <li>Make sure the Home tab is enabled.</li>
  <li>Under Layout, select <strong>Add section</strong>.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Spotlight</strong>.</li>
  <li>Choose a video to feature.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Publish</strong>.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Can the Featured Video Come From Another Channel?</h3>
<p>YouTube's current Help page says creators can highlight their video or any video on YouTube for returning subscribers.</p>
<p>Featuring another channel's video can make sense for:</p>
<ul>
  <li>A collaboration</li>
  <li>A partner organization</li>
  <li>An important interview hosted elsewhere</li>
  <li>A source or announcement the audience needs</li>
</ul>
<p>It also sends the viewer away from the creator's own channel, so use it intentionally.</p>

<h3>What Video Should Be Featured?</h3>
<p>Strong options include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The newest major upload</li>
  <li>The next episode in a recurring series</li>
  <li>A high-value video many subscribers have not watched</li>
  <li>A seasonal or timely announcement</li>
  <li>A channel update</li>
  <li>A live replay with lasting value</li>
  <li>A collaboration relevant to subscribers</li>
</ul>

<h3>Newest Video Is Not Always Best</h3>
<p>Do not automatically replace the featured video with every upload. A minor Short, narrow update, or unrelated experiment may be a weak Spotlight choice.</p>
<p>Choose the video that best serves a returning subscriber visiting the channel now.</p>

<h3>Use Subscriber Context</h3>
<p>A returning subscriber already knows at least something about the channel. The featured video does not need to repeat the full introductory pitch.</p>
<p>It can focus on:</p>
<ul>
  <li>What is new</li>
  <li>What the community should watch next</li>
  <li>What has changed</li>
  <li>What major project is underway</li>
  <li>Which underwatched video deserves attention</li>
</ul>

<h3>Featured Video for a New Upload</h3>
<p>Featuring a new upload may help channel visitors notice it, but this layout placement is not a substitute for strong <a href="/glossary/video-packaging/">Video Packaging</a>, notifications, recommendations, Search relevance, or audience demand.</p>

<h3>Featured Video for an Underwatched Video</h3>
<p>An older video can be featured when:</p>
<ul>
  <li>It remains accurate.</li>
  <li>It connects to a current topic.</li>
  <li>New subscribers may have missed it.</li>
  <li>It leads into a new series or launch.</li>
  <li>Its original packaging or timing limited exposure.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Featured Video for a Series</h3>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/video-series/">Video Series</a> can use Spotlight to direct returning subscribers to:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The newest episode</li>
  <li>The series introduction</li>
  <li>A season recap</li>
  <li>A major finale</li>
</ul>
<p>Use playlists and Home-tab rows to make the rest of the series easy to find.</p>

<h3>Featured Video for a Live Stream</h3>
<p>A creator may highlight a live replay or another YouTube video that explains an upcoming event. The exact suitability depends on the selected video's availability and current layout behavior.</p>
<p>A scheduled live stream also has its own watch page and reminder flow, so Spotlight should complement rather than replace those tools.</p>

<h3>Featured Video for a Seasonal Campaign</h3>
<p>Update the Spotlight around:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Tax season</li>
  <li>Holiday buying guides</li>
  <li>Annual product releases</li>
  <li>School-year transitions</li>
  <li>Sports seasons</li>
  <li>Fundraising or membership campaigns</li>
</ul>
<p>Remove outdated time-sensitive videos after the window closes.</p>

<h3>Featured Video and Home-Tab Organization</h3>
<p>The Spotlight video should work with the surrounding sections. A strong layout might contain:</p>
<ol>
  <li>Featured priority video</li>
  <li>Latest uploads</li>
  <li>Start Here playlist</li>
  <li>Current series</li>
  <li>Popular Shorts</li>
  <li>Topic-specific playlists</li>
</ol>

<h3>Up to 12 Custom Sections</h3>
<p>YouTube currently allows up to 12 custom Home-tab sections. The featured video is one part of the layout rather than the entire channel-navigation strategy.</p>

<h3>Do Not Confuse It With the Default Home Rows</h3>
<p>YouTube's Home tab can default to pre-populated sections such as Shorts, Uploads, created playlists, and public subscriptions. These are distinct from the Spotlight featured video.</p>

<h3>Can the Same Video Be the Trailer and Featured Video?</h3>
<p>A creator may choose the same video in both roles when it serves both audiences, but the messaging needs differ.</p>
<p>A broad channel introduction may be repetitive for returning subscribers. A current priority video may not introduce the channel well to non-subscribers.</p>

<h3>Featured Video Title and Thumbnail</h3>
<p>The selected video retains its normal title and thumbnail. Package it for the video's actual viewer promise rather than writing metadata that only makes sense in Spotlight.</p>

<h3>Does It Autoplay?</h3>
<p>The precise playback experience can vary by device, interface, and viewer settings. YouTube's official page defines the role and one-viewer suppression behavior but does not justify promising every returning subscriber the same autoplay experience with sound.</p>

<h3>Featured Video Visibility</h3>
<p>The chosen video must remain available to the intended viewer. A deleted, restricted, or inaccessible video cannot provide the intended Spotlight experience.</p>
<p>Review visibility, age restrictions, geographic availability, copyright status, and member access before featuring content.</p>

<h3>Members-Only Videos</h3>
<p>Home-tab sections can highlight memberships content for eligible channels, but the general featured video for returning subscribers should be chosen with access in mind.</p>
<p>A restricted <a href="/glossary/members-only-video/">Members-Only Video</a> is not useful to every ordinary subscriber.</p>

<h3>When to Update the Featured Video</h3>
<ul>
  <li>A major new video publishes.</li>
  <li>The current video becomes outdated.</li>
  <li>A campaign or season ends.</li>
  <li>The channel launches a new series.</li>
  <li>Returning-subscriber needs change.</li>
  <li>The selected video is no longer available.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Do Not Update It Automatically</h3>
<p>Set a review process instead:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Review after major uploads.</li>
  <li>Review monthly for active channels.</li>
  <li>Review before seasonal campaigns.</li>
  <li>Review after changes to channel positioning.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Measuring Featured-Video Performance</h3>
<p>YouTube does not provide a dedicated public metric labeled "Spotlight featured video conversion rate" in the cited layout documentation.</p>
<p>Use a combination of:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Channel Pages Traffic</li>
  <li>Video views and retention</li>
  <li>Subscriber gains</li>
  <li>Home-tab behavior where available</li>
  <li>Views on surrounding sections</li>
  <li>Qualitative community response</li>
</ul>

<h3>Channel Pages Traffic</h3>
<p><a href="/glossary/channel-pages-traffic/">Channel Pages Traffic</a> can indicate views originating from channel pages, but it does not necessarily isolate every Spotlight impression or explain why a viewer clicked.</p>

<h3>Featured Video and Returning Viewers</h3>
<p>The placement is for returning subscribers, while <a href="/glossary/returning-viewers/">Returning Viewers</a> is an Analytics audience category that can include subscribed and unsubscribed viewers who came back.</p>
<p>Do not treat the layout audience and Analytics label as identical populations.</p>

<h3>Accessibility</h3>
<p>The selected video should include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Accurate captions</li>
  <li>Readable visuals</li>
  <li>Clear audio</li>
  <li>Context for people who missed earlier uploads</li>
  <li>A title that explains its value</li>
</ul>

<h3>Common Featured-Video Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Confusing it with a channel trailer.</li>
  <li>Assuming it is a broad recommendation boost.</li>
  <li>Replacing it with every upload.</li>
  <li>Featuring inaccessible content.</li>
  <li>Using a video that returning subscribers have already broadly seen.</li>
  <li>Leaving a seasonal announcement active too long.</li>
  <li>Assuming every visit autoplays the same way.</li>
  <li>Ignoring the rest of the Home-tab layout.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Who sees a YouTube featured video?</h4>
<p>YouTube describes the Spotlight featured video as content for returning subscribers visiting the channel Home tab.</p>

<h4>Can I feature another channel's video?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube says the creator can highlight their video or any video on YouTube.</p>

<h4>Will viewers see it repeatedly?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube says it will not be shown again after that viewer watches it.</p>

<h4>Does featuring a video boost recommendations?</h4>
<p>YouTube documents it as a channel-layout placement, not a guaranteed broader recommendation boost.</p>
