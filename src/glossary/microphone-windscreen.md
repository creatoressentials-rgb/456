<h2>Microphone Windscreen</h2>
<p>A <strong>microphone windscreen</strong> is a protective cover or enclosure that reduces airflow reaching a microphone capsule.</p>
<p>It can reduce noise caused by:</p>
<ul>
<li>Outdoor wind</li>
<li>Breath</li>
<li>Fast microphone movement</li>
<li>Air conditioning</li>
<li>Fans</li>
<li>Walking or running</li>
<li>Vehicle movement</li>
</ul>
<p>A windscreen does not make a microphone waterproof or eliminate every wind condition.</p>

<p>A foam windscreen can also reduce plosives, so the categories overlap.</p>
<h3>Types of microphone windscreens</h3>
<h4>Foam windscreen</h4>
<p>A fitted foam cover.</p>
<p>Best for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Indoor breath noise</li>
<li>Light wind</li>
<li>Handheld microphones</li>
<li>Headset microphones</li>
<li>Moderate microphone movement</li>
</ul>
<h4>Furry windscreen</h4>
<p>Often called a windjammer, furry cover, or "dead cat."</p>
<p>Best for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Stronger outdoor wind</li>
<li>On-camera microphones</li>
<li>Shotgun microphones</li>
<li>Wireless transmitters</li>
<li>Field recording</li>
</ul>
<p>The fur breaks up turbulent airflow before it reaches the microphone.</p>
<h4>Basket or blimp windshield</h4>
<p>A rigid open structure surrounds the microphone and creates a pocket of still air.</p>
<p>Often combined with:</p>
<ul>
<li>Internal suspension</li>
<li>Furry outer cover</li>
<li>Shotgun microphone</li>
<li>Boom pole</li>
</ul>
<p>This provides stronger protection but is larger and more expensive.</p>
<h4>Lavalier wind protection</h4>
<p>Small foam, fur, or concealer systems protect a clip-on microphone.</p>
<p>Placement must also prevent clothing rustle.</p>

<h3>Why wind creates low-frequency rumble</h3>
<p>Wind creates pressure changes around the microphone capsule.</p>
<p>These changes can overload the capsule or preamp with powerful low-frequency noise.</p>
<p>Once severe wind rumble is recorded, editing may not restore the covered voice cleanly.</p>
<p>Prevention is more effective.</p>
<h3>Choosing protection for YouTube</h3>
<h4>Indoor podcast</h4>
<p>A foam windscreen or pop filter may be enough.</p>
<h4>Outdoor vlog</h4>
<p>Use fitted furry protection on the on-camera or wireless microphone.</p>
<h4>Walking interview</h4>
<p>Protect both transmitters and lavaliers, and secure clothing.</p>
<h4>Boom microphone</h4>
<p>Use foam in calm conditions and a blimp or furry system when wind increases.</p>
<h4>Fitness video</h4>
<p>Use secure wind protection because body movement creates airflow even indoors.</p>
<h3>Windscreen fit</h3>
<p>A windscreen should:</p>
<ul>
<li>Match microphone size</li>
<li>Cover acoustic openings</li>
<li>Remain secure</li>
<li>Avoid crushing the capsule area</li>
<li>Avoid blocking connectors or controls</li>
<li>Remain acoustically appropriate</li>
</ul>
<p>A loose cover can fall off or create rubbing noise.</p>
<h3>High-frequency effects</h3>
<p>Wind protection can slightly reduce high-frequency detail.</p>
<p>The effect increases when the material is:</p>
<ul>
<li>Too dense</li>
<li>Wet</li>
<li>Dirty</li>
<li>Crushed</li>
<li>Layered excessively</li>
<li>Poorly fitted</li>
</ul>
<p>Use only as much protection as conditions require.</p>
<h3>Wet and dirty windscreens</h3>
<p>Moisture and dirt can reduce performance and damage materials.</p>
<p>After outdoor use:</p>
<ul>
<li>Remove the cover</li>
<li>Let it dry fully</li>
<li>Follow cleaning instructions</li>
<li>Brush fur gently</li>
<li>Check foam deterioration</li>
<li>Store dry</li>
<li>Keep moisture away from the microphone</li>
</ul>
<h3>Windscreens and wireless microphones</h3>
<p>Compact wireless transmitters often include small furry covers.</p>
<p>Confirm they are:</p>
<ul>
<li>Locked securely</li>
<li>Not blocked by clothing</li>
<li>Positioned away from necklaces</li>
<li>Close enough to the mouth</li>
<li>Protected on every transmitter</li>
</ul>
<h3>What a windscreen cannot fix</h3>
<ul>
<li>Clothing rustle</li>
<li>Cable noise</li>
<li>Handling vibration</li>
<li>Electronic interference</li>
<li>Clipping</li>
<li>Distant microphone placement</li>
<li>Room echo</li>
<li>Wireless dropout</li>
<li>Extremely severe wind</li>
</ul>
<p>A shock mount, proper gain, and better placement may also be required.</p>
<h3>Common mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Using bare foam in strong wind</li>
<li>Assuming a pop filter is outdoor protection</li>
<li>Letting fur become wet and matted</li>
<li>Covering the wrong microphone openings</li>
<li>Using a loose universal cover</li>
<li>Forgetting wind created by movement</li>
<li>Hiding a lavalier under rubbing fabric</li>
<li>Expecting editing to repair severe rumble</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is a microphone windscreen the same as a pop filter?</h4>
<p>No. Windscreens primarily control airflow and wind, while pop filters primarily control speech plosives. Some foam products help with both.</p>
<h4>Is foam enough for outdoor recording?</h4>
<p>It can handle calm conditions or light wind. Furry or blimp-style protection is better for stronger wind.</p>
<h4>Why is a furry windscreen called a dead cat?</h4>
<p>It is informal industry slang based on its appearance. "Furry windscreen" or "windjammer" is clearer.</p>
<h4>Does a windscreen reduce sound quality?</h4>
<p>It can slightly reduce high frequencies, but properly fitted protection usually improves usable outdoor audio dramatically.</p>
