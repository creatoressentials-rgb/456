<h2>Screen Recording</h2>
<p><strong>Screen recording</strong> is the process of capturing activity on a computer, phone, tablet, or other display as a video file or live video source. Depending on the software and operating system, a creator may record an entire display, a selected area, one application window, one browser tab, or a game.</p>
<p>Screen recordings are widely used for software tutorials, gaming videos, online courses, product demonstrations, troubleshooting, presentations, reactions, and remote collaboration.</p>

<h3>What Can a Screen Recorder Capture?</h3>
<table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Capture Type</th><th>What It Includes</th><th>Common Use</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Display capture</td><td>An entire monitor or display</td><td>Workflows that move between several applications</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Window capture</td><td>One application window</td><td>Software demonstrations with fewer privacy risks</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Browser-tab capture</td><td>One selected browser tab</td><td>Web tutorials and tab audio in supported browsers</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Region capture</td><td>A selected portion of the screen</td><td>Focusing on one panel or hiding unrelated areas</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Game capture</td><td>A compatible game or graphics application</td><td>Gameplay with potentially more efficient capture</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Screen-and-camera capture</td><td>The display plus a webcam or camera view</td><td>Courses, reactions, presentations, and tutorials</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>The available choices depend on the operating system and recording application. OBS, for example, treats Display Capture and Game Capture as different source types rather than interchangeable names.</p>

<h3>Screen Recording vs. Screen Sharing</h3>
<table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Feature</th><th>Screen Recording</th><th>Screen Sharing</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Primary purpose</td><td>Create a video file or recorded production</td><td>Show a screen to people in real time</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Saved automatically</td><td>Usually, when configured</td><td>Not necessarily</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Editing</td><td>Can be trimmed, annotated, or combined later</td><td>Usually viewed live</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Audience</td><td>Can be published or archived</td><td>Meeting, class, stream, or remote session</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>A livestreaming application can also record and share a screen simultaneously. The distinction is the purpose and output, not always the software used.</p>

<h3>Microphone Audio vs. System Audio</h3>
<p>Screen video, microphone audio, and system audio are separate capture sources:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Microphone audio</strong> records the creator's voice or room sound through a selected input.</li>
  <li><strong>System audio</strong> records sound played by applications or the operating system.</li>
  <li><strong>Application or tab audio</strong> captures sound from one selected app or browser tab when supported.</li>
</ul>
<p>A screen recorder does not automatically include all three. Microsoft notes that some browser-based screen-recording workflows capture shared audio only when a browser tab is selected and its audio-sharing option is enabled. Apple separately controls screen, system-audio, and microphone permissions on current macOS versions.</p>

<h3>How to Prepare for a Screen Recording</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Close private messages, password managers, email, financial records, and unrelated tabs.</li>
  <li>Disable notifications and hide desktop files that should not appear.</li>
  <li>Choose the final aspect ratio and practical output resolution.</li>
  <li>Increase interface or browser zoom so text remains readable after compression.</li>
  <li>Set the cursor size and highlighting tools when viewers need to follow clicks.</li>
  <li>Select the correct microphone and system-audio source.</li>
  <li>Test a short recording and verify picture, cursor, microphone, and app audio.</li>
  <li>Confirm that enough storage space is available at the selected quality.</li>
</ol>

<h3>How Resolution and Scaling Affect Readability</h3>
<p>Recording a very high-resolution display does not guarantee readable tutorial text. If a 4K desktop is reduced to a small 1080p or vertical video, menus and labels may become too small.</p>
<p>Creators can improve clarity by:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Increasing display scaling or application zoom before recording</li>
  <li>Capturing only the relevant window or region</li>
  <li>Using deliberate cursor movement</li>
  <li>Adding close-up crops or zooms during editing</li>
  <li>Avoiding tiny text near the edges where mobile interfaces may cover it</li>
  <li>Testing the final video at actual phone-viewing size</li>
</ul>

<h3>Screen Recording With a Camera</h3>
<p>A creator can place a camera feed over the screen recording or switch between the two. The camera may come from a webcam, a <a href="/glossary/capture-card/">capture card</a>, or a <a href="/glossary/virtual-camera/">virtual camera</a> workflow.</p>
<p>Keep the camera box away from important controls, subtitles, and demonstrations. A consistent position helps viewers understand where information will appear.</p>

<h3>Why Do Some Apps Record as a Black Screen?</h3>
<p>Possible reasons include operating-system permissions, incompatible capture methods, hardware-accelerated graphics, protected video, application security, or digital-rights restrictions.</p>
<p>Apple states that some apps may not allow their windows, audio, or video to be recorded. Creators should not attempt to bypass protection on content they are not authorized to capture.</p>

<h3>Screen Recording Quality Settings</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Resolution:</strong> Match the content and delivery format without creating unnecessarily large files.</li>
  <li><strong>Frame rate:</strong> 30 fps is sufficient for many software tutorials; fast games or motion may benefit from 60 fps.</li>
  <li><strong>Encoder:</strong> A supported hardware encoder can reduce processor load.</li>
  <li><strong>Bitrate or quality setting:</strong> Text and rapid motion may need more data than a simple static presentation.</li>
  <li><strong>File format:</strong> Recording to a resilient container and remuxing later can reduce loss if the app or computer crashes.</li>
  <li><strong>Storage path:</strong> Record to a drive with enough sustained performance and free space.</li>
</ul>
<p>OBS recommends making a short test recording before an important session. The correct settings depend on the computer, content, encoder, storage, and desired output.</p>

<h3>Common Screen-Recording Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Recording the entire desktop when one window would reduce privacy risk.</li>
  <li>Assuming microphone audio also captures system audio.</li>
  <li>Leaving notifications, account names, personal files, or client information visible.</li>
  <li>Moving the cursor rapidly without explaining where viewers should look.</li>
  <li>Recording text too small to read after export.</li>
  <li>Using a frame rate or quality setting that overloads the computer.</li>
  <li>Recording protected content and interpreting a black screen as a software defect.</li>
  <li>Failing to check the saved file before closing the demonstrated application.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Does screen recording include sound?</h4>
<p>It can, but microphone, system, and application audio may need to be enabled separately. Support varies by platform, app, browser, and permissions.</p>

<h4>Can a website tell that the screen is being recorded?</h4>
<p>Some platforms, browsers, or secure applications may detect or restrict capture. Policies and technical behavior vary, so creators should follow applicable permissions, agreements, and laws.</p>

<h4>What is the best frame rate for screen recording?</h4>
<p>Thirty frames per second works for many tutorials and presentations. Sixty frames per second can better preserve fast cursor movement, animation, or gameplay but creates a heavier workload and larger files.</p>

<h4>Is screen recording the same as using a capture card?</h4>
<p>No. Screen-recording software captures content available within the device or operating system. A capture card brings an external video signal into the computer.</p>
