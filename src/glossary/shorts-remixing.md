<h2>Shorts Remixing</h2>
<p><strong>Shorts Remixing</strong> is YouTube's set of creation tools for making a new Short using eligible audio, video, layouts, templates, or generated transformations connected to existing YouTube content.</p>
<p>Remixing is not the same as downloading and reuploading another person's video. YouTube's in-product tools preserve source attribution, apply platform permissions, and connect the source and resulting remixes through player metadata and discovery pages.</p>

<h3>What Can Be Remixed?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Audio from many Shorts and long-form videos</li>
  <li>Video segments from eligible Shorts and videos</li>
  <li>Music from the Shorts audio library</li>
  <li>Shorts templates</li>
  <li>Eligible scenes transformed with AI remix features</li>
  <li>Content that has already been remixed</li>
</ul>
<p>Availability depends on privacy, copyright ownership, creator permissions, music licensing, account eligibility, device, language, and feature rollout.</p>

<h3>Main Shorts Remix Options</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Tool</th><th>What It Does</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Sound</td><td>Uses eligible original audio or music in a new Short</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Cut</td><td>Samples a short video segment from an eligible video</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Green Screen</td><td>Uses eligible video content as the background</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Collab</td><td>Places the source video and new recording together in a selected layout</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Template</td><td>Uses the timing and clip structure of an eligible Short while replacing its media</td></tr>
    <tr><td>AI remix</td><td>Transforms an eligible scene using a prompt or suggested action where available</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>How to Remix Another Short</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open the eligible Short in the YouTube mobile app.</li>
  <li>Tap <strong>Remix</strong>.</li>
  <li>Select an available option such as Sound, Cut, Green Screen, Collab, Template, or an AI action.</li>
  <li>Record or add your original contribution.</li>
  <li>Edit the result using the Shorts creation tools.</li>
  <li>Add the title, visibility, audience, and other settings.</li>
  <li>Upload the new Short.</li>
</ol>
<p>Not every tool appears on every source video.</p>

<h3>How to Remix a Long-Form Video</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open the eligible video's watch page in the YouTube mobile app.</li>
  <li>Tap <strong>Share</strong>.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Remix</strong>.</li>
  <li>Choose an available audio or visual option.</li>
  <li>Create and upload the Short.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Audio Remixing</h3>
<p>Audio remixing lets creators use eligible original audio or a licensed song available through YouTube's Shorts tools.</p>
<p>Music-video remix rules can allow:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Up to 90 seconds of eligible audio</li>
  <li>Shorter limits, such as 30 seconds, under some partner agreements</li>
  <li>One-to-five-second Cut samples from eligible music videos</li>
  <li>Green Screen use of eligible music-video visuals</li>
</ul>
<p>The available duration shown by YouTube controls the permitted use.</p>

<h3>Can You Remix a Remix?</h3>
<p>Yes. YouTube says creators can remix content that was already remixed. Available options can include audio, Cut, Collab, and Green Screen.</p>
<p>The source video and later remixes remain connected through pivot pages and player metadata.</p>

<h3>How Attribution Works</h3>
<p>YouTube's in-product remix tools automatically connect the remix to source material. Viewers can use sound, remix, and player links to explore related content.</p>
<p>Attribution does not mean the remix creator owns the source content. It identifies the relationship and uses YouTube's available permissions.</p>

<h3>Does Remixing Avoid Copyright?</h3>
<p>No. The in-product tools make eligible uses available under YouTube's current licensing and permissions. They do not create a general copyright exception for use outside the tools.</p>
<p>If a creator downloads copyrighted material or edits it elsewhere without permission, the upload can receive a <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a> claim, copyright removal request, or <a href="/glossary/copyright-strike/">copyright strike</a>.</p>

<h3>Why Is the Remix Option Missing?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The source is Private.</li>
  <li>The creator restricted remixing.</li>
  <li>A third-party copyright owner did not make the content available.</li>
  <li>The video has a claim that blocks visual remixing.</li>
  <li>The music agreement limits the available options.</li>
  <li>The feature is unavailable on the device, account, language, or region.</li>
  <li>The format is not supported.</li>
  <li>The source was edited, deleted, or made ineligible.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Are Videos Opted In Automatically?</h3>
<p>YouTube says Shorts and long-form content are generally made available for remixing by default when eligible. Creators can limit remixing in YouTube Studio.</p>
<p>For ordinary creators, current controls allow them to opt Shorts out of video remixing. Partners using YouTube Studio Content Manager can have broader audio and video controls.</p>

<h3>How to Restrict Remixing</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Content</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open the video.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Show more</strong>.</li>
  <li>Find <strong>Shorts remixing</strong>.</li>
  <li>Choose the allowed setting.</li>
  <li>Save the change.</li>
</ol>
<p>Creators can also edit applicable settings during upload or through bulk editing.</p>

<h3>What Happens When Someone Remixes Your Content?</h3>
<p>YouTube sends remix notifications in summary form. Its current guidance says a maximum of one summary notification can be sent per day and up to three times per week, even when several creators remix the content.</p>
<p>Creators can also review Top Remixed Analytics and traffic from remixed videos.</p>

<h3>What Happens If the Source Is Deleted or Restricted?</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Remix Type</th><th>Current YouTube Treatment</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Audio remix</td><td>Muted, changed to Unlisted, and scheduled for deletion in 30 days</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Video remix</td><td>Deleted after the source creator deletes or restricts the source</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>YouTube emails affected remix creators. Previously accrued views remain in the remix creator's lifetime view total.</p>

<h3>What Happens If the Source Is Trimmed or Blurred?</h3>
<p>Using the <a href="/glossary/youtube-trim-tool/">YouTube Trim Tool</a> or <a href="/glossary/youtube-blur-tool/">YouTube Blur Tool</a> on the source can trigger the same downstream consequences:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Audio remixes can be muted, made Unlisted, and scheduled for deletion.</li>
  <li>Video remixes can be deleted.</li>
</ul>
<p>Source creators should review remix activity before making irreversible Studio Editor changes.</p>

<h3>Remixing and Monetization</h3>
<p>A Short using remixed content is not automatically disqualified from monetization. However, the creator must add sufficient original value and comply with Shorts, copyright, advertiser-friendly, and <a href="/glossary/reused-content/">Reused Content</a> policies.</p>
<p>YouTube's Shorts monetization documentation explains that some third-party rights can affect Creator Pool calculations and revenue allocation.</p>

<h3>Remixing vs. Reused Content</h3>
<p>Using an official Remix tool does not guarantee that a channel passes YPP reused-content review. Reviewers evaluate the channel's overall originality and whether the creator adds meaningful commentary, transformation, participation, or value.</p>

<h3>Remix Analytics</h3>
<p>YouTube's Top Remixed report can show:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Total remixes</li>
  <li>Remix views</li>
  <li>Top source content being remixed</li>
  <li>Filters such as Sound, Cut, and Green Screen</li>
</ul>
<p>Traffic from visual remixes can appear under the Remixed Video traffic source.</p>

<h3>AI Remix Features</h3>
<p>YouTube has introduced upgraded AI remix tools that can transform eligible Shorts from prompts or suggestions. Current Help says the feature supports English-language prompts and is rolling out to most users.</p>
<p>Generated output can include new audio based on the prompt and scene context. Availability and controls remain subject to rollout and source remix permissions.</p>

<h3>Best Practices for Remix Creators</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Add a clear original contribution.</li>
  <li>Use source material only through eligible tools.</li>
  <li>Do not imply endorsement by the source creator.</li>
  <li>Keep commentary or participation central.</li>
  <li>Understand that the source can later be restricted or deleted.</li>
  <li>Preserve your original recordings and project files.</li>
  <li>Check music and monetization limitations.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Best Practices for Source Creators</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Review Shorts remixing settings before publishing.</li>
  <li>Use Top Remixed to identify content that sparks participation.</li>
  <li>Consider whether remixing supports discovery and community goals.</li>
  <li>Understand the consequences before restricting or editing a source.</li>
  <li>Keep records of permission and rights for content made available.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Common Shorts-Remixing Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Treating a remix as an unrestricted download license.</li>
  <li>Assuming attribution eliminates copyright requirements.</li>
  <li>Believing every video offers every remix tool.</li>
  <li>Ignoring default remix permissions.</li>
  <li>Assuming a remix survives if the source is deleted.</li>
  <li>Calling every compilation a transformative remix.</li>
  <li>Ignoring reused-content monetization review.</li>
  <li>Editing a source without considering existing remixes.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Can anyone remix my Short?</h4>
<p>Eligible content is generally opted in by default, but availability depends on rights and settings, and creators can restrict remixing.</p>

<h4>Can I remix a remix?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube supports remixing already remixed content when the applicable options remain available.</p>

<h4>Does YouTube notify the source creator?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube sends summary remix notifications, subject to its frequency limits.</p>

<h4>What happens if the source is deleted?</h4>
<p>Audio remixes are muted, made Unlisted, and scheduled for deletion; video remixes are deleted.</p>
