<h2>Shock Mount</h2>
<p>A <strong>shock mount</strong> is a microphone holder designed to reduce vibrations traveling into the microphone through a desk, stand, boom arm, floor, or mounting point.</p>
<p>Those vibrations can create:</p>
<ul>
<li>Low-frequency rumble</li>
<li>Desk thumps</li>
<li>Keyboard vibration</li>
<li>Footstep noise</li>
<li>Stand movement</li>
<li>Handling noise</li>
<li>Mechanical resonance</li>
</ul>
<p>A shock mount does not improve the microphone's electronic signal. It mechanically isolates the microphone from structure-borne vibration.</p>
<h3>How a shock mount works</h3>
<p>A shock mount suspends or cushions the microphone using materials such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>Elastic bands</li>
<li>Rubber</li>
<li>Silicone</li>
<li>Springs</li>
<li>Flexible suspension</li>
<li>Pneumatic isolation</li>
<li>Internal cartridge suspension</li>
</ul>
<p>The suspension allows the mount to absorb movement before it reaches the microphone capsule.</p>

</tr>

</table>
<p>Some microphones already contain enough internal isolation that an external shock mount adds little benefit.</p>

<table>
<thead>
<tr>

<p>A standard clip can work well when the microphone and stand are not being disturbed.</p>
<h3>Why YouTubers use shock mounts</h3>
<h4>Desk recording</h4>
<p>Typing, touching the desk, or moving a mouse can send vibration through a desk-mounted <a href="/glossary/boom-arm/">boom arm</a>.</p>
<h4>Podcasting</h4>
<p>Guests may bump the table or adjust the microphone.</p>
<h4>Voice-over</h4>
<p>Sensitive microphones can capture low-frequency vibration from floors and stands.</p>
<h4>Livestreaming</h4>
<p>Repeated desk movement during gaming can create distracting thumps.</p>
<h3>What a shock mount cannot fix</h3>
<p>A shock mount does not solve:</p>
<ul>
<li>Room echo</li>
<li>Background voices</li>
<li>Computer fan noise</li>
<li>Wind</li>
<li>Plosives</li>
<li>Electrical hum</li>
<li>Wireless interference</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/audio-clipping/">Audio clipping</a></li>
<li>Cable rubbing directly against the microphone</li>
<li>A microphone placed too far away</li>
</ul>
<p>It addresses mechanical vibration transmitted through the mount.</p>
<h3>Shock mount compatibility</h3>
<p>Check:</p>
<ul>
<li>Microphone diameter</li>
<li>Microphone shape</li>
<li>Weight</li>
<li>Thread size</li>
<li>Mounting orientation</li>
<li>Connector clearance</li>
<li>Cable clearance</li>
<li>Boom-arm capacity</li>
<li>Included adapter</li>
<li>Manufacturer compatibility</li>
</ul>
<p>A universal shock mount may not hold an unusually heavy, tapered, or side-address microphone securely.</p>
<h3>Thread sizes and adapters</h3>
<p>Common microphone mounting threads include:</p>
<ul>
<li>5/8-inch</li>
<li>3/8-inch</li>
<li>Other manufacturer-specific systems</li>
</ul>
<p>Adapters can connect different standards, but loose adapters can introduce movement or noise.</p>
<h3>Shock mounts and boom arms</h3>
<p>A shock mount and boom arm solve different problems.</p>
<p>The boom arm positions the microphone.</p>
<p>The shock mount isolates the microphone from vibration traveling through that arm.</p>
<p>A stable arm with good internal microphone isolation may be sufficient without an external shock mount.</p>
<h3>Shock mount maintenance</h3>
<p>Inspect:</p>
<ul>
<li>Stretched elastic</li>
<li>Cracked rubber</li>
<li>Loose screws</li>
<li>Worn threads</li>
<li>Bent frame</li>
<li>Cable contact</li>
<li>Microphone sag</li>
</ul>
<p>Elastic suspension can weaken over time and allow the microphone to tilt or touch the frame.</p>
<h3>Placement mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Letting the microphone touch the mount frame</li>
<li>Routing the cable tightly across the suspension</li>
<li>Using an undersized mount</li>
<li>Exceeding the mount's weight capacity</li>
<li>Allowing the mount to hit the desk</li>
<li>Assuming every vibration comes through the stand</li>
<li>Ignoring a loose desk clamp</li>
<li>Mounting the microphone in the wrong orientation</li>
</ul>
<h3>Does every YouTuber need a shock mount?</h3>
<p>No.</p>
<p>A shock mount is most valuable when:</p>
<ul>
<li>The microphone is desk-mounted</li>
<li>The desk is frequently bumped</li>
<li>The microphone is sensitive to low-frequency vibration</li>
<li>The room has footstep vibration</li>
<li>The microphone lacks sufficient internal isolation</li>
</ul>
<p>A creator using a handheld microphone, stable floor stand, or well-isolated broadcast microphone may not need one.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>What does a microphone shock mount do?</h4>
<p>It reduces vibration traveling from the desk, stand, or boom arm into the microphone.</p>
<h4>Does a shock mount improve sound quality?</h4>
<p>It can prevent rumble and thumps, but it does not change the microphone's basic tone or remove room noise.</p>
<h4>Can any shock mount fit any microphone?</h4>
<p>No. Diameter, shape, weight, mounting thread, and connector clearance must be compatible.</p>
<h4>Do microphones with internal shock mounts need an external one?</h4>
<p>Not always. Test the setup before buying another mount.</p>
