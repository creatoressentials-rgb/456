<h2>Cardioid Microphone</h2>
<p>A <strong>cardioid microphone</strong> captures sound primarily from the front and rejects the greatest amount of sound from directly behind it.</p>
<p>Its idealized <a href="/glossary/polar-pattern/">polar pattern</a> resembles a heart, which is where the name "cardioid" comes from.</p>
<p>Cardioid microphones are common for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Podcasting</li>
<li>Livestreaming</li>
<li>Voice-over</li>
<li>Gaming</li>
<li>Talking-head videos</li>
<li>Singing</li>
<li>Interviews</li>
<li>Desk recording</li>
</ul>

<p>The exact pattern varies by microphone and frequency.</p>
<h3>Why cardioid works well for creators</h3>
<p>A cardioid mic helps prioritize the creator's voice while reducing sound from behind the microphone.</p>
<p>This can help with:</p>
<ul>
<li>Keyboard noise</li>
<li>Computer fans</li>
<li>Room noise</li>
<li>Other speakers</li>
<li>Reflections</li>
<li>Equipment behind the microphone</li>
</ul>
<p>It does not erase background sound. Placement and microphone distance remain critical.</p>
<h3>Pointing a cardioid microphone correctly</h3>
<p>Microphones can be:</p>
<h4>End-address</h4>
<p>The creator speaks into the end.</p>
<p>Examples often include handheld vocal and broadcast-style microphones.</p>
<h4>Side-address</h4>
<p>The creator speaks into the marked front side, not the top.</p>
<p>Many studio condenser microphones are side-address.</p>
<p>Speaking into the wrong side can produce weak, muffled audio.</p>
<h3>Using the rear rejection</h3>
<p>Place unwanted sound toward the microphone's rear when practical.</p>
<p>For a desk setup:</p>
<ol>
<li>Point the front toward the creator.</li>
<li>Place the keyboard or computer toward the rear.</li>
<li>Keep the microphone close to the mouth.</li>
<li>Reduce gain accordingly.</li>
<li>Monitor for room reflections.</li>
</ol>
<p>The rear null cannot reject sound that reflects from walls and returns from the front or sides.</p>

<h3>Cardioid vs. supercardioid</h3>
<p>Supercardioid has:</p>
<ul>
<li>Narrower front pickup</li>
<li>Greater side rejection</li>
<li>A small rear pickup lobe</li>
</ul>
<p>Cardioid has:</p>
<ul>
<li>Wider front pickup</li>
<li>Strongest rejection directly behind</li>
<li>More forgiving speaker movement</li>
</ul>
<h3>Proximity effect</h3>
<p>A cardioid microphone often produces more bass when the speaker moves very close.</p>
<p>This can sound:</p>
<ul>
<li>Warm</li>
<li>Full</li>
<li>Radio-like</li>
</ul>
<p>It can also sound:</p>
<ul>
<li>Boomy</li>
<li>Muddy</li>
<li>Inconsistent</li>
</ul>
<p>Maintain a repeatable speaking distance.</p>
<h3>Cardioid microphones and room echo</h3>
<p>Cardioid rejection can reduce some room sound, but it cannot remove reverberation already reaching the microphone from the front and sides.</p>
<p>Improve results by:</p>
<ul>
<li>Moving closer</li>
<li>Lowering gain</li>
<li>Adding <a href="/glossary/acoustic-treatment/">acoustic treatment</a></li>
<li>Moving away from bare walls</li>
<li>Reducing reflective surfaces</li>
<li>Choosing a quieter room</li>
</ul>
<h3>Cardioid microphone placement</h3>
<h4>Podcast microphone</h4>
<p>Place roughly several inches from the mouth, slightly off-axis if plosives are strong.</p>
<h4>USB microphone</h4>
<p>Confirm which side contains the cardioid front.</p>
<h4>Camera microphone</h4>
<p>Aim the microphone toward the speaker and keep it as close as practical.</p>
<h4>Two-person interview</h4>
<p>Use one cardioid microphone per person when possible.</p>
<h3>Cardioid and plosives</h3>
<p>Close speech can create bursts from letters such as P and B.</p>
<p>Use:</p>
<ul>
<li><a href="/glossary/pop-filter/">Pop Filter</a></li>
<li><a href="/glossary/microphone-windscreen/">Microphone Windscreen</a></li>
<li>Slight off-axis placement</li>
<li>Proper speaking distance</li>
</ul>
<h3>Common mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Calling cardioid noise canceling</li>
<li>Speaking into the rear</li>
<li>Using too much gain</li>
<li>Placing the microphone too far away</li>
<li>Ignoring room reflections</li>
<li>Letting the speaker move outside the front pickup area</li>
<li>Assuming every cardioid pattern is identical</li>
<li>Placing a loud keyboard in front of the microphone</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Does a cardioid microphone remove background noise?</h4>
<p>No. It reduces sensitivity from certain directions, especially the rear, but it still records background sound and reflections.</p>
<h4>Which side of a cardioid microphone should I speak into?</h4>
<p>Use the designated front. Check whether the microphone is end-address or side-address.</p>
<h4>Is cardioid good for podcasting?</h4>
<p>Yes. It is a common choice because it prioritizes one speaker and offers useful rejection.</p>
<h4>Why does my cardioid microphone sound boomy?</h4>
<p>You may be too close and experiencing proximity effect.</p>
