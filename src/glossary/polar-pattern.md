<h2>Polar Pattern</h2>
<p>A <strong>polar pattern</strong> is a diagram showing how strongly a microphone captures sound arriving from different directions.</p>
<p>It helps creators understand whether a microphone mainly hears:</p>
<ul>
<li>Sound in front</li>
<li>Sound from every direction</li>
<li>Sound from the front and back</li>
<li>A narrow area in front</li>
<li>Sound from the sides</li>
</ul>
<p>A polar pattern describes <strong>directional sensitivity</strong>. It does not show microphone quality, recording range, frequency response, or how loud the microphone is.</p>
<h3>How to read a polar pattern</h3>
<p>A polar-pattern chart is usually viewed from above.</p>
<ul>
<li>The microphone sits in the center.</li>
<li>Zero degrees represents the front.</li>
<li>Ninety and 270 degrees represent the sides.</li>
<li>180 degrees represents the rear.</li>
<li>The plotted line shows relative sensitivity.</li>
</ul>
<p>A larger bulge in one direction means the microphone is more sensitive there.</p>

<h3>Cardioid pattern</h3>
<p>A cardioid pattern is heart-shaped.</p>
<p>It captures strongest from the front and rejects most strongly from directly behind.</p>
<p>This makes it useful when a creator wants to reduce:</p>
<ul>
<li>Keyboard noise behind the microphone</li>
<li>Computer fan noise</li>
<li>Room reflections from the rear</li>
<li>Other voices behind the mic</li>
</ul>
<p>The microphone must be pointed correctly. Speaking into the rear of a cardioid mic can sound quiet and dull.</p>
<h3>Omnidirectional pattern</h3>
<p>An omnidirectional microphone captures sound from all directions around the capsule.</p>
<p>It is useful when:</p>
<ul>
<li>The speaker moves their head</li>
<li>A lavalier is clipped to clothing</li>
<li>Several people surround one microphone</li>
<li>The creator wants room ambience</li>
</ul>
<p>"Omnidirectional" does not mean every sound is captured at exactly the same level or tone in every real environment.</p>
<h3>Figure-eight pattern</h3>
<p>A figure-eight microphone captures from the front and rear while rejecting the sides.</p>
<p>It can be useful for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Two people facing one another</li>
<li>Mid-side stereo recording</li>
<li>Rejecting sound from the sides</li>
<li>Certain interview arrangements</li>
</ul>
<p>It also captures whatever is behind the microphone, so room placement matters.</p>
<h3>Supercardioid and hypercardioid</h3>
<p>These patterns narrow the forward pickup compared with cardioid.</p>
<p>They provide stronger side rejection but include some sensitivity at the rear.</p>
<p>That means the noisiest area should not automatically be placed directly behind the microphone.</p>
<h3>Polar pattern vs. microphone type</h3>
<p>Polar pattern is separate from microphone construction.</p>
<p>A microphone can be:</p>
<ul>
<li>Dynamic and cardioid</li>
<li>Condenser and cardioid</li>
<li>Condenser and omnidirectional</li>
<li>Lavalier and omnidirectional</li>
<li>Shotgun and supercardioid or lobar</li>
<li>Multi-pattern condenser</li>
</ul>
<p>See <a href="/glossary/xlr-microphone/">Dynamic Microphone</a> and <a href="/glossary/usb-microphone/">Condenser Microphone</a>.</p>
<h3>Polar patterns and room noise</h3>
<p>A directional microphone can reduce unwanted sound, but it does not remove room noise automatically.</p>
<p>Results depend on:</p>
<ul>
<li>Microphone distance</li>
<li>Microphone orientation</li>
<li>Room reflections</li>
<li>Sound direction</li>
<li>Frequency</li>
<li>Microphone sensitivity</li>
<li>Recording level</li>
</ul>
<p>Moving a microphone closer to the creator often improves voice-to-room balance more than changing patterns.</p>
<h3>Polar patterns and frequency</h3>
<p>The ideal chart is usually measured at one frequency.</p>
<p>A real microphone may become:</p>
<ul>
<li>Less directional at low frequencies</li>
<li>More irregular at high frequencies</li>
<li>More sensitive to rear sound at selected frequencies</li>
</ul>
<p>Manufacturer frequency-specific charts provide more detail than one simplified diagram.</p>
<h3>Polar pattern and proximity effect</h3>
<p>Directional pressure-gradient microphones commonly exhibit proximity effect: bass increases as the speaker moves closer.</p>
<p>Omnidirectional pressure microphones generally show much less proximity effect.</p>
<p>This can make cardioid microphones sound warm up close but muddy when placement is excessive.</p>
<h3>Choosing a pattern for YouTube</h3>
<h4>Solo podcast or livestream</h4>
<p>Cardioid is usually the simplest choice.</p>
<h4>Lavalier microphone</h4>
<p>Omnidirectional is common because the speaker may turn their head.</p>
<h4>Two-person interview</h4>
<p>Use two separate microphones when possible. A figure-eight microphone can work when both speakers face opposite sides of one mic.</p>
<h4>On-camera microphone</h4>
<p>A supercardioid or shotgun-style pattern helps prioritize sound in front, but close placement still matters.</p>
<h4>Group discussion</h4>
<p>Separate microphones generally give more control. One omnidirectional microphone can work when convenience matters more than isolation.</p>
<h3>Common mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Speaking into the wrong side</li>
<li>Assuming directional means long-range</li>
<li>Placing noise inside the microphone's pickup area</li>
<li>Ignoring a supercardioid rear lobe</li>
<li>Choosing omnidirectional in a loud untreated room</li>
<li>Expecting the pattern to eliminate echoes</li>
<li>Reading a two-dimensional chart without checking microphone orientation</li>
<li>Confusing polar pattern with frequency response</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>What is the best polar pattern for YouTube?</h4>
<p>Cardioid is a strong default for solo desk recording. Other patterns suit lavaliers, groups, interviews, and location audio.</p>
<h4>Does a cardioid microphone block all rear sound?</h4>
<p>No. It is least sensitive at the rear, but reflections and off-axis frequencies can still be recorded.</p>
<h4>Is a shotgun microphone a polar pattern?</h4>
<p>Shotgun microphones usually use an interference tube with a lobar or highly directional response built from an underlying directional capsule.</p>
<h4>Can one microphone have several polar patterns?</h4>
<p>Yes. Multi-pattern microphones allow the user to select patterns such as cardioid, omnidirectional, and figure-eight.</p>
