<h2>Scheduled Video</h2>
<p>A <strong>scheduled video</strong> is a YouTube upload set to become Public automatically at a specific date, time, and time zone chosen by the creator.</p>
<p>YouTube describes scheduled publishing as scheduling a Private video to go Public later. Before the release time, the upload is not an ordinary public video and is not eligible for normal public discovery.</p>

<h3>How to Schedule a YouTube Video</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Sign in to YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Create</strong>, then <strong>Upload videos</strong>.</li>
  <li>Select the file and complete the video details.</li>
  <li>Open the <strong>Schedule</strong> card.</li>
  <li>Choose the publication date.</li>
  <li>Choose the publication time and time zone.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Schedule</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>Creators can also schedule through supported YouTube app workflows. Review the displayed time zone carefully when traveling or managing an audience in another region.</p>

<h3>What Is the Video's Visibility Before Publication?</h3>
<p>The scheduled upload is kept Private before its release. It does not appear as an ordinary Public video on the channel, in search, recommendations, or subscriber feeds before the scheduled time.</p>
<p>Scheduling is therefore not the same as making a video Unlisted. An Unlisted link can be opened by anyone who receives it; a normal scheduled upload remains Private until publication.</p>

<h3>What Happens at the Scheduled Time?</h3>
<p>YouTube changes the video to Public. Once Public, it can:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Appear on the channel's video surfaces</li>
  <li>Appear in subscriber feeds</li>
  <li>Send eligible notifications</li>
  <li>Appear in search results</li>
  <li>Become eligible for recommendations and related-video lists</li>
  <li>Be shared and embedded under the video's settings</li>
  <li>Receive comments when comments are enabled</li>
</ul>
<p>Public eligibility does not guarantee that all of these distribution events will occur.</p>

<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Scheduled Video</th><th>Scheduled Premiere</th></tr></thead>

</table>

<h3>Can You Change the Scheduled Time?</h3>
<p>Yes. YouTube allows creators to edit the publication time or publish the video immediately.</p>
<ol>
  <li>Open the video in YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Open Visibility.</li>
  <li>Keep or return the video to Private while editing the schedule.</li>
  <li>Select the new date, time, and time zone.</li>
  <li>Save the updated schedule.</li>
</ol>
<p>To publish immediately, change the visibility to Public and publish.</p>

<h3>Why Schedule Instead of Publishing Manually?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Release consistently when the creator is unavailable.</li>
  <li>Coordinate a video with an announcement, launch, event, or newsletter.</li>
  <li>Complete upload processing and <a href="/glossary/youtube-checks/">YouTube Checks</a> before release.</li>
  <li>Give collaborators time to review final packaging.</li>
  <li>Schedule for an audience in another time zone.</li>
  <li>Maintain a content calendar without logging in at publication time.</li>
  <li>Prepare several uploads while controlling when each becomes Public.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Does Scheduling Improve the Algorithm?</h3>
<p>Scheduling itself is not a known recommendation boost. It is a release-management tool.</p>
<p>YouTube says publication timing may affect immediate views because more viewers can be active at certain hours, but publish time is not known to determine long-term performance. Topic, packaging, viewer response, satisfaction, and relevance matter more than the use of the Schedule button.</p>

<h3>Subscriber Notifications for Scheduled Videos</h3>
<p>When the video becomes Public, it can be published to the Subscriptions feed and send eligible subscriber notifications.</p>
<p>Creators can disable <strong>Publish to subscriptions feed and notify subscribers</strong> for an upload. YouTube also limits each viewer to three upload and live-stream notifications from one channel in a 24-hour period.</p>
<p>Scheduling does not guarantee that every subscriber receives or opens a notification.</p>

<h3>Scheduling and YouTube Checks</h3>
<p>A strong workflow is to upload well before release and wait for:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Standard- and high-definition processing</li>
  <li><a href="/glossary/copyright-checks/">Copyright Checks</a></li>
  <li>Ad-suitability review</li>
  <li>Captions and transcript processing</li>
  <li>Manual chapter and timestamp review</li>
  <li>Thumbnail and title approval</li>
  <li>End-screen and card checks</li>
</ul>
<p>A schedule does not guarantee that every review has finished before release. Upload early enough to inspect the status.</p>

<h3>What If the Channel Has a Community Guidelines Strike?</h3>
<p>YouTube says a scheduled video will not publish during a Community Guidelines penalty period. The video remains Private during the freeze, and the creator must reschedule it after the penalty ends.</p>
<p>Do not assume the missed scheduled time will automatically retry after the restriction is lifted.</p>

<h3>Why Does the Published Date Look Different?</h3>
<p>YouTube says the date shown on a video's watch page is based on Pacific Standard Time. Creators in time zones ahead of Pacific Time may see a different date on the public watch page than in YouTube Studio.</p>
<p>This can happen when a video is scheduled near midnight or when the creator expects a local calendar date to display publicly. Plan using the published-date behavior when the displayed date matters for an event or series.</p>

<h3>Scheduled Videos and External Promotion</h3>
<p>For an ordinary scheduled upload, confirm that the public URL and viewing experience work as intended before sending a large audience to it. A Premiere is often better when creators need a shareable pre-release watch page and reminder experience.</p>
<p>Coordinate:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Email campaigns</li>
  <li>Social posts</li>
  <li>Website embeds</li>
  <li>Community posts</li>
  <li>Sponsor deadlines</li>
  <li>Press or partner announcements</li>
</ul>
<p>Allow a buffer for processing, check delays, or last-minute corrections.</p>

<h3>Can You Schedule an Unlisted Video?</h3>
<p>YouTube's scheduled publishing feature is specifically described as making a Private video Public at a chosen time. It is not an automatic Private-to-Unlisted scheduler.</p>
<p>Creators who need link-only access at a certain time must manage that visibility change through another workflow rather than assuming Schedule will publish as Unlisted.</p>

<h3>Common Scheduled-Video Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Assuming a scheduled video is Unlisted before release.</li>
  <li>Selecting the wrong time zone.</li>
  <li>Scheduling before HD processing or checks can finish.</li>
  <li>Confusing a normal scheduled upload with a Premiere.</li>
  <li>Assuming scheduling gives an algorithm advantage.</li>
  <li>Expecting every subscriber to receive a notification.</li>
  <li>Forgetting that a strike can prevent publication.</li>
  <li>Relying on the creator's local date when the watch page uses Pacific Time.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Is a scheduled YouTube video Private?</h4>
<p>It remains Private until YouTube changes it to Public at the scheduled time.</p>

<h4>Can I publish a scheduled video early?</h4>
<p>Yes. Change the visibility to Public and publish it immediately.</p>

<h4>Can I reschedule the video?</h4>
<p>Yes. Edit the scheduled date, time, and time zone while the video remains Private.</p>

<h4>Is scheduling the same as a Premiere?</h4>
<p>No. A Premiere adds a shared launch watch page, countdown, reminders, and live-chat experience.</p>
