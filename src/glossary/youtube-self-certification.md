<h2>YouTube Self-Certification</h2>
<p><strong>YouTube Self-Certification</strong> is a monetization program that asks eligible creators to rate their own videos against YouTube's advertiser-friendly content guidelines before turning on ads.</p>
<p>The creator completes an Ad suitability questionnaire describing sensitive content in the video. YouTube then compares the self-rating with automated systems and, when applicable, human reviewers.</p>

<h3>Who Uses Self-Certification?</h3>
<p>Self-Certification is associated with channels in the YouTube Partner Program that have received access to the feature. YouTube says access commonly appears a month or two after joining YPP, although availability can vary.</p>
<p>Creators must self-rate:</p>
<ul>
  <li>New videos for which they turn on ads</li>
  <li>Previously uploaded videos when they newly turn on ads</li>
</ul>
<p>Creators do not need to go back and rate every older video that already has ads enabled.</p>

<h3>How Does Self-Certification Work?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Upload a video in YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Turn monetization on.</li>
  <li>Open the Ad suitability page.</li>
  <li>Answer the questionnaire based on everything in the video and packaging.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>None of the above</strong> only when none of the listed categories applies.</li>
  <li>Submit the rating.</li>
  <li>Allow YouTube's <a href="/glossary/youtube-checks/">Checks</a> process to review the upload.</li>
  <li>Choose the video's visibility and publish or schedule it.</li>
</ol>
<p>If YouTube's systems disagree with the rating and the video is eligible, the creator may be able to request human review.</p>

<h3>What Does the Questionnaire Cover?</h3>
<p>The questionnaire follows YouTube's advertiser-friendly content categories, which can include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Inappropriate language</li>
  <li>Adult content</li>
  <li>Violence</li>
  <li>Shocking content</li>
  <li>Harmful or dangerous acts</li>
  <li>Drug-related content</li>
  <li>Firearms-related content</li>
  <li>Controversial issues</li>
  <li>Sensitive events</li>
  <li>Dishonest behavior</li>
  <li>Content inappropriate for children and families</li>
  <li>Tobacco-related content</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator should consider the full video, audio, thumbnail, title, description, and tags rather than rating only the most obvious scene.</p>

<h3>What Is Rating Accuracy?</h3>
<p>YouTube tracks how closely a creator's self-ratings match decisions from its automated systems and human policy specialists.</p>
<p>The rating status page can show:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The video being rated</li>
  <li>The date of the rating</li>
  <li>The creator's predicted monetization result</li>
  <li>YouTube's resulting rating</li>
  <li>Whether the review was automated or human</li>
  <li>Available actions, such as requesting review or viewing feedback</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube says it can typically begin determining rating accuracy after a creator has rated about 20 videos.</p>

<h3>Why Does Accuracy Matter?</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Rating History</th><th>How YouTube May Use the Creator's Input</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>High accuracy</td><td>YouTube may rely more heavily on the creator's rating for the initial monetization decision</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Low or unknown accuracy</td><td>YouTube relies more on automated systems and review processes</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Repeated serious inaccuracies</td><td>The channel's YouTube Partner Program eligibility may be reviewed</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>Accurate ratings can reduce uncertainty and may lead to fewer unexpected limited-ad decisions over time. They do not guarantee that every video will receive full monetization.</p>

<h3>Can Accurate Creators Monetize Immediately?</h3>
<p>YouTube says creators with a high accuracy rating history may have their input used for the initial monetization decision, allowing publication and monetization after upload.</p>
<p>YouTube still checks the video, and a discrepancy can change the result. Most discrepancies are found soon after upload, but the status can change later as additional review occurs.</p>

<h3>What If Your Accuracy Is New or Low?</h3>
<p>New Self-Certification participants and creators with low or unknown accuracy may see the checking icon while YouTube's systems complete the monetization decision.</p>
<p>Ads do not run while the video remains in the checking state. YouTube recommends uploading private or unlisted before the planned release so the decision can finish.</p>

<h3>Creator Rating vs. YouTube Rating</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Rating</th><th>What It Represents</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Your rating</td><td>The monetization status YouTube predicts based on the questionnaire answers you submitted</td></tr>
    <tr><td>YouTube rating</td><td>The status YouTube's automated systems or human reviewers believe applies</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Computer review</td><td>An automated monetization assessment</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Human review</td><td>A policy specialist's assessment, generally treated as the final decision after appeal</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>How to Improve Self-Certification Accuracy</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Read the current advertiser-friendly guidelines and examples.</li>
  <li>Review guideline updates rather than relying on old creator advice.</li>
  <li>Watch the complete finished video before submitting the rating.</li>
  <li>Include opening language, background audio, on-screen text, and graphics.</li>
  <li>Review the title, thumbnail, description, and tags.</li>
  <li>Use YouTube's reviewer feedback to identify disagreements.</li>
  <li>Rate borderline content conservatively according to the written examples.</li>
  <li>Keep internal notes for recurring content formats.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Can You Request Feedback?</h3>
<p>The rating status page may let creators request a review or view feedback after a policy specialist has assessed the video. Feedback can identify the broad policy area where the creator and YouTube disagreed.</p>
<p>For some limited- or no-ad appeals submitted through desktop Self-Certification, YouTube may provide policy timestamps. These timestamps clarify where the reviewer found an issue; they are not editing instructions.</p>

<h3>Can You Edit the Video and Request Another Review?</h3>
<p>YouTube states that videos, thumbnails, and titles edited in YouTube Studio to remove advertiser-policy content are not eligible for re-review through that flow.</p>
<p>This makes it important to upload carefully and understand the appeal process before changing the published video. Editing may also be irreversible through Studio's old Revert to original feature.</p>

<h3>Self-Certification for Made for Kids Content</h3>
<p>YouTube says Self-Certification can apply to content marked Made for Kids. Creators should also review YouTube's specific advertiser-friendly guidance for kids and family content.</p>
<p>Made for Kids status affects advertising behavior and other platform features, but it does not remove the need for an accurate self-rating when monetization is enabled.</p>

<h3>Does Self-Certification Guarantee Ads?</h3>
<p>No. Turning on ads and submitting a self-rating does not guarantee that ads will run. YouTube still reviews the video, and advertiser demand, copyright, invalid traffic, policy restrictions, and other monetization factors can affect earnings.</p>
<p>A creator cannot make non-advertiser-friendly content eligible merely by selecting None of the above.</p>

<h3>What Happens After an Inaccurate Rating?</h3>
<p>An occasional good-faith mistake can become part of the creator's accuracy history and reviewer feedback. Repeated, egregious inaccuracies can lead YouTube to review the channel's YPP eligibility.</p>
<p>The correct goal is not to guess the greenest answer. It is to accurately describe the content under YouTube's current guidelines.</p>

<h3>Common Self-Certification Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Selecting None of the above without reviewing the complete video.</li>
  <li>Rating only the footage and ignoring the title or thumbnail.</li>
  <li>Using Community Guidelines as the advertiser-friendly standard.</li>
  <li>Assuming one accurate rating guarantees all future videos are green.</li>
  <li>Appealing every disagreement without studying reviewer feedback.</li>
  <li>Following outdated examples after YouTube updates a guideline.</li>
  <li>Believing a high accuracy score prevents all automated checks.</li>
  <li>Editing a video before understanding whether re-review remains available.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>How many videos does YouTube need to determine accuracy?</h4>
<p>YouTube says it can typically determine rating accuracy after about 20 rated videos.</p>

<h4>Can Self-Certification give a video full monetization automatically?</h4>
<p>For creators with a strong accuracy history, YouTube may rely more on the submitted rating initially, but later review can still change the result.</p>

<h4>Can a wrong rating remove a channel from YPP?</h4>
<p>YouTube says repeated, egregious inaccuracies may cause the channel's YPP eligibility to be reviewed.</p>

<h4>Is Self-Certification available immediately after joining YPP?</h4>
<p>YouTube says access typically appears a month or two after joining, though availability can vary.</p>
