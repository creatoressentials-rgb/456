<h2>Affiliate Commission</h2>
<p>An <strong>affiliate commission</strong> is performance-based compensation paid to a creator, publisher, or other affiliate for generating a tracked and approved action for a brand.</p>
<p>The action may be:</p>
<ul>
<li>A completed sale</li>
<li>Qualified lead</li>
<li>App installation</li>
<li>Subscription</li>
<li>Free-trial registration</li>
<li>Phone call</li>
<li>New customer</li>
<li>Product tag purchase</li>
<li>Another program-defined conversion</li>
</ul>
<p>Commission is earned according to the <a href="/glossary/affiliate-marketing">affiliate program</a>'s <a href="/glossary/attribution/">attribution</a> and validation rules—not simply because someone clicked an <a href="/glossary/affiliate-link">affiliate link</a>.</p>
<h3>Common affiliate commission structures</h3>
<h4>Percentage of sale</h4>
<p>The affiliate receives a percentage of commissionable revenue.</p>
<p>Example: 8% of a $100 qualifying sale produces an $8 commission before later adjustments.</p>
<p>This is a form of <a href="/glossary/revenue-share">revenue share</a>.</p>
<h4>Fixed amount per sale</h4>
<p>The affiliate receives a fixed payment for each approved purchase regardless of order value.</p>
<h4>Cost per lead</h4>
<p>The affiliate receives a payment for a qualified form submission, quote request, application, or other lead.</p>
<p>The program should define what makes the lead valid.</p>
<h4>Cost per install</h4>
<p>The affiliate receives a payment after a user installs an app or software product. Some programs require additional activity before approval.</p>
<h4>Subscription bounty</h4>
<p>The affiliate receives a fixed payment for a qualifying membership, free trial, or subscription registration.</p>
<h4>Recurring commission</h4>
<p>The creator receives commission on qualifying renewal payments for a defined period or for as long as the referred customer remains eligible.</p>
<h4>Tiered commission</h4>
<p>The rate increases after the affiliate reaches a sales, revenue, or conversion threshold.</p>
<h4>New-customer commission</h4>
<p>The program pays a higher rate for customers who have not previously purchased or subscribed.</p>
<h4>Custom commission</h4>
<p>A brand offers an individual creator a different rate than the standard public program.</p>
<p><a href="/glossary/youtube-shopping/">YouTube Shopping</a> currently allows participating merchants to offer eligible creators custom commission rates for specified campaigns or products.</p>

<p>A sponsorship can include both a guaranteed fee and affiliate commission.</p>
<h3>Commission rate vs. actual earnings</h3>
<p>A 10% commission rate does not mean the creator receives 10% of every customer payment.</p>
<p>The program may exclude:</p>
<ul>
<li>Sales tax</li>
<li>Shipping</li>
<li>Gift wrapping</li>
<li>Discounts</li>
<li>Gift cards</li>
<li>Certain product categories</li>
<li>Marketplace sellers</li>
<li>Subscription renewals</li>
<li>Existing customers</li>
<li>Canceled orders</li>
<li>Returned products</li>
<li>Fraudulent transactions</li>
<li>Purchases outside the tracking window</li>
<li>Self-purchases</li>
<li>Purchases that violate program terms</li>
</ul>
<p>The rate must be applied to the program's definition of <strong>qualifying</strong> or <strong>commissionable</strong> revenue.</p>
<h3>Pending, approved, and reversed commissions</h3>
<h4>Pending</h4>
<p>The system has recorded a conversion, but the amount remains subject to review, returns, cancellations, fraud checks, or another validation period.</p>
<h4>Approved or locked</h4>
<p>The program has validated the action under its rules. The amount may still wait for the scheduled payment cycle or minimum threshold.</p>
<h4>Reversed or declined</h4>
<p>The commission is removed because the order was returned, canceled, fraudulent, duplicated, ineligible, or otherwise disqualified.</p>
<h4>Paid</h4>
<p>The program has issued the commission through its payment system.</p>
<p>YouTube explains that approved Shopping affiliate commissions are calculated after returns are deducted from pending commissions. It says return windows are commonly 30 to 90 days, which is why affiliate earnings may remain pending.</p>
<h3>Attribution determines who receives commission</h3>
<p>Programs may use:</p>
<ul>
<li>Last click</li>
<li>First click</li>
<li>Coupon code</li>
<li>Product tag</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/view-through-attribution/">View-through attribution</a></li>
<li>Multi-touch or position-based credit</li>
<li>New-customer priority</li>
<li>Preferred publisher rules</li>
<li>Cross-device matching</li>
<li>Server-to-server tracking</li>
</ul>
<p>A customer can see one creator's content, click another affiliate's link, and purchase through a coupon extension. The affiliate who receives credit depends on the program's attribution rules.</p>
<p>Awin, for example, offers coupon attribution that can credit the publisher assigned an exclusive code even when no affiliate cookie is present.</p>
<h3>Affiliate commission and cookies</h3>
<p>An affiliate click can place tracking data in the viewer's browser. The <strong>cookie window</strong> is the period during which the referral may remain eligible.</p>
<p>Tracking can fail or change when:</p>
<ul>
<li>The viewer deletes or blocks cookies</li>
<li>The purchase occurs on another device</li>
<li>The viewer changes browsers</li>
<li>Another affiliate receives the last qualifying click</li>
<li>The user moves from an app to a website</li>
<li>Consent settings block tracking</li>
<li>The advertiser's tracking implementation breaks</li>
<li>The order happens after the attribution window</li>
</ul>
<p>Modern programs may supplement cookies with server-side tracking, account matching, product tags, or coupon attribution.</p>

<h3>Affiliate commission disclosure</h3>
<p>An affiliate commission is a financial relationship that may affect how viewers evaluate a recommendation.</p>
<p>The FTC says a disclosure such as <strong>“Paid link”</strong> placed next to an <a href="/glossary/affiliate-link/">affiliate link</a> can adequately communicate the nature of the link. The FTC has also cautioned that <strong>“commissionable link”</strong> may not be clear to ordinary consumers.</p>
<p>Use direct language such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>“I earn a commission from purchases made through these links.”</li>
<li>“This is an affiliate link, which means I may earn a commission.”</li>
<li>“As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.”</li>
</ul>
<p>Program-specific disclosure requirements can apply in addition to general legal standards.</p>
<h3>Affiliate-commission red flags</h3>
<p>Review programs that:</p>
<ul>
<li>Change rates without meaningful notice</li>
<li>Have unclear reversal rules</li>
<li>Provide no transaction reporting</li>
<li>Use an undefined attribution window</li>
<li>Hold pending commissions indefinitely</li>
<li>Permit unlimited retroactive adjustments</li>
<li>Exclude broad product groups without clear documentation</li>
<li>Require misleading claims</li>
<li>Forbid required disclosure</li>
<li>Allow the brand to use creator content without separate <a href="/glossary/usage-rights">usage rights</a></li>
<li>Have a payment threshold the creator is unlikely to reach</li>
<li>Erase approved commission after termination without cause</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Does every affiliate-link click earn a commission?</h4>
<p>No. The viewer normally must complete a qualifying action within the attribution rules, and the transaction must survive validation, returns, and other program conditions.</p>
<h4>Why was an affiliate commission reversed?</h4>
<p>Common reasons include returns, cancellations, duplicate orders, fraud, ineligible products, self-referrals, or failure to satisfy program rules.</p>
<h4>Can affiliate commission rates change?</h4>
<p>Yes. Programs may change standard rates or offer temporary and creator-specific rates. Review the agreement and monitor current terms.</p>
<h4>Is affiliate commission guaranteed income?</h4>
<p>No. Earnings depend on traffic, conversion, attribution, program rules, product availability, returns, and the brand's continued program participation.</p>
