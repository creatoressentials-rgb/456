<h2>Focal Length</h2>
<p><strong>Focal length</strong> is an optical measurement that describes how wide or narrow a camera lens sees.</p>
<p>It is usually shown in millimeters, such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>16mm</li>
<li>24mm</li>
<li>35mm</li>
<li>50mm</li>
<li>85mm</li>
<li>24–70mm</li>
</ul>
<p>A shorter focal length generally captures more of the scene. A longer focal length captures a narrower portion and makes distant subjects appear larger in the frame.</p>
<p>Focal length is not the physical length of the lens.</p>
<h3>What focal length controls</h3>
<p>Focal length strongly affects:</p>
<ul>
<li>Angle of view</li>
<li>Framing</li>
<li>Magnification</li>
<li>Working distance</li>
<li>Background appearance</li>
<li>Camera shake visibility</li>
</ul>
<p>It does not independently determine image quality or perspective.</p>

<h3>Common focal-length categories</h3>
<p>The exact boundaries vary, especially across sensor sizes.</p>
<h4>Ultra-wide</h4>
<p>Often used for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Action cameras</li>
<li>Very small rooms</li>
<li>Architecture</li>
<li>Dramatic environmental shots</li>
</ul>
<p>It can distort or exaggerate nearby features.</p>
<h4>Wide angle</h4>
<p>Often used for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Vlogging</li>
<li>Small studios</li>
<li>Real estate</li>
<li>Group shots</li>
<li>Showing the environment</li>
</ul>
<h4>Standard or normal</h4>
<p>Often used for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Talking heads</li>
<li>Interviews</li>
<li>General-purpose video</li>
<li>Product demonstrations</li>
</ul>
<h4>Telephoto</h4>
<p>Often used for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Sports</li>
<li>Events</li>
<li>Wildlife</li>
<li>Stage recording</li>
<li>Tight framing from a distance</li>
</ul>
<h3>Focal length and angle of view</h3>
<p>Angle of view is how much of the scene fits inside the frame.</p>
<p>From the same camera position:</p>
<ul>
<li>16mm captures a wide scene.</li>
<li>35mm captures a moderate scene.</li>
<li>85mm captures a narrow scene.</li>
<li>200mm captures a small distant area.</li>
</ul>
<p>Nikon describes focal length as controlling both angle of view and magnification.</p>
<h3>Focal length and sensor size</h3>
<p>The same focal length can look different on cameras with different sensor sizes.</p>
<p>For example, a 24mm lens captures:</p>
<ul>
<li>A wider field of view on a full-frame camera</li>
<li>A narrower field of view on an APS-C camera</li>
</ul>
<p>This is why camera guides sometimes use <strong>35mm-equivalent focal length</strong>.</p>
<p>The lens remains a 24mm lens. The smaller sensor records a smaller portion of the lens's projected image.</p>
<h3>Full-frame equivalent focal length</h3>
<p>Equivalent focal length is a comparison tool.</p>
<p>A common APS-C crop factor is approximately:</p>
<ul>
<li>1.5× for many Sony, Nikon, and Fujifilm cameras</li>
<li>1.6× for many Canon APS-C cameras</li>
</ul>
<p>A 24mm lens on a 1.5× APS-C camera gives a field of view similar to approximately 36mm on full frame.</p>
<p>The exact crop factor depends on the camera.</p>
<h3>Focal length and perspective</h3>
<p>Focal length does not directly change perspective when the camera remains in the same position.</p>
<p>Camera position changes perspective.</p>
<p>Consider a talking-head shot:</p>
<ol>
<li>A wide lens is placed very close to the creator.</li>
<li>The face appears exaggerated because of the close camera position.</li>
<li>The camera moves farther back.</li>
<li>A longer lens is used to restore the original framing.</li>
<li>Facial proportions and background size look different.</li>
</ol>
<p>The camera movement caused the perspective change. The focal length changed the framing.</p>
<h3>Focal length and working distance</h3>
<p><strong>Working distance</strong> is how far the camera must be from the subject to achieve a desired shot.</p>
<p>For the same framing:</p>
<ul>
<li>A wide lens usually requires the camera to be closer.</li>
<li>A long lens usually requires the camera to be farther away.</li>
</ul>
<p>This matters in a small YouTube studio. A lens may produce a pleasing image but require more room than the creator has.</p>
<h3>Focal length and background blur</h3>
<p>Longer focal lengths can make background blur easier to notice when:</p>
<ul>
<li>The subject is framed similarly</li>
<li>The camera moves farther away</li>
<li>The background remains distant</li>
<li>Aperture and sensor size are considered</li>
</ul>
<p>Background blur is not determined by focal length alone.</p>
<p>It also depends on:</p>
<ul>
<li><a href="/glossary/aperture/">Aperture</a></li>
<li>Sensor size</li>
<li>Subject distance</li>
<li>Background distance</li>
<li>Focus distance</li>
</ul>
<h3>Focal length and camera shake</h3>
<p>Longer focal lengths magnify small movements.</p>
<p>A telephoto lens may need:</p>
<ul>
<li>Faster shutter speed</li>
<li>Tripod</li>
<li>Optical stabilization</li>
<li>In-body stabilization</li>
<li>Careful handheld technique</li>
</ul>
<p>A wide lens often makes handheld movement less obvious, although it can still show rolling or edge movement.</p>

<p>Room size, sensor size, camera position, and desired style can change the best choice.</p>
<h3>Reading a lens name</h3>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/prime-lens/">prime lens</a> has one focal length:</p>
<ul>
<li>24mm</li>
<li>35mm</li>
<li>50mm</li>
</ul>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/zoom-lens/">zoom lens</a> shows a range:</p>
<ul>
<li>16–50mm</li>
<li>18–55mm</li>
<li>24–70mm</li>
</ul>
<p>The first number is the widest setting. The second is the longest setting.</p>
<h3>Focal length mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Comparing millimeters without considering sensor size</li>
<li>Calling focal length the physical lens length</li>
<li>Assuming a longer lens automatically creates better background blur</li>
<li>Ignoring how much room the camera needs</li>
<li>Blaming focal length for perspective caused by camera position</li>
<li>Choosing a vlogging lens too narrow for arm's-length use</li>
<li>Assuming digital zoom changes the optical focal length</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>What focal length is best for YouTube?</h4>
<p>It depends on sensor size, room size, camera distance, and content style. Small-room creators usually need a wider lens than creators filming from across a studio.</p>
<h4>Is 50mm good for talking-head videos?</h4>
<p>It can be excellent when the camera can be placed far enough away. On an APS-C camera, 50mm may be too tight for a small room.</p>
<h4>Does a longer focal length blur the background more?</h4>
<p>It can make blur appear stronger under comparable framing, but aperture, sensor size, subject distance, and background distance also matter.</p>
<h4>Does focal length change perspective?</h4>
<p>Camera position primarily controls perspective. Changing focal length changes framing from a fixed position.</p>
