<h2>Copyright Strike</h2>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> A copyright strike is a formal, manual takedown notice — legally equivalent to a DMCA request — filed by a rights holder and processed by YouTube against a specific video. Unlike a <a href="/glossary/copyright-claim/">Copyright Claim</a>, a strike is a real penalty against your <strong>channel</strong>, not just a video. Get three strikes within a 90-day window and YouTube terminates the channel outright.</p>
<p>If you're staring at a strike notice right now: don't panic, but don't ignore it either. Here's what actually happens and what your options are.</p>

<h3>How a strike happens</h3>
<p>A strike is not something <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a> does automatically. Content ID scans uploads and flags matches against a database of registered content — that produces a Copyright Claim, which is automated, applies only to the video in question, and mainly affects who gets the ad money.</p>
<p>A strike happens when a rights holder decides the automated system isn't enough and files a <strong>copyright removal request</strong> directly with YouTube — the same legal mechanism behind DMCA takedowns off-platform. YouTube reviews the request, and if it appears valid, the video comes down and the strike lands on your channel. This is a person or company making a legal claim, not an algorithm making a match.</p>
<p>One indirect path into a strike: disputing a Content ID claim without a real basis. If you dispute and the rights holder disagrees, they can escalate to a formal removal request — and that can turn an automated claim into a manual strike.</p>

<h3>What happens after one strike</h3>
<ul>
<li>The video is removed from YouTube.</li>
<li>The strike stays active on your channel. It only starts its 90-day countdown once you complete <strong>Copyright School</strong> — a short video plus a 4-question quiz. Skip it, and the strike doesn't expire.</li>
<li>Your channel is generally treated as ineligible for monetization and the Partner Program while a strike is active, cutting off <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">ad revenue</a> on the channel until it's resolved.</li>
<li>If the removed content was an active live stream, live-streaming access is specifically restricted for 7 days (14 days if it happens a second time).</li>
</ul>
<p>A note on accuracy: you'll see claims online that a first copyright strike triggers a blanket one-week freeze on <em>all</em> uploads. That specific restriction belongs to YouTube's separate Community Guidelines strike system, not the copyright strike system — don't confuse the two policies when assessing your risk.</p>

<h3>What happens at three strikes in 90 days</h3>
<p>Three active copyright strikes inside a rolling 90-day window means:</p>
<ul>
<li>Your channel is subject to termination, along with any channels linked to your account.</li>
<li>All uploaded videos become inaccessible.</li>
<li>You're barred from creating a new channel.</li>
<li>The reverse also applies: if a channel linked to yours racks up 3 strikes, your channel is subject to termination too.</li>
</ul>
<p>There's no ambiguity in this one — it's YouTube's stated policy, not a scare tactic.</p>

<h3>How to get a strike resolved</h3>
<p>There are three official paths:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Wait it out.</strong> Complete Copyright School, then wait 90 days from when the strike was issued. No contact with the claimant required — just a clean 90 days with fewer than 3 active strikes.</li>
<li><strong>Request a retraction.</strong> Contact the person or company that filed the claim and ask them to withdraw it. If they agree, YouTube removes the strike immediately — no 90-day wait. This is usually the fastest resolution when you're in the wrong or the claimant is willing to work with you.</li>
<li><strong>Submit a counter notification.</strong> This is the option to know the most about before clicking anything.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>On counter notifications specifically:</strong> this is not an in-app appeal button. It is a formal legal document. You're required to provide your full legal name and physical address (which YouTube is legally obligated to share with the claimant), and to swear "under penalty of perjury" that the content was removed by mistake or misidentification. Once submitted, the claimant has 10 business days to respond with evidence that they've taken legal action — such as filing suit — to keep the content down. If they don't, the video is reinstated. If they do, you're now party to an actual legal dispute.</p>
<p>This entry isn't legal advice, and whether a counter notification is the right move depends on facts about your specific video (licensing, fair use, permissions) that only you and, ideally, a qualified attorney can assess. Understand the weight of what you're signing before you submit one.</p>
<p>Deleting the video does not clear a strike, except in the rare case where YouTube's notice specifically offers a 7-day "scheduled removal" window before the strike takes effect.</p>

<h3>Copyright Strike vs. Copyright Claim</h3>
<p>These get used interchangeably by creators constantly, and that habit causes real damage — treating a strike like a minor claim (or panicking over a claim like it's a strike) leads to bad decisions. The distinction that matters:</p>
<table>
<thead><tr><th></th><th>Copyright Claim</th><th>Copyright Strike</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>Triggered by</strong></td><td>Automated Content ID match</td><td>Manual legal takedown request from the rights holder</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Applies to</strong></td><td>The specific video</td><td>The entire channel</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Immediate effect</strong></td><td>Video usually stays up; ad revenue may reroute to the claimant, or the video may be blocked in some regions</td><td>Video is removed</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Channel risk</strong></td><td>None directly</td><td>Real — 3 strikes in 90 days terminates the channel</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Resolution</strong></td><td>Wait it out, edit the video, or dispute the claim</td><td>Wait 90 days + Copyright School, get a retraction, or file a legal counter notification</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Does a Content ID claim turn into a copyright strike?</h4>
<p>Not typically — they're separate systems. But disputing a claim without a valid reason can prompt the rights holder to escalate to a formal removal request, which can result in a strike.</p>
<h4>Can I just delete the video to make the strike go away?</h4>
<p>No. Once a strike is issued, deleting the video doesn't clear it from your channel. The only exception is if YouTube's notice specifically gave you a 7-day window to delete the content before the strike took effect.</p>
<h4>How long does a copyright strike last?</h4>
<p>90 days from the date it was issued — but only once you've completed Copyright School. Without that, the strike stays active indefinitely.</p>
