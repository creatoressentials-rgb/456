<h2>Private Video</h2>
<p>A <strong>Private video</strong> is a YouTube upload that can be watched only by the creator and the specific viewers the creator chooses to invite.</p>
<p>Private videos do not appear on the channel's Videos tab, in YouTube search, recommendations, related-video lists, or subscriber feeds. Comments are not available.</p>

<h3>Who Can Watch a Private Video?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The video owner</li>
  <li>Authorized channel users with appropriate access</li>
  <li>Specific viewers invited through YouTube's Share privately workflow</li>
</ul>
<p>Invited viewers must sign in with the Google or YouTube account associated with the email address the creator approved. Sending the ordinary video URL to someone else does not grant access.</p>

<h3>How to Share a Private Video</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Sign in to YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Content</strong>.</li>
  <li>Choose the Private video.</li>
  <li>Open the <strong>Visibility</strong> box.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Share privately</strong>.</li>
  <li>Enter the viewer's email address.</li>
  <li>Save the invitation.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube's older-unlisted security documentation states that Private videos can be shared with up to 50 email addresses. The invited viewers need compatible Google or YouTube accounts.</p>

<h3>Can Private Videos Have Comments?</h3>
<p>No. YouTube says comments are not available on Private videos. Comments that existed while the video was Public or Unlisted are not necessarily deleted, but they are unavailable while the video remains Private.</p>
<p>If feedback is required, use an Unlisted video, a separate review platform, email, a shared document, or another commenting system.</p>

<h3>Can Private Videos Be Embedded?</h3>
<p>A Private video cannot be used as an ordinary unrestricted website embed. Visitors who are not specifically approved and signed in cannot watch it merely because the player appears on a webpage.</p>
<p>Changing an embedded Unlisted or Public video to Private can break playback on websites, apps, learning systems, and client portals.</p>

<h3>Can YouTube Review Private Videos?</h3>
<p>Yes. YouTube explicitly says its systems and human reviewers may review Private videos for:</p>
<ul>
  <li><a href="/glossary/ad-suitability-checks/">Ad suitability</a></li>
  <li><a href="/glossary/copyright-checks/">Copyright</a></li>
  <li>Abuse prevention</li>
  <li>Community Guidelines enforcement</li>
  <li>Safety and platform integrity</li>
</ul>
<p>Private means restricted viewer access. It does not mean the file is exempt from automated processing, moderation, legal requests, or YouTube's terms.</p>

<h3>Useful Reasons to Choose Private</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Store a draft before release.</li>
  <li>Allow a small group of approved collaborators to review a video.</li>
  <li>Upload early for processing and <a href="/glossary/youtube-checks/">YouTube Checks</a>.</li>
  <li>Temporarily remove a video from public access without deleting it.</li>
  <li>Prepare a scheduled release.</li>
  <li>Preserve an older upload and its Analytics while limiting access.</li>
  <li>Share a family video with a small named group when YouTube's access model is sufficient.</li>
</ul>

<h3>When Private Is Not Secure Enough</h3>
<p>Private YouTube video access is stronger than Unlisted, but it is not a substitute for purpose-built secure storage or regulated data systems.</p>
<p>Avoid uploading material that YouTube should not possess or process, including:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Passwords and authentication secrets</li>
  <li>Unredacted tax and financial documents</li>
  <li>Protected health information</li>
  <li>Privileged legal material</li>
  <li>Trade secrets requiring contractual data controls</li>
  <li>Government identifiers</li>
  <li>Content prohibited by YouTube policies</li>
</ul>

<h3>Does Private Preserve Views and Comments?</h3>
<p>Changing an existing video to Private normally preserves the upload's underlying URL, view count, comments, and Analytics history. Public viewers lose access, and comments become unavailable while it remains Private.</p>
<p>Making it Public or Unlisted again can restore eligible access without creating a new upload.</p>

<h3>Private Videos and Scheduling</h3>
<p>YouTube's scheduled publishing system keeps a video Private until the chosen publication time. This lets creators upload early, complete processing and checks, and then automatically release the same upload as Public.</p>
<p>A manually Private video will not become Public unless the creator publishes or schedules it.</p>

<h3>Private Videos and Subscriber Notifications</h3>
<p>Private videos do not appear in subscriber feeds and do not send ordinary upload notifications. When a Private video is later made Public for the first time, publication can use normal feed and notification behavior, subject to the creator's notification setting and YouTube's limits.</p>

<h3>Why Can't an Invited Viewer Watch?</h3>
<p>Common reasons include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The viewer is signed in to a different Google Account.</li>
  <li>The creator invited the wrong email address.</li>
  <li>The invitation was not saved.</li>
  <li>The video was removed or restricted.</li>
  <li>The viewer's managed account or organization blocks YouTube access.</li>
  <li>Age, country, or other policy restrictions apply.</li>
  <li>The creator reached the private-sharing limit.</li>
</ul>
<p>Confirm the exact approved account instead of repeatedly resending the ordinary link.</p>

<h3>Can Invited Viewers Download or Record It?</h3>
<p>Private visibility does not guarantee that an authorized viewer cannot capture the screen, record the audio, photograph the display, or otherwise reproduce the content.</p>
<p>Use contracts, watermarking, secure review platforms, and minimum necessary sharing when leak prevention matters. No consumer video-sharing permission completely controls what an authorized viewer does after playback reaches their device.</p>

<h3>Common Private-Video Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Sending the link without inviting the viewer's exact account.</li>
  <li>Expecting comments.</li>
  <li>Embedding the video and assuming approved viewers will have a smooth login experience.</li>
  <li>Treating Private as end-to-end encrypted confidential storage.</li>
  <li>Assuming YouTube cannot review the file.</li>
  <li>Uploading regulated or highly sensitive records.</li>
  <li>Confusing a manually Private video with a scheduled release.</li>
  <li>Deleting a video when changing it to Private would preserve the upload history.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Can anyone with a Private video link watch?</h4>
<p>No. The viewer must be invited and signed in to the approved account.</p>

<h4>How many people can a Private video be shared with?</h4>
<p>YouTube's Help documentation states that Private videos can be shared with up to 50 email addresses.</p>

<h4>Can Private videos have comments?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube does not make comments available on Private videos.</p>

<h4>Can YouTube scan a Private video?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube says its systems and reviewers may review Private videos for copyright, ad suitability, and abuse prevention.</p>
