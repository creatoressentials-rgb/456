<h2>Made for Kids</h2>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> "Made for Kids" (MFK) is the audience label YouTube requires every channel and video to carry, in order to comply with the U.S. Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA). If your content is set to MFK, YouTube limits the data it collects on viewers and turns off features that rely on that data — personalized ads, comments, notifications, Channel Memberships, and more. It's not optional, and it's not just for kids' channels: every creator has to make the call, even if the answer is "no."</p>

<h3>The legal background, briefly</h3>
<p>In September 2019, Google and YouTube paid a record $170 million ($136M to the FTC, $34M to New York) to settle allegations that YouTube collected data from viewers of child-directed channels without parental consent — a violation of COPPA. That settlement is why the MFK setting exists: as part of the deal, YouTube built a system for creators to flag child-directed content so it can restrict data collection accordingly.</p>
<p>Since then, every channel owner has been legally required to designate their content — regardless of where they're located. This isn't a YouTube house rule; it's tied to a federal law, and misclassifying content carries real regulatory risk on top of any platform-level consequences (more on that below). For general monetization eligibility and policy strikes beyond MFK specifically, see YouTube Monetization &amp; Policies.</p>

<h3>How to tell if your content counts as "made for kids"</h3>
<p>YouTube doesn't get to define this — the FTC does, under COPPA. According to the FTC's published guidance, a video is "child-directed" if children are the primary audience, <em>or</em> if children aren't the primary audience but the video still targets them (what COPPA calls "mixed audience" content — this isn't a separate setting, it still gets labeled MFK).</p>
<p>The FTC's stated factors to weigh:</p>
<ul>
<li>Subject matter of the video (e.g., preschool-level educational content)</li>
<li>Whether children are your intended audience — including what your metadata suggests — or the actual audience</li>
<li>Whether the video includes child actors or models</li>
<li>Whether it features characters, celebrities, or toys that appeal to children, including animated or cartoon figures</li>
<li>Whether the language is intended for children to understand</li>
<li>Whether it includes activities that appeal to children (play-acting, simple songs or games, early education)</li>
<li>Whether it includes songs, stories, or poems for children</li>
<li>Any other evidence of your actual audience (survey data, for example)</li>
<li>Whether the content is advertised to children</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>What doesn't automatically make content MFK:</strong> being family-friendly, covering a topic "traditionally associated with kids," or the fact that kids might stumble onto it. YouTube's own examples of general audience (not MFK) content include DIY/hobbyist videos, family vlogs, mod/avatar tutorials, and gaming videos with adult humor. A disclaimer alone ("this is for 13+ audiences") doesn't settle it either — the FTC weighs it against the other factors.</p>
<p>This determination is genuinely fact-specific, and getting it wrong has consequences under federal law. We're laying out YouTube's and the FTC's own published factors here, not offering a compliance verdict — if you're unsure where your content lands, especially in a "mixed audience" gray zone, talk to a lawyer or consult YouTube's own guidance directly.</p>

<h3>How to set it: channel default vs. per-video</h3>
<p>You can handle this two ways in <a href="/glossary/youtube-studio/">YouTube Studio</a> (web only — the mobile app doesn't support the channel-level setting):</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Channel-level default.</strong> Settings → Channel → Advanced Settings → Audience. Choose "made for kids," "not made for kids," or "review for every video." This applies to all existing and future videos, but...</li>
<li><strong>Per-video override.</strong> Every upload has an Audience field on the Basic Info tab. A per-video setting overrides the channel default — so if your channel default is "not made for kids" but one video is squarely kid-directed, you set that video individually.</li>
</ol>
<p>Live streams (including archived ones) have their own audience setting in the <a href="/glossary/live-control-room/">Live Control Room</a>, with the same logic.</p>
<p>If you never touch this setting, YouTube's machine-learning systems may set it for you based on what looks "clearly directed to young audiences" — but YouTube is explicit that you shouldn't rely on this. Its systems aren't built to make the legal determination for you.</p>

<h3>What gets disabled when content is MFK</h3>
<p>On a <strong>video or live stream</strong> set to MFK, these are turned off:</p>
<ul>
<li>Personalized advertising (contextual ads only)</li>
<li>Comments</li>
<li>Notification bell</li>
<li>Cards and <a href="/glossary/end-screens/">end screens</a></li>
<li>Video watermarks</li>
<li>Channel Memberships</li>
<li>Donate button, live chat, and live chat donations</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/super-chat/">Super Chat</a> and <a href="/glossary/super-stickers/">Super Stickers</a></li>
<li>Merchandise shelf and ticketing</li>
<li>Save to playlist / Save to Watch Later</li>
<li>Miniplayer playback</li>
<li>Autoplay on home</li>
<li>Likes/dislikes on YouTube Music</li>
</ul>
<p>If you set your <strong>entire channel</strong> to MFK, you additionally lose Channel Memberships, the notification bell, and Community posts at the channel level (on top of everything above applying to every video).</p>
<p>Practically: this wipes out most of the tools that drive repeat engagement and fan-funding revenue. See <a href="/glossary/channel-memberships/">Channel Memberships</a> and <a href="/glossary/fan-funding/">Fan Funding</a> for how those normally work — both are unavailable on MFK content, full stop.</p>

<h3>The honest revenue impact</h3>
<p>Here's the part most articles either skip or oversell: <strong>MFK doesn't zero out your ad revenue — it removes personalized targeting, which pulls your <a href="/glossary/cost-per-mille-cpm/">CPM</a> down, sometimes a lot, but "sometimes a lot" is doing real work in that sentence.</strong></p>
<p>Without personal data to target against, YouTube can only sell contextual ads (matched to the video's content, not the viewer). Contextual ads generally pay less than personalized ones, but the gap varies wildly by <a href="/glossary/niche/">niche</a>, advertiser demand, region, and season — there's no single, YouTube-published "MFK tax" percentage. Creator-reported figures range from under $1 to around $7 <a href="/glossary/youtube-cpm/">CPM</a> depending on the niche, with educational and toy-review content typically outperforming generic entertainment, and Q4 seeing the usual seasonal bump. Treat any single number you see quoted — including ours — as a rough range, not a guarantee.</p>
<p>This connects to two other terms worth understanding if you're modeling revenue: <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">Ad Revenue</a> (the underlying mechanic) and <a href="/glossary/youtube-rpm/">YouTube RPM</a> and <a href="/glossary/playback-based-cpm/">Playback-Based CPM</a> (how the rate you actually see is calculated). MFK affects the ad-buy side of that equation, not views or watch time directly.</p>
<p>What MFK does <em>not</em> touch: brand sponsorships, affiliate links, off-platform merch stores, or licensing deals — none of those run through YouTube's ad system, so COPPA's targeting restrictions don't apply to them. For a lot of successful kids' channels, these off-platform streams end up mattering more than AdSense.</p>

<h3>The stakes for getting it wrong</h3>
<p>Failing to label child-directed content as MFK isn't just a YouTube policy issue — it's a COPPA compliance issue with the FTC directly. Civil penalties under COPPA are assessed per violation and are periodically adjusted for inflation, so don't anchor on a specific number without checking the FTC's current schedule. On the YouTube side, if the platform's systems detect content that should have been labeled MFK, they can override your setting; once that happens as an enforcement action (rather than your own initial default), you may lose the ability to change it yourself and would need to appeal.</p>
<p><strong>This is not a call to make casually.</strong> If your content sits anywhere near the "mixed audience" line, get a real legal opinion rather than guessing from a blog post — this one included.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Can some videos on my channel be "made for kids" and others not?</h4>
<p>Yes. Set a channel-level default for convenience, then override it per video in the Basic Info tab during upload or edit. The per-video setting always wins over the channel default.</p>
<h4>Does "made for kids" hurt my discoverability, or help it?</h4>
<p>Both, depending on your audience. YouTube says MFK videos are more likely to be recommended alongside other kids' content — a plus if that's genuinely your audience, a drag if your content is general-audience and gets mislabeled. This ties into how <a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">Suggested Videos</a> surfaces content — recommendations lean toward keeping kids' content with kids' content.</p>
<h4>If YouTube auto-sets my video as MFK and I disagree, can I change it?</h4>
<p>If you haven't set an audience yet and YouTube applied a default, you can usually change it. If you'd already set your own audience and YouTube later overrode it for error or abuse, you'll see "Set to made for kids" and won't be able to edit it directly — you'll need to file an appeal in YouTube Studio (one appeal per video).</p>
