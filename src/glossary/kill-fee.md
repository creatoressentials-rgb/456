<h2>Kill Fee</h2>
<p>A <strong>kill fee</strong> is compensation a client or brand agrees to pay when it cancels, terminates, or abandons a creator project under circumstances defined in the contract.</p>
<p>The fee recognizes that the creator may have reserved time, rejected other work, developed concepts, booked contractors, rented equipment, or completed part of the production before cancellation.</p>
<p>A kill fee is not automatic. The agreement should say when it applies, how it is calculated, and what other amounts remain due.</p>
<h3>Why creator contracts use kill fees</h3>
<p>A canceled campaign can still cost the creator money and time. The creator may have:</p>
<ul>
<li>Reserved a publication date</li>
<li>Blocked a competing <a href="/glossary/sponsorship">sponsorship</a></li>
<li>Completed research or scripting</li>
<li>Purchased props or products</li>
<li>Hired editors, talent, crew, or locations</li>
<li>Traveled or paid nonrefundable expenses</li>
<li>Filmed all or part of the content</li>
<li>Used included <a href="/glossary/revision-round">revision rounds</a></li>
<li>Delayed other work to meet the campaign deadline</li>
<li>Lost the opportunity to replace the project</li>
</ul>
<p>A kill fee provides a predictable cancellation remedy instead of leaving every loss to a later dispute.</p>
<h3>What can be due after cancellation?</h3>
<p>A creator cancellation clause may require one or more of the following:</p>
<h4>Payment for completed work</h4>
<p>The brand pays for work already performed through the cancellation date. This can include completed milestones, hourly work, or a proportional share of the project fee.</p>
<h4>Nonrefundable expenses</h4>
<p>The brand reimburses approved travel, rentals, talent, props, shipping, production services, and other committed costs.</p>
<h4>Kill fee</h4>
<p>The brand pays an additional fixed amount or percentage because the project was canceled.</p>
<h4>Full remaining fee</h4>
<p>Some contracts require the full project fee after a late-stage cancellation, particularly when production is complete or the publication date was reserved.</p>
<h4>Rescheduling fee</h4>
<p>The brand pays a separate amount when it postpones rather than permanently cancels the campaign.</p>
<p>These items should not be counted twice unless the contract intentionally provides for each one.</p>

<h3>When should a kill fee apply?</h3>
<p>Possible triggers include:</p>
<ul>
<li>The brand cancels after signing</li>
<li>The brand cancels after concept approval</li>
<li>The brand cancels after production begins</li>
<li>The brand stops responding for a defined period</li>
<li>The product launch or campaign is abandoned</li>
<li>The brand repeatedly delays required materials</li>
<li>The brand rejects compliant work for reasons outside the brief</li>
<li>A campaign is canceled after the creator declined competing work</li>
<li>The brand requests an indefinite hold rather than approving or canceling</li>
</ul>
<p>The contract should treat creator breach separately. A creator who fails to perform may not be entitled to the same cancellation payment as a creator whose completed work is canceled for the brand's convenience.</p>

<p>These are examples, not industry-mandated percentages. The amount should match the project, lost opportunity, expenses, and governing law.</p>
<h3>What the kill-fee clause should define</h3>
<p>Include:</p>
<ul>
<li>The cancellation events that trigger payment</li>
<li>The amount or formula</li>
<li>Milestone-based increases</li>
<li>Payment for work already completed</li>
<li>Treatment of deposits</li>
<li>Expense reimbursement</li>
<li>Rescheduling rules</li>
<li>Notice method and effective cancellation date</li>
<li>Payment deadline</li>
<li>Whether exclusivity ends immediately</li>
<li>Whether the creator can accept replacement work</li>
<li>Ownership and <a href="/glossary/content-licensing">content licensing</a> for drafts and completed assets</li>
<li>Whether the brand may use canceled work</li>
<li>What happens to confidential information and products</li>
<li>Force-majeure treatment</li>
<li>Creator default and cure rights</li>
<li>Platform or regulatory events</li>
<li>Any cap or alternative remedy</li>
</ul>
<h3>Who owns canceled work?</h3>
<p>Cancellation does not automatically give the brand rights to use concepts, drafts, raw footage, or final content.</p>
<p>The contract should coordinate the kill-fee provision with <a href="/glossary/content-ownership">content ownership</a>. Common approaches include:</p>
<ul>
<li>The creator retains all rights in unpaid work.</li>
<li>The brand receives no usage rights unless it pays the full agreed license fee.</li>
<li>The brand may use completed work after paying a stated buyout.</li>
<li>Brand-confidential materials must still be returned or destroyed.</li>
<li>The creator may reuse general concepts but not confidential campaign information.</li>
</ul>
<p>A payment for cancellation and a license to use the content are separate economic terms.</p>
<h3>Kill fee vs. termination for breach</h3>
<p>A contract should distinguish:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Termination for convenience:</strong> The brand ends the project without alleging creator wrongdoing.</li>
<li><strong>Termination for cause:</strong> One party materially breaches and does not cure within the agreed period.</li>
<li><strong>Mutual cancellation:</strong> Both sides agree to stop.</li>
<li><strong>Force majeure:</strong> An uncontrollable event prevents performance.</li>
<li><strong>Rescheduling:</strong> The project continues on a new timeline.</li>
</ul>
<p>The payment outcome can differ for each category.</p>
<h3>Is a kill fee enforceable?</h3>
<p>Enforceability depends on the contract wording, governing law, and circumstances. Predetermined charges can be evaluated under rules governing liquidated damages and penalty clauses. A clause designed to reasonably address expected loss is different from an excessive amount intended only to punish cancellation.</p>
<p>Creators and brands should obtain legal advice for high-value or disputed agreements.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>What percentage is a standard kill fee?</h4>
<p>There is no universal creator-industry percentage. The amount can depend on project stage, reserved time, expenses, lost opportunities, and the governing contract.</p>
<h4>Is a kill fee the same as a nonrefundable deposit?</h4>
<p>No. A deposit is normally paid before or during the project. A kill fee is triggered by qualifying cancellation. The agreement can credit the deposit toward the kill fee.</p>
<h4>Does a kill fee let the brand use the canceled content?</h4>
<p>Not automatically. Usage rights and ownership should be addressed separately. The brand may need to pay an additional license or full buyout before using the work.</p>
<h4>Can a creator charge a kill fee when the brand stops responding?</h4>
<p>Yes if the contract treats prolonged nonresponse as cancellation or creator termination for brand breach. Define the notice and cure period rather than assuming ghosting automatically triggers payment.</p>
