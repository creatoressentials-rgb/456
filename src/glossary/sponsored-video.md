<h2>Sponsored Video</h2>
<p>A <strong>sponsored video</strong> is a video that contains or was produced under a commercial relationship between a creator and a brand. The sponsor provides money, products, services, or another benefit, and the creator agrees to feature, discuss, demonstrate, endorse, or otherwise include the brand in the video.</p>
<p>A sponsored video can be fully dedicated to the sponsor or contain a shorter sponsored integration inside a broader unsponsored topic. It is one form of <a href="/glossary/sponsored-content/">sponsored content</a>.</p>
<p>The exact relationship should be explained through clear disclosure, and the <a href="/glossary/brand-deal/">brand deal</a> should define the scope, payment, rights, review process, and publication requirements.</p>

<p>A dedicated sponsored video is not automatically a positive review. The creator’s statements must still reflect honest experience, and the agreement should not require unsupported praise.</p>
<h3>Sponsored video vs. YouTube ad</h3>
<p>A sponsored video is creator content. A YouTube ad is delivered through YouTube’s advertising system before, during, after, or around content.</p>
<p>That distinction has several consequences:</p>
<ul>
<li>YouTube may still run platform ads on a sponsored video.</li>
<li>YouTube Premium does not remove a <a href="/glossary/sponsorship/">sponsorship</a> that is part of the creator’s video.</li>
<li>The sponsor and creator negotiate the video separately from YouTube <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">ad revenue</a>.</li>
<li>The creator must handle <a href="/glossary/sponsorship-disclosure/">sponsorship disclosure</a> and any applicable legal obligations.</li>
<li>Brand <a href="/glossary/usage-rights/">usage rights</a> are not automatically created by uploading the video.</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube says that when a creator declares paid promotion, it may replace an ordinary ad that conflicts with the sponsor, but ads can still run.</p>
<h3>YouTube’s rule on embedded third-party ads</h3>
<p>YouTube permits paid product placements and endorsements that comply with its policies and applicable law. However, YouTube says creators cannot embed third-party advertisements that duplicate ad formats YouTube already offers, including video pre-rolls, mid-rolls, post-rolls, and bumpers.</p>
<p>This means a creator-integrated sponsorship must be part of the creator’s content rather than simply inserting a separate advertiser-produced commercial where YouTube sells a comparable ad placement.</p>
<p>Creators should review current platform policy before accepting a pre-produced clip from a sponsor.</p>
<h3>What sponsored-video deliverables should define</h3>
<p>A precise <a href="/glossary/deliverables/">deliverable</a> can identify:</p>
<ul>
<li>Creator channel and video format</li>
<li>Integrated or dedicated structure</li>
<li>Sponsored-segment duration and placement</li>
<li>Product demonstration or required visuals</li>
<li>Approved factual talking points</li>
<li>Prohibited claims or competitor references</li>
<li>Verbal and on-screen disclosure</li>
<li>Call to action, URL, code, pinned comment, or product tag</li>
<li>Concept, script, rough-cut, and final approval stages</li>
<li>Included <a href="/glossary/revision-round/">revision rounds</a></li>
<li>Publication date and minimum live period</li>
<li>Reporting metrics and reporting date</li>
<li>Organic reposting, paid amplification, editing, and licensing rights</li>
<li>Exclusivity and category restrictions</li>
<li>Payment schedule and cancellation terms</li>
</ul>
<p>“Sponsored YouTube video” is not enough by itself. The creator and brand should agree on whether the sponsor reviews only the sponsored segment or the entire video, and which channel elements—title, thumbnail, description, pinned comment, and end screen—are included.</p>
<h3>Typical sponsored-video workflow</h3>
<ol>
<li><strong>Opportunity and fit review:</strong> The creator evaluates the product, audience relevance, claims, fee, and reputation.</li>
<li><strong>Brief and negotiation:</strong> The parties agree on the objective, concept, deliverables, rights, exclusivity, and schedule.</li>
<li><strong>Contract and payment setup:</strong> The agreement defines compensation, invoicing, cancellation, and approval authority.</li>
<li><strong>Product testing or research:</strong> The creator obtains enough real experience to speak truthfully.</li>
<li><strong>Concept or script review:</strong> The brand checks factual requirements without rewriting the creator’s honest opinion.</li>
<li><strong>Production and edit:</strong> The creator records and assembles the video.</li>
<li><strong>Review and revisions:</strong> Feedback follows the agreed round limits.</li>
<li><strong>Disclosure and publication:</strong> The creator uses the platform declaration and applicable viewer-facing disclosure.</li>
<li><strong>Reporting:</strong> The creator sends the agreed metrics after the stated period.</li>
</ol>
<h3>Disclosure on a sponsored video</h3>
<p>YouTube instructs creators to select the paid-promotion declaration when a video contains a paid product placement, sponsorship, endorsement, or other commercial relationship. YouTube then shows a paid-promotion message to viewers.</p>
<p>That platform label may not be the only required disclosure. The U.S. Federal Trade Commission says a video endorsement disclosure should appear in the video, not only in the description, and should be hard to miss. It also says disclosures are more likely to be noticed when made in both audio and video.</p>
<p>Clear examples include:</p>
<ul>
<li>“This video is sponsored by Brand.”</li>
<li>“Brand paid for this portion of the video.”</li>
<li>“Brand sent me this product for free and is sponsoring today’s video.”</li>
</ul>
<p>The disclosure should appear when viewers encounter the sponsored message rather than being hidden after the integration or at the bottom of a long description.</p>
<h3>Brand partner access and promotion</h3>
<p>YouTube allows eligible brand partner access for long-form videos and Shorts associated with brand deals. Depending on the setup, access can allow the brand or its agency to view certain organic and paid performance metrics and promote the video through creator-partnership advertising tools.</p>
<p>Granting access is a commercial decision. YouTube advises creators to discuss usage rights with advertisers and obtain the necessary agreements independently. The contract should state whether paid promotion, data access, and the creator’s name or likeness are included and for how long.</p>
<h3>Sponsored-video pricing factors</h3>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/creator-rate-card/">creator rate card</a> can provide a baseline, but a custom quote may consider:</p>
<ul>
<li>Typical views for comparable videos</li>
<li>Integrated versus dedicated format</li>
<li>Segment length and placement</li>
<li>Production complexity and expenses</li>
<li>Number of assets and platform versions</li>
<li>Revision and approval burden</li>
<li>Publication schedule and rush work</li>
<li>Usage rights and paid amplification</li>
<li>Exclusivity and opportunity cost</li>
<li>Minimum live period</li>
<li>Regulated or high-risk claims</li>
</ul>
<p>A dedicated video often requires more time and gives the brand more prominence, but a short integration on a highly trusted channel can also carry substantial value.</p>
<h3>Sponsored videos and creator trust</h3>
<p>A creator can protect audience trust by:</p>
<ul>
<li>Choosing products that fit the channel</li>
<li>Testing the product before making experiential claims</li>
<li>Disclosing clearly and early</li>
<li>Separating facts supplied by the brand from the creator’s opinion</li>
<li>Declining scripts that sound unnatural or deceptive</li>
<li>Limiting the number of unrelated sponsors</li>
<li>Explaining relevant limitations or conditions</li>
<li>Avoiding urgency, savings, health, or earnings claims that cannot be supported</li>
</ul>
<p>Sponsorship does not automatically make a video dishonest. Hidden relationships and misleading claims create the larger trust problem.</p>
<h3>Common sponsored-video mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Treating every sponsorship as a dedicated video:</strong> Many deals are integrations.</li>
<li><strong>Accepting a pre-produced third-party commercial without checking policy:</strong> YouTube restricts embedded ads that duplicate its own formats.</li>
<li><strong>Disclosing only in the description:</strong> Many viewers never open it.</li>
<li><strong>Guaranteeing organic performance casually:</strong> Views and sales are not fully controlled by the creator.</li>
<li><strong>Allowing unlimited revisions:</strong> The <a href="/glossary/approval-process/">approval process</a> becomes open-ended.</li>
<li><strong>Ignoring usage rights:</strong> The brand may expect to run the video as an ad.</li>
<li><strong>Giving the brand full editorial control:</strong> The creator may be pushed into an untruthful endorsement.</li>
<li><strong>Publishing before the agreement is settled:</strong> Payment, rights, and cancellation remain exposed.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is every sponsored video fully about the sponsor?</h4>
<p>No. A video can contain a short sponsored integration while the main video covers another topic. A dedicated sponsored video centers most or all of the content on the sponsor.</p>
<h4>Is a sponsored video the same as a YouTube ad?</h4>
<p>No. A sponsored video is creator content produced under a commercial relationship. <a href="/glossary/youtube-ads/">YouTube ads</a> are delivered separately through the platform’s advertising system.</p>
<h4>Can YouTube run ads on a sponsored video?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube says ads may still run, although it may replace an ad that conflicts with the declared brand partner.</p>
<h4>Does YouTube Premium remove sponsored segments?</h4>
<p>No. Premium may remove platform-served ads, but a sponsorship integrated into the video remains part of the content.</p>
<h4>Can a brand use the sponsored video in paid ads?</h4>
<p>Only under the agreed rights. The creator should define paid amplification, duration, platforms, editing permission, and compensation before granting access or licensing the content.</p>
