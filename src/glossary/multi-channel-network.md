<h2>Multi-Channel <a href="/glossary/dropped-frames/">Network</a></h2>
<p>A <strong>multi-channel network</strong>, abbreviated <strong>MCN</strong>, is a third-party company that affiliates with multiple YouTube channels and provides services to those channels.</p>
<p>YouTube says MCN services may include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Audience development</li>
<li>Content programming</li>
<li>Creator collaborations</li>
<li>Digital rights management</li>
<li>Monetization</li>
<li>Media sales</li>
<li>Other channel support</li>
</ul>
<p>An MCN is not owned or endorsed by YouTube merely because it can link channels through YouTube systems.</p>
<h3>How an MCN relationship works</h3>
<p>A typical relationship can involve:</p>
<ol>
<li>Creator signs a private contract with the MCN.</li>
<li>Channel becomes linked to the MCN's content-owner account.</li>
<li>MCN receives defined YouTube management capabilities.</li>
<li>YouTube revenue may flow through the network arrangement.</li>
<li>MCN provides contracted services.</li>
<li>MCN retains a fee or <a href="/glossary/revenue-share/">revenue share</a>.</li>
<li>Relationship ends under the contract and YouTube unlinking process.</li>
</ol>
<p>The private agreement and YouTube technical relationship are separate but connected.</p>

<h3>MCNs and the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a></h3>
<p>YouTube states that channels affiliated with MCNs must still be reviewed and comply with YouTube monetization policies.</p>
<p>Joining an MCN does not guarantee:</p>
<ul>
<li>YPP approval</li>
<li>Monetization</li>
<li>Higher <a href="/glossary/cost-per-mille-cpm/">CPM</a></li>
<li>Sponsorships</li>
<li>More views</li>
<li>Copyright protection</li>
<li>Dedicated YouTube support</li>
</ul>
<p>Creators should be skeptical of guarantees.</p>
<h3>Affiliate and owned-and-operated channels</h3>
<p>YouTube documentation distinguishes network channel relationships, including affiliate channels managed at scale through an affiliate content owner.</p>
<p>An MCN may also own or directly operate some channels.</p>
<p>A creator should understand whether the network:</p>
<ul>
<li>Owns the channel</li>
<li>Merely manages it</li>
<li>Represents rights</li>
<li>Receives revenue</li>
<li>Controls Content ID claims</li>
<li>Can change monetization settings</li>
</ul>

<h3>Leaving an MCN</h3>
<p>YouTube provides an in-product process for eligible channels to request removal of MCN access.</p>
<p>The network generally has a response period, but leaving the technical network does not automatically eliminate private contractual obligations.</p>
<p>Creators should:</p>
<ul>
<li>Review the agreement</li>
<li>Save reports</li>
<li>Confirm unpaid revenue</li>
<li>Document claims</li>
<li>Revoke unrelated permissions</li>
<li>Confirm Content ID release</li>
<li>Secure account ownership</li>
<li>Obtain professional advice when needed</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Does joining an MCN guarantee YouTube monetization?</h4>
<p>No. MCN-affiliated channels must still satisfy YouTube Partner Program and monetization policies.</p>
<h4>Does an MCN own the channel?</h4>
<p>Not automatically. Ownership depends on the account structure and contract.</p>
<h4>Can a creator leave an MCN?</h4>
<p>YouTube provides a technical removal-request process for eligible channels, but the creator must also consider the private agreement.</p>
<h4>Is an MCN the same as a <a href="/glossary/creator-agency/">creator agency</a>?</h4>
<p>No. An agency can provide services without linking channels to a YouTube network content owner.</p>
