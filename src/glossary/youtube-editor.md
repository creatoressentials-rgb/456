<h2>YouTube Editor</h2>
<p><strong>YouTube Editor</strong> usually refers to the browser-based video editor inside YouTube Studio. It allows creators to make limited changes to an already uploaded video without deleting it and uploading a replacement.</p>
<p>The current Studio Editor is designed for corrections and simple post-publication adjustments. It is not a full nonlinear editing application like Adobe Premiere Pro, Final Cut Pro, DaVinci Resolve, or CapCut.</p>

<h3>Where Is YouTube Studio Editor?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Sign in to YouTube Studio on a computer.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Content</strong>.</li>
  <li>Click the title or thumbnail of the video you want to edit.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Editor</strong> from the left menu.</li>
</ol>
<p>Several Editor features require a computer. YouTube's mobile Studio app offers some video-management and claim-resolution tools, but it does not reproduce every desktop Editor function.</p>

<h3>What Can YouTube Editor Do?</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Tool</th><th>Current Use</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Trim and cut</td><td>Remove the beginning, end, or selected middle sections of an uploaded video</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Face blur</td><td>Detect faces and apply blur to selected people</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Custom blur</td><td>Blur a selected rectangle or oval, with fixed-position or tracked movement</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Audio</td><td>Add licensed music from YouTube's Audio Library to eligible videos</td></tr>
    <tr><td>End screen</td><td>Add or adjust end-screen elements and timing</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Info cards</td><td>Add cards pointing viewers to eligible videos, playlists, channels, or links where available</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Claim-removal tools</td><td>Trim claimed segments, replace claimed music, or erase audio when the claim supports those actions</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>What YouTube Editor Cannot Replace</h3>
<p>YouTube Studio Editor is not intended for full production editing. It generally does not provide the complete control needed to:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Rearrange many clips into a new sequence</li>
  <li>Add multiple video layers</li>
  <li>Create advanced transitions</li>
  <li>Perform detailed color correction or grading</li>
  <li>Add custom motion graphics and animated titles</li>
  <li>Mix multiple dialogue, music, and sound-effect tracks precisely</li>
  <li>Change framing throughout the video with keyframed crops</li>
  <li>Replace the entire uploaded video file while keeping the same URL</li>
  <li>Restore content that was never included in the uploaded file</li>
</ul>
<p>Complete the main edit before upload. Use Studio Editor for targeted corrections rather than as the primary production workflow.</p>

<h3>Why Use YouTube Editor?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Remove an accidental pause, mistake, private detail, or outdated statement.</li>
  <li>Cut a long introduction from a published video.</li>
  <li>Blur a face, license plate, screen, address, or personal document.</li>
  <li>Remove a segment involved in a <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a> claim.</li>
  <li>Add Audio Library music to an eligible upload.</li>
  <li>Adjust end screens after the final runtime changes.</li>
  <li>Preserve the video's URL and accumulated performance instead of re-uploading for a small correction.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Are Saved Editor Changes Reversible?</h3>
<p>As of June 2025, YouTube says creators cannot undo saved YouTube Studio Editor changes through the previous <strong>Revert to original</strong> feature.</p>
<p>This means you should:</p>
<ol>
  <li>Keep the original master file.</li>
  <li>Download or archive a copy before editing when the master is unavailable.</li>
  <li>Preview every cut and blur carefully.</li>
  <li>Confirm that no necessary dialogue, captions, or context will be removed.</li>
  <li>Save only after accepting that the Studio version cannot be restored through Revert to original.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube's Help guidance specifically recommends downloading the video or using Google Takeout before making irreversible edits when needed.</p>

<h3>Preview vs. Save</h3>
<p>While an edit is still unsaved, creators can preview it, undo individual planned cuts, delete a cut, or discard the pending changes. The irreversible warning applies after the edit is saved and processed.</p>
<p>Use Preview from several points around every edit. A cut can remove part of a word, create an abrupt audio transition, change the meaning of a sentence, or make captions and chapters inaccurate.</p>

<h3>How Long Does Processing Take?</h3>
<p>Processing time varies with the video's duration, resolution, complexity, platform demand, and edit type. While the change processes, YouTube may show a <strong>Video editing is in progress</strong> status and an estimated time remaining.</p>
<p>You generally cannot make another Editor change while the current edit is processing. The video can remain available in its previous state until processing finishes.</p>

<h3>Video-Length and View-Count Limits</h3>
<p>YouTube says trimming and Audio Library editing are available for videos shorter than six hours.</p>
<p>For an unedited video with more than 100,000 views, creators outside the YouTube Partner Program may not be able to save Editor changes. Face blurring can remain available as an exception. The view-count restriction does not apply to YPP channels under the current guidance.</p>

<h3>Does Editing Keep the Same URL and Analytics?</h3>
<p>For trimming, YouTube explicitly says the video's URL, view count, and comments remain the same because the creator does not need to re-upload the video.</p>
<p>This is a major advantage over deleting and replacing a video. However, changing the runtime can affect:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Audience-retention timelines</li>
  <li>Manual chapter timestamps</li>
  <li>Descriptions containing timestamps</li>
  <li>Captions and transcript timing</li>
  <li>End-screen placement</li>
  <li>External links pointing to a timestamp</li>
  <li>Clips and remixed content derived from the original</li>
</ul>

<h3>How Edits Affect Remixed Shorts</h3>
<p>YouTube says trimming or blurring an original video in Studio can affect Shorts created from remixed content. Depending on the remix type, audio remixes may be muted, made unlisted, or scheduled for deletion, and video remixes may be deleted.</p>
<p>Before editing a heavily remixed video, consider whether the correction is important enough to justify the impact on derivative Shorts.</p>

<h3>YouTube Editor and Copyright Claims</h3>
<p>When a video has a <a href="/glossary/copyright-claim/">copyright claim</a>, YouTube may offer targeted options such as:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Trim out the claimed segment</li>
  <li>Replace a claimed song with Audio Library music</li>
  <li>Erase only the claimed song when separation is possible</li>
  <li>Mute all sound in the claimed section</li>
</ul>
<p>The claim is removed only when all claimed material is successfully eliminated or replaced under the tool's conditions.</p>

<h3>Adding Audio Library Music</h3>
<p>YouTube Studio Editor can add licensed tracks from the YouTube Audio Library. Creators can preview a song, add it to the timeline, move its starting point, adjust its duration, and add multiple tracks.</p>
<p>This is not a full audio mixer. Dialogue balancing, noise repair, advanced fades, sound design, and detailed track automation are better completed before upload.</p>

<h3>Editor vs. Re-uploading</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Use Studio Editor When</th><th>Re-edit and Upload a New Video When</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>The correction is a simple cut, blur, or supported audio change</td><td>The video needs major restructuring or replacement footage</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Preserving the existing URL, views, and comments matters</td><td>The current upload has widespread technical or creative problems</td></tr>
    <tr><td>The published video has accumulated valuable search or recommendation history</td><td>A new version should be clearly distinguished from the original</td></tr>
    <tr><td>The edit can be previewed safely within Studio</td><td>You need a reversible project timeline and full-quality master control</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>Recommended Editing Workflow</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Locate and preserve the original master.</li>
  <li>Document the exact correction and timestamps.</li>
  <li>Check for chapters, captions, cards, end screens, links, and remixes that may be affected.</li>
  <li>Make the smallest edit that solves the problem.</li>
  <li>Preview before, during, and after every cut or blur.</li>
  <li>Save the edit.</li>
  <li>Wait for processing to finish before making another change.</li>
  <li>Review the public result on mobile, desktop, and television where relevant.</li>
  <li>Update timestamps, descriptions, and related materials.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Common YouTube Editor Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Using Studio Editor as the only copy of the production.</li>
  <li>Saving without realizing Revert to original is no longer available.</li>
  <li>Cutting a section without checking captions, chapters, and end screens.</li>
  <li>Expecting transitions, color correction, and multi-track video editing.</li>
  <li>Making several irreversible edits at once.</li>
  <li>Editing a remixed source video without considering the derivative Shorts.</li>
  <li>Assuming processing is instant.</li>
  <li>Deleting and re-uploading when a simple supported correction would preserve the URL.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Is YouTube Editor free?</h4>
<p>Yes. It is included in YouTube Studio for supported videos and eligible channel features.</p>

<h4>Can YouTube Editor combine multiple uploaded videos?</h4>
<p>No. The current Studio Editor is mainly for modifying one uploaded video, not assembling several videos into a new project.</p>

<h4>Can I undo a saved trim or blur?</h4>
<p>YouTube says Revert to original has not been available for saved Studio Editor changes since June 2025.</p>

<h4>Will trimming delete my views and comments?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube says trimming keeps the same URL, view count, and comments.</p>
