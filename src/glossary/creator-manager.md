<h2>Creator Manager</h2>
<p>A <strong>creator manager</strong> is a professional who helps a creator develop, organize, protect, and monetize a career or creator-led business.</p>
<p>A manager may support:</p>
<ul>
<li>Career strategy</li>
<li>Brand deals</li>
<li>Negotiation</li>
<li>Partnerships</li>
<li>Scheduling</li>
<li>Team coordination</li>
<li>Product opportunities</li>
<li>Platform relationships</li>
<li>Public positioning</li>
<li>Business development</li>
<li>Long-term planning</li>
</ul>
<p>IAB describes talent management as supporting individuals through opportunities, contract negotiation, and career guidance.</p>

<p>The labels are used inconsistently. The contract and applicable law matter more than the title.</p>
<h3>Creator manager vs. creator agency</h3>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/creator-agency">creator agency</a> is a company that can provide representation, production, campaign, or service functions.</p>
<p>A creator manager is a role focused on an individual creator or roster.</p>
<p>A manager can work inside an agency, and an agency can provide management.</p>
<h3>Creator manager vs. YouTube Partner Manager</h3>
<p>These are different.</p>
<h4>Private creator manager</h4>
<ul>
<li>Chosen and paid by the creator</li>
<li>May negotiate commercial deals</li>
<li>Can receive authorized access</li>
<li>Owes duties according to the private agreement</li>
</ul>
<h4>YouTube Partner Manager</h4>
<ul>
<li>Part of an invite-only YouTube program</li>
<li>Helps eligible creators use YouTube effectively</li>
<li>Provides platform support, education, and opportunities</li>
<li>Is not the creator's private talent representative</li>
</ul>
<p>Creators should not assume a YouTube employee will negotiate outside contracts or manage the business.</p>
<h3>Common manager responsibilities</h3>
<h4>Opportunity management</h4>
<ul>
<li>Review inbound offers</li>
<li>Conduct outreach</li>
<li>Qualify brands</li>
<li>Maintain pipeline</li>
<li>Coordinate deadlines</li>
</ul>
<h4>Deal support</h4>
<ul>
<li>Negotiate business terms</li>
<li>Coordinate contract review</li>
<li>Track deliverables</li>
<li>Confirm payment</li>
<li>Manage renewals</li>
</ul>
<p>A manager is not automatically a lawyer and should not present legal advice beyond permitted scope.</p>
<h4>Career strategy</h4>
<ul>
<li>Platform expansion</li>
<li>Content positioning</li>
<li>Partnerships</li>
<li>Product strategy</li>
<li>Hiring</li>
<li>Long-term brand development</li>
</ul>
<h4>Team coordination</h4>
<ul>
<li>Editors</li>
<li>Producers</li>
<li>Assistants</li>
<li>Public relations</li>
<li>Accountants</li>
<li>Attorneys</li>
<li>Agencies</li>
<li>Vendors</li>
</ul>
<h3>Compensation</h3>
<p>Managers can be paid through:</p>
<ul>
<li>Percentage commission</li>
<li>Retainer</li>
<li>Salary</li>
<li>Project fee</li>
<li>Hybrid structure</li>
</ul>
<p>A commission clause should define whether it applies to:</p>
<ul>
<li>Deals the manager sourced</li>
<li>Deals the creator sourced</li>
<li>Existing sponsors</li>
<li>Platform revenue</li>
<li>Affiliate revenue</li>
<li>Products</li>
<li>Memberships</li>
<li>Licensing</li>
<li>Renewals</li>
<li>Post-termination income</li>
</ul>
<p>“20% of all income” is much broader than 20% of negotiated brand partnerships.</p>
<h3>Manager authority</h3>
<p>The agreement should state whether the manager can:</p>
<ul>
<li>Negotiate</li>
<li>Accept offers</li>
<li>Sign agreements</li>
<li>Approve creative</li>
<li>Submit invoices</li>
<li>Receive payment</li>
<li>Access analytics</li>
<li>Invite team members</li>
<li>Publish content</li>
<li>Communicate publicly</li>
</ul>
<p>Creators should not grant more authority than needed.</p>
<h3>YouTube access</h3>
<p>YouTube allows creators to add authorized agents or managers to Creator Partnerships and offers channel permissions for role-based access.</p>
<p>Use official permissions rather than sharing:</p>
<ul>
<li>Password</li>
<li>Recovery code</li>
<li>Two-factor code</li>
<li>Personal Gmail access</li>
<li>Unrelated Google assets</li>
</ul>
<p>Review access when the relationship ends.</p>
<h3>Manager contract checklist</h3>
<p>Define:</p>
<ul>
<li>Services</li>
<li>Term</li>
<li>Exclusivity</li>
<li>Territory</li>
<li>Platforms</li>
<li>Commission rate</li>
<li>Commission base</li>
<li>Existing relationships</li>
<li>Expenses</li>
<li>Signing authority</li>
<li>Payment flow</li>
<li>Data access</li>
<li>Confidentiality</li>
<li>Conflicts</li>
<li>Post-term commission</li>
<li>Termination</li>
<li>Dispute process</li>
<li>Account handoff</li>
</ul>
<h3>Post-termination commission</h3>
<p>Some agreements let a manager receive commission after termination on deals negotiated during the relationship.</p>
<p>A fair clause should identify:</p>
<ul>
<li>Covered contracts</li>
<li>Covered renewals</li>
<li>Duration</li>
<li>Declining commission, if any</li>
<li>Whether manager involvement is required</li>
<li>Treatment of options</li>
<li>Treatment of new work from the same brand</li>
</ul>
<p>An unlimited commission on unrelated future work creates substantial risk.</p>
<h3>Conflicts of interest</h3>
<p>A manager may face conflicts when they:</p>
<ul>
<li>Represent competing creators</li>
<li>Receive brand-side fees</li>
<li>Own an agency or production company</li>
<li>Recommend vendors they own</li>
<li>Prioritize higher commission over audience fit</li>
<li>Control financial reporting</li>
<li>Invest in a creator product</li>
</ul>
<p>Conflicts should be disclosed.</p>
<h3>Warning signs</h3>
<ul>
<li>Guaranteed deals or fame</li>
<li>Pressure to sign immediately</li>
<li>No written scope</li>
<li>Broad ownership of accounts</li>
<li>Commission on every revenue source</li>
<li>Indefinite exclusivity</li>
<li>Hidden agency fees</li>
<li>Refusal to provide reporting</li>
<li>Password demands</li>
<li>No termination process</li>
<li>Signing authority without limits</li>
<li>Discouraging independent legal review</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>What percentage does a creator manager charge?</h4>
<p>There is no universal rate. The percentage and revenue base depend on services, market, bargaining power, and contract scope.</p>
<h4>Is a creator manager a lawyer?</h4>
<p>Not automatically. Managers can coordinate deals, but legal advice and contract interpretation may require qualified counsel.</p>
<h4>Is a YouTube Partner Manager my talent manager?</h4>
<p>No. A YouTube Partner Manager is part of an invite-only platform support program.</p>
<h4>Should a manager have my YouTube password?</h4>
<p>No. Use official channel permissions and authorized deal access instead of sharing passwords or authentication codes.</p>
