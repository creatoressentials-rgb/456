<h2>Affiliate Program</h2>
<p>An <strong>affiliate program</strong> is a structured marketing arrangement in which a brand or merchant compensates approved creators, publishers, or other partners for generating tracked and validated results.</p>
<p>Those results may include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Sales</li>
<li>Leads</li>
<li>New customers</li>
<li>Subscriptions</li>
<li>App installations</li>
<li>Trial registrations</li>
<li>Qualified calls</li>
<li>Other program-defined conversions</li>
</ul>
<p>The person or company promoting the offer is the <strong>affiliate</strong>. The brand operating the program is commonly called the <strong>advertiser</strong>, <strong>merchant</strong>, or <strong>program owner</strong>.</p>
<h3>How an affiliate program works</h3>
<p>A typical affiliate program follows this process:</p>
<ol>
<li>A creator applies to the program.</li>
<li>The brand or platform reviews the creator and promotional properties.</li>
<li>The creator accepts the program agreement.</li>
<li>The program assigns an affiliate or publisher ID.</li>
<li>The creator receives an <a href="/glossary/affiliate-link">affiliate link</a>, referral code, product tag, or other tracking method.</li>
<li>The creator publishes eligible promotional content.</li>
<li>A customer completes a qualifying action.</li>
<li>The tracking system applies the attribution rules.</li>
<li>The conversion remains pending during fraud, return, or compliance review.</li>
<li>The program approves, reverses, or declines the <a href="/glossary/affiliate-commission">affiliate commission</a>.</li>
<li>Approved earnings are paid under the program's <a href="/glossary/payment-terms">payment terms</a>.</li>
</ol>
<p>A creator is not normally paid for every click or initial order. The action must satisfy the complete program rules.</p>
<h3>Direct affiliate program vs. affiliate network</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>Direct affiliate program</th>
  <th>Network-hosted affiliate program</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Operated directly by one brand or its private software</td>
  <td>Hosted through an <a href="/glossary/affiliate-network">affiliate network</a> or partnership platform</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Creator manages a separate brand account</td>
  <td>Creator may access several advertisers through one dashboard</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Brand controls tracking, reports, and payment</td>
  <td>Network may facilitate tracking, reporting, and payments</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Program terms come primarily from the brand</td>
  <td>Both network terms and advertiser terms can apply</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Support comes from the brand</td>
  <td>Support may be split between the network and advertiser</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>An affiliate network can contain many programs. Joining the network does not automatically enroll or approve the creator for every advertiser.</p>
<h3>Affiliate program vs. referral program</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>Affiliate program</th>
  <th><a href="/glossary/referral-program">Referral program</a></th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Designed for creators, publishers, marketers, or commercial partners</td>
  <td>Often designed for existing customers or users</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Compensation is commonly cash commission</td>
  <td>Reward may be credit, discount, cash, points, or free service</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Promotion may be ongoing and professional</td>
  <td>Often based on personal word-of-mouth invitations</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Detailed promotional rules are common</td>
  <td>Simpler refer-a-friend conditions are common</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Affiliate may not be an existing customer</td>
  <td>Referrer is usually an existing customer or user</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The names overlap. Some companies call a commercial partner program a referral program, while others call customer rewards affiliate commissions. The rules and relationship matter more than the label.</p>
<h3>What an affiliate program agreement should define</h3>
<h4>Eligibility</h4>
<p>The program can restrict participation based on:</p>
<ul>
<li>Country</li>
<li>Age</li>
<li>Business type</li>
<li>Audience or niche</li>
<li>Website or channel quality</li>
<li>Platform</li>
<li>Content category</li>
<li>Traffic source</li>
<li>Tax documentation</li>
<li>Existing customer status</li>
</ul>
<p>Approval can be program-wide or advertiser-specific.</p>
<h4>Promotional properties</h4>
<p>The creator may need to identify every channel, website, app, newsletter, or account used for promotion.</p>
<p>Adding an unapproved property later can violate program rules.</p>
<h4>Qualifying actions</h4>
<p>The program should define exactly what earns commission:</p>
<ul>
<li>Completed purchase</li>
<li>Paid subscription</li>
<li>New-customer order</li>
<li>Approved lead</li>
<li>App installation plus required activity</li>
<li>Trial that converts to paid</li>
<li>Product-specific transaction</li>
</ul>
<p>Shopify's affiliate documentation, for example, distinguishes a referral from an eligible paid-plan conversion and allows reversal for fraudulent or refunded stores.</p>
<h4>Commission structure</h4>
<p>The program may offer:</p>
<ul>
<li>Percentage of sale</li>
<li>Fixed bounty</li>
<li>Tiered rate</li>
<li>Recurring commission</li>
<li>New-customer bonus</li>
<li>Product-specific rate</li>
<li>Temporary promotion</li>
<li>Custom creator rate</li>
<li>Hybrid guaranteed fee and commission</li>
</ul>
<p>See <a href="/glossary/affiliate-commission">affiliate commission</a> for the detailed payout models.</p>
<h4>Attribution</h4>
<p>The agreement should identify:</p>
<ul>
<li>Attribution window</li>
<li>Last-click, first-click, or another model</li>
<li>Coupon-code treatment</li>
<li>Cross-device tracking</li>
<li>Existing-customer rules</li>
<li>App and web attribution</li>
<li>Conflicts with other partners</li>
<li>Product tags</li>
<li>Manual adjustments</li>
</ul>
<h4>Validation</h4>
<p>Conversions can be held or reversed because of:</p>
<ul>
<li>Returns</li>
<li>Refunds</li>
<li>Chargebacks</li>
<li>Fraud</li>
<li>Duplicate transactions</li>
<li>Self-referrals</li>
<li>Ineligible products</li>
<li>Canceled subscriptions</li>
<li>Program violations</li>
<li>Unqualified leads</li>
</ul>
<h4>Payment terms</h4>
<p>Review:</p>
<ul>
<li>Payment schedule</li>
<li>Minimum payout threshold</li>
<li>Currency</li>
<li>Payment method</li>
<li>Tax forms</li>
<li>Fees</li>
<li>Pending period</li>
<li>Reserve</li>
<li>Unclaimed balances</li>
<li>Post-termination payments</li>
</ul>
<h3>Common affiliate program restrictions</h3>
<p>Programs may restrict:</p>
<ul>
<li>Paid search using brand trademarks</li>
<li>Domain names containing trademarks</li>
<li>Coupon or deal sites</li>
<li>Email marketing</li>
<li>Text messaging</li>
<li>Browser extensions</li>
<li>Sub-affiliate networks</li>
<li>Social-platform use</li>
<li>Link shortening</li>
<li>Offline promotion</li>
<li>Incentivized traffic</li>
<li>Self-purchases</li>
<li>False scarcity claims</li>
<li>Unsupported product claims</li>
<li>Posting private discount codes</li>
<li>Advertising to children</li>
<li>Certain countries or products</li>
</ul>
<p>Creators should read the current rules rather than relying on another affiliate's summary.</p>
<h3>Disclosure and endorsement rules</h3>
<p>Affiliate compensation is a financial relationship.</p>
<p>The FTC says creators should clearly disclose material connections that could affect how viewers evaluate a recommendation. A disclosure should appear close to the endorsement and use understandable language.</p>
<p>Examples include:</p>
<ul>
<li>“I earn a commission from purchases through these links.”</li>
<li>“This video contains affiliate links.”</li>
<li>“Paid link.”</li>
<li>“As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.”</li>
</ul>
<p>Amazon Associates separately requires its program identification statement and legally compliant disclosures around affiliate promotions.</p>
<h3>Can an affiliate program change its terms?</h3>
<p>Many programs reserve the right to change:</p>
<ul>
<li>Commission rates</li>
<li>Eligible products</li>
<li>Attribution windows</li>
<li>Promotional restrictions</li>
<li>Payment thresholds</li>
<li>Geographic eligibility</li>
<li>Program availability</li>
</ul>
<p>Creators should:</p>
<ul>
<li>Save copies of important terms</li>
<li>Monitor program announcements</li>
<li>Check commission rates regularly</li>
<li>Update old content and links</li>
<li>Diversify merchants</li>
<li>Track effective earnings rather than only advertised rates</li>
</ul>
<p>A program can remain technically active while the economic offer becomes much less valuable.</p>
<h3>Evaluating an affiliate program</h3>
<p>Consider:</p>
<ul>
<li>Product-audience fit</li>
<li>Brand reputation</li>
<li>Product quality</li>
<li>Conversion rate</li>
<li>Commission rate</li>
<li>Average order value</li>
<li>Recurring revenue</li>
<li>Attribution window</li>
<li>Return rate</li>
<li>Reporting quality</li>
<li>Payment reliability</li>
<li>Customer support</li>
<li>Program stability</li>
<li>Promotional restrictions</li>
<li>Disclosure requirements</li>
<li>Network or platform fees</li>
<li>Link maintenance</li>
<li>Existing <a href="/glossary/sponsorship">sponsorship</a> conflicts</li>
</ul>
<p>A high commission percentage does not compensate for a weak product, poor tracking, or high reversal rate.</p>
<h3>Affiliate program red flags</h3>
<p>Watch for programs that:</p>
<ul>
<li>Require payment to join without a clear legitimate service</li>
<li>Promise guaranteed earnings</li>
<li>Focus mainly on recruiting more affiliates</li>
<li>Provide no written terms</li>
<li>Hide commission validation rules</li>
<li>Offer no transaction reporting</li>
<li>Permit retroactive rate changes without notice</li>
<li>Hold commissions indefinitely</li>
<li>Discourage legally required disclosure</li>
<li>Demand broad <a href="/glossary/usage-rights">usage rights</a> to creator content</li>
<li>Require misleading claims</li>
<li>Have no identifiable advertiser or customer product</li>
</ul>
<p>Affiliate marketing pays for customer results. It should not depend primarily on recruiting other participants into the program.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is an affiliate program free to join?</h4>
<p>Many legitimate programs are free, including major brand programs, but not every program is. Creators should understand any fee and what service it purchases.</p>
<h4>Does joining an affiliate program guarantee commission?</h4>
<p>No. The creator must generate qualifying, attributed, and approved actions under the program rules.</p>
<h4>Can a creator join several affiliate programs?</h4>
<p>Usually yes unless a contract or exclusivity rule restricts it. Creators should avoid conflicting claims and maintain accurate links and disclosures.</p>
<h4>Is an affiliate program the same as an affiliate network?</h4>
<p>No. The program belongs to an advertiser. A network can host or connect creators with many separate advertiser programs.</p>
