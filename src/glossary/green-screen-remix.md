<h2><a href="/glossary/green-screen/">Green Screen</a> Remix</h2>
<p><strong>Green Screen Remix</strong> is a <a href="/glossary/youtube-shorts/">YouTube Shorts</a> tool that uses an eligible YouTube video or Short as the background of a new recording. You appear in the foreground while the source content plays behind you, making it easy to add commentary, reactions, explanations, or visual storytelling without downloading the original video.</p>
<p>The feature is called Green Screen because it creates a composited background effect. You do not need a physical green screen.</p>

<h3>How Green Screen Remix works</h3>
<p>The Shorts camera digitally separates you from your surroundings and places the selected source video behind you. You can position and size yourself in the frame, then record while the background content plays.</p>
<p>For eligible music-video content, YouTube says creators can use:</p>
<ul>
<li>The source visuals without the source audio.</li>
<li>Both the source visuals and audio.</li>
</ul>
<p>The exact controls can vary by source, music rights, device, and app version.</p>

<h3>How to create a Green Screen Remix</h3>
<p>Green Screen Remix is created in the YouTube mobile app.</p>
<h4>From another Short</h4>
<ol>
<li>Open the Short you want to use as a background.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Remix</strong>.</li>
<li>Choose <strong>Green screen</strong>.</li>
<li>Adjust your position and the background segment.</li>
<li>Record your foreground video.</li>
<li>Add text, filters, audio, and other edits, then publish.</li>
</ol>
<h4>From a long-form video</h4>
<ol>
<li>Open the eligible video in the YouTube mobile app.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Share</strong>.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Remix</strong>, then <strong>Green screen</strong>.</li>
<li>Choose the section and record your Short.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube has moved the Remix options for regular videos into the Share menu in its current interface. Screenshots in older tutorials may show a separate Remix button below the player.</p>

<h3>What content can be used as a Green Screen Remix?</h3>
<p>The Green Screen option appears only for content that is eligible for visual remixing. It may be unavailable when:</p>
<ul>
<li>The source creator has disabled video remixing.</li>
<li>The source is private, deleted, or restricted.</li>
<li>A third-party claim prevents visual remix features.</li>
<li>The rights holder has not made the content available for Shorts in your territory.</li>
<li>The app, account, or device has not received the feature or latest interface.</li>
</ul>
<p>For music-related Shorts, YouTube notes that third-party claims can block visual options even when the audio remains available to remix.</p>

<h3><a href="/glossary/attribution/">Attribution</a> and discovery</h3>
<p>YouTube attributes Green Screen Remixes to the original source. Viewers can follow that attribution to discover the video that appears in the background.</p>
<p>For the original creator, views produced by visual remixes may appear under <strong>Remixed video</strong> in <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">YouTube traffic sources</a>. Remix reports can also show total remixes and top-remixed content.</p>
<p>The attribution tells viewers where the background came from. It does not mean the source creator endorsed your commentary or participated in making the Short.</p>

<h3>What happens if the source is changed or removed?</h3>
<p>Green Screen Remix is a visual remix that depends on the source video. If the original creator deletes the source, restricts video remixing, or makes certain edits through <a href="/glossary/youtube-studio/">YouTube Studio</a>, YouTube may delete the dependent Green Screen Short.</p>
<p>Creators should account for that risk when using another channel's video as a core part of evergreen content. Your recorded foreground may be original, but the published remix still relies on access to the background source.</p>

<h3>Copyright and monetization considerations</h3>
<p>Using YouTube's built-in Green Screen Remix tool is different from downloading a video and placing it behind yourself in an external editor. The in-product tool provides attribution and operates within the permissions available for that source.</p>
<p>That does not guarantee monetization. The finished Short must still comply with copyright rules, Community Guidelines, and <a href="/glossary/youtube-channel/">YouTube channel</a> monetization policies. A channel must also qualify for the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a> before earning eligible Shorts revenue.</p>
<p>Creators should add meaningful original commentary, performance, education, or creative context. Simply placing your face over another video with little contribution may create <a href="/glossary/reused-content/">reused content</a> concerns during channel review.</p>

<h3>Effective ways to use Green Screen Remix</h3>
<p>Green Screen works well for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Pointing to a chart, screenshot, product, or visual detail.</li>
<li>Explaining why a clip is accurate, misleading, or incomplete.</li>
<li>Reacting while keeping the original moment visible.</li>
<li>Narrating a location, event, or historical scene.</li>
<li>Teaching a technique while the example plays behind you.</li>
<li>Reviewing a transformation, design, thumbnail, or editing choice.</li>
</ul>
<p>Keep yourself from covering the most important part of the background. Position your face and captions where they do not block the visual evidence viewers need to understand.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Do I need a physical green screen?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube creates the foreground-and-background effect in software through the Shorts camera.</p>
<h4>Can Green Screen Remix use the source audio?</h4>
<p>For eligible content, the source may be available with its audio or as visuals only. Music licensing and the available editor controls can affect the options.</p>
<h4>Is Green Screen Remix the same as uploading a background from my phone?</h4>
<p>No. A gallery background uses your local photo or video. Green Screen Remix uses eligible content already on YouTube and attributes the Short to that source.</p>
<h4>Can my Green Screen Short disappear if the source is deleted?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube may delete visual remixes when the original video is deleted or no longer permits remixing.</p>
