<h2>Shorts Feed Ads</h2>
<p><strong>Shorts Feed ads</strong> are ads shown between videos while viewers scroll through the YouTube Shorts Feed. Eligible creators share in the pooled revenue from those ads through the Shorts Monetization Module.</p>
<p>The ads are not assigned directly to the Short immediately before or after them. YouTube combines eligible Shorts Feed ad revenue into a monthly pool and allocates creator revenue based on eligible engaged viewing.</p>

<h3>Where Do Shorts Feed Ads Appear?</h3>
<p>YouTube says eligible Shorts revenue sharing applies to ads viewed <strong>between videos in the Shorts Feed</strong>. The ad may be a skippable video or image ad presented as the viewer scrolls.</p>
<p>The following are not treated as ordinary Creator Pool revenue under YouTube's current policy:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Ads shown when the Shorts Feed first opens, such as a Shorts Masthead</li>
  <li>Ads on navigational pages within the Shorts player</li>
  <li>Revenue associated with image posts appearing in the Shorts Feed</li>
  <li>Ads or views outside the eligible Shorts Feed monetization model</li>
</ul>

<h3>How Shorts Ad Revenue Sharing Works</h3>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Pool revenue:</strong> YouTube combines monthly revenue from ads shown between Shorts.</li>
  <li><strong>Calculate the Creator Pool:</strong> Revenue is divided between the Creator Pool and music-licensing costs based on music usage across eligible Shorts.</li>
  <li><strong>Allocate the Creator Pool:</strong> Each monetizing creator receives an allocation based on their share of eligible engaged Shorts views in each country.</li>
  <li><strong>Apply the revenue share:</strong> The creator keeps 45% of the revenue allocated to them.</li>
</ol>

<h3>What Is the Creator Pool?</h3>
<p>The <strong>Creator Pool</strong> is the portion of eligible Shorts Feed ad revenue available for allocation to monetizing Shorts creators after YouTube accounts for music licensing.</p>
<p>The pool is calculated monthly and by country. A creator does not receive the money from one specific advertisement attached to one specific Short. The creator earns a share of the applicable pool based on eligible engaged-view share.</p>

<h3>How Does Music Affect the Creator Pool?</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Music Used in a Short</th><th>Revenue Associated With Its Engaged Views</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>No music</td><td>All associated revenue is added to the Creator Pool</td></tr>
    <tr><td>One music track</td><td>Half is added to the Creator Pool and half covers music licensing</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Two music tracks</td><td>One third is added to the Creator Pool and two thirds cover music licensing</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>These splits affect the calculation of the overall pool. YouTube says a creator's own use of music does not reduce that creator's later allocation percentage or 45% revenue-share rate once the pool is allocated.</p>

<h3>Does Using Music Reduce a Creator's 45% Share?</h3>
<p>No. YouTube states that monetizing creators keep 45% of their allocated revenue whether or not their individual Shorts use music.</p>
<p>The distinction is:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Music usage helps determine how much associated revenue enters the overall Creator Pool.</li>
  <li>The creator's allocation is based on their eligible engaged-view share.</li>
  <li>The creator then receives 45% of the allocated amount.</li>
</ul>
<p>This does not mean music has no effect on the total money available across the ecosystem. It means YouTube does not subtract a separate music penalty from the individual creator's final percentage after allocation.</p>

<h3>How Is a Creator's Allocation Calculated?</h3>
<p>YouTube distributes each country's Creator Pool according to a creator's share of total eligible engaged views from monetizing creators' Shorts in that country.</p>
<p>For example, if a creator receives 2% of the eligible engaged Shorts views in a country during the month, the creator can be allocated 2% of that country's applicable Creator Pool before the 45% creator share is applied.</p>
<p>This example describes the structure, not a guaranteed payout. The pool size, eligible viewing, geography, music usage, invalid traffic, and revenue adjustments all vary.</p>

<h3>What Is the Creator Revenue Share?</h3>
<p>YouTube says creators receive <strong>45% of the revenue allocated to them</strong> from the Creator Pool.</p>
<p>This is not 45% of:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Every ad shown after the creator's Short</li>
  <li>The total worldwide Shorts advertising revenue</li>
  <li>The advertiser's gross campaign budget</li>
  <li>All views displayed in the public view counter</li>
</ul>
<p>The 45% is applied after the monthly pool is calculated and the creator's eligible allocation is determined.</p>

<h3>Which Views Can Earn Shorts Ad Revenue?</h3>
<p>Only monetizing partners who have accepted the Shorts Monetization Module can earn from eligible Shorts engaged views. Eligible content must also comply with YouTube's monetization and advertiser-friendly policies.</p>
<p>Revenue can be excluded for:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Shorts from creators who are not eligible or have not accepted the module</li>
  <li>Views earned before the module was accepted</li>
  <li>Shorts determined to be ineligible for monetization</li>
  <li>Non-original, reused, or otherwise ineligible content</li>
  <li>Artificial or fake viewing activity</li>
  <li>Views affected by certain third-party rights</li>
  <li>Shorts uploaded by music partners under the separate music-partner system</li>
</ul>

<h3>When Does Revenue Sharing Begin?</h3>
<p>Revenue sharing starts from the date the creator accepts the Shorts Monetization Module. YouTube does not retroactively pay Shorts Feed ad revenue for views that occurred before acceptance.</p>
<p>After acceptance, eligible engaged views of Shorts on the channel can be considered for sharing, including eligible older Shorts that continue receiving views.</p>

<h3>Shorts Feed Ads vs. Watch Page Ads</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Shorts Feed Ads</th><th>Watch Page Ads</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Where ads appear</td><td>Between Shorts in the scrolling feed</td><td>Before, during, after, or around long-form and live videos</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Creator calculation</td><td>Pooled revenue allocated by eligible engaged-view share</td><td>Share of net ad revenue associated with qualifying Watch Page content</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Creator share</td><td>45% of allocated Creator Pool revenue</td><td>55% of net qualifying ad revenue</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Music</td><td>Handled through pool and licensing calculations</td><td>Claims and licensing can affect the individual video's revenue</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Required agreement</td><td>Shorts Monetization Module</td><td>Watch Page Monetization Module</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>Shorts Feed Ads vs. YouTube Premium Revenue</h3>
<p>Both revenue streams can apply to eligible Shorts under the Shorts Monetization Module, but they are funded differently:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Shorts Feed ads:</strong> Funded by ads shown between Shorts.</li>
  <li><strong>YouTube Premium:</strong> Funded by Premium membership revenue allocated to Shorts viewing.</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube says creators receive 45% of the net Premium revenue allocated to their Shorts as well. A portion of Premium revenue also supports music licensing.</p>

<h3>How Does Remixed or Third-Party Content Affect Revenue?</h3>
<p>When a Short includes qualifying remixed or third-party content, YouTube may divide the views used for allocation between the uploader and relevant third-party rights holders.</p>
<p>Content ownership and monetization eligibility remain separate from merely having access to a remix tool. A creator can use a permitted YouTube feature while still receiving a different revenue allocation because another party contributed protected material.</p>

<h3>Where Can Creators See Shorts Revenue?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Analytics</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open the <strong>Revenue</strong> tab.</li>
  <li>Review Shorts Feed ad revenue and estimated daily revenue when available.</li>
  <li>Use content-type filters or Advanced Mode for deeper comparison.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube says Shorts RPM is calculated per 1,000 engaged views, reflecting the engaged-view metric used for Shorts revenue sharing.</p>

<h3>Why Is Shorts RPM Different From Long-Form RPM?</h3>
<p>Shorts and long-form videos use different advertising experiences and revenue-sharing systems. Shorts ads appear between videos and fund a pool, while long-form ads can appear around a particular Watch Page playback.</p>
<p>Do not compare the two RPM values as though they represent the same inventory, viewer intent, ad load, video length, or revenue calculation.</p>

<h3>Can You Turn Off Shorts Feed Monetization?</h3>
<p>YouTube says creators who no longer want to monetize Shorts views through the module can contact Creator Support to opt out of the Shorts Monetization Module.</p>
<p>Opting out removes future related ad and Premium earning eligibility. It does not create a per-Short switch that lets the creator selectively collect pooled revenue on only certain eligible Shorts.</p>

<h3>Do Ads Appear Directly on a Creator's Short?</h3>
<p>The core Shorts Feed model places ads between Shorts, not as a pre-roll attached to each individual Short. A creator can earn from the pool even when no ad appears immediately adjacent to their particular Short.</p>
<p>Likewise, seeing an ad after a Short does not mean that exact ad's revenue goes only to the creator whose Short was shown before it.</p>

<h3>Common Shorts-Feed-Ad Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Assuming the ad after a Short belongs to that Short's creator.</li>
  <li>Applying the 45% share to total feed ad revenue before pool allocation.</li>
  <li>Believing music reduces the individual creator's final 45% rate.</li>
  <li>Expecting retroactive revenue for views before module acceptance.</li>
  <li>Using public view count instead of eligible engaged views.</li>
  <li>Comparing Shorts RPM directly with long-form RPM.</li>
  <li>Ignoring geography because pools are allocated by country.</li>
  <li>Assuming every Short in the feed is monetization-eligible.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Where are Shorts Feed ads shown?</h4>
<p>They are shown between Shorts as viewers scroll the Shorts Feed.</p>

<h4>How much of Shorts ad revenue do creators keep?</h4>
<p>YouTube says creators receive 45% of the revenue allocated to them from the Creator Pool.</p>

<h4>Does using music lower my 45% rate?</h4>
<p>No. Music affects the pool calculation, but YouTube says creators receive the same 45% share of their allocation whether or not their Short uses music.</p>

<h4>Can views before accepting the module earn later?</h4>
<p>No. Shorts revenue sharing begins from the date the Shorts Monetization Module is accepted.</p>
