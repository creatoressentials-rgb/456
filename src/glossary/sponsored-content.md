<h2>Sponsored Content</h2>
<p><strong>Sponsored content</strong> is content created, published, or influenced as part of a commercial relationship between a creator and a brand. The creator may receive money, free products, services, travel, commissions, licensing fees, or another material benefit in exchange for featuring, discussing, demonstrating, or promoting the brand.</p>
<p>The term is broad. Sponsored content can be a full <a href="/glossary/sponsored-video/">sponsored video</a>, a short integration inside an otherwise independent video, a product placement, an endorsement, a livestream segment, a Short, a community post, or content produced for the brand’s own advertising channels.</p>
<p>YouTube commonly uses the wider term <strong>paid promotion</strong> for paid product placements, sponsorships, endorsements, and other commercial relationships that may have influenced a video.</p>
<h3>What counts as sponsored content?</h3>
<p>A creator-brand relationship may produce sponsored content when the creator receives:</p>
<ul>
<li>A guaranteed production or publishing fee</li>
<li>Free products or services</li>
<li>Travel, lodging, event access, or tickets</li>
<li>Affiliate commissions or performance bonuses</li>
<li>A licensing or usage-rights fee</li>
<li>A discount or benefit unavailable to the public</li>
<li>Ongoing ambassador compensation</li>
<li>Another financial or business benefit</li>
</ul>
<p>Cash is not required. The important question is whether a connection exists that viewers would reasonably want to know when evaluating the creator’s recommendation or presentation.</p>
<h3>Common forms of sponsored content</h3>
<h4>Sponsored integration</h4>
<p>A paid segment appears inside a longer creator video. The rest of the video may cover an unrelated topic. The <a href="/glossary/deliverables/">deliverables</a> can define the segment length, placement, message, link, disclosure, and review process.</p>
<h4>Dedicated sponsored content</h4>
<p>The entire video, Short, post, or livestream is centered on the brand, product, service, or campaign. Dedicated content gives the sponsor more prominence but does not eliminate the creator’s obligation to be truthful.</p>
<h4>Paid product placement</h4>
<p>The product, message, or brand is integrated directly into the content in exchange for compensation. It may appear visually, be used in a demonstration, or be discussed by the creator.</p>
<h4>Endorsement</h4>
<p>The creator communicates an opinion, belief, finding, or experience that viewers are likely to understand as the creator’s own. The endorsement must reflect the creator’s honest experience and cannot make claims that would be deceptive if made directly by the advertiser.</p>
<h4>Brand-produced creator content</h4>
<p>A creator may make footage that the brand publishes on its own channels or uses in advertising without posting it to the creator’s audience. This is still commercial creator content even though it may not appear on the creator’s channel.</p>
<h4>Affiliate content</h4>
<p>The creator earns a commission from tracked purchases, leads, or other actions. Affiliate content can be sponsored content when the financial relationship may affect how viewers interpret the recommendation.</p>

<h3>Sponsored content vs. branded content</h3>
<p>The phrases are often used interchangeably. <strong>Sponsored content</strong> emphasizes that a commercial relationship funded or influenced the content. <strong>Branded content</strong> can mean creator content featuring a brand, but marketers may also use it for entertainment or editorial-style content produced by a company itself.</p>
<p>Because the terminology is inconsistent, creators should focus on the actual relationship, compensation, control, rights, and disclosure—not only the label in the brief.</p>
<h3>YouTube’s paid-promotion declaration</h3>
<p>When a YouTube video contains a paid product placement, sponsorship, endorsement, or another commercial relationship, YouTube instructs the creator to declare the paid promotion in Studio. YouTube then displays a paid-promotion message at the beginning and may apply other platform handling.</p>
<p>The declaration does not prevent YouTube from showing ordinary ads on the video. YouTube says it may replace an ad that conflicts with the brand partner.</p>
<p>Creators must also follow YouTube’s advertising policies and Community Guidelines. Paid promotion does not make otherwise prohibited content acceptable.</p>
<h3>Viewer-facing disclosure</h3>
<p>Platform settings may not satisfy every legal requirement. In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission says a material connection should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously with the endorsement.</p>
<p>For video, the FTC says the disclosure should be <strong>in the video and not only in the description</strong>. It also notes that viewers are more likely to notice a disclosure made both visually and audibly because some people watch without sound and others may miss on-screen text.</p>
<p>Clear language includes phrases such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>“This video is sponsored by Brand.”</li>
<li>“Brand paid me to make this video.”</li>
<li>“Brand sent me this product for free.”</li>
<li>“I earn a commission if you buy through this link.”</li>
</ul>
<p>Vague terms such as “collab,” “sp,” “thanks,” or an unexplained brand tag may not communicate the relationship clearly.</p>
<p>Disclosure requirements vary by country, product category, platform, and campaign. Creators and brands should identify the rules that apply to them.</p>
<h3>Sponsored content and creative control</h3>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/creative-brief/">creative brief</a> may specify accurate product facts, required talking points, prohibited claims, visual requirements, and a call to action. The creator should still retain enough control to speak in a way that is natural and truthful for the channel.</p>
<p>A brand should not require:</p>
<ul>
<li>An opinion the creator does not hold</li>
<li>A claim the creator cannot support</li>
<li>A statement about an experience the creator did not have</li>
<li>Concealment of the commercial relationship</li>
<li>A misleading comparison or demonstration</li>
</ul>
<p>The agreement should define which parts the brand can approve and how many <a href="/glossary/revision-round/">revision rounds</a> are included.</p>
<h3>Usage rights and amplification</h3>
<p>Posting sponsored content on the creator’s channel does not automatically grant the brand unlimited rights to reuse it. The contract can separately address organic reposting, paid advertising, editing, cutdowns, email, websites, retail displays, television, territory, duration, and use of the creator’s name and likeness.</p>
<p>On YouTube, brand partner access may allow a brand to view certain performance data and promote the creator’s video through eligible advertising tools. The creator should understand and price the separate rights before granting access.</p>
<h3>Common sponsored-content mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Assuming free products do not require disclosure:</strong> Non-cash benefits can still matter to viewers.</li>
<li><strong>Disclosing only in the description:</strong> Video viewers may never open it.</li>
<li><strong>Relying only on the platform label:</strong> Local law may require an additional clear disclosure.</li>
<li><strong>Hiding the disclosure after a long introduction:</strong> Viewers should understand the relationship when they encounter the endorsement.</li>
<li><strong>Making unsupported claims:</strong> The creator remains responsible for truthful communication.</li>
<li><strong>Giving away broad rights unintentionally:</strong> Organic posting and paid advertising are different uses.</li>
<li><strong>Letting revisions remove authenticity:</strong> A sponsor cannot truthfully rewrite the creator’s experience.</li>
<li><strong>Confusing sponsorship with platform ads:</strong> The two systems operate separately.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is sponsored content always paid with money?</h4>
<p>No. Free products, services, travel, commissions, and other benefits may create a material connection even without a cash fee.</p>
<h4>Is all branded content sponsored content?</h4>
<p>Not necessarily. A company can create branded entertainment without hiring an outside creator, and a creator can mention a brand independently. The actual commercial relationship determines whether sponsorship disclosure is needed.</p>
<h4>Does YouTube allow sponsored content?</h4>
<p>Yes, paid product placements and endorsements are allowed when they comply with YouTube policies and applicable law. Creators must declare applicable paid promotions in Studio.</p>
<h4>Does the YouTube paid-promotion label replace a spoken disclosure?</h4>
<p>Not necessarily. The FTC tells creators not to assume a platform tool is sufficient and says video disclosure should be in the video rather than only in the description.</p>
<h4>Can YouTube still place ads on sponsored content?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube says ordinary ads may still run on videos that contain paid promotion.</p>
