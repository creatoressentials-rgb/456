<h2>Subscriber-Only Chat</h2>
<p><strong>Subscriber-only chat</strong> is a YouTube live-chat participant setting that allows only viewers subscribed to the channel to send messages.</p>
<p>The creator can also require viewers to have been subscribed for a selected minimum length of time before they can participate.</p>

<h3>Who Can Watch and Who Can Chat?</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Viewer</th><th>Can Watch a Public Stream?</th><th>Can Send Messages?</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Subscriber who meets the time requirement</td><td>Yes</td><td>Yes</td></tr>
    <tr><td>New subscriber who has not waited long enough</td><td>Yes</td><td>No, until the requirement is met</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Non-subscriber</td><td>Yes</td><td>No</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Channel member</td><td>Yes</td><td>Generally eligible as a subscriber, subject to the displayed participant settings</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>The setting controls chat participation. It does not make the stream itself subscriber-only.</p>

<h3>How to Enable Subscriber-Only Chat</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Go to Live Control Room.</li>
  <li>Select or create the live stream.</li>
  <li>Click <strong>Edit</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open <strong>Live chat</strong>.</li>
  <li>Under who can send a message, select <strong>Subscribers</strong>.</li>
  <li>Optionally choose how long viewers must be subscribed.</li>
  <li>Save the setting.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube says the setting can be changed before or during a live stream or Premiere.</p>

<h3>How to Enable It for a Premiere</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Content</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open the scheduled Premiere.</li>
  <li>Expand the additional details.</li>
  <li>Open the Live chat settings.</li>
  <li>Under Participants mode, select <strong>Subscribers</strong>.</li>
  <li>Save the Premiere.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Minimum Subscription Time</h3>
<p>The creator can choose a waiting requirement so a viewer cannot subscribe and immediately send messages.</p>
<p>This can help reduce:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Drive-by spam</li>
  <li>Harassment from newly created accounts</li>
  <li>Raid behavior</li>
  <li>Repeated unsubscribe-and-resubscribe abuse</li>
  <li>Low-context participation during sensitive streams</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube displays the required subscription time to viewers. The exact selectable options can change, so use the choices shown in the current Live Control Room instead of relying on an old tutorial.</p>

<h3>What Happens to Subscription Privacy?</h3>
<p>YouTube says that when a viewer participates in subscriber-only live chat, other viewers can publicly see that the person is subscribed to the channel.</p>
<p>This can apply even when the viewer normally keeps channel subscriptions private. Participating in the restricted chat publicly confirms the relationship for that interaction.</p>

<h3>Subscriber-Only vs. Members-Only Chat</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Subscriber-Only Chat</th><th><a href="/glossary/members-only-chat/">Members-Only Chat</a></th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Payment required</td><td>No</td><td>Yes, through an active channel membership or qualifying gift</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Minimum relationship</td><td>Subscription, optionally for a required period</td><td>Active membership</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Public video can remain viewable</td><td>Yes</td><td>Yes</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Can both run together?</td><td>No</td><td>No</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>YouTube says members-only chat and subscriber-only chat cannot be enabled at the same time.</p>

<h3>Subscriber-Only Chat vs. Slow Mode</h3>
<p><strong>Slow Mode</strong> limits how frequently eligible participants can send messages. Subscriber-only chat controls which viewers can participate.</p>
<p>YouTube allows the two settings to run at the same time. A viewer may need to:</p>
<ol>
  <li>Be subscribed for the required period, and</li>
  <li>Wait for the configured message delay between posts.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Subscriber-Only vs. Live Commentary Mode</h3>
<p>Live Commentary Mode allows only creator-approved users to post. Subscriber-only chat allows the broader group of qualifying subscribers.</p>
<p>Use Commentary Mode for a controlled panel or official commentary. Use subscriber-only mode when the goal is broader audience participation with a basic relationship requirement.</p>

<h3>Does Subscribing Cost Money?</h3>
<p>No. A YouTube channel subscription is free. It is different from a paid <a href="/glossary/channel-memberships/">Channel Membership</a>.</p>
<p>Creators should not describe subscriber-only chat as paid access.</p>

<h3>Why Use Subscriber-Only Chat?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Reduce spam while keeping the stream public</li>
  <li>Encourage viewers to subscribe</li>
  <li>Build a more familiar chat community</li>
  <li>Slow down sudden raids</li>
  <li>Give regular viewers more participation space</li>
  <li>Require a small commitment without charging viewers</li>
</ul>

<h3>Potential Downsides</h3>
<ul>
  <li>New viewers cannot ask immediate questions.</li>
  <li>Some viewers may subscribe only to chat and leave later.</li>
  <li>Participation can fall sharply on small channels.</li>
  <li>Viewers may misunderstand the subscription-time requirement.</li>
  <li>The setting can make an urgent support stream less accessible.</li>
  <li>Subscription privacy becomes public for participants.</li>
</ul>

<h3>When to Use a Waiting Period</h3>
<p>A longer subscription requirement can be useful during:</p>
<ul>
  <li>High-profile events</li>
  <li>Streams experiencing spam attacks</li>
  <li>Controversial discussions</li>
  <li>Large collaborations</li>
  <li>Events with valuable giveaways governed by separate rules</li>
</ul>
<p>A shorter or zero waiting requirement is more welcoming for product demonstrations, educational Q&amp;As, and new-audience streams.</p>

<h3>Can a Viewer Unsubscribe After Chatting?</h3>
<p>Yes. YouTube subscriptions are voluntary. Subscriber-only chat does not create a permanent commitment.</p>
<p>The setting can encourage subscriptions, but creators should evaluate whether those viewers return and watch rather than judging success only by temporary subscriber gains.</p>

<h3>Does Subscriber-Only Chat Stop Spam?</h3>
<p>It can reduce low-effort spam, but it does not eliminate abuse. A determined viewer can subscribe and wait.</p>
<p>Use it with:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Moderators</li>
  <li>Blocked words</li>
  <li>Held potentially inappropriate messages</li>
  <li>User timeouts</li>
  <li>Hidden users</li>
  <li>Slow Mode</li>
  <li>Clear chat guidelines</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to Explain the Setting</h3>
<p>Use clear language such as:</p>
<ul>
  <li>"Everyone can watch; subscribers can chat."</li>
  <li>"Subscribe for ten minutes before posting so we can reduce spam."</li>
  <li>"Subscriptions are free—channel membership is not required."</li>
</ul>
<p>The actual required time should match the current setting displayed to viewers.</p>

<h3>Subscriber-Only Chat and Live Chat Replay</h3>
<p>If live chat replay remains enabled, messages from qualifying subscribers can replay alongside the archived stream.</p>
<p>Participants should understand that chat messages can remain publicly associated with their channels after the broadcast.</p>

<h3>Does It Affect Recommendations?</h3>
<p>YouTube does not describe subscriber-only chat as a direct recommendation-ranking control. It is a moderation and participation setting.</p>
<p>It can affect the community experience, but enabling it does not guarantee more reach, retention, or subscribers.</p>

<h3>How to Evaluate the Setting</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Compare total chat participants.</li>
  <li>Review spam and moderation workload.</li>
  <li>Track subscriber gains and later cancellations.</li>
  <li>Compare average concurrent viewers.</li>
  <li>Review watch time and audience feedback.</li>
  <li>Test different requirements on comparable streams.</li>
  <li>Keep the restriction only when it improves the experience.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Common Subscriber-Only-Chat Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Calling it a paid membership feature.</li>
  <li>Assuming non-subscribers cannot watch.</li>
  <li>Running it with members-only chat.</li>
  <li>Confusing the subscription requirement with Slow Mode.</li>
  <li>Forgetting that participation reveals subscription status.</li>
  <li>Using a long waiting period for every stream.</li>
  <li>Assuming it eliminates moderation needs.</li>
  <li>Judging success only by short-term subscriber growth.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Does subscriber-only chat require payment?</h4>
<p>No. Channel subscriptions are free.</p>

<h4>Can non-subscribers still watch?</h4>
<p>Yes. The setting restricts chat participation rather than public video access.</p>

<h4>Can it run with Slow Mode?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube allows subscriber-only chat and Slow Mode at the same time.</p>

<h4>Can it run with members-only chat?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube says the two participant modes cannot be enabled together.</p>
