<h2>Series Playlist</h2>
<p>A <strong>YouTube series playlist</strong> is a playlist marked as an official set of creator-owned videos that should be viewed together.</p>
<p>YouTube says it may use this information to modify how videos in the series are presented or discovered and to feature or recommend other videos from the playlist while someone watches a series video.</p>

<h3>Series Playlist vs. Ordinary Playlist</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Official Series Playlist</th><th>Ordinary Playlist</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Marked as an official set intended to be viewed together</td><td>General collection of videos</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Requires a verified account</td><td>Anybody can create playlists</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Contains only videos uploaded by the creator and owned by them</td><td>Can collect eligible videos from many channels</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Each video can belong to only one official series playlist</td><td>A video can appear in many ordinary playlists</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>Series Playlist vs. Video Series</h3>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/video-series/">Video Series</a> is the creative relationship among episodes. A series playlist is a technical playlist setting.</p>
<p>A creator can have:</p>
<ul>
  <li>A creative series without an official series playlist</li>
  <li>An ordinary playlist containing a series</li>
  <li>An official series playlist for eligible creator-owned episodes</li>
  <li>A topic playlist that is not a creative series</li>
</ul>

<h3>What YouTube Says the Setting Does</h3>
<p>YouTube's current Help documentation says adding videos to a series playlist allows other videos in the playlist to be featured and recommended when someone is viewing a video in the series.</p>
<p>YouTube may use the information to change presentation or discovery. The wording describes a possible platform use—not a guarantee that the next episode will always be recommended.</p>

<h3>Official Requirements</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The account must be verified.</li>
  <li>A video cannot appear in more than one official series playlist.</li>
  <li>Only videos uploaded by the creator can be included.</li>
  <li>The creator must have the rights to those videos.</li>
  <li>The setting is enabled through playlist settings on a computer.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Verification Requirement</h3>
<p>A verified account is required to enable the official series setting. This refers to YouTube's account feature-verification process, not the public verification badge shown for some notable channels.</p>
<p>Creators should review the current feature-eligibility controls in Studio if the option is missing.</p>

<h3>One Official Series Per Video</h3>
<p>A video cannot be assigned to more than one official series playlist.</p>
<p>This requires creators to choose the most meaningful sequence when a video fits several topics. It can still appear in other ordinary playlists for organization.</p>

<h3>Creator-Owned Videos Only</h3>
<p>An official series playlist can include only videos uploaded by the creator and videos for which the creator has the necessary rights.</p>
<p>A playlist containing collaborations hosted on another channel, curated third-party videos, or viewer submissions may still work as an ordinary playlist but cannot satisfy the official-series ownership rule.</p>

<h3>How to Set an Official Series Playlist</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Create or open the playlist on a computer.</li>
  <li>Open the playlist settings.</li>
  <li>Turn on <strong>Set as official series for this playlist</strong>.</li>
  <li>Confirm that every video was uploaded by the channel and is rights-compliant.</li>
  <li>Review the episode order.</li>
  <li>Save the settings.</li>
</ol>
<p>The exact interface can change, but YouTube's current Help documentation requires the computer playlist-settings workflow.</p>

<h3>How to Create an Ordinary Playlist First</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open the watch page of a video or Short.</li>
  <li>Select More and then Save.</li>
  <li>Select Create new playlist.</li>
  <li>Enter a playlist name.</li>
  <li>Choose the privacy setting.</li>
  <li>Create the playlist.</li>
  <li>Add the remaining eligible videos.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Playlist Privacy</h3>
<p>Ordinary playlists can be Public, Unlisted, or Private under current controls. For discovery and viewer navigation, the playlist must be accessible to the intended audience.</p>
<p>A private playlist cannot function as a public series destination.</p>

<h3>Does It Guarantee Autoplay?</h3>
<p>No. Playlist playback behavior can depend on how the viewer enters the playlist and on current product behavior. The official-series setting does not guarantee that every viewer will automatically continue through every episode.</p>

<h3>Does It Guarantee Recommendations?</h3>
<p>No. YouTube says it may use the series information to modify presentation or discovery. Viewer relevance, satisfaction, watch behavior, packaging, and other recommendation systems still matter.</p>

<h3>Does Order Matter?</h3>
<p>Order matters when the episodes build on one another. Arrange videos according to the viewer's intended path:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Episode order</li>
  <li>Beginner to advanced</li>
  <li>Chronological story</li>
  <li>Setup to implementation</li>
  <li>Problem to solution</li>
</ul>
<p>For independent recurring episodes, use the order that best serves current viewers, such as newest first or a curated starting order.</p>

<h3>Series Playlist for Courses</h3>
<p>A course playlist can organize prerequisites and lessons:</p>
<ol>
  <li>Introduction and expected outcome</li>
  <li>Required setup</li>
  <li>Foundational concepts</li>
  <li>Core lessons</li>
  <li>Advanced application</li>
  <li>Final project or next steps</li>
</ol>

<h3>Series Playlist for Story Content</h3>
<p>Use chronological order and clear episode naming. Avoid beginning every title with only "Episode 7" when a new viewer also needs to understand the episode's specific promise.</p>

<h3>Series Playlist for Recurring Shows</h3>
<p>Independent episodes can be ordered newest first when current topics matter most, or curated around a recommended starting point.</p>
<p>The series setting signals that the videos belong together even when viewers do not need to start with Episode 1.</p>

<h3>Series Playlist for Shorts</h3>
<p>YouTube lets creators save Shorts to playlists, but some playlist-reordering controls differ for playlists containing only Shorts. Review the current desktop behavior before relying on a strict sequence.</p>
<p>Shorts Feed viewing is also distinct from playlist viewing, so a playlist does not guarantee that Short viewers experience the episodes in order.</p>

<h3>Playlist Title</h3>
<p>Use a clear title that explains the viewer outcome.</p>
<p>Weak:</p>
<blockquote>My Videos Part 1</blockquote>
<p>Stronger:</p>
<blockquote>Beginner YouTube Audio Setup: Complete Series</blockquote>

<h3>Playlist Description</h3>
<p>Explain:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Who the series is for</li>
  <li>What viewers will learn or experience</li>
  <li>Whether order matters</li>
  <li>What equipment or prerequisites are needed</li>
  <li>Whether the series is complete or ongoing</li>
</ul>

<h3>Episode Titles</h3>
<p>Each title should communicate its own value while preserving series identity.</p>
<p>Example:</p>
<blockquote>How to Position a Microphone for Clear Speech | Home Studio Series 2</blockquote>
<p>This works better for discovery than a title containing only the series name and episode number.</p>

<h3>Playlist Thumbnail</h3>
<p>YouTube supports playlist thumbnails under current playlist controls. Use an image that represents the full series rather than one narrow episode.</p>
<p>Keep visual consistency with the episode packaging without making every thumbnail indistinguishable.</p>

<h3>Series Branding</h3>
<p>Useful recurring signals include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Series name</li>
  <li>Consistent host or format</li>
  <li>Recognizable thumbnail element</li>
  <li>Episode numbers when order matters</li>
  <li>Recurring intro or graphic</li>
  <li>Clear playlist navigation</li>
</ul>

<h3>Link Episodes Together</h3>
<p>Use:</p>
<ul>
  <li>End screens</li>
  <li>Cards</li>
  <li>Descriptions</li>
  <li>Pinned comments</li>
  <li>Channel Home-tab sections</li>
  <li>Verbal next-episode bridges</li>
</ul>
<p>The official series setting should support clear human navigation rather than replace it.</p>

<h3>Use End Screens</h3>
<p>At the end of an episode, recommend the next logical video or the series playlist. Use <a href="/glossary/end-screens-traffic/">End Screens Traffic</a> to evaluate these handoffs.</p>

<h3>Use a YouTube Outro</h3>
<p>The closing sequence can explain why the next episode matters while the playlist or video element appears. Do not end with a generic request that gives no reason to continue.</p>

<h3>Add the Playlist to the Home Tab</h3>
<p>YouTube's Home-tab customization supports single, created, and multiple-playlist sections. Add an important series where channel visitors can find it.</p>
<p>Use <a href="/glossary/channel-pages-traffic/">Channel Pages Traffic</a> and playlist analytics to evaluate discovery.</p>

<h3>Series Playlist and Playlist Traffic</h3>
<p><a href="/glossary/playlist-traffic/">Playlist Traffic</a> identifies views that occur from playlist contexts. It helps measure playlist entry but does not prove the official-series setting caused every view.</p>

<h3>Playlist Analytics</h3>
<p>YouTube Analytics can provide playlist data when the playlist is Public and received views in the selected period.</p>
<p>Review:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Playlist starts</li>
  <li>Views from playlist contexts</li>
  <li>Watch time</li>
  <li>Average view duration</li>
  <li>Episode drop-off</li>
  <li>End-screen and card traffic</li>
  <li>Views outside the playlist</li>
</ul>

<h3>Video Ownership in Analytics</h3>
<p>YouTube's engagement Help documentation says playlist reports can show data regardless of video ownership for ordinary playlists. That broader Analytics behavior does not change the official series playlist requirement that included videos be uploaded and owned by the creator.</p>

<h3>Deleting a Playlist</h3>
<p>YouTube says deleting a playlist removes its URL and title from visibility and playlist Analytics attribution, although associated watch time remains in broader reports and the old playlist may remain in viewer watch histories.</p>
<p>Do not delete an established series without considering broken links, embeds, and navigation.</p>

<h3>Changing the Official Series</h3>
<p>If a video needs to move into another official series, remove it from the current official series first. Review end screens, descriptions, and channel sections after reorganizing.</p>

<h3>Rights and Policy</h3>
<p>The creator must own the rights to the included videos. Playlists themselves are also subject to YouTube policy.</p>
<p>A playlist can violate policy when its collection, title, description, or context promotes prohibited content even if individual videos are hosted elsewhere.</p>

<h3>When Not to Use the Official Setting</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The playlist is a broad topic collection rather than one intended series.</li>
  <li>It contains videos from other channels.</li>
  <li>The same video needs to serve another more important official series.</li>
  <li>The creator does not own all included rights.</li>
  <li>The intended order is unclear or constantly changing.</li>
</ul>

<h3>When the Official Setting Is Useful</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Numbered courses</li>
  <li>Multi-part documentaries</li>
  <li>Story seasons</li>
  <li>Build or challenge progressions</li>
  <li>Recurring creator-owned programs</li>
  <li>Ordered tutorials</li>
</ul>

<h3>Do Not Overstate SEO Benefits</h3>
<p>An official series playlist gives YouTube additional information about how the videos relate. It does not create a guaranteed Search ranking, recommendation, autoplay sequence, or subscriber increase.</p>

<h3>Series Maintenance</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Review episode order.</li>
  <li>Update the title and description.</li>
  <li>Replace outdated prerequisites.</li>
  <li>Check visibility and rights.</li>
  <li>Repair end screens and links.</li>
  <li>Add new episodes.</li>
  <li>Remove videos that no longer fit.</li>
  <li>Review playlist Analytics.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Common Series-Playlist Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Confusing a creative series with the official setting.</li>
  <li>Assuming every playlist should be official.</li>
  <li>Putting one video in two official series playlists.</li>
  <li>Adding videos from other channels.</li>
  <li>Assuming the setting guarantees recommendations or autoplay.</li>
  <li>Ignoring episode order.</li>
  <li>Using titles that communicate only episode numbers.</li>
  <li>Relying on the setting without end screens and clear navigation.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>What makes a playlist an official series?</h4>
<p>The creator turns on "Set as official series for this playlist" in playlist settings while meeting YouTube's verification, ownership, and video-assignment requirements.</p>

<h4>Can one video be in two series playlists?</h4>
<p>It can appear in multiple ordinary playlists, but YouTube says it cannot appear in more than one official series playlist.</p>

<h4>Can I include another creator's video?</h4>
<p>Not in an official series playlist. YouTube requires videos uploaded by the creator and owned by them.</p>

<h4>Does the setting guarantee recommendations?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube says it may use the information to change presentation or discovery.</p>
