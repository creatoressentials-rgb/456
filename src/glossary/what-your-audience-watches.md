<h2>What Your Audience Watches</h2>
<p><strong>What your audience watches</strong> is a YouTube Analytics report showing other videos, Shorts, live streams, and podcasts that your viewers watched outside your channel during the past seven days.</p>
<p>The report helps creators identify audience interests, topic patterns, packaging styles, collaboration opportunities, and adjacent content ideas.</p>

<h3>What Does the Report Include?</h3>
<p>YouTube currently describes the report as covering:</p>
<ul>
<li>Videos</li>
<li>Shorts</li>
<li>Live streams</li>
<li>Podcasts</li>
<li>Content watched outside the creator's own channel</li>
<li>Viewing activity from the past seven days</li>
</ul>
<p>For Official Artist Channels, YouTube says the report does not show videos where the channel owner is the primary artist, even when that video exists outside the Official Artist Channel.</p>

<h3>Where Can You Find the Report?</h3>
<ol>
<li>Sign in to YouTube Studio.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Analytics</strong>.</li>
<li>Open the <strong>Audience</strong> tab.</li>
<li>Find the <strong>What your audience watches</strong> card.</li>
</ol>
<p>The report can be limited or unavailable when the channel lacks sufficient relevant audience data. YouTube does not publish the exact privacy threshold.</p>

<h3>What Time Period Does It Use?</h3>
<p>The report covers the past seven days. This is shorter than several neighboring Audience reports:</p>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Audience Report</th><th>Current Time Window</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>What your audience watches</td><td>Past seven days</td></tr>
<tr><td>Channels your audience watches</td><td>Past 28 days</td></tr>
<tr><td>When your viewers are on YouTube</td><td>Last 28 days</td></tr>
<tr><td>Formats your viewers watch</td><td>Last 28 days</td></tr>
<tr><td><a href="/glossary/monthly-audience/">Monthly audience</a></td><td>Rolling previous 28 days</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Because the content report is recent, it can change quickly around news, trends, holidays, releases, and viral events.</p>

<h3>What Can Creators Learn From It?</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Topic patterns:</strong> Subjects viewers repeatedly choose outside the channel</li>
<li><strong>Title patterns:</strong> Questions, promises, formats, and wording that attract the audience</li>
<li><strong>Thumbnail patterns:</strong> Visual styles, subjects, and levels of complexity that receive attention</li>
<li><strong>Format preferences:</strong> Interest in interviews, explainers, reactions, tutorials, documentaries, Shorts, or live content</li>
<li><strong>Adjacent interests:</strong> Topics that overlap with the channel without being direct copies</li>
<li><strong>Collaboration opportunities:</strong> Creators and channels serving a related audience</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to Generate Video Ideas From the Report</h3>
<ol>
<li>Record recurring topics rather than reacting to one isolated video.</li>
<li>Identify the audience need behind the successful content.</li>
<li>Compare that need with the channel's expertise and promise.</li>
<li>Find an original angle, example, format, or audience segment.</li>
<li>Review whether the idea belongs on the channel or only interests a small overlap.</li>
<li>Test the idea through a video, Short, post, or live discussion.</li>
<li>Measure whether the channel's own viewers respond.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Use Patterns, Not Copies</h3>
<p>YouTube recommends examining themes in titles and thumbnails and considering topics based on what viewers watch. That does not mean creators should copy another video's wording, thumbnail, script, footage, or unique creative expression.</p>
<p>A useful analysis asks:</p>
<ul>
<li>What viewer problem is this solving?</li>
<li>What curiosity or emotion is the packaging creating?</li>
<li>What format is the audience choosing?</li>
<li>What can my channel contribute that is genuinely different?</li>
<li>Does the idea fit my existing audience promise?</li>
</ul>

<h3>What Your Audience Watches vs. Channels Your Audience Watches</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Report</th><th>Focus</th><th>Best Use</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>What your audience watches</td><td>Specific outside videos, Shorts, live streams, and podcasts from seven days</td><td>Recent topics, titles, thumbnails, and content ideas</td></tr>
<tr><td>Channels your audience watches</td><td>Other channels consistently watched over 28 days</td><td>Broader audience interests and collaboration research</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>A popular individual video may reflect a short-lived event, while repeated channel overlap may signal a more durable audience relationship.</p>

<h3>What Your Audience Watches vs. Trends Tab</h3>
<p>The Audience report is based on the behavior of people who watch your channel. YouTube's Trends tab focuses on what your audience and viewers across YouTube are searching for and can surface content gaps.</p>
<p>Use the Audience report for demonstrated viewing overlap and the Trends tab for search demand and idea exploration.</p>

<h3>Can You Use It for Thumbnail Ideas?</h3>
<p>Yes. YouTube explicitly says the report can help with thumbnail and title ideas. Look for structural patterns such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>One clear subject versus many small elements</li>
<li>Before-and-after presentation</li>
<li>Faces, products, charts, or results</li>
<li>Question-based versus outcome-based titles</li>
<li>Series naming and recurring visual systems</li>
</ul>
<p>Adapt the communication principle to your own video rather than imitating another creator's protected design or making a promise your content does not deliver.</p>

<h3>Can the Report Identify Competitors?</h3>
<p>It can reveal content and channels competing for the same viewers' attention, but "competitor" may be too narrow. The audience may watch complementary creators, entertainment, news, and unrelated formats.</p>
<p>Potential uses include collaboration, guest research, affiliate opportunities, format inspiration, and identifying topics your audience understands already.</p>

<h3>Why Might the Report Be Missing?</h3>
<p>YouTube may limit audience data when:</p>
<ul>
<li>The channel does not have enough relevant data.</li>
<li>The audience is watching Made for Kids content.</li>
<li>Privacy thresholds are not met.</li>
<li>Content or account conditions limit reporting.</li>
<li>The report differs between desktop and mobile platforms.</li>
</ul>
<p>The exact thresholds are not published and can change.</p>

<h3>Common Audience-Watches Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Assuming the report shows only videos from direct competitors.</li>
<li>Calling it a 28-day report when YouTube currently says seven days.</li>
<li>Copying thumbnails, titles, or video concepts instead of identifying patterns.</li>
<li>Building the channel around one temporary viral interest.</li>
<li>Assuming every listed video was watched by most of the audience.</li>
<li>Ignoring Shorts, live streams, and podcasts included in the report.</li>
<li>Confusing the report with search demand or the Trends tab.</li>
<li>Assuming missing data means the audience watches nothing else.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Does the report show content watched on my channel?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube defines it as content your viewers watched outside your channel.</p>
<h4>How far back does the report go?</h4>
<p>YouTube currently says it covers the past seven days.</p>
<h4>Does it include Shorts and live streams?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube says it can include videos, Shorts, live streams, and podcasts.</p>
<h4>Should I make videos about everything listed?</h4>
<p>No. Look for repeated interests that fit your expertise, audience promise, and production goals.</p>
