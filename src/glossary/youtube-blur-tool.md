<h2>YouTube Blur Tool</h2>
<p>The <strong>YouTube Blur Tool</strong> is a feature in YouTube Studio Editor that lets creators obscure faces or selected areas of an already uploaded video.</p>
<p>It is useful for hiding identities, license plates, addresses, computer screens, personal documents, children, bystanders, confidential information, and other sensitive details without replacing the entire upload.</p>

<h3>Where Is the Blur Tool?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Sign in to YouTube Studio on a computer.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Content</strong>.</li>
  <li>Choose the video.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Editor</strong>.</li>
  <li>Under Video editor, select <strong>Blur</strong>.</li>
  <li>Choose <strong>Face blur</strong> or <strong>Custom blur</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube's current Blur tool is a desktop Studio feature. Mobile video-management options do not provide the complete desktop blur workflow.</p>

<h3>Face Blur vs. Custom Blur</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Mode</th><th>How It Works</th><th>Useful For</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Face blur</td><td>YouTube processes the video, identifies detected faces, and lets the creator choose which faces to blur</td><td>People, bystanders, minors, interview participants, and crowds</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Custom blur</td><td>The creator draws and positions a blur box over a selected area</td><td>License plates, screens, documents, addresses, logos, and objects</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>Face detection can save time, but the creator remains responsible for reviewing the entire result. A face may be missed, confused with another face, obscured, or visible only briefly.</p>

<h3>How to Add Face Blur</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Select <strong>Blur</strong> and then <strong>Face blur</strong>.</li>
  <li>Wait while YouTube processes the video and detects faces.</li>
  <li>Select the faces that should be blurred.</li>
  <li>Apply the selections.</li>
  <li>Drag or adjust blur regions as needed.</li>
  <li>Preview the complete section.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Save</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>Review every appearance of the person. Detection may behave differently as the face turns, leaves the frame, becomes covered, or changes size.</p>

<h3>How to Add Custom Blur</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Select <strong>Blur</strong> and then <strong>Custom blur</strong>.</li>
  <li>Draw or resize the blur box over the sensitive area.</li>
  <li>Choose a rectangle or oval shape.</li>
  <li>Set the start and end time on the timeline.</li>
  <li>Select tracking or fixed-position behavior.</li>
  <li>Add additional blur boxes when several areas need coverage.</li>
  <li>Preview the sequence frame by frame where possible.</li>
  <li>Save the edit.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Track Object vs. Fix Blur Position</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Setting</th><th>Behavior</th><th>Best Use</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Track object</td><td>The blur attempts to follow a moving subject or object</td><td>Walking people, moving vehicles, handheld documents, and shifting screens</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Fix blur position</td><td>The blur stays in the same place within the video frame</td><td>Static overlays, fixed monitors, logos, addresses, or a stationary portion of the image</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>Automatic tracking is not guaranteed to stay perfectly attached. Fast motion, occlusion, camera cuts, similar nearby objects, and changes in lighting can make the box drift.</p>

<h3>Blur Shape, Size, and Timing</h3>
<p>YouTube currently allows creators to:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Choose a rectangular or oval blur shape</li>
  <li>Move the blur box</li>
  <li>Resize the blur area</li>
  <li>Set when the blur starts and ends</li>
  <li>Track a moving object</li>
  <li>Fix the blur in one frame position</li>
  <li>Add several blur regions</li>
</ul>
<p>Make the region larger than the sensitive detail when tracking is uncertain. A blur that reveals the edge of a license plate or face for several frames may fail its privacy purpose.</p>

<h3>What Should Creators Blur?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Faces of people who did not consent to publication</li>
  <li>Children when identity or safety is a concern</li>
  <li>Street addresses and house numbers</li>
  <li>Vehicle license plates</li>
  <li>Phone numbers and email addresses</li>
  <li>Financial records and tax documents</li>
  <li>Passwords, recovery codes, and account identifiers</li>
  <li>Medical or employment information</li>
  <li>Client data appearing in a <a href="/glossary/screen-recording/">screen recording</a></li>
  <li>Copyrighted or trademarked content when obscuring it is actually appropriate</li>
</ul>
<p>Blurring does not automatically make an otherwise unlawful or policy-violating video acceptable. It is a visual privacy and correction tool, not legal permission.</p>

<h3>Can Blur Remove Information Completely?</h3>
<p>A visual blur obscures the selected image area, but it does not remove related audio, captions, descriptions, metadata, reflections, or spoken information.</p>
<p>Before saving, check whether the sensitive detail also appears in:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Another camera angle</li>
  <li>A mirror or reflective surface</li>
  <li>The thumbnail</li>
  <li>Captions or transcript</li>
  <li>The title or description</li>
  <li>Spoken dialogue</li>
  <li>Other uploaded versions or clips</li>
</ul>

<h3>Are Saved Blur Changes Reversible?</h3>
<p>No through the former Studio restore workflow. As of June 2025, YouTube says saved Editor changes cannot be undone using <strong>Revert to original</strong>.</p>
<p>Keep the original master and consider downloading the uploaded video before saving. If the blur covers the wrong person or hides too much of the frame, Studio cannot restore the unblurred original through that feature.</p>

<h3>View-Count Editing Restrictions</h3>
<p>YouTube says an unedited video with more than 100,000 views may have restrictions on saving Studio Editor changes when the channel is not in the YouTube Partner Program.</p>
<p>Face blur is specifically identified as an exception that may remain available. The restriction does not apply to YPP channels under the current Help guidance.</p>

<h3>How Long Does Blur Processing Take?</h3>
<p>Face detection and saved edits require processing. Timing depends on video length, resolution, motion, number of faces, number of blur regions, and platform demand.</p>
<p>You may see a Video editing is in progress status. Avoid beginning another Studio edit until processing finishes, and review the public result afterward.</p>

<h3>Blur Tool vs. Editing Before Upload</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Blur in YouTube Studio</th><th>Blur in a Full Video Editor</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Preserves the existing uploaded video and URL</td><td>Provides greater control over masks, feathering, keyframes, and tracking</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Useful for urgent published-video corrections</td><td>Best when privacy needs are known before publication</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Offers face detection and simple custom regions</td><td>Can handle complex occlusion, multiple layers, and manual frame corrections</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Saved changes are not revertible through Studio</td><td>The original project and master can remain fully reversible</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>When privacy is critical, blur before upload in a full editing application and then verify the final export. Use YouTube's tool as an additional correction option, not the only privacy control.</p>

<h3>Blur and Remixed Shorts</h3>
<p>YouTube says blurring or trimming an original video in Studio can affect Shorts created by remixing that content. Video remixes may be deleted, while audio remixes may be muted, made unlisted, or scheduled for deletion.</p>
<p>Consider the impact on derivative content before editing a widely remixed source video.</p>

<h3>How to Review a Blur Edit</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Preview several seconds before the blur begins.</li>
  <li>Watch the full blurred interval at normal speed.</li>
  <li>Scrub through fast movement and camera cuts.</li>
  <li>Check the first and final frames of the blur.</li>
  <li>Confirm that the region does not drift away.</li>
  <li>Look for reflections and secondary appearances.</li>
  <li>Check that the blur does not hide necessary instructional information.</li>
  <li>Review the processed public version on a large screen.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Common Blur-Tool Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Assuming face detection finds every face.</li>
  <li>Using a blur box that is too small for movement.</li>
  <li>Choosing tracking when a fixed blur would be more reliable.</li>
  <li>Forgetting the sensitive detail appears in reflections or another scene.</li>
  <li>Saving without keeping an original copy.</li>
  <li>Believing blur also removes audio or transcript information.</li>
  <li>Failing to inspect the first and last frames of the effect.</li>
  <li>Assuming blur creates legal permission to publish private or copyrighted content.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Can YouTube automatically blur faces?</h4>
<p>YouTube can detect faces and let the creator choose which detected faces to blur. The creator should still review the entire result.</p>

<h4>Can the blur follow a moving object?</h4>
<p>Yes. Custom blur includes a Track object option, but tracking can drift and should be checked carefully.</p>

<h4>Can I remove a saved blur later?</h4>
<p>YouTube says saved Studio Editor changes cannot be undone through Revert to original as of June 2025.</p>

<h4>Can I blur several areas?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube allows multiple custom blur boxes with separate timing and placement.</p>
