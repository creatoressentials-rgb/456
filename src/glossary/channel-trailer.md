<h2>Channel Trailer</h2>
<p>A <strong>YouTube channel trailer</strong> is a video selected for people who have not subscribed when they visit the channel's Home tab. Its purpose is to preview the channel, explain what viewers can expect, and give the right visitors a reason to subscribe or watch more.</p>
<p>The trailer is a channel-layout feature. It is not the same as a video trailer, Premiere trailer, advertisement, or featured video for returning subscribers.</p>

<h3>Who Sees the Channel Trailer?</h3>
<p>YouTube's current Help documentation describes the channel trailer as a preview for people who have not subscribed.</p>
<p>The trailer belongs to the channel Home tab experience. It is not guaranteed to appear when someone lands directly on a video, playlist, Shorts page, or another channel tab.</p>

<h3>What Happens After a Viewer Watches It?</h3>
<p>YouTube says that once a viewer watches the channel trailer, it will not be shown to that viewer again.</p>
<p>This makes the trailer an introduction rather than a permanent autoplay advertisement shown on every channel visit.</p>

<h3>Channel Trailer vs. Featured Video</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Channel Trailer</th><th>Featured Video</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Designed for people who have not subscribed</td><td>Designed for returning subscribers</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Introduces the channel promise</td><td>Highlights a useful current or priority video</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Selected under the Channel trailer section</td><td>Selected under the Spotlight section</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Stops appearing after that viewer watches it</td><td>Also stops appearing after that viewer watches it</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>Channel Trailer vs. Featured Section</h3>
<p>A featured section is a row on the Home tab that can highlight videos, Shorts, live streams, playlists, memberships, channels, posts, or other supported content.</p>
<p>The channel trailer occupies a dedicated introductory position for non-subscribers rather than appearing as one row among the broader channel sections.</p>

<h3>Channel Trailer vs. Premiere Trailer</h3>
<p>A Premiere trailer previews one scheduled Premiere. A channel trailer previews the overall channel for non-subscribers.</p>
<p>They use different controls and serve different viewer decisions.</p>

<h3>Channel Trailer vs. Advertisement</h3>
<p>The channel trailer is selected by the creator for the channel Home tab. It is not an ad campaign placement and does not purchase reach.</p>
<p>YouTube says ads do not show on a channel trailer by default unless the selected video contains third-party claimed content.</p>

<h3>How to Add a Channel Trailer</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Sign in to YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Customization</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open the <strong>Home tab</strong>.</li>
  <li>Make sure the Home tab is enabled.</li>
  <li>Under Layout, select <strong>Add section</strong>.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Channel trailer</strong>.</li>
  <li>Choose the video.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Publish</strong>.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Home Tab Requirement</h3>
<p>The trailer appears through the channel Home tab layout. If the Home tab is turned off, the creator is not presenting the trailer through that layout.</p>
<p>YouTube currently lets creators manage the Home tab through desktop Studio and supported Studio-app controls.</p>

<h3>Can Any Video Be a Channel Trailer?</h3>
<p>The creator selects a video from YouTube. A purpose-built trailer can be effective, but an existing video can work when it clearly communicates the channel's value.</p>
<p>Do not select a video merely because it has the most views. It should help a new visitor decide whether the wider channel is relevant.</p>

<h3>Purpose-Built Trailer vs. Existing Video</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Purpose-Built Trailer</th><th>Existing Video</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Can explain the overall channel directly</td><td>Shows the real content experience immediately</td></tr>
    <tr><td>May become outdated as the channel changes</td><td>May cover only one narrow topic</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Can include a clear subscribe promise</td><td>Can provide more substantial immediate value</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>What the Trailer Should Communicate</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Who the channel is for</li>
  <li>What problems, interests, or experiences it covers</li>
  <li>What makes the channel distinct</li>
  <li>What formats or recurring programs viewers can expect</li>
  <li>Why subscribing or continuing to watch is useful</li>
</ul>

<h3>A Clear Channel Promise</h3>
<p>Use one concise sentence:</p>
<blockquote>This channel helps beginner YouTubers choose equipment, improve production, understand Analytics, and build a sustainable creator business.</blockquote>
<p>The trailer should demonstrate that promise rather than relying only on broad claims such as "entertainment for everyone."</p>

<h3>Start With Viewer Relevance</h3>
<p>A new visitor first needs to know whether the channel is for them.</p>
<p>Possible opening:</p>
<blockquote>If you are building a YouTube channel and feel overwhelmed by cameras, editing, Analytics, and monetization, this channel turns those subjects into practical guides.</blockquote>

<h3>Show, Do Not Only Tell</h3>
<p>Use clips that demonstrate:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The host or creator</li>
  <li>Representative topics</li>
  <li>Production quality</li>
  <li>Recurring segments</li>
  <li>Results, transformations, humor, or storytelling style</li>
  <li>The range of long-form, Shorts, and live content when relevant</li>
</ul>

<h3>How Long Should a Channel Trailer Be?</h3>
<p>YouTube does not publish one required trailer length in its current layout Help page.</p>
<p>The trailer should be long enough to communicate the channel promise and short enough to avoid turning an introduction into a complete channel history. A strong existing video may be longer when it immediately proves the channel's value.</p>

<h3>Do Not Copy a Fixed 30- or 60-Second Rule</h3>
<p>Many creator guides recommend short trailers, but these are strategy conventions rather than YouTube requirements.</p>
<p>Measure whether visitors continue into other videos or subscribe rather than judging the trailer only by its duration.</p>

<h3>Trailer Structure</h3>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Relevance:</strong> Identify the intended viewer.</li>
  <li><strong>Promise:</strong> Explain what the channel helps them do or experience.</li>
  <li><strong>Proof:</strong> Show representative clips, results, or expertise.</li>
  <li><strong>Programming:</strong> Explain useful recurring topics or formats.</li>
  <li><strong>Next step:</strong> Invite the viewer to subscribe or choose a starting video.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Should the Creator Introduce Themselves?</h3>
<p>Introduce the creator when identity, experience, personality, or access is part of the channel value.</p>
<p>Keep the introduction relevant:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Experience related to the topic</li>
  <li>Reason the channel exists</li>
  <li>Perspective the viewer can expect</li>
</ul>
<p>A long biography can delay the viewer-focused promise.</p>

<h3>Subscription Call to Action</h3>
<p>Explain what the subscription represents:</p>
<blockquote>Subscribe for two practical creator guides each month, plus Shorts that answer common equipment and Analytics questions.</blockquote>
<p>Do not promise a publishing frequency the channel cannot maintain.</p>

<h3>Give a Starting Point</h3>
<p>Some visitors need a useful next video more than a subscription request.</p>
<p>The trailer can recommend:</p>
<ul>
  <li>A beginner playlist</li>
  <li>The strongest foundational guide</li>
  <li>A current series</li>
  <li>A video matched to the audience's most common problem</li>
</ul>

<h3>Channel Trailer and Home-Tab Sections</h3>
<p>YouTube allows up to 12 custom Home-tab sections. The trailer should work with the rows below it.</p>
<p>Useful supporting sections can include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Start Here</li>
  <li>Popular tutorials</li>
  <li>Latest uploads</li>
  <li>Shorts</li>
  <li>Live replays</li>
  <li>Topic playlists</li>
  <li>Members-only content where eligible</li>
</ul>

<h3>Use the For You Section Carefully</h3>
<p>YouTube's For You section personalizes Home-tab content based on what the individual viewer has watched. It can complement the trailer by helping different visitors find relevant content.</p>
<p>The trailer should still communicate the overall channel identity rather than depending entirely on personalization.</p>

<h3>Channel Trailer and Video Packaging</h3>
<p>The selected video's title and thumbnail remain part of its identity. Use <a href="/glossary/video-packaging/">Video Packaging</a> that makes sense both on the watch page and in the channel layout.</p>
<p>A title such as "Welcome to My Channel" may communicate less value in Search or recommendations than a viewer-focused title.</p>

<h3>Do Channel Trailers Get Views?</h3>
<p>The trailer is a normal YouTube video selected for a channel-layout role. Creators should not assume every Home-tab impression becomes a counted view or that trailer views have special recommendation value.</p>
<p>Use available video Analytics and channel behavior rather than treating the trailer as a separate guaranteed traffic source.</p>

<h3>Does the Trailer Autoplay?</h3>
<p>The exact playback experience can vary by device, user setting, interface, and current YouTube behavior. YouTube's current Help page defines who the trailer is for and where it is selected, but creators should not build the strategy around an unsupported guarantee that every visit autoplays identically with sound.</p>

<h3>Trailer Captions and Accessibility</h3>
<p>Add accurate captions and make the message understandable when sound is unavailable.</p>
<p>Avoid:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Fast unreadable montages</li>
  <li>Important text under interface elements</li>
  <li>Music that overwhelms speech</li>
  <li>Visual references without explanation</li>
  <li>Flashing effects</li>
</ul>

<h3>Music and Rights</h3>
<p>The trailer must follow the same copyright and licensing rules as other videos. YouTube notes that third-party claimed content can change the default ad behavior on a channel trailer.</p>
<p>Use music and footage the creator has the rights to publish and monetize.</p>

<h3>When to Update the Trailer</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The channel promise changes.</li>
  <li>The host or brand changes.</li>
  <li>The featured formats are no longer representative.</li>
  <li>The upload promise is inaccurate.</li>
  <li>The visuals look materially outdated.</li>
  <li>A stronger existing video now introduces the channel better.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Do Not Replace It Constantly</h3>
<p>Frequent replacement can prevent the creator from learning whether one trailer works. Update when the channel or evidence changes, not merely because a new video was published.</p>

<h3>Measure Trailer Effectiveness</h3>
<p>Possible indicators include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Retention on the trailer video</li>
  <li>Subscriber gains associated with the video</li>
  <li>Channel-page traffic</li>
  <li>Views on recommended starting content</li>
  <li>Changes in Home-tab visitor behavior</li>
  <li>Qualitative comments from new viewers</li>
</ul>
<p>Use <a href="/glossary/channel-pages-traffic/">Channel Pages Traffic</a> to understand traffic attributed to channel surfaces, while recognizing that it does not isolate every trailer interaction.</p>

<h3>Channel Trailer for an Established Channel</h3>
<p>An established channel can choose:</p>
<ul>
  <li>A concise brand introduction</li>
  <li>A high-value foundational video</li>
  <li>A showcase reel with a clear promise</li>
  <li>A current campaign introduction</li>
</ul>
<p>The best choice depends on what a new non-subscriber needs to understand.</p>

<h3>Channel Trailer for a New Channel</h3>
<p>A new channel may not have enough representative footage for a montage. A straightforward video can explain:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The audience</li>
  <li>The first several planned topics</li>
  <li>The creator's relevant perspective</li>
  <li>The realistic publishing plan</li>
</ul>
<p>A normal high-value first video may be more persuasive than a trailer filled with future promises.</p>

<h3>Common Channel-Trailer Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Confusing it with the returning-subscriber featured video.</li>
  <li>Beginning with a long biography.</li>
  <li>Using vague channel claims.</li>
  <li>Promising an unsustainable cadence.</li>
  <li>Selecting the most-viewed video even when it poorly represents the channel.</li>
  <li>Assuming every visit autoplays the same way.</li>
  <li>Leaving an outdated trailer active.</li>
  <li>Failing to give viewers a useful starting point.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Who sees a YouTube channel trailer?</h4>
<p>It is intended for people who have not subscribed when they visit the channel Home tab.</p>

<h4>Will the same viewer see it every time?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube says it will not be shown again after that viewer watches it.</p>

<h4>Does the trailer need to be a separate video?</h4>
<p>No. A creator can select an existing video when it clearly introduces the channel.</p>

<h4>Do ads show on a channel trailer?</h4>
<p>By default they do not, unless the selected video contains third-party claimed content.</p>
