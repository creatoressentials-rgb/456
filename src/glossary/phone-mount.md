<h2>Phone Mount</h2>
<p>A <strong>phone mount</strong> is a holder or adapter that secures a smartphone to another support, such as a tripod, desk arm, camera cage, vehicle mount, gimbal, light stand, or overhead rig. It keeps the phone from being held by hand and provides a connection between the device and the larger support system.</p>
<p>For video creators, a phone mount is the part that grips or attaches to the phone. It is not necessarily the tripod, arm, clamp, or stand beneath it.</p>

<h3>How Does a Phone Mount Work?</h3>
<p>A phone mount normally has two interfaces:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Device interface:</strong> The clamp, magnetic surface, cage, or locking mechanism that holds the phone.</li>
  <li><strong>Support interface:</strong> The thread, cold-shoe foot, Arca-type plate, GoPro-style connection, or proprietary attachment that connects the holder to other equipment.</li>
</ul>
<p>Some mounts also include a rotating joint, ball head, tilt hinge, or multiple accessory points for changing orientation and attaching a microphone or light.</p>

<h3>Types of Phone Mounts</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Spring clamp:</strong> Uses spring tension to grip the sides of the phone. It is quick to load but must fit the phone and case correctly.</li>
  <li><strong>Screw-tightened clamp:</strong> Uses a knob or threaded mechanism to adjust and lock the grip more deliberately.</li>
  <li><strong>Magnetic mount:</strong> Attaches to a compatible phone, case, magnetic ring, or mounting system.</li>
  <li><strong>Mechanical-magnetic mount:</strong> Combines magnets for alignment with a physical lock for added retention.</li>
  <li><strong>Phone cage:</strong> Surrounds or frames the phone and adds multiple accessory and mounting points.</li>
  <li><strong>Adhesive or case-specific mount:</strong> Connects through a dedicated case, bonded adapter, or proprietary plate.</li>
</ul>

<h3>What Does "Universal Phone Mount" Mean?</h3>
<p>In product listings, <em>universal</em> usually means the holder fits a stated range of phone widths or works with several brands. It does not mean the mount fits every phone, every protective case, every tripod, or every accessory combination.</p>
<p>Before buying, check phone width, thickness, case shape, button placement, camera-bump clearance, device weight, and the mount's support connection. A clamp may technically fit the phone but still press a side button or block a lens.</p>

<h3>How to Choose a Phone Mount</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Device fit:</strong> Measure the phone with the case installed if you plan to leave it on.</li>
  <li><strong>Grip security:</strong> Consider whether the mount will remain stationary or be used while moving.</li>
  <li><strong>Orientation:</strong> Confirm that it locks securely in both landscape and portrait positions if you need both.</li>
  <li><strong>Support interface:</strong> Match the thread, shoe, plate, or proprietary connection to your existing gear.</li>
  <li><strong>Load capacity:</strong> Include the phone, case, lens attachment, filter, microphone receiver, and any mounted accessories.</li>
  <li><strong>Lens clearance:</strong> Make sure the holder does not enter the camera's field of view or block an ultrawide lens.</li>
  <li><strong>Button clearance:</strong> Check that the jaws do not hold down the power or volume controls.</li>
  <li><strong>Accessory mounting:</strong> Extra cold shoes and threaded holes can reduce the need for a separate cage.</li>
  <li><strong>Adjustment lock:</strong> A rotating mount should lock firmly rather than relying on friction alone.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to Use a Phone Mount Safely</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Confirm that the phone and case fall within the stated fit range.</li>
  <li>Inspect the clamp pads, magnetic surface, threads, and locking parts for damage.</li>
  <li>Attach the mount securely to the support before inserting the phone when practical.</li>
  <li>Center the phone in the holder and verify that all jaws or locks are fully engaged.</li>
  <li>Rotate the phone slowly and retighten the orientation joint.</li>
  <li>Support the phone with one hand while testing the mount's lock.</li>
  <li>Add cables and accessories one at a time, checking balance after each change.</li>
  <li>Use a safety tether when the phone is mounted overhead or in another elevated, high-consequence position.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Common Phone-Mount Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Buying based on phone model alone without accounting for the case.</li>
  <li>Assuming all magnetic mounts work equally with every phone or magnetic ring.</li>
  <li>Treating a cold shoe as an electrical hot shoe.</li>
  <li>Using a mount's accessory shoe for a heavy item that shifts the phone off balance.</li>
  <li>Failing to tighten the mount after rotating from landscape to portrait.</li>
  <li>Trusting the word "universal" instead of checking dimensions and interfaces.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Can a phone mount attach to any tripod?</h4>
<p>Many phone mounts use a common 1/4-inch-20 threaded hole, but not every tripod or mount uses the same interface. Check whether the tripod has a matching screw, quick-release plate, or adapter.</p>

<h4>Is a magnetic phone mount safe for video?</h4>
<p>It can be when the phone, case, magnet, support, and intended movement are compatible. A stationary desk setup has different retention demands from a moving vehicle, handheld rig, or overhead mount.</p>

<h4>What is a cold shoe on a phone mount?</h4>
<p>A cold shoe is a small physical accessory slot commonly used for microphones, compact lights, and receivers. Unlike an electronic hot shoe, it usually does not provide power, audio, or data.</p>

<h4>Can a phone mount hold a tablet?</h4>
<p>Only when the mount is specifically designed for the tablet's dimensions and weight. A phone holder may open wide enough but still lack the grip, depth, and load capacity required for a larger device.</p>
