<h2>YouTube Advanced Mode</h2>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> YouTube Advanced Mode is the expanded analytics view inside YouTube Studio. It's where you go when the default Analytics tabs (Overview, Content, Audience, etc.) don't cut it — Advanced Mode adds custom filters, side-by-side comparisons, extra metrics, and the ability to group and export data. If a glossary term on this site tells you a metric is "only visible in Advanced Mode," this is the screen it means.</p>

<h3>What Advanced Mode unlocks that the default view doesn't</h3>
<p>The default Analytics tabs are built for a fast check-in: did the video do okay, where did views come from, how's watch time trending. Advanced Mode is built for actually investigating a question. It gives you:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>More metrics</strong> — grouped into categories like Reach, Interactions, Revenue, Members, Playlists, Clips, and End screens, several of which don't appear on the default tabs at all. This is where you'll find <a href="/glossary/engaged-views/">Engaged Views</a> and <a href="/glossary/unique-viewers/">Unique Viewers</a>, for example.</li>
<li><strong>Filters</strong> — narrow any report by geography, device, subscription status, content type, and more. Filters grey out automatically if they don't apply to the current view or if a video doesn't have enough traffic to support them.</li>
<li><strong>Comparisons</strong> — put two videos, two groups, or two time periods side by side, and add a secondary metric to compare at the same time.</li>
<li><strong>Breakdowns</strong> — re-slice the same data by dimension: traffic source, geography, date, audience, monetization, or engagement type. This is the fastest way to go from "views dropped" to "views dropped specifically from <a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">Suggested Videos</a>."</li>
<li><strong>Groups</strong> — custom collections of up to 500 videos, playlists, or channels, analyzed together without making anything public. Unlike a playlist, nobody sees a group but you and your channel's collaborators.</li>
<li><strong>Export</strong> — download the current report (capped at 500 rows; use the YouTube Reporting API for bigger pulls).</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to access Advanced Mode</h3>
<ol>
<li>Sign in to YouTube Studio.</li>
<li>Click <strong>Analytics</strong> in the left menu.</li>
<li>Under any report, click <strong>Advanced Mode</strong> or <strong>See More</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>It's a desktop/browser feature — most of it isn't available on mobile, so don't go looking for it in the app.</p>
<p>Once you're in, the top navigation also gives you shortcuts to <strong>Popular</strong> reports (top content, <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">traffic sources</a>, and geographies over the last 28 days), <strong>Special reports</strong> (audience age/gender, retention rate, first-24-hours performance, real-time data), and any <strong>Saved reports</strong> you've built.</p>

<h3>Useful ways to actually use it</h3>
<p><strong>Compare content types, not just individual videos.</strong> Build a group for one content pillar or format and another group for a different one, then use Add Comparison to see which one wins on watch time, subscriber conversion, or any metric you pick. This is more useful than eyeballing single-video stats, which get noisy fast.</p>
<p><strong>Drill into traffic sources instead of just glancing at the summary.</strong> Rows in blue are clickable — click "YouTube search" under traffic sources and you'll see the actual search terms sending you views. Break down by traffic source to check whether growth is coming from algorithmic discovery like <a href="/glossary/browse-features/">Browse Features</a> and Suggested Videos, or from loyal-audience behavior like subscriptions and channel pages — those tell very different stories about whether growth is sustainable.</p>
<p><strong>Filter by viewer type to separate casual traffic from your real audience.</strong> Advanced Mode lets you filter and segment by audience type, which is how you'd isolate <a href="/glossary/regular-viewers/">regular viewers</a> and <a href="/glossary/returning-viewers/">returning viewers</a> from one-time visitors when judging whether a video is actually building an audience or just getting a traffic spike.</p>
<p><strong>Check revenue metrics beyond the summary card.</strong> The Revenue metrics category is where you'll find figures tied to <a href="/glossary/monetized-playbacks/">monetized playbacks</a>, <a href="/glossary/playback-based-cpm/">playback-based CPM</a>, and <a href="/glossary/youtube-rpm/">YouTube RPM</a> — useful for understanding <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">ad revenue</a> patterns by video, group, or traffic source rather than just channel-wide totals. Membership-related metrics (tied to <a href="/glossary/channel-memberships/">channel memberships</a>) live in their own category the same way.</p>
<p><strong>Compare a video's first 24 hours or first 7 days across your catalog.</strong> This normalizes for video age, which raw lifetime totals don't — useful if you post irregularly or are testing a new thumbnail or hook style and want a fair comparison.</p>
<p><strong>Save the reports you check regularly.</strong> You can save up to 50 custom reports and pull them up from the top navigation instead of rebuilding the same filters and breakdowns every month.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Is Advanced Mode available on mobile?</h4>
<p>No. It's a desktop/browser feature inside YouTube Studio. Some controls and reports aren't available on mobile at all.</p>
<h4>Do I need a certain subscriber count or monetization status to use it?</h4>
<p>No — Advanced Mode is part of standard YouTube Studio Analytics and isn't gated behind monetization or a subscriber threshold. If you can see the Analytics tab, you can open Advanced Mode.</p>
<h4>How much data can I export, and how many groups or saved reports can I keep?</h4>
<p>Groups can hold up to 500 videos, playlists, or channels each, and you can save up to 50 custom reports. Exports from the current view are capped at 500 rows — for larger pulls, YouTube points you to the Reporting API.</p>
