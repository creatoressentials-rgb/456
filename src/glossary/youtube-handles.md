<h2>YouTube Handles</h2>
<p>A <strong>YouTube handle</strong> is the unique, @-prefixed identifier assigned to every <a href="/glossary/youtube-channel/">YouTube channel</a> — for example, <code>@creatoressentials</code>. It's distinct from your channel's display name, forms your channel's URL (<code>youtube.com/@yourhandle</code>), and is what viewers use to tag or @-mention you in comments and <a href="/glossary/youtube-communities/">Community</a> posts.</p>
<p>YouTube introduced handles on October 10, 2022, and began rolling them out to creators the following month, in <strong>November 2022</strong>, in phases based on factors like subscriber count and channel activity. Handles replaced the old "Custom URL" system as the primary way to claim a memorable, brandable link — but unlike Custom URLs, which required a subscriber minimum, every channel is eligible for a handle from day one.</p>

<h3>Why handles exist</h3>
<p>Before handles, channels were identified primarily by a long, non-memorable channel ID (<code>youtube.com/channel/UC...</code>) or, for eligible channels, a Custom URL. Neither system guaranteed a unique, easy-to-share identity, and neither made it simple to tag another creator. Handles solved both problems: every handle is globally unique, and typing <code>@</code> plus a handle is now the standard way to reference a channel anywhere on YouTube.</p>

<h3>How to change your YouTube handle</h3>
<p><strong>On desktop:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Sign in to <a href="/glossary/youtube-studio/">YouTube Studio</a>.</li>
<li>In the left menu, go to <strong>Customization &gt; Profile</strong> (sometimes labeled <strong>Basic info</strong>).</li>
<li>Find the <strong>Handle</strong> field and enter your new handle. YouTube will flag it immediately if it's unavailable or breaks a naming rule.</li>
<li>Click <strong>Publish</strong> to confirm the change.</li>
</ol>
<p>Alternatively, go straight to <code>youtube.com/handle</code>, click <strong>Change handle</strong>, enter your choice, and confirm.</p>
<p><strong>On mobile (YouTube app):</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Tap your <a href="/glossary/profile-picture/">profile picture</a>, then <strong>Your channel</strong>.</li>
<li>Tap the edit (pencil) icon next to your handle.</li>
<li>Enter your new handle and tap <strong>Save</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>On mobile (YouTube Studio app):</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Tap your profile picture, then <strong>Edit channel profile</strong>.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Handle</strong>, enter your new choice, and tap <strong>Save</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>On a channel with multiple people managing it, only accounts with sufficient <a href="/glossary/channel-permissions/">channel permissions</a> (Owner or Manager-level access) can change the handle — Editors and lower access levels generally cannot.</p>

<h3>How often can you change your handle?</h3>
<p>YouTube's official rule: <strong>you can change your handle twice within a 14-day period.</strong> Once you hit that limit, changes are locked until the rolling 14-day window resets. (Many articles simplify this to "once every 14 days" — that's not the actual rule; you get two changes per 14-day window, which is enough to revert a mistake and then pick a permanent handle.)</p>
<p>When you change your handle, YouTube holds your <strong>previous handle for 14 days</strong> in case you want to switch back. During that window, both your old and new handle URLs work and point to your channel. After 14 days, your old handle is released and becomes available for anyone else to claim.</p>
<p>If a channel is deleted, its handle is reported to become available again after a holding period — but the exact number of days isn't published in YouTube's Help Center and figures vary by source (commonly cited as somewhere in the 30–60 day range). Don't rely on a specific number if you're trying to reclaim a handle from a deleted channel.</p>

<h3>Does changing your handle break old links or hurt <a href="/glossary/seo/">SEO</a>?</h3>
<p>No, in the sense that your links won't break: legacy Custom URLs and username URLs continue to work and automatically redirect to your current handle URL, and your previous handle URL keeps working for 14 days after a change. YouTube hasn't published data on search-ranking impact from a handle change, so treat any specific claim (like a fixed "recovery time") as unverified. The safest practice is still to update your external links, bio, and email signature to the new handle promptly, even though old links will redirect.</p>

<h3>@-mentioning and handles</h3>
<p>Handles are what make @-mentioning possible anywhere on YouTube — in comments, video descriptions, Live Chat, and <a href="/glossary/youtube-communities/">Community</a> posts. Typing <code>@</code> followed by a handle creates a clickable tag linking straight to that channel, which is why handles matter for shoutouts, <a href="/glossary/youtube-collaborations/">collaborations</a>, and cross-promotion between creators.</p>

<h3>Impersonation and handle squatting</h3>
<p>There's no handle-specific "squatting" process — impersonation involving handles falls under YouTube's general impersonation policy, which prohibits deceptively copying another channel's branding, content, or username, and under YouTube's trademark complaint process for rights holders. If someone is using a handle to impersonate you or your brand, report the channel through YouTube's standard impersonation-report flow rather than expecting a dedicated "handle dispute" tool.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Is a YouTube handle the same as my channel name?</h4>
<p>No. Your channel name is your display title and can be duplicated across multiple channels. Your handle is your unique <code>@username</code>, used for your URL and for tagging. Changing one does not change the other.</p>
<h4>How many times can I change my YouTube handle?</h4>
<p>Twice within any rolling 14-day period. After that, you'll need to wait for the window to reset before changing it again.</p>
<h4>Will I lose my verification badge if I change my handle?</h4>
<p>No. Changing your handle does not affect your verification badge. Changing your channel <em>name</em>, however, does remove it, and you'd need to reapply.</p>
<h4>Do old YouTube URLs still work after switching to a handle?</h4>
<p>Yes. Legacy Custom URLs (<code>/c/</code>) and username URLs (<code>/user/</code>) still work and redirect to your handle URL, and channel ID URLs never change.</p>
