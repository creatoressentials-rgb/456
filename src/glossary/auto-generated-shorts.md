<h2>Auto-Generated Shorts</h2>
<p><strong>Auto-Generated Shorts</strong> is creator shorthand for the ways YouTube itself — rather than the creator manually editing a clip — turns existing long-form video into Shorts-format content that can appear on a channel's Shorts shelf. In practice, this covers three distinct YouTube mechanisms: automatic reclassification of videos that already meet Shorts specs, the "Remix"/"Edit into a Short" system (which is opt-out by default), and YouTube's own experimental tools that auto-select highlight segments from a video.</p>
<p>It is <strong>not</strong> the same thing as <a href="/glossary/auto-generated-clips/">Auto-Generated Clips</a>, which refers specifically to YouTube's highlight-moment clipping experiments (like the "Create a video highlight" test), and it is not the same as a creator sitting down and manually cutting their own Short in the Shorts editor. This entry focuses on the automatic/system-driven side of Shorts creation and covers what's confirmed, what's opt-out, and what's simply internet folklore.</p>

<h3>Fact vs. Myth</h3>
<p><strong>Myth:</strong> "YouTube can silently publish a brand-new Short to my channel with content I never selected, and I have no way to stop it."</p>
<p><strong>Fact:</strong> Every documented mechanism either reclassifies a video you already uploaded in a Shorts-eligible format, or requires a human (you or another creator/viewer using Remix) to actively select and publish before anything goes live. The confusion largely stems from three overlapping features getting lumped together: automatic classification, the opt-out-by-default Remix system, and unrelated AI experiments like Reimagine (a viewer-facing generative remix tool for existing Shorts) and a 2025 unblur/denoise processing test. None of these amount to YouTube inventing and publishing wholly new Shorts from your catalog without a trigger.</p>

<h3>Why This Happens: The Default Opt-In</h3>
<p>Since 2021–2022, YouTube has made public videos <strong>eligible for Shorts remixing by default</strong>. That means:</p>
<ul>
<li>New uploads are automatically opted in to letting others sample a short clip via Remix.</li>
<li>Your own long-form videos are automatically eligible for you to cut into a Short using "Edit into a Short."</li>
<li>Music videos with copyrighted content from YouTube's music partners are the main exception — not automatically remixable.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you don't want your content sampled by other creators (or want to prevent your own catalog from showing the "Edit into a Short" prompt), you have to opt out — it will not happen by default only if you take action.</p>

<h3>Monetization and Reused-Content Implications</h3>
<p>Turning your own long-form video into a Short does not, by itself, trigger YouTube's <a href="/glossary/reused-content/">Reused Content</a> policy — YouTube's monetization policy explicitly allows monetizing "content that primarily features the uploading creator" and Shorts that add original segments or audio to existing material. Where it gets more complex:</p>
<ul>
<li>Shorts revenue is calculated through a <strong>separate ad-revenue pool</strong> from long-form video, based on <a href="/glossary/engaged-views/">Engaged Views</a> within the Shorts Feed — this is distinct from <a href="/glossary/monetized-playbacks/">Monetized Playbacks</a> and <a href="/glossary/playback-based-cpm/">Playback-Based CPM</a> on long-form content, and separate from your overall <a href="/glossary/youtube-rpm/">YouTube RPM</a>.</li>
<li>If a Short includes third-party music or content, revenue may be split with rightsholders before it reaches the Creator Pool — a <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a> claim can also affect eligibility or trigger a <a href="/glossary/copyright-claim/">Copyright Claim</a>.</li>
<li>Non-original Shorts — straight reuploads, unedited third-party clips, or template-only compilations — are excluded from counting as eligible engaged views for Shorts ad revenue, separate from any <a href="/glossary/copyright-strike/">Copyright Strike</a> risk.</li>
<li>For a full picture of how these rules fit into your channel's eligibility, see YouTube Monetization and Policies and <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">Ad Revenue</a>.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Credit, Ownership, and Traffic <a href="/glossary/attribution/">Attribution</a></h3>
<ul>
<li>A Short cut from your <strong>own</strong> long-form video stays on your channel and links back to the source video — no ownership changes hands.</li>
<li>A Short someone else creates by remixing your public video is uploaded to <strong>their</strong> channel, with attribution/a link back to your original — you don't get a <a href="/glossary/revenue-share/">revenue share</a> from their Short, but you may gain new viewers.</li>
<li>Traffic driven by remixes of your content shows up in your Reach reports under a distinct "Remixed video" source, separate from <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">Traffic Sources</a> like <a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">Suggested Videos</a> or <a href="/glossary/browse-features/">Browse Features</a>.</li>
<li>Watch time and view counts for the Short and the original long-form video are tracked separately; a Short's performance does not add to the original video's watch time, though it can indirectly drive new viewers back to it — a distinction worth tracking alongside <a href="/glossary/returning-viewers/">Returning Viewers</a> and <a href="/glossary/unique-viewers/">Unique Viewers</a> metrics for your channel.</li>
</ul>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Does YouTube really create and publish Shorts from my videos without my permission?</h4>
<p>Not in the sense of inventing new content with no trigger. What actually happens is either automatic classification (a vertical/square video under 3 minutes is treated as a Short) or a remix/highlight tool that still needs a person — you or another creator — to select and publish. Public videos are opted into being <em>available</em> for this by default, which is where most of the "automatic" perception comes from.</p>
<h4>Can I stop my long-form videos from being turned into Shorts by other people?</h4>
<p>Yes. In YouTube Studio, open the video, click "Show more," and turn off "Shorts remixing." You can do this per video, in bulk, or set it as your default for future uploads.</p>
<h4>Does an auto-generated or remixed Short count as reused content and hurt my monetization?</h4>
<p>Not automatically. YouTube's reused-content policy protects content that primarily features you as the creator or that adds meaningful original material. Straight reuploads or template-only compilations are the type of content actually at risk — see <a href="/glossary/reused-content/">Reused Content</a> for the full breakdown.</p>
<h4>Is this the same as YouTube's "Reimagine" AI feature or the 2025 AI video-enhancement controversy?</h4>
<p>No. Reimagine lets viewers generate a new AI clip from a frame of an existing Short, and the 2025 story was about undisclosed AI video-quality processing. Both are separate from whether/how a Short gets created from your long-form catalog, though all three get lumped together in creator discussions.</p>
<h4>Is this the same as Auto-Generated Clips?</h4>
<p>No. Auto-Generated Clips refers to YouTube's highlight-moment clipping tools that surface the most engaging segment of a long video (like the "Create a video highlight" test). Auto-Generated Shorts, as used on this site, covers the broader set of ways a Short can be produced or surfaced from your content without you manually building it from scratch in the Shorts editor.</p>
