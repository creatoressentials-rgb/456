<h2>Cut Remix</h2>
<p><strong>Cut Remix</strong> is a YouTube Shorts tool that lets you place a <strong>1–5 second segment</strong> from an eligible YouTube video or Short into a new Short. The sampled segment includes both the source video and its audio, and YouTube attributes the remix back to the original content.</p>
<p>Creators commonly use Cut as a hook or reference point before adding their own reaction, explanation, demonstration, joke, or continuation.</p>

<h3>How Cut Remix works</h3>
<p>Cut takes a very short excerpt from existing YouTube content and inserts it into the Shorts creation workflow. You select the exact portion of the source you want to reference, then record or add your original portion around it.</p>
<p>The short sample is meant to provide context—not replace the need for original content. A useful Cut Remix normally gives viewers a reason to watch beyond the borrowed five seconds.</p>

<h3>How long can a Cut Remix be?</h3>
<p>The source segment can be <strong>between 1 and 5 seconds</strong>. This limit applies to the sampled portion, not necessarily to the total length of your finished Short.</p>
<p>The selected Cut includes:</p>
<ul>
<li>The source video frames.</li>
<li>The source audio that plays during those frames.</li>
<li>Attribution connecting the new Short to the original video.</li>
</ul>
<p>For audio without the source visuals, use <a href="/glossary/sound-remix/">Sound Remix</a> instead.</p>

<h3>How to create a Cut Remix</h3>
<p>Cut is available in the YouTube mobile app when the source content is eligible.</p>
<h4>From another Short</h4>
<ol>
<li>Open the Short you want to sample.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Remix</strong>.</li>
<li>Choose <strong>Cut this video</strong> or <strong>Cut</strong>.</li>
<li>Select a 1–5 second segment.</li>
<li>Choose an available layout if prompted.</li>
<li>Record and edit the rest of your Short.</li>
<li>Add upload details and publish.</li>
</ol>
<h4>From a long-form video</h4>
<ol>
<li>Open the video in the YouTube mobile app.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Share</strong>.</li>
<li>Choose <strong>Remix</strong>, then <strong>Cut</strong>.</li>
<li>Select the source segment and create the rest of the Short.</li>
</ol>
<p>Older instructions may tell you to tap Remix directly below the player. YouTube's current creator update says the Remix tools for regular videos are moving into the Share menu.</p>

<h3>What content is eligible for Cut Remix?</h3>
<p>The Cut option appears only when the source is available for visual remixing. It may not appear when:</p>
<ul>
<li>The creator has turned off visual remixing.</li>
<li>The source is private, removed, or otherwise restricted.</li>
<li>A third-party copyright claim blocks visual remix features.</li>
<li>Music or territorial licensing prevents the video from being sampled.</li>
<li>The app, account, device, or region does not currently support the option.</li>
</ul>
<p>The absence of Cut is a source-specific eligibility issue in many cases. It is not proof that your own channel has a copyright problem.</p>

<h3>Attribution and analytics</h3>
<p>YouTube links a Cut-created Short back to the original work. This gives viewers a way to find the source and gives the original creator visibility into remix activity.</p>
<p>For source creators, referrals from Cut and other visual remixes may appear as <strong>Remixed video</strong> traffic in <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">YouTube traffic sources</a>. Shorts Analytics can also show remixes and top-remixed content.</p>
<p>Attribution does not mean the original creator helped produce the new Short. It identifies the sampled source and reflects that the built-in remix tool was used.</p>

<h3>Copyright and built-in remixing</h3>
<p>Using YouTube's Cut tool is different from downloading someone else's video and manually reuploading a clip. YouTube's built-in remix features include the permissions and attribution associated with that in-product use.</p>
<p>That permission is not unlimited. The Short must still follow YouTube policies, and music licensing can affect availability. Content edited outside the YouTube creation tools may receive a <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a> match, a <a href="/glossary/copyright-claim/">copyright claim</a>, or a <a href="/glossary/copyright-strike/">copyright strike</a> if the use is not authorized.</p>
<p>A five-second duration is not a universal copyright exemption. Copyright questions depend on permission, licensing, and the circumstances of the use—not a mythical "safe" number of seconds.</p>

<h3>What happens if the source is deleted or restricted?</h3>
<p>Cut is a visual remix. If the original creator deletes the source, restricts visual remixing, or makes certain source edits, YouTube may delete the Cut Remix that depends on it.</p>
<p>The source creator can also prevent future Cut remixes by changing the Shorts remixing setting in YouTube Studio. Existing visual remixes may be affected when access is withdrawn.</p>

<h3>Can Cut Remix content be monetized?</h3>
<p>A Cut Remix can only earn revenue when the channel and video meet the applicable YouTube monetization rules. Access to the tool is not the same as approval for monetization.</p>
<p>The finished Short should contain meaningful original value. A channel that repeatedly surrounds brief third-party clips with little or no transformation may face <a href="/glossary/reused-content/">reused content</a> issues during monetization review.</p>
<p>Strong Cut Remixes use the excerpt as evidence, setup, or context and make the creator's own contribution the reason the Short exists.</p>

<h3>Practical ways to use Cut Remix</h3>
<p>Creators can use Cut to:</p>
<ul>
<li>Quote a surprising moment before explaining it.</li>
<li>Respond to a claim or opinion.</li>
<li>Show the first step of a trend, then demonstrate their own version.</li>
<li>Reference a mistake before teaching the correct method.</li>
<li>Continue a joke, story, or visual chain reaction.</li>
<li>Compare an old prediction with what happened later.</li>
</ul>
<p>Start with a segment that is immediately understandable. Five seconds is short, so the source moment should make sense without a long introduction.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>What is the maximum Cut Remix length?</h4>
<p>You can sample a 1–5 second segment from eligible source content. The completed Short can include additional original footage.</p>
<h4>Does Cut Remix include the source audio?</h4>
<p>Yes. Cut samples both the video and audio from the selected segment. Use Sound Remix when you only want the audio.</p>
<h4>Is Cut the same as clipping my own long-form video?</h4>
<p>Not exactly. Edit into a Short lets you select up to 60 seconds from your own eligible video, while Cut is a 1–5 second remix format that can reference eligible content across YouTube.</p>
<h4>Is any five-second clip legal to use?</h4>
<p>No. There is no universal five-second copyright rule. Using the built-in Cut tool carries YouTube's in-product permissions and attribution, while manually copying a clip can create separate copyright risks.</p>
