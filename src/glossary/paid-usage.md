<h2>Paid Usage</h2>
<p><strong>Paid usage</strong> is permission for a brand or advertiser to use creator content in paid advertising, promoted distribution, or another media placement funded by advertising spend.</p>
<p>It is a type of <a href="/glossary/content-licensing">content licensing</a>. The creator may still own the content while granting the brand defined advertising rights for specified platforms, locations, formats, and dates.</p>
<p>Paid usage is broader than one platform feature. Meta <a href="/glossary/partnership-ads">partnership ads</a>, YouTube creator partnership campaigns, boosted posts, allowlisting, and ads run from a brand account are different technical methods that may rely on paid-usage permission.</p>
<h3>Examples of paid usage</h3>
<p>A brand may request the right to:</p>
<ul>
<li>Turn a creator's Instagram Reel into a Meta partnership ad</li>
<li>Promote a creator's YouTube video through a creator partnership campaign</li>
<li>Upload the creator's video to the brand's ad account</li>
<li>Use excerpts in paid social ads</li>
<li>Run the content in display or connected-television advertising</li>
<li>Place the creator's image in sponsored retail-media placements</li>
<li>Promote a sponsored post beyond its organic audience</li>
<li>Create several ad versions from one approved asset</li>
<li>Use the content in paid email acquisition or sponsored placements</li>
</ul>
<p>A brand reposting content on its ordinary unpaid social feed is generally <strong>organic usage</strong>, not paid usage, unless the post is later boosted or used as an ad.</p>

<p>The contract right and the platform permission should match. A brand should not receive a 12-month technical authorization when the paid-usage license lasts only 30 days.</p>
<h3>Paid usage from a brand account</h3>
<p>The brand may download or receive the content and run it as an ad under the brand's identity.</p>
<p>Define whether the brand can:</p>
<ul>
<li>Crop or resize the asset</li>
<li>Add headlines, captions, or calls to action</li>
<li>Change music or voice-over</li>
<li>Combine it with other footage</li>
<li>Translate or dub it</li>
<li>Use the creator's name, handle, likeness, or testimonial</li>
<li>Create still images from the video</li>
<li>Test alternate hooks or endings</li>
<li>Use raw footage or only the final approved version</li>
</ul>
<h3>Paid usage through the creator's identity</h3>
<p>Some ad systems let the brand promote content associated with the creator's account, handle, page, or post.</p>
<p>This can have additional value because the ad may appear to come from, or visibly feature, the creator's identity. The contract should define:</p>
<ul>
<li>Which account or handle is authorized</li>
<li>Whether the creator's profile image and name can appear</li>
<li>Whether new ad creative can be created under the creator identity</li>
<li>Whether only an existing post can be promoted</li>
<li>Who controls targeting, budget, copy, and landing pages</li>
<li>Which claims and calls to action are permitted</li>
<li>How the creator can inspect active ads</li>
<li>When permissions must be revoked</li>
<li>Whether the creator receives performance reports</li>
</ul>
<p>Technical account permission is not the same as permission to use the creator's identity in every form of advertising.</p>
<h3>What a paid-usage clause should define</h3>
<h4>Licensed content</h4>
<p>Name the exact video, post, cut, photograph, audio clip, or version. Avoid a grant covering all content the creator produces during the relationship unless that is intentional.</p>
<h4>Platforms and media</h4>
<p>Specify Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, TikTok, websites, email, streaming television, retail media, search, display, or other channels.</p>
<h4>Term</h4>
<p>Use clear start and end dates. State whether the term begins at contract signing, delivery, first ad launch, or publication.</p>
<h4>Territory</h4>
<p>Define local, national, global, or named markets. Online ads can cross borders, so the agreement should address reasonable incidental reach.</p>
<h4>Ad identity</h4>
<p>State whether the ad runs from:</p>
<ul>
<li>The brand account</li>
<li>The creator account</li>
<li>A partnership identity</li>
<li>A retailer or affiliate account</li>
<li>An agency account</li>
<li>Multiple regional accounts</li>
</ul>
<h4>Editing rights</h4>
<p>Define permitted versions, crops, captions, translations, and derivative works. Require creator approval for material changes when appropriate.</p>
<h4>Sublicensing</h4>
<p>State whether the brand may authorize agencies, retailers, distributors, affiliates, or media partners to use the content.</p>
<h4>Ad spend and impressions</h4>
<p>Some agreements limit paid media by duration only. Others also define spend, impressions, markets, or campaign objectives.</p>
<h4>Renewal</h4>
<p>State the renewal rate, notice deadline, and whether use stops automatically unless both parties sign an extension.</p>
<h4>Shutdown and archiving</h4>
<p>Define when active ads must stop, whether saved campaign data can remain, and whether organic posts may stay visible.</p>
<h3>Does platform permission grant paid-usage rights?</h3>
<p>Not by itself.</p>
<p>Meta requires the advertiser to receive partnership-ad permission from the creator or partner. Meta also permits creators to revoke technical permissions. Those platform controls do not fully define the parties' contractual rights, fees, or remedies.</p>
<p>YouTube similarly says creators should negotiate video usage rights and obtain necessary agreements independently when sharing brand partner access.</p>
<h3>Pricing paid usage</h3>
<p>Paid usage can be priced through:</p>
<ul>
<li>A flat fee for a fixed term</li>
<li>A monthly licensing fee</li>
<li>A platform-by-platform fee</li>
<li>A percentage of the base production fee</li>
<li>A fee tied to advertising spend</li>
<li>A renewal schedule</li>
<li>A minimum fee plus performance compensation</li>
<li>A broader buyout</li>
</ul>
<p>No universal percentage applies to every campaign. Value changes with duration, advertising reach, creator identity, editing rights, exclusivity, markets, and whether the brand receives raw files.</p>
<h3>Paid-usage red flags</h3>
<p>Review requests for:</p>
<ul>
<li>“Unlimited paid usage” with no platform or term</li>
<li>Global use for a small local campaign fee</li>
<li>Perpetual use of the creator's identity</li>
<li>Unlimited edits and derivative works</li>
<li>Authorization for any affiliate or third party</li>
<li>Use in sensitive or regulated categories not discussed</li>
<li>Ad copy the creator cannot review</li>
<li>No deadline for removing ads</li>
<li>Automatic renewal without a new fee</li>
<li>Technical access extending beyond the contract</li>
<li>AI training or synthetic likeness rights hidden in usage language</li>
<li>Paid use combined with broad <a href="/glossary/category-exclusivity">category exclusivity</a></li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is paid usage the same as boosting?</h4>
<p>No. Paid usage is the contractual permission. Boosting is one platform method of paying to increase distribution of a post.</p>
<h4>Does paid usage transfer ownership?</h4>
<p>Not automatically. The creator can retain ownership while granting a limited advertising license.</p>
<h4>Can a creator revoke partnership-ad permission?</h4>
<p>Meta says creators or partners can revoke partnership-ad permissions. The creator should also check the contract because technical revocation does not automatically eliminate a valid contractual obligation.</p>
<h4>Should paid usage have an end date?</h4>
<p>Usually, a defined term makes the scope easier to price and enforce. A brand requesting no end date is asking for <a href="/glossary/perpetual-usage-rights">perpetual usage rights</a>, which should be evaluated separately.</p>
