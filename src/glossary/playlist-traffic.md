<h2>Playlist Traffic</h2>
<p><strong>Playlist traffic</strong> is YouTube Analytics traffic from any playlist that includes one of your videos.</p>
<p>The playlist can belong to you, another creator, or a viewer. YouTube also includes traffic from users' Liked Videos and Favorite Videos playlists in this category.</p>

<h3>What Can Generate Playlist Traffic?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Your own public playlist</li>
  <li>Another creator's playlist</li>
  <li>A viewer-created playlist</li>
  <li>A channel's series or topic playlist</li>
  <li>A user's Liked Videos playlist</li>
  <li>A user's Favorite Videos playlist</li>
  <li>A playlist used as a YouTube podcast</li>
</ul>
<p>The video must be viewed in a playlist-related context for the traffic to be attributed to Playlists.</p>

<h3>Where Can You See Playlist Traffic?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Analytics</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open <strong>Content</strong> for channel-level performance or <strong>Reach</strong> for a specific video.</li>
  <li>Find <strong>How viewers find your videos</strong>.</li>
  <li>Select the Playlists traffic source.</li>
  <li>Review <strong>Playlists featuring this video</strong> when available.</li>
</ol>
<p>The detailed report can show which playlists led viewers to the content.</p>

<h3>Playlist Traffic vs. Playlist Analytics</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Report</th><th>What It Measures</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Playlist traffic source</td><td>Views of your content attributed to any playlist containing the video</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Playlist Analytics</td><td>Aggregated performance for videos within a playlist and viewer activity in that playlist context</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Top playlists card</td><td>Your strongest public playlists within the selected period</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>A video can receive Playlist traffic from a playlist you do not own. Your own Playlist Analytics provides broader reporting for playlists you manage.</p>

<h3>Your Playlist vs. Another Creator's Playlist</h3>
<p>YouTube's traffic-source definition includes both. Another creator can place your public or Unlisted video in a playlist and send viewers to it.</p>
<p>This can happen through:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Curated resource lists</li>
  <li>Industry tutorials</li>
  <li>Music or entertainment collections</li>
  <li>Classroom and training playlists</li>
  <li>Collaboration series</li>
  <li>News or event collections</li>
</ul>

<h3>Liked Videos and Favorites</h3>
<p>YouTube explicitly includes users' Liked Videos and Favorite Videos playlists in Playlist traffic.</p>
<p>This means some Playlist traffic may come from personal viewing behavior rather than a public curated playlist. A viewer may like a video, revisit it later through Liked Videos, and generate playlist-attributed viewing.</p>

<h3>Does Playlist Traffic Mean Sequential Viewing?</h3>
<p>Not always. A viewer can open one item from a playlist without watching several videos in sequence.</p>
<p>Playlist traffic can include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Starting from the playlist page</li>
  <li>Continuing automatically to the next item</li>
  <li>Selecting one video from the playlist panel</li>
  <li>Returning through Liked Videos</li>
  <li>Opening a shared playlist link</li>
</ul>

<h3>Playlist Traffic vs. Suggested Videos</h3>
<p><a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">Suggested Videos</a> includes recommendations next to or after another video and links in descriptions. Playlist traffic comes from a playlist context.</p>
<p>A playlist can influence continued viewing without every transition being classified as Suggested. Use the traffic-source report rather than assuming all sequential views share one source.</p>

<h3>Playlist Traffic vs. Browse Features</h3>
<p>A playlist may first be discovered through Browse, Search, a channel page, or an external link. Once the viewer enters the playlist and watches a video through that context, the resulting video traffic can be attributed to Playlists.</p>
<p>The source describes the immediate path to the video view, not necessarily the first place the viewer discovered the playlist.</p>

<h3>How Playlist Analytics Works</h3>
<p>YouTube says Playlist Analytics uses video-group reporting to aggregate data for videos in a playlist.</p>
<p>Metrics can include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Views from playlist</li>
  <li>Playlist watch time</li>
  <li>Playlist average duration</li>
  <li>Overview, Content, Audience, and Revenue reporting</li>
</ul>
<p>Some overall metrics exclude videos owned by other channels, while playlist-context metrics can include them.</p>

<h3>Why a Playlist May Not Appear in Analytics</h3>
<p>YouTube says playlist reporting only shows playlists that:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Received views during the selected period</li>
  <li>Are public for the applicable playlist reports</li>
</ul>
<p>Low-volume playlist names may also be limited in traffic-source reporting.</p>

<h3>How to Build Useful Playlists</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Define one viewer goal or topic.</li>
  <li>Choose videos that genuinely belong together.</li>
  <li>Order them logically.</li>
  <li>Place the strongest starting video first.</li>
  <li>Write a clear searchable playlist title.</li>
  <li>Add a useful description.</li>
  <li>Remove outdated or duplicate items.</li>
  <li>Feature the playlist on the channel page.</li>
  <li>Link to it from relevant videos and descriptions.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Can Playlists Increase Watch Time?</h3>
<p>They can make related videos easier to continue watching, but playlist placement does not guarantee autoplay, retention, or recommendations.</p>
<p>Continued viewing depends on:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Topic continuity</li>
  <li>The value of the next video</li>
  <li>Packaging</li>
  <li>Viewer intent</li>
  <li>Playlist order</li>
  <li>Device and playback settings</li>
</ul>

<h3>Series Playlists</h3>
<p>A series playlist tells YouTube that videos should be viewed together as an official set. Series playlists have separate eligibility and setup rules and should not be confused with ordinary Playlist traffic.</p>
<p>Ordinary playlists can still generate traffic and organize content without being marked as an official series.</p>

<h3>Public, Unlisted, and Private Playlists</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Public:</strong> Can appear in public channel and discovery contexts.</li>
  <li><strong>Unlisted:</strong> Accessible to people with the playlist link.</li>
  <li><strong>Private:</strong> Restricted under the playlist's access settings.</li>
</ul>
<p>Public playlist visibility is especially important for YouTube's standard Top Playlist analytics reporting.</p>

<h3>How to Evaluate Playlist Traffic</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Identify the playlists sending views.</li>
  <li>Compare watch time and average view duration.</li>
  <li>Check whether viewers continue to another video.</li>
  <li>Review the strongest entry points.</li>
  <li>Compare your playlists with third-party playlists.</li>
  <li>Update playlist order and descriptions.</li>
  <li>Measure changes over a consistent period.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Playlist Traffic and Podcasts</h3>
<p>On YouTube, a podcast is represented through a designated playlist containing video episodes. Views from podcast playlist contexts can contribute to Playlist traffic and playlist-level reporting.</p>
<p>Podcast classification also enables additional YouTube and YouTube Music features, subject to current eligibility rules.</p>

<h3>Common Playlist-Traffic Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Assuming it only comes from playlists you own.</li>
  <li>Ignoring Liked Videos and Favorites.</li>
  <li>Treating every playlist view as sequential autoplay.</li>
  <li>Confusing traffic-source reporting with Playlist Analytics.</li>
  <li>Creating broad playlists with no viewer goal.</li>
  <li>Leaving outdated videos near the beginning.</li>
  <li>Assuming adding a video guarantees traffic.</li>
  <li>Expecting private or inactive playlists in public Top Playlist reports.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>What counts as Playlist traffic?</h4>
<p>Traffic from any playlist containing your video, including your own, another creator's, Liked Videos, and Favorites.</p>

<h4>Can another person's playlist send me views?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube's definition includes playlists owned by other creators and viewers.</p>

<h4>Does Playlist traffic mean the viewer watched the whole playlist?</h4>
<p>No. It only indicates that the video view came through a playlist context.</p>

<h4>Why is a playlist missing from Analytics?</h4>
<p>It may have no views in the selected period, be non-public for the report, or have insufficient reportable data.</p>
