<h2>YouTube Premium Revenue</h2>
<p><strong>YouTube Premium revenue</strong> is the creator revenue generated when paying YouTube Premium members watch eligible content. Premium viewers generally watch without ordinary YouTube ads, but part of their subscription fees is distributed to creators.</p>
<p>YouTube says Premium membership revenue is distributed to video creators based on how much members watch their content. It is a separate revenue source from advertising, even though both can appear together in YouTube Analytics revenue reports.</p>

<h3>How Does YouTube Premium Revenue Work?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>A viewer pays for a YouTube Premium membership.</li>
  <li>The member watches eligible videos, live content, or Shorts.</li>
  <li>YouTube allocates a portion of Premium membership revenue to creator content.</li>
  <li>The creator receives revenue according to the applicable monetization module and viewing allocation.</li>
  <li>Estimated earnings appear in YouTube Analytics and are later finalized through AdSense for YouTube.</li>
</ol>
<p>The creator does not need an ad to appear during the playback. Premium revenue exists specifically because Premium members receive an ad-free viewing experience while still supporting eligible creators.</p>

<h3>What Must Creators Accept?</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Content</th><th>Required Module</th><th>Additional Requirement</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Long-form videos</td><td>Watch Page Monetization Module</td><td>Turn on Watch Page ads for the eligible content</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Live streams and eligible live replays</td><td>Watch Page Monetization Module</td><td>Use the applicable Watch Page monetization settings</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Shorts</td><td>Shorts Monetization Module</td><td>Eligible engaged Shorts views after the module is accepted</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>Joining the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a> at an earlier fan-funding threshold does not automatically unlock Premium revenue. Creators must meet the higher YPP eligibility level for advertising and Premium revenue and accept the relevant contract module.</p>

<h3>Is Premium Revenue the Same as Ad Revenue?</h3>
<p>No. The two revenue streams come from different sources:</p>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Revenue Source</th><th>What Funds It?</th><th>What Triggers Creator Earnings?</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td><a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">Ad revenue</a></td><td>Advertiser spending</td><td>Eligible advertising activity around monetized viewing</td></tr>
    <tr><td>YouTube Premium revenue</td><td>Premium membership fees</td><td>Eligible Premium-member viewing of the creator's content</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Fan funding</td><td>Viewer purchases</td><td>Memberships, Supers, and Super Thanks transactions</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>A Premium playback does not become an <a href="/glossary/monetized-playbacks/">estimated monetized playback</a> merely because it earns subscription revenue. Monetized playbacks specifically concern playbacks with at least one ad impression.</p>

<h3>How Is Long-Form Premium Revenue Allocated?</h3>
<p>YouTube says Premium membership fees are distributed to creators based on how much Premium members watch their content. The public Help documentation does not provide one universal fixed creator percentage for every long-form Premium playback.</p>
<p>Do not apply the 55% Watch Page advertising share mechanically to Premium subscription revenue. The 55% figure in YouTube's earnings overview refers to net Watch Page ad revenue, while Premium revenue is described as an allocation from membership fees based on viewing.</p>

<h3>How Is Shorts Premium Revenue Calculated?</h3>
<p>For eligible Shorts, YouTube says:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Premium revenue is allocated based on the creator's share of subscription Shorts views within each country.</li>
  <li>A portion of Premium revenue is used to cover music-licensing costs.</li>
  <li>YouTube pays creators 45% of the net Premium revenue allocated to them for Shorts.</li>
</ul>
<p>This is related to, but separate from, the Shorts Feed advertising pool. Both systems use Shorts viewing allocation and account for music licensing, but one is funded by subscriptions and the other by ads shown between Shorts.</p>

<h3>Does Watch Time Matter?</h3>
<p>Yes. YouTube explicitly ties Premium creator distribution to how much members watch the creator's content. Longer viewing can therefore contribute more allocation than a brief click, subject to YouTube's calculation and the member's overall subscription activity.</p>
<p>This does not mean creators should artificially lengthen videos. A longer video that viewers abandon may produce less useful viewing than a shorter video that fully satisfies its audience.</p>

<h3>Do Offline Downloads Earn Premium Revenue?</h3>
<p>YouTube Premium allows members to download supported videos for offline mobile viewing and use background playback. YouTube describes these benefits as helping creators receive more watch time.</p>
<p>Offline and background activity must still be processed and attributed by YouTube. Creators should use their revenue reports rather than attempting to calculate income from download counts alone.</p>

<h3>Does Every Premium View Earn the Same Amount?</h3>
<p>No. YouTube does not publish one flat price per Premium view. The value can vary based on factors such as:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The subscriber's country or region</li>
  <li>The membership plan and recognized revenue</li>
  <li>How much eligible content the member watches</li>
  <li>The creator's share of that viewing</li>
  <li>Whether the content is long-form or Shorts</li>
  <li>Music-licensing allocation for Shorts</li>
  <li>Revenue adjustments, taxes, and applicable agreements</li>
</ul>

<h3>Where Can Creators See Premium Revenue?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Analytics</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open the <strong>Revenue</strong> tab.</li>
  <li>Review estimated revenue and the available revenue-source breakdown.</li>
  <li>Use Advanced Mode or content filters when deeper analysis is available.</li>
</ol>
<p>Revenue reports can combine sources in summary cards. Watch Page revenue reporting may include advertising and Premium revenue for the selected content and period, so review the displayed labels carefully.</p>

<h3>Why Is Premium Revenue Missing or Low?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The channel has not reached full ad-and-Premium YPP eligibility.</li>
  <li>The relevant monetization module was not accepted.</li>
  <li>Watch Page ads are not turned on for long-form content.</li>
  <li>The channel has few Premium viewers.</li>
  <li>Premium members watched only briefly.</li>
  <li>The content or views were ineligible under monetization policies.</li>
  <li>The Shorts views occurred before the Shorts Monetization Module was accepted.</li>
  <li>Estimated revenue has not finished processing.</li>
  <li>Adjustments, claims, invalid traffic, or disputes changed the estimate.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Does Turning Off Ads Stop Premium Revenue?</h3>
<p>For long-form content, YouTube's current instructions say creators must accept the Watch Page Monetization Module and turn on Watch Page ads to earn Premium revenue on that content. This is true even though Premium viewers themselves normally do not see those ads.</p>
<p>For Shorts, the creator must accept the Shorts Monetization Module. Shorts ad and Premium eligibility begin from the date the module is accepted rather than being applied retroactively to earlier views.</p>

<h3>Premium Revenue and Copyright Claims</h3>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/copyright-claim/">copyright claim</a> can affect who receives revenue. Rights holders may monetize claimed content, and music or third-party ownership can change the creator's entitlement.</p>
<p>Passing <a href="/glossary/copyright-checks/">Copyright Checks</a> does not guarantee that no later claim will affect Premium or advertising revenue.</p>

<h3>Premium Revenue vs. YouTube RPM</h3>
<p><a href="/glossary/youtube-rpm/">YouTube RPM</a> can include several creator revenue sources after YouTube's revenue share, including ads, memberships, YouTube Premium, Super Chat, and Super Stickers.</p>
<p>Premium revenue can therefore raise overall RPM even when fewer ads are shown. A change in RPM does not reveal the Premium contribution unless the creator examines the revenue-source breakdown.</p>

<h3>Can Creators Opt Out of YouTube Premium?</h3>
<p>Creators do not upload a separate Premium-only copy or charge Premium members directly. Eligible YouTube content remains available to ordinary ad-supported viewers and Premium members under the platform's viewing model.</p>
<p>Creators can opt out of optional monetization modules through YouTube's support process, but doing so removes the related earning opportunity rather than removing Premium members from the general audience.</p>

<h3>Common Premium-Revenue Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Assuming Premium members do not support creators because they see no ads.</li>
  <li>Treating Premium revenue as an ad impression.</li>
  <li>Applying the 55% Watch Page ad share directly to all Premium income.</li>
  <li>Assuming every Premium view has one fixed dollar value.</li>
  <li>Ignoring the separate Shorts Premium calculation.</li>
  <li>Expecting revenue from views before accepting the relevant module.</li>
  <li>Confusing Premium watch time with estimated monetized playbacks.</li>
  <li>Trying to calculate revenue from downloads without Analytics data.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Do creators get paid when Premium members watch?</h4>
<p>Yes, when the creator and content meet the applicable monetization requirements and the relevant contract module has been accepted.</p>

<h4>Is Premium revenue based on views or watch time?</h4>
<p>YouTube says membership revenue is distributed based on how much Premium members watch the creator's content.</p>

<h4>Do Premium viewers count toward watch time?</h4>
<p>Yes. Premium viewing, including supported background and offline viewing, can contribute viewing activity when YouTube processes it.</p>

<h4>What percentage do creators receive from Shorts Premium revenue?</h4>
<p>YouTube says creators receive 45% of the net Premium revenue allocated to them for eligible Shorts.</p>
