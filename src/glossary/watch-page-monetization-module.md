<h2>Watch Page Monetization Module</h2>
<p>The <strong>Watch Page Monetization Module</strong> is an optional YouTube Partner Program agreement that lets eligible creators earn advertising and YouTube Premium revenue from long-form videos and live-streaming content viewed on YouTube Watch Pages or in supported embedded YouTube players.</p>
<p>Accepting the module establishes the applicable revenue-sharing terms. It does not automatically make every video monetized, guarantee ads, or override copyright, advertiser-friendly, or other YouTube policies.</p>

<h3>What Is a YouTube Watch Page?</h3>
<p>YouTube defines the Watch Page as pages within YouTube, YouTube Music, and YouTube Kids dedicated to the description and playback of long-form or live-streaming videos.</p>
<p>The module also covers eligible public videos played inside the YouTube Video Player on other websites or applications. A video does not need to be watched only on youtube.com to fall under the Watch Page revenue model.</p>

<h3>What Revenue Does the Module Unlock?</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Revenue Source</th><th>How It Works</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td><a href="/glossary/watch-page-ads/">Watch Page ads</a></td><td>Eligible ads can appear before, during, after, or around public long-form and live content</td></tr>
    <tr><td><a href="/glossary/youtube-premium-revenue/">YouTube Premium revenue</a></td><td>A portion of Premium membership fees can be allocated when Premium members watch eligible content</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Embedded-player revenue</td><td>The Watch Page revenue share can apply when eligible public content is viewed through the YouTube player on other sites or apps</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>The module does not cover Shorts Feed ad revenue or fan-funding products. Those use separate contract modules.</p>

<h3>Who Can Accept the Module?</h3>
<p>The creator must qualify for the full advertising and YouTube Premium level of the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a>. Expanded-YPP access to fan funding at a lower threshold does not by itself unlock Watch Page revenue.</p>
<p>Creators must also:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Accept YouTube's Base Terms</li>
  <li>Meet the applicable subscriber and watch-hours or Shorts-view threshold</li>
  <li>Pass YPP channel review</li>
  <li>Live in a supported country or region</li>
  <li>Link an active AdSense for YouTube account</li>
  <li>Remain compliant with monetization policies</li>
</ul>

<h3>How Do You Accept It?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Earn</strong>.</li>
  <li>Find the Watch Page Ads earning option.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Get started</strong>.</li>
  <li>Review the Watch Page Monetization Module.</li>
  <li>Accept the terms.</li>
  <li>Turn on monetization for eligible videos or use channel upload defaults.</li>
</ol>
<p>Creators can view accepted agreements in YouTube Studio under Settings and Agreements when that interface is available.</p>

<h3>Does Acceptance Turn Ads On Automatically?</h3>
<p>No. Accepting the module makes Watch Page ad and Premium earning available. The creator still needs to turn on monetization for eligible videos.</p>
<p>YouTube also says choosing to turn on ads does not mean ads will automatically appear. Before ads serve, the content can undergo automated or human review for advertiser suitability.</p>

<h3>What Is the Creator Revenue Share?</h3>
<p>YouTube's published partner earnings overview states that creators who accept the module and turn on Watch Page ads receive <strong>55% of net revenue</strong> from qualifying ads displayed or streamed on their public Watch Page videos.</p>
<p>The same stated ad-revenue share applies when eligible public videos play inside the YouTube Video Player on other websites or applications.</p>
<p>The 55% figure applies to net qualifying advertising revenue. It should not automatically be applied to:</p>
<ul>
  <li>YouTube Premium membership allocation</li>
  <li>Shorts Feed advertising</li>
  <li>Channel memberships or Supers</li>
  <li>Gross advertiser spending before deductions</li>
  <li>Revenue controlled by copyright owners</li>
</ul>

<h3>How Does Premium Revenue Work Under the Module?</h3>
<p>To earn Premium revenue from long-form and live content, YouTube currently says creators must accept the Watch Page Monetization Module and turn on Watch Page ads for the content.</p>
<p>Premium viewers normally do not see ordinary ads. Their subscription-fee revenue is distributed based on eligible member viewing rather than an ad impression. The public module documentation does not state that every Premium playback uses the 55% advertising split.</p>

<h3>What Content Is Covered?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Eligible public long-form videos</li>
  <li>Eligible public live streams</li>
  <li>Eligible archived live-stream videos</li>
  <li>Public Watch Page playback through YouTube, YouTube Music, or YouTube Kids</li>
  <li>Eligible public playback through supported embedded YouTube players</li>
</ul>
<p>The module does not convert content watched in the Shorts Feed into Watch Page revenue. Shorts Feed ads use the Shorts Monetization Module.</p>

<h3>Public Visibility and the Module</h3>
<p>YouTube's stated 55% advertising share refers to ads on public videos. Private and Unlisted uploads can be processed and checked, but they are not ordinary public Watch Page inventory for broad ad serving.</p>
<p>Creators often upload as Private or Unlisted so processing and <a href="/glossary/ad-suitability-checks/">Ad Suitability Checks</a> can finish, then change the video to Public for monetized release.</p>

<h3>What Policies Apply?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>YouTube channel monetization policies</li>
  <li>Advertiser-friendly content guidelines</li>
  <li>Community Guidelines</li>
  <li>Copyright rules</li>
  <li>AdSense program policies</li>
  <li>YouTube's Terms of Service</li>
  <li>Any rights-clearance and country pass-through terms in the agreement</li>
</ul>
<p>Accepting the contract confirms that the creator has the necessary rights to the visual and audio elements being monetized.</p>

<h3>Watch Page Module vs. Shorts Module</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Watch Page Module</th><th>Shorts Module</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Primary content</td><td>Long-form and live content</td><td>Eligible Shorts in the Shorts Feed</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Ad location</td><td>Before, during, after, or around Watch Page playback</td><td>Between Shorts in the feed</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Published creator share</td><td>55% of net qualifying ad revenue</td><td>45% of allocated Creator Pool revenue</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Premium revenue</td><td>Eligible long-form and live Premium viewing</td><td>Eligible Shorts Premium viewing</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Retroactivity</td><td>Applies according to accepted terms and active monetization</td><td>Views before module acceptance are not eligible for Shorts sharing</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>Watch Page Module vs. Commerce Product Module</h3>
<p>The Watch Page module concerns advertising and Premium revenue. The <a href="/glossary/commerce-product-module/">Commerce Product Module</a> concerns fan funding such as memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, and Super Thanks.</p>
<p>A creator can accept one optional module without necessarily accepting every other available module. Each creates separate earning rights and obligations.</p>

<h3>Can Creators Opt Out?</h3>
<p>YouTube says creators can opt out of specific monetization modules after acceptance by contacting Creator Support.</p>
<p>Opting out removes future earning eligibility under that module. It does not necessarily stop YouTube from displaying ads under its general Terms of Service when the creator is no longer entitled to a revenue share.</p>

<h3>How Copyright Affects Module Earnings</h3>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a> claim can redirect, share, block, or otherwise affect revenue. The module gives the channel a contractual earning pathway; it does not prove the uploader owns every revenue right in a particular video.</p>
<p>Creator Music licenses, revenue-sharing tracks, and other rights arrangements can also modify the amount ultimately received.</p>

<h3>How Earnings Appear</h3>
<p>Estimated Watch Page and Premium revenue can appear in the Revenue tab of YouTube Analytics. YouTube's Watch Page ad revenue metric can include revenue from AdSense for YouTube ads, DoubleClick ads, and YouTube Premium for the selected range.</p>
<p>Finalized earnings are not finalized inside YouTube Analytics. They appear in AdSense for YouTube after the monthly finalization process.</p>

<h3>Common Watch Page Module Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Assuming module acceptance monetizes every video automatically.</li>
  <li>Applying the 55% advertising split to every Premium dollar.</li>
  <li>Using the module to describe Shorts Feed revenue.</li>
  <li>Ignoring public visibility and individual-video monetization settings.</li>
  <li>Assuming acceptance overrides copyright or ad-suitability rules.</li>
  <li>Confusing agreement acceptance with finalized payment.</li>
  <li>Believing embedded views cannot earn Watch Page revenue.</li>
  <li>Assuming a green monetization icon guarantees an ad on every view.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>What does the Watch Page Monetization Module unlock?</h4>
<p>It enables eligible advertising and YouTube Premium revenue for long-form and live content viewed on Watch Pages or supported embedded players.</p>

<h4>What percentage does YouTube pay?</h4>
<p>YouTube states that creators receive 55% of net qualifying Watch Page advertising revenue.</p>

<h4>Does accepting the module turn ads on?</h4>
<p>No. Creators still need to enable monetization, and YouTube decides whether eligible ads serve.</p>

<h4>Does it cover Shorts Feed ads?</h4>
<p>No. Shorts Feed ad and Premium revenue require the separate Shorts Monetization Module.</p>
