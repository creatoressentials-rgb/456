<h2>Clean HDMI</h2>
<p><strong>Clean HDMI</strong> is a camera's HDMI video output without visible menus, focus boxes, battery icons, exposure data, recording indicators, or other interface graphics over the image.</p>
<p>A clean feed is useful when connecting a camera to a capture card, external recorder, video switcher, monitor, or livestreaming system. Viewers receive the camera image rather than the information normally shown to the camera operator.</p>

<h3>What Makes an HDMI Output "Clean"?</h3>
<p>The defining feature is the absence of unwanted on-screen overlays. Depending on the camera, the setting may be called <em>HDMI Info Display Off</em>, <em>Output Display Off</em>, <em>Clean Output</em>, or something similar.</p>
<p>Clean HDMI does not necessarily mean that every graphic is unavailable. Some cameras can selectively output timecode, recording status, or other information. For livestreaming, the usual goal is a full-frame picture with no operator interface visible to the audience.</p>

<p>"Clean" describes the absence of overlays. It is not a complete quality specification. Two cameras can both provide clean HDMI while offering very different resolution, frame rate, bit depth, color sampling, autofocus, and recording options.</p>

<h3>How Is Clean HDMI Used?</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Livestreaming:</strong> Send a mirrorless camera or camcorder into a capture card without displaying camera controls.</li>
  <li><strong>Video conferencing:</strong> Use a camera as a high-quality webcam through HDMI.</li>
  <li><strong>External recording:</strong> Record the camera's output on a compatible monitor-recorder.</li>
  <li><strong>Multi-camera production:</strong> Feed several clean camera signals into a video switcher.</li>
  <li><strong>On-set monitoring:</strong> Display the picture on a larger screen without distracting operator information.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to Check Whether a Camera Has Clean HDMI</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Read the camera's official specifications and manual.</li>
  <li>Look for settings related to HDMI information, output display, external recording, or display overlays.</li>
  <li>Confirm the output resolution and frame rates for both standby and recording modes.</li>
  <li>Check whether internal recording can continue while clean HDMI is active.</li>
  <li>Verify autofocus, face detection, and overheating behavior during a long test.</li>
  <li>Connect the actual camera, cable, capture device, and software you plan to use.</li>
  <li>Run a full-length test rather than assuming a brief preview proves the setup is reliable.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Clean HDMI Setup for YouTube Creators</h3>
<p>A basic camera-to-computer setup usually includes:</p>
<ul>
  <li>A camera with a compatible clean HDMI mode</li>
  <li>The correct full-size, mini, or micro HDMI cable</li>
  <li>A capture card that supports the camera's output format</li>
  <li>Continuous power or a sufficiently long-lasting battery solution</li>
  <li>A stable <a href="/glossary/tripod/">tripod</a> or camera support</li>
  <li>Streaming or recording software</li>
  <li>A separate microphone when the camera's HDMI audio is not suitable</li>
</ul>

<h3>Common Clean-HDMI Problems</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Icons remain visible:</strong> The HDMI information-display setting may still be enabled.</li>
  <li><strong>The image changes when recording starts:</strong> The camera may use different HDMI modes during standby and internal recording.</li>
  <li><strong>The camera screen turns off:</strong> Some models route the display exclusively to HDMI in certain modes.</li>
  <li><strong>The feed shuts down:</strong> Power saving, heat, battery limits, or recording restrictions may interrupt long sessions.</li>
  <li><strong>The capture card shows no signal:</strong> The camera may be outputting an unsupported resolution, frame rate, HDR mode, or color format.</li>
  <li><strong>Autofocus boxes appear:</strong> Some cameras remove exposure information but still output focus indicators unless another setting is changed.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Is clean HDMI the same as uncompressed HDMI?</h4>
<p>No. Clean HDMI means the visible camera interface has been removed. Compression, bit depth, chroma sampling, resolution, and other signal properties are separate specifications.</p>

<h4>Can every camera provide clean HDMI?</h4>
<p>No. Some cameras always output interface graphics, offer clean output only in certain modes, or apply restrictions during internal recording.</p>

<h4>Does clean HDMI include sound?</h4>
<p>It can, but not always. Confirm that the camera embeds audio over HDMI and that the capture device accepts it. Many creators still use a dedicated microphone connected elsewhere.</p>

<h4>Can a clean HDMI camera run all day?</h4>
<p>Not automatically. Long sessions depend on continuous power, heat management, sleep settings, cable reliability, and any camera-specific runtime limits.</p>
