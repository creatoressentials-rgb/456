<h2>Usage Rights</h2>
<p><strong>Usage rights</strong> are the permissions a creator gives another party to use specified content in specified ways.</p>
<p>In creator contracts, the phrase normally refers to a <a href="/glossary/content-licensing">content license</a>. The brand may receive permission to repost, publish, edit, advertise, distribute, display, or otherwise use a video, Short, photograph, recording, or other asset.</p>
<p>Usage rights do not automatically equal <a href="/glossary/content-ownership">content ownership</a>. A creator can remain the copyright owner while granting the brand a limited license.</p>
<h3>Why usage rights need a defined scope</h3>
<p>A statement such as “the brand receives usage rights” is incomplete. It does not explain:</p>
<ul>
<li>Which content is covered</li>
<li>Which party can use it</li>
<li>Where it can appear</li>
<li>Whether use is organic or paid</li>
<li>How long permission lasts</li>
<li>Which countries are covered</li>
<li>Whether the brand can edit it</li>
<li>Whether agencies or retailers can use it</li>
<li>Whether the creator's name and likeness are included</li>
<li>Whether the rights are exclusive</li>
<li>Whether raw files are included</li>
<li>What happens after expiration</li>
</ul>
<p>Each of these terms can materially affect the value of the deal.</p>
<h3>Common types of usage rights</h3>
<h4>Organic social usage</h4>
<p>The brand may repost or publish the content on its unpaid social profiles.</p>
<p>The agreement should state whether the brand can:</p>
<ul>
<li>Download and re-upload the asset</li>
<li>Use the platform's repost or collaboration tools</li>
<li>Add captions or links</li>
<li>Leave the post visible after the active campaign ends</li>
<li>Publish through regional or retailer accounts</li>
</ul>
<h4>Website and ecommerce usage</h4>
<p>The brand may use the asset on:</p>
<ul>
<li>Its website</li>
<li>Product pages</li>
<li>Landing pages</li>
<li>Blog posts</li>
<li>Online marketplaces</li>
<li>Retailer pages</li>
<li>Email campaigns</li>
</ul>
<p>A product-page endorsement can remain commercially active long after the creator's original post, so term and update obligations matter.</p>
<h4><a href="/glossary/paid-usage/">Paid usage</a></h4>
<p>The brand may use the content in advertising. See <a href="/glossary/paid-usage">paid usage</a>.</p>
<p>Paid use can include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Social ads</li>
<li>Boosted posts</li>
<li>Search or display advertising</li>
<li>Retail media</li>
<li>Connected television</li>
<li>Sponsored email placements</li>
<li>Ads using the creator's handle or identity</li>
</ul>
<p>Paid usage is normally broader and more valuable than leaving an old organic repost online.</p>
<h4>Broadcast, print, and out-of-home usage</h4>
<p>The content or creator identity may appear in:</p>
<ul>
<li>Television advertising</li>
<li>Streaming-video ads</li>
<li>Radio</li>
<li>Print</li>
<li>Billboards</li>
<li>In-store displays</li>
<li>Trade-show materials</li>
<li>Packaging</li>
</ul>
<p>These media should not be assumed from a social-media campaign.</p>
<h4>Internal usage</h4>
<p>The brand may use content for internal presentations, sales meetings, training, investor materials, or research. Internal rights should still identify who can access the asset and whether external publication is prohibited.</p>

<h3>The elements of a usage-rights clause</h3>
<h4>Licensed content</h4>
<p>Identify each asset, version, cut, photograph, post, or raw file. Avoid grants covering every piece of content the creator makes unless that scope is intentional.</p>
<h4>Permitted purpose</h4>
<p>State whether the content may be used for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Organic marketing</li>
<li>Paid advertising</li>
<li>Public relations</li>
<li>Ecommerce</li>
<li>Internal use</li>
<li>Retailer support</li>
<li>Training</li>
<li>Editorial publication</li>
<li>Another specified purpose</li>
</ul>
<h4>Media and platforms</h4>
<p>List the actual channels. “Digital media” can be broader than YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok.</p>
<h4>Term</h4>
<p>Use a start date and end date, such as 90 days after first ad launch. If the right has no expiration, it is a form of <a href="/glossary/perpetual-usage-rights">perpetual usage rights</a>.</p>
<h4>Territory</h4>
<p>Define the geographic market. Worldwide rights can carry more value and risk than a U.S.-only campaign.</p>
<h4>Editing and derivative works</h4>
<p>State whether the brand can:</p>
<ul>
<li>Crop and resize</li>
<li>Add captions</li>
<li>Translate or dub</li>
<li>Change music</li>
<li>Create still images</li>
<li>Combine clips</li>
<li>Add new claims</li>
<li>Generate alternate hooks</li>
<li>Use artificial-intelligence tools</li>
<li>Create synthetic voice or likeness versions</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator may require approval for changes that alter meaning.</p>
<h4>Sublicensing</h4>
<p>Define whether the brand can authorize:</p>
<ul>
<li>Advertising agencies</li>
<li>Retailers</li>
<li>Distributors</li>
<li>Affiliates</li>
<li>Parent companies</li>
<li>Subsidiaries</li>
<li>Media partners</li>
<li>Other third parties</li>
</ul>
<h4>Exclusivity</h4>
<p>A nonexclusive license allows the creator to grant similar rights elsewhere. An exclusive license gives the recipient exclusive control of the specified rights within the grant.</p>
<p>Under U.S. copyright law, an exclusive license is treated as a transfer of copyright ownership in the rights granted, while a nonexclusive license is not.</p>
<h4>Identity rights</h4>
<p>Permission to use the content does not always answer whether the brand may separately use the creator's:</p>
<ul>
<li>Name</li>
<li>Handle</li>
<li>Photograph</li>
<li>Voice</li>
<li>Likeness</li>
<li>Biography</li>
<li>Logo</li>
<li>Testimonial</li>
<li>Audience data</li>
</ul>
<p>These rights should be addressed directly.</p>
<h3>Platform permissions are not the full license</h3>
<p>YouTube brand partner access can let a brand view specified metrics and promote eligible creator videos. YouTube advises creators to discuss usage rights and obtain the necessary agreements independently.</p>
<p>Meta similarly requires partnership-ad permission when an advertiser wants to run an ad using a partner's handle. That technical permission does not define compensation, ownership, editing, territory, or all contractual remedies.</p>
<p>A platform button can activate a capability. The written agreement should define whether the brand is legally entitled to use it.</p>
<h3>Usage rights and raw files</h3>
<p>Rights to the final content do not automatically include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Raw footage</li>
<li>Unused takes</li>
<li>Project files</li>
<li>Layered graphics</li>
<li>Scripts</li>
<li>Thumbnail source files</li>
<li>Audio sessions</li>
<li>Production notes</li>
<li>Templates and presets</li>
</ul>
<p>List these as separate <a href="/glossary/deliverables">deliverables</a>, then define how the brand may use them.</p>
<h3>Usage-rights pricing factors</h3>
<p>The fee can depend on:</p>
<ul>
<li>Organic or paid use</li>
<li>Duration</li>
<li>Platforms</li>
<li>Territory</li>
<li>Advertising spend</li>
<li>Editing rights</li>
<li>Sublicensing</li>
<li>Exclusivity</li>
<li>Creator identity</li>
<li>Raw files</li>
<li>Number of assets</li>
<li>Product category</li>
<li>Renewal rights</li>
<li>Risk of long-term association</li>
</ul>
<p>There is no universal usage-rights percentage. A brand's request should be priced based on the actual commercial permission.</p>
<h3>Usage-rights red flags</h3>
<p>Review language such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>All rights in perpetuity</li>
<li>All media now known or later developed</li>
<li>Worldwide, irrevocable, transferable, and sublicensable</li>
<li>Unlimited edits</li>
<li>Rights to every draft and raw file</li>
<li>Use of name, image, voice, and likeness for any purpose</li>
<li>Artificial-intelligence training rights</li>
<li>Ownership hidden inside a usage clause</li>
<li>Paid advertising without a term</li>
<li>Rights beginning before full payment</li>
<li>No obligation to stop active ads</li>
<li>No restriction on third-party use</li>
</ul>
<p>A broad license may be appropriate at the right price, but it should be understood rather than treated as boilerplate.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Are usage rights the same as ownership?</h4>
<p>No. Usage rights can grant permission while the creator retains copyright ownership.</p>
<h4>Does a brand need usage rights to repost creator content?</h4>
<p>Generally, the brand should have permission through the contract, platform tool, or another valid license. Tagging the brand does not automatically grant unlimited reuse.</p>
<h4>Are paid ads included in ordinary usage rights?</h4>
<p>Only if the agreement includes them. Organic reposting and paid advertising should be defined separately.</p>
<h4>Can usage rights include the creator's likeness?</h4>
<p>They can, but the agreement should say so. Copyright permission for a video and permission to use the creator's identity are related but distinct issues.</p>
