<h2>Recurring Revenue</h2>
<p><strong>Recurring revenue</strong> is income a creator or business can reasonably expect to receive repeatedly on an agreed schedule while the customer or member relationship remains active.</p>
<p>Common schedules include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Monthly</li>
<li>Annual</li>
<li>Quarterly</li>
<li>Weekly</li>
<li>Per billing cycle</li>
<li>Another contractually defined interval</li>
</ul>
<p>Creator recurring revenue can come from:</p>
<ul>
<li>Memberships</li>
<li>Paid newsletters</li>
<li>Software subscriptions</li>
<li>Communities</li>
<li>Retainers</li>
<li>Licensing</li>
<li>Subscription courses</li>
<li>Product boxes</li>
<li>Recurring <a href="/glossary/affiliate-commission/">affiliate commission</a></li>
<li>Ongoing <a href="/glossary/sponsorship/">sponsorship</a> agreements</li>
<li>Maintenance plans</li>
</ul>
<p>Recurring revenue is predictable relative to one-time sales, but it is not guaranteed.</p>
<h3>How recurring revenue works</h3>
<p>A recurring-revenue relationship normally has:</p>
<ol>
<li>A continuing offer</li>
<li>A customer or member</li>
<li>A billing interval</li>
<li>Permission or contract for renewal</li>
<li>Ongoing value or access</li>
<li>A cancellation or expiration process</li>
<li>Payment processing</li>
<li>Customer <a href="/glossary/retention/">retention</a></li>
</ol>
<p>When the customer cancels, fails to pay, downgrades, or does not renew, the expected revenue changes.</p>
<h3>Recurring revenue vs. repeat revenue</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>Recurring revenue</th>
  <th>Repeat revenue</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Customer has an ongoing subscription, membership, or contract</td>
  <td>Customer independently chooses to buy again</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Future payments are expected under the relationship</td>
  <td>Future purchase is not scheduled</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Cancellation or nonrenewal ends the stream</td>
  <td>No cancellation is needed</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>MRR or ARR can often be calculated</td>
  <td>Usually counted as ordinary repeat sales</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Example: $10 monthly membership</td>
  <td>Example: buyer purchases two separate templates months apart</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>A creator with loyal customers can have repeat sales without contractual recurring revenue.</p>

<p>A membership is recurring revenue even when it requires substantial active work.</p>
<h3>Common creator recurring-revenue models</h3>
<h4>Membership</h4>
<p>Members pay for ongoing content, community, access, recognition, or benefits.</p>
<p>Examples:</p>
<ul>
<li><a href="/glossary/patreon-membership-model">Patreon membership</a></li>
<li><a href="/glossary/channel-memberships">YouTube channel memberships</a></li>
<li>Paid Discord community</li>
<li>Private podcast</li>
<li>Member newsletter</li>
</ul>
<h4>Subscription content</h4>
<p>Customers pay for ongoing delivery or access.</p>
<p>Examples:</p>
<ul>
<li>Newsletter</li>
<li>Course library</li>
<li>Research</li>
<li>Templates</li>
<li>Resource database</li>
<li>Stock library</li>
</ul>
<h4>Software or app</h4>
<p>Customers pay monthly or annually to use a tool.</p>
<h4>Retainer</h4>
<p>A brand or client pays a recurring amount for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Consulting</li>
<li>Production capacity</li>
<li>Strategy</li>
<li>Editing</li>
<li>Channel management</li>
<li>Sponsorship access</li>
<li>Ongoing <a href="/glossary/deliverables/">deliverables</a></li>
</ul>
<p>A retainer should define included capacity and unused work.</p>
<h4>Recurring affiliate commission</h4>
<p>A creator receives commission on eligible subscription renewals from referred customers.</p>
<p>The program must define duration, eligibility, cancellations, and post-termination payments.</p>
<h4>Licensing</h4>
<p>A brand pays regularly to use content, intellectual property, or a product.</p>
<h4>Product subscription</h4>
<p>The creator sells a recurring physical or digital bundle.</p>
<h3>Monthly recurring revenue</h3>
<p><strong>Monthly recurring revenue</strong>, or <strong>MRR</strong>, is a normalized measure of recurring revenue expected for one month.</p>
<p>A simplified calculation can add the monthly value of active subscriptions.</p>
<p>Examples:</p>
<ul>
<li>100 members at $10 per month = $1,000 MRR</li>
<li>10 annual members paying $120 per year can be normalized to $100 MRR</li>
<li>One-time setup fees are normally excluded</li>
<li>One-time product purchases are normally excluded</li>
</ul>
<p>MRR is a management metric, not the same as:</p>
<ul>
<li>Cash collected this month</li>
<li>Accounting revenue recognized this month</li>
<li>Gross sales</li>
<li>Profit</li>
<li>Bank balance</li>
</ul>
<p>Stripe explicitly notes that MRR is not GAAP revenue.</p>
<h3>Annual recurring revenue</h3>
<p><strong>Annual recurring revenue</strong>, or <strong>ARR</strong>, is recurring revenue normalized to an annual amount.</p>
<p>A simplified subscription business may calculate:</p>
<p><strong>ARR = MRR × 12</strong></p>
<p>That shortcut is not always accurate when:</p>
<ul>
<li>Memberships are seasonal</li>
<li>Contracts have variable usage charges</li>
<li>Annual plans include discounts</li>
<li>Churn is high</li>
<li>One-time fees are mixed in</li>
<li>Revenue is not truly recurring</li>
<li>Foreign currency fluctuates</li>
</ul>
<p>ARR should represent recurring contract or subscription value, not every dollar earned during a calendar year.</p>
<h3>Annual prepayment</h3>
<p>A creator can receive one annual payment upfront while recognizing the business obligation over the year.</p>
<p>For example:</p>
<ul>
<li>Member pays $120 in July</li>
<li>Cash received in July: $120</li>
<li>Normalized MRR: $10</li>
<li>Membership access owed: 12 months</li>
</ul>
<p>Cash, MRR, tax reporting, and accounting recognition can differ.</p>
<p>Creators should avoid spending all annual prepayments without reserving resources to deliver future benefits.</p>
<h3>What changes MRR?</h3>
<p>Stripe describes four major MRR movements:</p>
<h4>New revenue</h4>
<p>New customer or member joins.</p>
<h4>Expansion</h4>
<p>Existing customer upgrades, adds seats, or pays more.</p>
<h4>Contraction</h4>
<p>Existing customer downgrades or receives a recurring discount.</p>
<h4>Churn</h4>
<p>Customer cancels or moves from recurring revenue to zero.</p>
<p>Failed payments can create involuntary churn even when the customer did not intend to cancel.</p>
<h3>Churn</h3>
<p><strong>Churn</strong> measures customers or recurring revenue lost during a period.</p>
<p>Creators can track:</p>
<ul>
<li>Member churn</li>
<li>Revenue churn</li>
<li>Voluntary cancellation</li>
<li>Failed-payment churn</li>
<li>Downgrade</li>
<li>Annual nonrenewal</li>
<li>Free-trial conversion</li>
</ul>
<p>A membership can add many new people while remaining unhealthy if nearly as many cancel.</p>
<h3>Gross vs. net recurring revenue retention</h3>
<h4>Gross retention</h4>
<p>Measures how much recurring revenue remains from existing customers before expansion.</p>
<p>It reflects:</p>
<ul>
<li>Churn</li>
<li>Downgrades</li>
<li>Contraction</li>
</ul>
<h4>Net retention</h4>
<p>Includes expansion from existing customers.</p>
<p>A creator with tier upgrades can have stronger net retention than gross retention.</p>
<p>These metrics are more common in subscription software but can help larger creator memberships and communities understand sustainability.</p>
<h3>Recurring revenue quality</h3>
<p>Not all recurring revenue is equally valuable.</p>
<p>Higher-quality recurring revenue may have:</p>
<ul>
<li>Strong retention</li>
<li>Low refund rate</li>
<li>Low fulfillment cost</li>
<li>Diverse customers</li>
<li>Clear value</li>
<li>Stable pricing</li>
<li>Reliable payments</li>
<li>Low support burden</li>
<li>Annual commitment</li>
<li>Direct customer relationship</li>
</ul>
<p>Lower-quality recurring revenue may depend on:</p>
<ul>
<li>One large sponsor</li>
<li>Constant discounts</li>
<li>Expensive physical benefits</li>
<li>High churn</li>
<li>One platform</li>
<li>High failed-payment rate</li>
<li>Benefits that create burnout</li>
<li>Customers who do not use the product</li>
<li>A short-term promotional spike</li>
</ul>
<h3>Recurring revenue and fees</h3>
<p>Gross recurring revenue can be reduced by:</p>
<ul>
<li>Platform fees</li>
<li>Payment processing</li>
<li>App-store fees</li>
<li>Refunds</li>
<li>Chargebacks</li>
<li>Sales tax or VAT</li>
<li>Currency conversion</li>
<li>Benefit fulfillment</li>
<li>Software</li>
<li>Moderation</li>
<li>Contractors</li>
<li>Customer support</li>
<li>Failed payments</li>
</ul>
<p>Creators should track net contribution, not only MRR.</p>
<h3>Membership recurring revenue</h3>
<p>The <a href="/glossary/patreon-membership-model">Patreon or membership model</a> can provide monthly or annual recurring revenue.</p>
<p>However, membership revenue changes through:</p>
<ul>
<li>New members</li>
<li>Upgrades</li>
<li>Downgrades</li>
<li>Cancellations</li>
<li>Annual renewals</li>
<li>Failed payments</li>
<li>Refunds</li>
<li>Price changes</li>
<li>Free-to-paid conversion</li>
</ul>
<p>A recurring billing system does not guarantee member retention.</p>
<h3>How to improve recurring revenue</h3>
<p>Creators can:</p>
<ul>
<li>Improve onboarding</li>
<li>Set a clear membership promise</li>
<li>Deliver benefits consistently</li>
<li>Reduce benefit complexity</li>
<li>Offer annual plans</li>
<li>Recover failed payments</li>
<li>Ask why members cancel</li>
<li>Build an archive</li>
<li>Improve community</li>
<li>Add upgrade paths</li>
<li>Avoid overdiscounting</li>
<li>Track cohort retention</li>
<li>Diversify acquisition</li>
<li>Keep direct customer contact</li>
<li>Price sustainably</li>
</ul>
<p>Growth cannot permanently compensate for severe churn.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">ad revenue</a> recurring revenue?</h4>
<p>Usually not in the subscription sense. Ads can generate repeated revenue, but no individual customer normally commits to recurring payments.</p>
<h4>Is MRR the money collected this month?</h4>
<p>No. MRR is a normalized management metric for recurring value. Cash collected can include annual prepayments and one-time charges.</p>
<h4>Is recurring revenue guaranteed?</h4>
<p>No. Customers can cancel, downgrade, fail payment, request refunds, or not renew.</p>
<h4>Are retainers recurring revenue?</h4>
<p>Yes when a client is contractually expected to pay a recurring amount for ongoing services or reserved capacity.</p>
