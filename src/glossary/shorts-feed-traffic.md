<h2>Shorts Feed Traffic</h2>
<p><strong>Shorts Feed traffic</strong> is YouTube Analytics traffic generated when viewers watch a Short through YouTube's vertical Shorts viewing experience.</p>
<p>YouTube's traffic-source documentation describes this source as traffic from the Shorts vertical viewing experience. It tells creators where a view originated, not how long the viewer watched or whether the playback became an engaged view.</p>

<h3>Where Does Shorts Feed Traffic Come From?</h3>
<p>A viewer can enter the Shorts player through several YouTube surfaces and then continue swiping through the feed. Examples include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Tapping the Shorts tab in the YouTube app</li>
  <li>Selecting a Short from a Shorts shelf on Home</li>
  <li>Opening a Short from search results</li>
  <li>Selecting a Short from a channel page</li>
  <li>Following a link that opens the Short in the vertical player</li>
  <li>Continuing to the next Short by swiping</li>
</ul>
<p>Once a playback is attributed to the vertical Shorts experience, the resulting traffic can appear under Shorts Feed.</p>

<h3>Shorts Feed Traffic vs. Shown in Feed</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Metric</th><th>What It Measures</th><th>Core Question</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Shorts Feed traffic</td><td>Views attributed to the Shorts vertical viewing experience</td><td>Where did the views come from?</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Shown in feed</td><td>The number of times the Short appeared in the Shorts Feed</td><td>How often was the Short presented in the feed?</td></tr>
    <tr><td><a href="/glossary/stayed-to-watch/">Stayed to watch</a></td><td>The percentage of feed presentations where viewers stayed past the initial seconds rather than swiping away</td><td>How often did viewers choose to continue?</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>Shown in feed is an exposure count. Shorts Feed traffic is an attributed view source. The numbers should not be expected to match one-for-one.</p>

<h3>Shorts Feed Traffic vs. Total Shorts Views</h3>
<p>A Short can receive views from several sources:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Shorts Feed</li>
  <li>YouTube Search</li>
  <li>Channel Pages</li>
  <li>Browse Features</li>
  <li>Suggested Videos</li>
  <li>Playlists</li>
  <li>External websites and apps</li>
  <li>Direct or Unknown</li>
  <li>Notifications</li>
  <li>Sound pages and remixes</li>
</ul>
<p>Total Shorts views can therefore be higher than Shorts Feed traffic. A Short does not need to receive most of its views from the feed to be a legitimate Short.</p>

<h3>Shorts Feed Traffic vs. Engaged Views</h3>
<p><a href="/glossary/engaged-views/">Engaged views</a> count how many times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short, excluding loops. Shorts Feed traffic identifies the source of the playback.</p>
<p>A feed playback can contribute to the newer Shorts Views count when the Short begins playing, even when the viewer swipes quickly. It may not qualify as an engaged view.</p>

<h3>Where Can You Find the Report?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Analytics</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open the <strong>Content</strong> tab.</li>
  <li>Select the <strong>Shorts</strong> chip.</li>
  <li>Find <strong>How viewers find your Shorts</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open the expanded report or Advanced Mode for more detail.</li>
</ol>
<p>At the individual-Short level, open the Short's Analytics and review its Reach or content-discovery reporting.</p>

<h3>Does Shorts Feed Traffic Count as Thumbnail Impressions?</h3>
<p>The Shorts Feed uses a full-screen, auto-playing vertical experience rather than the standard thumbnail-click funnel. Creators should use <strong>Shown in feed</strong> and <strong>How many chose to view</strong> to understand feed exposure and viewer choice.</p>
<p>Do not use ordinary impressions click-through rate as the primary feed metric. Registered YouTube impressions are built around eligible thumbnail displays, while Shorts Feed viewing begins through the vertical player.</p>

<h3>What Does High Shorts Feed Traffic Mean?</h3>
<p>A high share can mean YouTube is finding viewers for the Short within the vertical feed. It does not by itself prove that viewers watched deeply or were satisfied.</p>
<p>Review it with:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Shown in feed</li>
  <li>Stayed to watch</li>
  <li>Engaged views</li>
  <li>Average view duration</li>
  <li>Average percentage viewed</li>
  <li>Likes and comments</li>
  <li>Subscribers gained</li>
  <li>Return viewing</li>
</ul>

<h3>What Does Low Shorts Feed Traffic Mean?</h3>
<p>Low feed traffic can have several explanations:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The Short received more traffic from Search, Channel Pages, or External sources.</li>
  <li>YouTube has not shown the Short broadly in the feed.</li>
  <li>The topic has a narrow audience.</li>
  <li>The content is being discovered through an existing subscriber base.</li>
  <li>The Short is new and reporting is still developing.</li>
  <li>The content has eligibility, copyright, age, or policy restrictions.</li>
  <li>The upload was not classified or distributed as expected.</li>
</ul>
<p>Low feed traffic is not proof of a shadow ban or permanent channel penalty.</p>

<h3>Does Every Short Get Tested in the Feed?</h3>
<p>YouTube does not publish a guarantee that every Short receives a particular number of feed presentations, a fixed test audience, or a distribution window of a specific length.</p>
<p>Creators should avoid claims such as:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Every Short receives exactly 500 test impressions.</li>
  <li>YouTube always decides a Short's future within one hour.</li>
  <li>A specific swipe rate instantly stops all distribution.</li>
  <li>Deleting and re-uploading resets a guaranteed feed test.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How Shorts Feed Recommendations Work</h3>
<p>YouTube recommendations are personalized. The platform can consider viewer history, interests, feedback, and how similar viewers respond to content.</p>
<p>For creators, useful response signals include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Whether viewers stay past the opening</li>
  <li>How long engaged viewers watch</li>
  <li>Whether they replay</li>
  <li>Likes, dislikes, shares, and comments</li>
  <li>Not interested or other negative feedback</li>
  <li>Whether viewers continue using YouTube satisfactorily</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube does not publish the exact weighting of these signals for Shorts Feed distribution.</p>

<h3>Shorts Feed Traffic and Search Traffic</h3>
<p>Search traffic can be valuable even when feed traffic is modest. A how-to or troubleshooting Short may attract viewers with a strong specific need through <a href="/glossary/youtube-search-terms/">YouTube Search Terms</a>.</p>
<p>Compare the audiences separately. Feed viewers are often deciding instantly while scrolling, whereas Search viewers intentionally selected a result.</p>

<h3>Shorts Feed Traffic and External Sharing</h3>
<p><a href="/glossary/external-traffic/">External Traffic</a> can introduce viewers from Google Search, social platforms, messages, websites, and embeds. If the link opens in the Shorts player, later viewing behavior may involve the vertical feed, but the original attributed view can still reflect the path YouTube records.</p>
<p>Do not assume every Short opened from another platform will appear as Shorts Feed traffic.</p>

<h3>Shorts Feed Traffic and Monetization</h3>
<p>Ads shown between Shorts in the feed fund the Shorts Creator Pool. Eligible revenue sharing requires the <a href="/glossary/shorts-monetization-module/">Shorts Monetization Module</a> and uses eligible engaged views rather than the broader post-2025 public Shorts Views count.</p>
<p>Receiving Shorts Feed traffic does not automatically mean the channel or views are monetization-eligible.</p>

<h3>How to Improve Shorts Feed Performance</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Make the subject understandable immediately.</li>
  <li>Start with movement, information, conflict, or a clear result when appropriate.</li>
  <li>Remove unnecessary greetings and setup.</li>
  <li>Use readable on-screen text.</li>
  <li>Deliver the title's promise quickly.</li>
  <li>Keep pacing appropriate to the idea rather than cutting randomly.</li>
  <li>Use clear audio and framing.</li>
  <li>Study several Shorts instead of one isolated upload.</li>
  <li>Compare Shorts with similar topics and lengths.</li>
</ol>

<h3>How to Analyze the Funnel</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Check Shown in feed for exposure.</li>
  <li>Check Viewed vs. Swiped Away for the initial decision.</li>
  <li>Check engaged views for meaningful starts.</li>
  <li>Review retention among engaged viewers.</li>
  <li>Review likes, comments, shares, and subscribers.</li>
  <li>Compare traffic sources.</li>
  <li>Identify whether the problem is distribution, opening appeal, or deeper retention.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Common Shorts-Feed-Traffic Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Calling Shown in feed the same thing as feed views.</li>
  <li>Assuming every Shorts view came from the feed.</li>
  <li>Using thumbnail CTR as the main feed-selection metric.</li>
  <li>Confusing feed traffic with engaged views.</li>
  <li>Assuming low traffic proves a shadow ban.</li>
  <li>Believing YouTube publishes a fixed test-audience formula.</li>
  <li>Ignoring Search, External, and Channel Pages traffic.</li>
  <li>Judging performance from traffic volume without watch quality.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>What is Shorts Feed traffic?</h4>
<p>It is traffic from YouTube's vertical Shorts viewing experience.</p>

<h4>Is Shorts Feed traffic the same as Shown in feed?</h4>
<p>No. Shown in feed counts presentations; Shorts Feed traffic attributes views to the vertical experience.</p>

<h4>Do all Shorts views come from the feed?</h4>
<p>No. Shorts can also receive Search, Channel Pages, Browse, Suggested, Playlist, External, and other traffic.</p>

<h4>Does feed traffic guarantee revenue?</h4>
<p>No. Monetization requires YPP eligibility, the Shorts module, eligible content, and eligible engaged views.</p>
