<h2>Affiliate <a href="/glossary/dropped-frames/">Network</a></h2>
<p>An <strong>affiliate network</strong> is a company or platform that connects advertisers with affiliates and provides infrastructure for managing affiliate relationships.</p>
<p>A network may help with:</p>
<ul>
<li>Advertiser discovery</li>
<li>Affiliate applications</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/affiliate-link">Affiliate link</a> generation</li>
<li>Click and <a href="/glossary/conversion-tracking/">conversion tracking</a></li>
<li><a href="/glossary/attribution/">Attribution</a></li>
<li>Commission calculation</li>
<li>Reporting</li>
<li>Payments</li>
<li>Compliance</li>
<li>Fraud prevention</li>
<li>Program communication</li>
<li>Technical support</li>
</ul>
<p>The network is not the same as the individual advertiser's <a href="/glossary/affiliate-program/">affiliate program</a>.</p>
<h3>How an affiliate network works</h3>
<p>A typical process is:</p>
<ol>
<li>An advertiser joins or launches a program through the network.</li>
<li>A creator creates a publisher or affiliate account.</li>
<li>The creator lists approved promotional properties, such as a channel, website, newsletter, or social profile.</li>
<li>The creator applies to individual advertiser programs.</li>
<li>Each advertiser approves or declines the application.</li>
<li>The creator receives links, codes, product data, or other promotional tools.</li>
<li>The network tracks clicks and qualifying actions.</li>
<li>The advertiser validates transactions.</li>
<li>The network calculates and reports <a href="/glossary/affiliate-commission">affiliate commission</a>.</li>
<li>The network facilitates payment according to its rules.</li>
</ol>
<p>Joining the network does not normally guarantee approval by every advertiser.</p>

<p>A creator is often subject to both sets of terms.</p>
<h3>Affiliate network vs. direct program</h3>
<h4>Network program</h4>
<p>The creator manages several brands through one platform and may receive consolidated reporting or payments.</p>
<h4>Direct program</h4>
<p>The creator joins a program operated by the merchant or its private software without a public affiliate marketplace.</p>
<p>Direct programs can offer closer relationships or custom terms, but the creator may need separate accounts, reports, tax forms, and payment thresholds for each brand.</p>

<h3>What affiliate networks track</h3>
<p>Networks can use:</p>
<ul>
<li>Cookies</li>
<li>URL parameters</li>
<li>Server-to-server postbacks</li>
<li>Account or customer matching</li>
<li>Coupon codes</li>
<li>Product feeds</li>
<li>App-install tracking</li>
<li>Call tracking</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/view-through-attribution/">View-through attribution</a></li>
<li>Cross-device systems</li>
<li>Advertiser transaction imports</li>
</ul>
<p>Awin explains that affiliate cookies record click information used to connect the advertiser, publisher, and commission. It also provides coupon attribution that can credit a publisher based on an exclusive code even without the usual cookie.</p>
<h3>Network tracking is not perfect</h3>
<p>Tracking can be affected by:</p>
<ul>
<li>Browser privacy settings</li>
<li>Consent choices</li>
<li>Cookie deletion</li>
<li>App-to-web movement</li>
<li>Cross-device purchases</li>
<li>Broken advertiser code</li>
<li>Ad blockers</li>
<li>Redirect problems</li>
<li>Incorrect product feeds</li>
<li>Coupon extensions</li>
<li>Other affiliate clicks</li>
<li>Returns and cancellations</li>
<li>Advertiser reporting delays</li>
</ul>
<p>Creators should compare network reports with link analytics and notify support when tracking appears broken.</p>
<h3>Network payments</h3>
<p>An affiliate network may:</p>
<ul>
<li>Aggregate approved commission from multiple advertisers</li>
<li>Apply a minimum payment threshold</li>
<li>Pay on a scheduled cycle</li>
<li>Require tax and identity verification</li>
<li>Convert currencies</li>
<li>Offer several payment methods</li>
<li>Hold pending transactions during validation</li>
<li>Reverse returned or invalid sales</li>
<li>Delay payment when an advertiser has not funded its account</li>
<li>Deduct permitted fees</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator should review whether the network guarantees payment for approved transactions or depends on advertiser funding.</p>
<h3>Program and network approval</h3>
<p>A creator may pass several reviews:</p>
<ol>
<li>Network account review</li>
<li>Promotional-property review</li>
<li>Individual advertiser application</li>
<li>Compliance review</li>
<li>Ongoing transaction-quality monitoring</li>
</ol>
<p>An account can be restricted for inaccurate information, prohibited traffic, missing disclosures, trademark misuse, fraud, spam, or other violations.</p>
<h3>Choosing an affiliate network</h3>
<p>Evaluate:</p>
<ul>
<li>Relevant advertisers</li>
<li>Creator eligibility</li>
<li>Geographic coverage</li>
<li>Tracking quality</li>
<li>Deep-link tools</li>
<li>Coupon and product-tag support</li>
<li>Reporting detail</li>
<li>Payment threshold</li>
<li>Currency and fees</li>
<li>Payment reliability</li>
<li>Support</li>
<li>Program terms</li>
<li>Disclosure tools</li>
<li>Fraud and brand-safety policies</li>
<li>Data access</li>
<li>API or export availability</li>
<li>Account-closure and appeal process</li>
</ul>
<p>The network with the most advertisers is not automatically the best fit for a specific audience.</p>
<h3>Affiliate-network fees</h3>
<p>The advertiser, affiliate, or both may pay fees depending on the platform.</p>
<p>Potential fees include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Setup fee</li>
<li>Monthly platform fee</li>
<li>Tracking fee</li>
<li>Percentage override on publisher commission</li>
<li>Percentage of sales revenue</li>
<li>Managed-service fee</li>
<li>Currency-conversion fee</li>
<li>Payment-processing fee</li>
</ul>
<p>impact.com's glossary notes that affiliate-network pricing is often tied to revenue generated or publisher payouts rather than only a fixed cost. Actual pricing varies by contract.</p>
<h3>Creator responsibilities</h3>
<p>A network does not take over the creator's responsibility to:</p>
<ul>
<li>Make honest claims</li>
<li>Use clear disclosures</li>
<li>Follow program restrictions</li>
<li>Keep links current</li>
<li>Protect account credentials</li>
<li>Maintain accurate tax information</li>
<li>Avoid fraudulent traffic</li>
<li>Respect trademarks</li>
<li>Monitor changed rates</li>
<li>Preserve audience trust</li>
</ul>
<h3>Affiliate-network red flags</h3>
<p>Review:</p>
<ul>
<li>High payout threshold</li>
<li>Unclear payment funding</li>
<li>Weak reporting</li>
<li>No support for tracking disputes</li>
<li>Long pending periods</li>
<li>Retroactive rate changes</li>
<li>Broad rights to creator content</li>
<li>Mandatory exclusivity unrelated to the program</li>
<li>Unclear account-closure procedure</li>
<li>Fees deducted from publisher earnings</li>
<li>Programs with frequent reversals</li>
<li>Advertisers that prohibit legally required disclosures</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is Amazon Associates an affiliate network?</h4>
<p>Amazon Associates is primarily Amazon's own affiliate program rather than a marketplace of unrelated advertiser programs.</p>
<h4>Does joining a network approve a creator for every brand?</h4>
<p>No. Advertisers commonly review and approve affiliates separately.</p>
<h4>Does the network pay the creator?</h4>
<p>Many networks facilitate or aggregate payments, but funding and payment protection vary. Review the network and advertiser terms.</p>
<h4>Can creators use more than one affiliate network?</h4>
<p>Usually yes unless a contract restricts it. Creators often use multiple networks and direct programs to access different merchants.</p>
