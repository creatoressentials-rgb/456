<h2>Membership Perks</h2>
<p><strong>Membership perks</strong> are the exclusive benefits a YouTube creator offers viewers who make recurring monthly channel-membership payments or receive temporary gifted access.</p>
<p>Perks can include badges, emoji, posts, early access, exclusive videos and Shorts, live streams, chat access, recognition features, and other creator-provided benefits that follow YouTube's membership policies.</p>

<h3>Common YouTube Membership Perks</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Perk</th><th>What Members Receive</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Loyalty badges</td><td>A public badge beside the member's name in channel comments and live chats</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Custom emoji</td><td>Channel emoji for comments and live chat</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Members-only posts</td><td>Exclusive text, images, GIFs, polls, videos, and updates</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Early access</td><td>Videos available to members before becoming public</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Exclusive videos and Shorts</td><td>Content restricted to qualifying membership levels</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Members-only live streams</td><td>Live broadcasts restricted to qualifying members</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Members-only live chat</td><td>Chat participation during otherwise public streams</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Member Milestone Chats</td><td>Highlighted monthly messages beginning after the first consecutive month</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Recognition shelf</td><td>Public member avatars on the channel when the creator enables the shelf</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Other creator perks</td><td>Additional scalable benefits that comply with policy</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>How Membership Levels Work</h3>
<p>YouTube currently allows a creator to build up to <strong>six membership levels</strong> with different prices.</p>
<p>Each level must include between <strong>one and five perks</strong>.</p>

<h3>Perks Stack Upward</h3>
<p>Higher-priced levels automatically include the benefits assigned to lower-priced levels.</p>
<p>Example:</p>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Level</th><th>New Perks at This Level</th><th>Total Access</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Supporter</td><td>Badge, emoji, members-only posts</td><td>Supporter perks</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Insider</td><td>Early access and monthly live stream</td><td>Supporter plus Insider perks</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Producer</td><td>Quarterly group workshop</td><td>All lower perks plus Producer perk</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>A creator cannot use the normal tier system to remove lower-level benefits from higher levels.</p>

<h3>How to Add Levels and Perks</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio on a computer.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Earn</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open <strong>Memberships</strong>.</li>
  <li>Edit the levels and perks section.</li>
  <li>Add the level name, price, and one to five perks.</li>
  <li>Publish the program changes.</li>
  <li>Wait for YouTube's policy review.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube says the level and perk review generally takes about one day.</p>

<h3>What Makes a Strong Perk?</h3>
<p>YouTube recommends perks that are:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Valuable:</strong> Members understand why the benefit matters.</li>
  <li><strong>Scalable:</strong> The perk still works when membership grows.</li>
  <li><strong>Recurring:</strong> Members receive continuing value rather than a one-time promise.</li>
  <li><strong>Sustainable:</strong> The creator can consistently deliver it.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Lightweight vs. High-Effort Perks</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Lightweight Perks</th><th>Higher-Effort Perks</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Badge and emoji</td><td>Original exclusive video series</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Early-access videos</td><td>Monthly workshops</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Members-only polls</td><td>Detailed project critiques</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Chat access during an existing stream</td><td>Separate live events</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Behind-the-scenes posts</td><td>Custom physical fulfillment</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>YouTube recommends keeping the basic level easy to deliver and reserving labor-intensive benefits for higher levels.</p>

<h3>Why Viewers Become Members</h3>
<p>Members may be motivated by:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Supporting the creator</li>
  <li>Feeling closer to the community</li>
  <li>Recognition</li>
  <li>Exclusive content</li>
  <li>Early access</li>
  <li>Direct participation</li>
  <li>Access to a smaller discussion</li>
</ul>
<p>Not every supporter expects a large volume of exclusive work. A simple, honest membership can outperform an unsustainable promise.</p>

<h3>Perks YouTube Prohibits</h3>
<p>YouTube's current membership policies prohibit perks including:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Downloads of content, including music, available on YouTube</li>
  <li>In-person one-to-one meetings</li>
  <li>Randomly awarded benefits for only some members, including contests, lotteries, and sweepstakes</li>
  <li>Perks marketed, directed, or targeted to children</li>
  <li>Perks that may be attractive to or inappropriate for children</li>
  <li>Perks encouraging children to ask parents to join</li>
</ul>
<p>These restrictions apply whether or not the perk is written in the official membership window.</p>

<h3>Why Random Giveaways Are a Problem</h3>
<p>A creator cannot make membership itself an entry into a random prize drawing for only selected members.</p>
<p>This differs from giving the same guaranteed benefit to everyone at a level. Creators must also follow YouTube's broader contest rules and applicable law for any public promotion.</p>

<h3>Can You Offer Downloads?</h3>
<p>YouTube prohibits offering downloads of content available on YouTube, including music, as a members-only perk.</p>
<p>This prevents a creator from advertising "download my members-only YouTube videos" as the benefit. Separate original resources require careful rights, policy, and legal review and should not be presented as a way to bypass YouTube's restriction.</p>

<h3>Can You Offer One-on-One Calls?</h3>
<p>YouTube's policy specifically prohibits <strong>in-person one-to-one meetings</strong> as a membership perk. Other coaching, consultation, or group-access models require separate review for scalability, safety, local law, and YouTube policy.</p>
<p>Do not advertise a perk before confirming that its exact format is permitted.</p>

<h3>Members-Only Content as a Perk</h3>
<p>Content perks can include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Members-only videos</li>
  <li>Members-only Shorts</li>
  <li>Members-only posts and polls</li>
  <li>Members-only live streams</li>
  <li>Early access to public videos</li>
  <li>Extended cuts and behind-the-scenes material</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator should state whether content is permanently exclusive or becomes public later.</p>

<h3>Recognition Perks</h3>
<p>Recognition can include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Badges that reflect membership tenure</li>
  <li>Custom emoji</li>
  <li>Member Milestone Chats</li>
  <li>New-member messages during live streams</li>
  <li>The Member Recognition Shelf</li>
  <li>Group acknowledgements in videos or posts</li>
</ul>
<p>Member status and channel name may be publicly visible or shared with approved third-party tools.</p>

<h3>Gift Membership Access</h3>
<p>A recipient of a <a href="/glossary/gift-membership/">Gift Membership</a> receives access to the applicable membership perks for up to one month.</p>
<p>Gift access does not automatically renew or charge the recipient. Promotional gifts funded by YouTube do not generate creator revenue.</p>

<h3>Membership Perks and the Commerce Product Module</h3>
<p>The <a href="/glossary/commerce-product-module/">Commerce Product Module</a> governs creator earnings from channel memberships and other covered fan-funding products.</p>
<p>Accepting the module does not remove the need to meet channel-membership eligibility or deliver the promised perks.</p>

<h3>Perk Review and Approval</h3>
<p>YouTube reviews levels and perks before they go live. A perk can be rejected or later removed if it violates policy.</p>
<p>Creators remain responsible for:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Accurately describing the benefit</li>
  <li>Delivering it consistently</li>
  <li>Complying with relevant laws</li>
  <li>Updating the membership offer when circumstances change</li>
  <li>Handling member expectations transparently</li>
</ul>

<h3>What Happens If a Level Is Removed?</h3>
<p>YouTube says removing a membership level deletes the members in that level. Those members lose perk access immediately and receive a refund of their last month's payment.</p>
<p>Do not remove a level casually when a price or perk adjustment could be handled through a less disruptive program change.</p>

<h3>Can Perks Change?</h3>
<p>Creators may update the membership offer, and YouTube tells viewers that perks can change over time.</p>
<p>Best practice is to:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Give advance notice</li>
  <li>Explain why the change is necessary</li>
  <li>Replace removed value where reasonable</li>
  <li>Update the Join and See Perks descriptions</li>
  <li>Avoid retroactively changing a promise without communication</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to Choose Membership Perks</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Identify why the audience wants to support the channel.</li>
  <li>Choose one simple entry-level offer.</li>
  <li>Estimate the monthly time and cost.</li>
  <li>Make the benefit repeatable at ten times the current membership.</li>
  <li>Use a public or members-only YouTube poll for feedback.</li>
  <li>Set a clear delivery schedule.</li>
  <li>Review policy before publishing.</li>
  <li>Track retention and member feedback.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Perks and Membership Retention</h3>
<p>Membership retention depends on both emotional support and delivered value. Useful indicators include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Total and active members</li>
  <li>Cancellations</li>
  <li>Members by level</li>
  <li>Membership revenue</li>
  <li>Attendance at exclusive streams</li>
  <li>Views of exclusive content</li>
  <li>Poll and post participation</li>
  <li>Direct member feedback</li>
</ul>

<h3>A Sustainable Example</h3>
<p>A simple program might include:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Level 1:</strong> Badge, emoji, members-only posts, and early access</li>
  <li><strong>Level 2:</strong> All Level 1 perks plus one monthly members-only live stream</li>
  <li><strong>Level 3:</strong> All lower perks plus a quarterly group workshop</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator should avoid adding a level merely to increase price. Each level needs a clear, deliverable reason to exist.</p>

<h3>Common Membership-Perk Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Creating too many levels at launch.</li>
  <li>Offering more than five perks in one official level description.</li>
  <li>Forgetting that higher levels inherit lower-level perks.</li>
  <li>Promising labor-intensive benefits at a low price.</li>
  <li>Running members-only lotteries or random giveaways.</li>
  <li>Offering downloads of YouTube-hosted content.</li>
  <li>Failing to deliver recurring benefits.</li>
  <li>Removing a level without understanding the refund consequences.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>How many membership levels can a channel have?</h4>
<p>YouTube currently allows up to six levels.</p>

<h4>How many perks can each level list?</h4>
<p>Each level must have between one and five perks.</p>

<h4>Do higher levels receive lower-level perks?</h4>
<p>Yes. Perks automatically stack upward.</p>

<h4>Can membership include a random giveaway?</h4>
<p>YouTube prohibits perks where only selected members randomly receive a benefit, including contests, lotteries, and sweepstakes.</p>
