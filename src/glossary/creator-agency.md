<h2>Creator Agency</h2>
<p>A <strong>creator agency</strong> is a company that provides representation, campaign, production, growth, sales, or operational services within the <a href="/glossary/creator-economy/">creator economy</a>.</p>
<p>The phrase can describe several different businesses:</p>
<ul>
<li>Talent representation agency</li>
<li>Influencer marketing agency</li>
<li>Campaign management agency</li>
<li>Creative or production agency</li>
<li>Channel management company</li>
<li>Media sales agency</li>
<li>Rights-management provider</li>
<li>Full-service creator business agency</li>
</ul>
<p>The contract and actual services matter more than the label.</p>
<h3>Types of creator agencies</h3>
<h4>Talent representation agency</h4>
<p>Represents creators in negotiations and commercial opportunities.</p>
<p>Services may include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Brand outreach</li>
<li>Deal negotiation</li>
<li>Contract coordination</li>
<li>Rate strategy</li>
<li>Career positioning</li>
<li>Licensing</li>
<li>Speaking or appearance opportunities</li>
</ul>
<h4>Influencer marketing agency</h4>
<p>Works primarily for brands to plan and execute creator campaigns.</p>
<p>It may handle:</p>
<ul>
<li>Creator discovery</li>
<li>Briefing</li>
<li>Contracting</li>
<li>Approvals</li>
<li>Payment</li>
<li>Measurement</li>
<li>Reporting</li>
</ul>
<h4>Creator service agency</h4>
<p>Provides services directly to creators, such as:</p>
<ul>
<li>Editing</li>
<li>Thumbnail design</li>
<li>Channel strategy</li>
<li>Production</li>
<li>Analytics</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/sponsorship/">Sponsorship</a> sales</li>
<li>Commerce</li>
<li>Rights management</li>
</ul>
<h4>Full-service agency</h4>
<p>Combines representation, production, strategy, and business operations.</p>
<h3>Creator agency vs. <a href="/glossary/creator-manager/">creator manager</a></h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>Creator agency</th>
  <th><a href="/glossary/creator-manager">Creator manager</a></th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Company or team</td>
  <td>Individual role or management firm</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>May serve many creators and brands</td>
  <td>Usually supports a defined creator roster</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Can specialize in campaigns or services</td>
  <td>Often coordinates long-term career and business</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>May represent the brand buying the campaign</td>

</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Work can be project-based</td>
  <td>Relationship is often ongoing</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>A manager can work inside an agency. An agency can provide management without being the creator's exclusive representative.</p>
<h3>Creator agency vs. MCN</h3>
<p>YouTube defines a <a href="/glossary/multi-channel-network/">multi-channel network</a> as a third-party provider affiliated with multiple channels.</p>
<p>MCNs may provide:</p>
<ul>
<li>Audience development</li>
<li>Content programming</li>
<li>Collaborations</li>
<li>Rights management</li>
<li>Media sales</li>
<li>Monetization support</li>
</ul>
<p>A creator agency does not need to link channels to an MCN content-owner system. Many agencies support independent channels.</p>
<h3>Fee structures</h3>
<p>Agencies can charge:</p>
<ul>
<li>Percentage commission</li>
<li>Monthly retainer</li>
<li>Project fee</li>
<li>Campaign markup</li>
<li>Production fee</li>
<li>Media-buying fee</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/revenue-share/">Revenue share</a></li>
<li>Success fee</li>
<li>Hybrid arrangement</li>
</ul>
<p>The agreement should define the fee base.</p>
<p>For example, “20% commission” should state whether it applies to:</p>
<ul>
<li>Gross creator compensation</li>
<li>Net cash after expenses</li>
<li>Usage fees</li>
<li>Affiliate income</li>
<li>Existing relationships</li>
<li>Product sales</li>
<li>Renewals</li>
<li>Opportunities received after termination</li>
</ul>
<h3>Conflicts of interest</h3>
<p>Potential conflicts arise when an agency:</p>
<ul>
<li>Represents the creator and the brand</li>
<li>Receives fees from both sides</li>
<li>Favors higher agency margin over creator fit</li>
<li>Owns the campaign platform</li>
<li>Controls reporting</li>
<li>Uses creator data for competitors</li>
<li>Bundles undisclosed markups</li>
<li>Prioritizes agency-owned creators</li>
</ul>
<p>Conflicts should be disclosed and managed.</p>
<h3>Agency contract checklist</h3>
<p>Review:</p>
<ul>
<li>Services</li>
<li>Territory</li>
<li>Platforms</li>
<li>Exclusivity</li>
<li>Term</li>
<li>Renewal</li>
<li>Commission</li>
<li>Expenses</li>
<li>Approval authority</li>
<li>Contract-signing authority</li>
<li>Payment flow</li>
<li>Data access</li>
<li>Confidentiality</li>
<li>Intellectual property</li>
<li>Existing relationships</li>
<li>Post-termination commission</li>
<li>Audit rights</li>
<li>Termination</li>
<li>Dispute process</li>
</ul>
<p>A creator should know whether the agency can bind them to a deal or only present opportunities.</p>
<h3>Advertising compliance</h3>
<p>An agency can manage the campaign, but it cannot make deceptive claims lawful.</p>
<p>Creators, brands, and agencies should coordinate:</p>
<ul>
<li><a href="/glossary/sponsorship-disclosure">Sponsorship disclosure</a></li>
<li>Claim substantiation</li>
<li>Product experience</li>
<li>Approval</li>
<li>Platform paid-promotion tools</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/usage-rights/">Usage rights</a></li>
<li>Reporting</li>
</ul>
<p>The FTC's endorsement guidance applies to advertisers and endorsers, and agencies can create risk when they instruct creators poorly.</p>
<h3>Choosing a creator agency</h3>
<p>Ask:</p>
<ul>
<li>Which side do you represent?</li>
<li>What creators or brands do you already serve?</li>
<li>How are you paid?</li>
<li>What is exclusive?</li>
<li>Who owns relationships and data?</li>
<li>Who negotiates contracts?</li>
<li>Who reviews legal terms?</li>
<li>Can I see reporting?</li>
<li>What happens after termination?</li>
<li>Can you provide references?</li>
<li>What work remains my responsibility?</li>
</ul>
<h3>Agency warning signs</h3>
<ul>
<li>Guaranteed fame or revenue</li>
<li>Upfront pressure without clear services</li>
<li>Hidden brand-side fees</li>
<li>Indefinite exclusivity</li>
<li>Commission on every income source</li>
<li>No reporting</li>
<li>Password requests</li>
<li>Ownership of creator accounts</li>
<li>Ability to sign deals without limits</li>
<li>No termination path</li>
<li>Poor disclosure practices</li>
<li>Unclear client conflicts</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is a creator agency the same as a talent agency?</h4>
<p>Not always. Some represent talent, while others work for brands or sell production, campaign, and channel services.</p>
<h4>Is a creator agency the same as an MCN?</h4>
<p>No. An MCN has a YouTube-specific channel affiliation model. An agency can support creators without linking their channels.</p>
<h4>How do creator agencies get paid?</h4>
<p>Common models include commission, retainers, project fees, markups, and revenue share.</p>
<h4>Can an agency represent both a brand and creator?</h4>
<p>It can, but the conflict and compensation should be disclosed and the creator should understand whose interests the agency is obligated to serve.</p>
