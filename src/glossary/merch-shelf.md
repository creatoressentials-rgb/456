<h2>Merch Shelf</h2>
<p>A <strong>merch shelf</strong> is a YouTube shopping surface that displays products associated with an eligible creator or channel.</p>
<p>The phrase originally became common for the row of official creator merchandise shown below a video. YouTube still uses <strong>merch shelf</strong> in some current Help Center and channel documentation, while its broader commerce system is now called <a href="/glossary/youtube-shopping">YouTube Shopping</a>.</p>
<p>Depending on the channel, device, location, content, and current YouTube design, viewers may see products through:</p>
<ul>
<li>A product shelf below or beside a video</li>
<li>The channel's Store tab</li>
<li>Product links in a video description</li>
<li>A Shopping button in videos, Shorts, or livestreams</li>
<li>Product tags</li>
<li>Product collections</li>
<li>A connected retailer or creator store</li>
</ul>
<h3>How a YouTube merch shelf works</h3>
<p>A typical creator-owned merchandise workflow is:</p>
<ol>
<li>The creator becomes eligible for YouTube Shopping.</li>
<li>The creator connects a supported store or retailer in YouTube Studio.</li>
<li>Eligible products from that store are imported or selected.</li>
<li>YouTube and Google Merchant Center systems review the products under applicable policies.</li>
<li>The creator arranges products and chooses which products to feature.</li>
<li>Viewers see the products on eligible YouTube surfaces.</li>
<li>A viewer selects a product and continues to the connected store or retailer.</li>
<li>The merchant processes payment, fulfillment, shipping, returns, and customer support.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube provides the discovery and shopping surfaces, but it is not necessarily the seller.</p>

<p>“Merch shelf” is therefore best understood as one YouTube Shopping surface, not the creator's complete ecommerce operation.</p>
<h3>What products can appear?</h3>
<p>A connected creator store may feature:</p>
<ul>
<li>Shirts</li>
<li>Hoodies</li>
<li>Hats</li>
<li>Mugs</li>
<li>Posters</li>
<li>Stickers</li>
<li>Accessories</li>
<li>Books</li>
<li>Music</li>
<li>Collectibles</li>
<li>Other approved branded merchandise</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube Shopping can also support products from participating brands for eligible affiliate creators. Those products are not necessarily the creator's own merchandise.</p>
<p>The creator should disclose whether an item is:</p>
<ul>
<li>Creator-owned</li>
<li>Affiliate-linked</li>
<li>Sponsored</li>
<li>Gifted</li>
<li>A product collaboration</li>
<li>Sold by a third-party merchant</li>
</ul>
<h3>What information can viewers see?</h3>
<p>Product displays can include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Product image</li>
<li>Product name</li>
<li>Price</li>
<li>Store or retailer</li>
<li>Link or button to visit the store</li>
<li>Product collection</li>
<li>Tagged relationship to the video</li>
</ul>
<p>Prices and availability can change after the video is published. The connected merchant remains the best source for current product details.</p>

<h3>How creators manage merch shelf products</h3>
<p>YouTube currently lets eligible creators manage products from YouTube Studio. A creator may be able to:</p>
<ul>
<li>Connect or disconnect a store</li>
<li>Choose featured products</li>
<li>Reorder products</li>
<li>Create collections</li>
<li>Tag products in videos</li>
<li>Temporarily turn off the Shopping display</li>
<li>Review product-policy status</li>
<li>Remove outdated products</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator should also maintain the underlying store because YouTube cannot correct inventory, fulfillment, or customer-service problems on the creator's behalf.</p>
<h3>Merch shelf eligibility</h3>
<p>Eligibility can depend on current:</p>
<ul>
<li>YouTube Partner Program participation</li>
<li>Channel and country availability</li>
<li>Audience settings</li>
<li>Channel policy standing</li>
<li>Store or retailer support</li>
<li>Product compliance</li>
<li>Content type</li>
</ul>
<p>Some Shopping features are unavailable or limited on content set as <strong>made for kids</strong>. YouTube's eligibility rules and supported retailers can change, so creators should check YouTube Studio and current Help Center documentation rather than relying on an old subscriber threshold.</p>
<h3>Merch shelf vs. ordinary links</h3>
<p>A creator can place a store link in the description without using the merch shelf.</p>
<p>The shelf provides additional advantages:</p>
<ul>
<li>Product images appear inside YouTube</li>
<li>Products can be connected to specific content</li>
<li>Viewers can browse without first finding a description link</li>
<li>The creator can arrange featured products</li>
<li>The video can continue in a mini-player while viewers browse on supported devices</li>
<li>Shopping analytics may connect product activity to content</li>
</ul>
<p>An ordinary link can still be useful for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Unsupported stores</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/digital-product">Digital products</a></li>
<li>Services</li>
<li>International alternatives</li>
<li>Email capture</li>
<li>Products not eligible for YouTube Shopping</li>
</ul>
<h3>Merch shelf merchandising</h3>
<p>Displaying products is only one part of <a href="/glossary/merchandising">merchandising</a>.</p>
<p>Creators should decide:</p>
<ul>
<li>Which products match the video</li>
<li>Which item appears first</li>
<li>Whether the price fits the audience</li>
<li>When to launch a collection</li>
<li>How many products to show</li>
<li>Whether the product image is clear</li>
<li>Whether inventory is available</li>
<li>How the creator mentions the product</li>
<li>Whether a limited release is genuine</li>
<li>How the item supports the channel brand</li>
</ul>
<p>A shelf with ten unrelated products can be less effective than a small collection clearly connected to the content.</p>
<h3>Disclosure and creator products</h3>
<p>A creator generally does not need to hide the fact that they own and profit from their own merchandise. Clear wording such as “my store,” “our merch,” or “products from my company” helps viewers understand the relationship.</p>
<p>Additional disclosure may be needed when:</p>
<ul>
<li>The product belongs to another brand</li>
<li>The creator earns affiliate commission</li>
<li>The item is sponsored</li>
<li>The creator received the item for free</li>
<li>A commercial relationship is not obvious</li>
</ul>
<p>See <a href="/glossary/sponsorship-disclosure">sponsorship disclosure</a> and <a href="/glossary/affiliate-link">affiliate link</a>.</p>
<h3>Merch shelf analytics</h3>
<p>Creators can evaluate:</p>
<ul>
<li>Product clicks</li>
<li>Product-page visits</li>
<li>Sales</li>
<li>Conversion rate</li>
<li>Revenue</li>
<li>Average order value</li>
<li>Top products</li>
<li>Video-level product performance</li>
<li>Returns</li>
<li>Inventory</li>
<li>Profit after product and fulfillment costs</li>
</ul>
<p>Merch revenue is not the same as profit. Product cost, platform fees, payment processing, shipping support, returns, design, and promotion can significantly reduce the amount retained.</p>
<h3>Common merch shelf problems</h3>
<h4>Product not appearing</h4>
<p>Possible causes include policy review, inventory, integration errors, unsupported location, channel eligibility, or the Shopping switch being disabled.</p>
<h4>Incorrect price</h4>
<p>The product feed may be stale or the connected merchant may have changed the listing.</p>
<h4>Out-of-stock item</h4>
<p>Remove or deprioritize unavailable merchandise to avoid sending viewers to a dead product page.</p>
<h4>Store disconnected</h4>
<p>Reconnect the store through the Shopping area of YouTube Studio and verify the external commerce account.</p>
<h4>Product rejected</h4>
<p>Review the specific Google Merchant Center or YouTube policy reason rather than repeatedly resubmitting the same listing.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is the YouTube merch shelf discontinued?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube still refers to the merch shelf in current documentation, but it now operates within the broader YouTube Shopping system and may also be called the product shelf.</p>
<h4>Does YouTube manufacture or ship the merchandise?</h4>
<p>Usually no. The connected retailer, ecommerce platform, manufacturer, or fulfillment provider handles the underlying order and shipping.</p>
<h4>Can a creator show affiliate products on the merch shelf?</h4>
<p>Eligible creators can tag products from participating brands through YouTube Shopping. Availability depends on current program, country, merchant, and channel eligibility.</p>
<h4>Can the creator choose the product order?</h4>
<p>YouTube allows eligible creators to manage and arrange products or collections through its current Shopping tools, subject to product review and platform design.</p>
