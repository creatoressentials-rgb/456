<h2>Shorts Draft</h2>
<p>A <strong>Shorts Draft</strong> is an unfinished Short saved inside the YouTube mobile app so the creator can return to edit or upload it later.</p>
<p>YouTube says Shorts drafts are stored on the device—not in the creator's YouTube channel or cloud account. Deleting the YouTube app or replacing the device causes the drafts to be lost.</p>

<h3>What Can a Shorts Draft Save?</h3>
<p>A draft can preserve the Short's current mobile creation state, including edits made before saving and exiting.</p>
<p>Depending on the stage and tools used, this can include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Recorded clips</li>
  <li>Imported photos or videos</li>
  <li>Clip order</li>
  <li>Text and timing</li>
  <li>Filters and effects</li>
  <li>Music or audio choices</li>
  <li>Voiceover or text-to-speech</li>
  <li>Upload details entered before choosing Save Draft</li>
</ul>
<p>Feature availability and preservation can differ by app version and editing tool.</p>

<h3>How to Save a Short as a Draft</h3>
<p>YouTube offers more than one save point in the mobile workflow:</p>
<ol>
  <li>Create or import the Short.</li>
  <li>Tap Close and choose <strong>Save and exit</strong>, or continue through editing.</li>
  <li>On the final details screen, select <strong>Save draft</strong> instead of Upload Short.</li>
</ol>
<p>Saving earlier protects the current editing work without publishing the Short.</p>

<h3>How to Find Your Drafts</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open the YouTube app on the same device.</li>
  <li>Tap <strong>Create</strong>.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Short</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open <strong>Drafts</strong> in the lower-right area when available.</li>
  <li>Select a draft to edit or delete it from the device.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Where Are Shorts Drafts Stored?</h3>
<p>YouTube explicitly says drafts are saved to the device rather than the YouTube channel.</p>
<p>This means they do not automatically:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Sync to another phone</li>
  <li>Appear on desktop YouTube Studio</li>
  <li>Transfer by signing in to the same Google Account</li>
  <li>Survive app deletion</li>
  <li>Survive replacing the device</li>
  <li>Function as a cloud backup</li>
</ul>

<h3>Is There a Draft Limit?</h3>
<p>YouTube's current Shorts creation Help says there is no limit to the number of drafts a creator can keep at one time.</p>
<p>Practical limits can still come from device storage, app performance, operating-system behavior, and project complexity.</p>

<h3>What Causes Drafts to Disappear?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Deleting the YouTube app</li>
  <li>Replacing or losing the phone</li>
  <li>Clearing app data or storage</li>
  <li>Resetting the device</li>
  <li>Deleting the draft manually</li>
  <li>Operating-system cleanup or corruption</li>
  <li>App bugs or failed updates</li>
  <li>Using a different device</li>
</ul>
<p>Because YouTube does not store them on the channel, signing back in does not guarantee recovery.</p>

<h3>Shorts Draft vs. Upload Draft</h3>
<p>A standard video upload can be saved as a draft on the Content page when the upload flow closes before settings are finished. A Shorts creation draft is the unfinished mobile editing project stored on the device.</p>
<p>These are not the same storage system. Do not assume desktop or Studio upload-draft behavior applies to mobile Shorts projects.</p>

<h3>Can You Recover a Deleted Draft?</h3>
<p>YouTube does not provide a recycle bin or cloud restore for deleted Shorts drafts. Once the locally stored project is deleted or lost, recovery may not be possible.</p>
<p>Device backups might preserve some app data in limited circumstances, but creators should not rely on them as a supported YouTube recovery method.</p>

<h3>Can Drafts Be Moved to Another Phone?</h3>
<p>YouTube does not provide a supported account-sync or export function for transferring the editable Shorts draft project to another device.</p>
<p>To protect the content, save the original clips and completed exports outside YouTube before changing phones.</p>

<h3>Can You Download a Draft?</h3>
<p>The YouTube draft interface is designed for reopening, editing, deleting, or uploading the project. It is not a general project-file export system.</p>
<p>Use your camera roll or an external editor when you need a portable master file.</p>

<h3>Drafts and Copyrighted Music</h3>
<p>Music availability can change between saving and publishing because of licensing, region, account, or platform changes.</p>
<p>A draft using a sound can later require:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Selecting another sound</li>
  <li>Shortening the music use</li>
  <li>Removing unavailable audio</li>
  <li>Reviewing claim or blocking conditions</li>
</ul>

<h3>Drafts and App Updates</h3>
<p>Routine updates should not be treated as a backup event. Before a major app reinstall, phone reset, storage cleanup, or device migration, preserve the original media separately.</p>
<p>Do not uninstall the app as a troubleshooting step when irreplaceable drafts exist.</p>

<h3>How to Protect Important Drafts</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Record source clips with the device camera when possible.</li>
  <li>Keep imported files in the camera roll.</li>
  <li>Export a review copy from an external editor.</li>
  <li>Back up media to cloud or local storage.</li>
  <li>Write down text, captions, and timing notes.</li>
  <li>Publish important finished work as Private if appropriate.</li>
  <li>Do not depend on the YouTube draft as the only copy.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Draft Workflow for Creators</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Capture:</strong> Save original footage outside the app.</li>
  <li><strong>Edit:</strong> Use a draft for unfinished mobile adjustments.</li>
  <li><strong>Review:</strong> Check text, sound, crop, and pacing.</li>
  <li><strong>Backup:</strong> Preserve source files and notes.</li>
  <li><strong>Upload:</strong> Complete title, thumbnail frame, audience, and visibility.</li>
  <li><strong>Archive:</strong> Keep a master in the creator's backup system.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Why Does a Draft Look Different When Reopened?</h3>
<p>Possible reasons include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>An effect or sound changed availability.</li>
  <li>The app updated.</li>
  <li>Text or interface rendering changed.</li>
  <li>A source file was moved or deleted.</li>
  <li>The device has limited storage or memory.</li>
  <li>The draft did not finish saving correctly.</li>
</ul>
<p>Preview the full Short before uploading.</p>

<h3>Can Drafts Be Scheduled?</h3>
<p>A locally stored draft must first complete the upload process. Scheduling is a publication setting for uploaded content rather than a timer that publishes a device-only draft.</p>
<p>Do not assume a draft will upload automatically while the phone is offline or the app is closed.</p>

<h3>Common Shorts-Draft Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Assuming drafts sync with the Google Account.</li>
  <li>Deleting the app to fix a problem.</li>
  <li>Replacing the phone before exporting source media.</li>
  <li>Treating the draft as a backup.</li>
  <li>Confusing a Shorts draft with a Studio upload draft.</li>
  <li>Expecting deleted drafts in a recycle bin.</li>
  <li>Assuming saved music will remain available forever.</li>
  <li>Keeping the only copy of important footage inside the app.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Are Shorts drafts saved to my channel?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube says they are stored on the device.</p>

<h4>Will drafts transfer to a new phone?</h4>
<p>No supported account-sync transfer is provided. Replacing the device causes local drafts to be lost.</p>

<h4>How many drafts can I save?</h4>
<p>YouTube currently says there is no limit, although device storage remains a practical limitation.</p>

<h4>Can I recover a deleted draft?</h4>
<p>YouTube does not provide a supported cloud restore or recycle bin for Shorts drafts.</p>
