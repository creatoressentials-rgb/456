<h2>Auto-Dubbing</h2>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Auto-dubbing is a free YouTube feature that uses AI to translate your video's spoken audio and generate a new voice track in another language, then attaches it to your existing upload as a selectable audio track. Viewers who speak that language can watch your video dubbed automatically — no extra upload, no translator, no cost.</p>

<h3>How it works</h3>
<p>Auto-dubbing runs in three steps, all handled by YouTube automatically once you upload:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Transcribe and translate</strong> — YouTube detects your video's original spoken language, transcribes it, and machine-translates the script.</li>
<li><strong>Generate a voice track</strong> — an AI voice reads the translated script, timed to roughly match your original pacing. Some languages support <strong>Expressive Speech</strong>, which carries over more of your original pitch and energy instead of a flat, generated read.</li>
<li><strong>Attach it as a new audio track</strong> — the dub doesn't create a new video. It's added to your existing upload, and viewers pick their language from the player's Audio Tracks setting or from their own preferred language settings on YouTube.</li>
</ol>
<p>The dub is <strong>audio-only</strong> — it doesn't touch the picture, so a face-to-camera speaker's lips still move to the original language. YouTube has an experimental lip-sync feature in limited testing, but as of mid-2026 it hasn't shipped broadly.</p>

<h3>Turning it on and managing dubs</h3>
<p>Auto-dubbing is on by default for eligible channels. To manage it:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Settings → Content → Automatic dubbing</strong> — turn the feature on or off for your whole channel.</li>
<li><strong>Publish manually</strong> — a toggle in the same settings panel that holds every new dub for your review instead of publishing it automatically. Worth turning on once you have a global audience worth protecting.</li>
<li><strong>Studio → Content → [video] → Languages tab</strong> — this is where you manage dubs per video: <strong>preview</strong> a dub before it's live, <strong>review the transcript</strong>, and <strong>publish, unpublish, or delete</strong> individual language tracks.</li>
<li><strong>You cannot edit a dub's script or voice line-by-line.</strong> If a dub has a bad mistranslation or garbled audio, your only options are to unpublish/delete it or replace it with your own manually uploaded audio track in a different language.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Languages and eligibility</h3>
<p>Coverage is directional and still expanding, so treat any flat "X languages" number as a snapshot, not a ceiling — check YouTube's official help documentation for the current list before you plan around it. As of mid-2026:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Dubbing into English</strong> works from roughly 29 source languages (Arabic, Hindi, Japanese, Spanish, and more).</li>
<li><strong>Dubbing from English</strong> currently covers about 20 target languages.</li>
<li><strong>Expressive Speech</strong> (the more natural-sounding voice mode) is live on a growing subset of those languages — more than were listed when YouTube first announced the upgrade earlier in 2026, so don't assume an older article's list is current.</li>
</ul>
<p>A video is <strong>ineligible</strong> for auto-dubbing if it's longer than 120 minutes, has little or no spoken content, has speech too fast to dub cleanly, has an audio language YouTube can't detect, or carries an active <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a> claim.</p>

<h3>How it affects reach and revenue</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Reach:</strong> Dubbed tracks let non-native-language viewers discover and watch your content as if it were made for them. YouTube says having dubs published carries no discovery penalty for your original video — if anything, it opens up new <a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">suggested videos</a> and <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">traffic sources</a> exposure in the dubbed language's market. Use YouTube Analytics' audio track filter to see which dubbed languages are actually pulling <a href="/glossary/engaged-views/">engaged views</a> and whether those <a href="/glossary/unique-viewers/">unique viewers</a> turn into <a href="/glossary/returning-viewers/">returning viewers</a> or even <a href="/glossary/regular-viewers/">regular viewers</a> over time.</li>
<li><strong>Revenue:</strong> A dubbed view is still a normal <a href="/glossary/monetized-playbacks/">monetized playback</a> — it's just monetized at the ad rates of the viewer's market. Because <a href="/glossary/playback-based-cpm/">playback-based CPM</a> varies a lot by country, a wave of new dubbed views from a lower-<a href="/glossary/cost-per-mille-cpm/">CPM</a> region can grow your total <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">ad revenue</a> in absolute terms while pulling your overall <a href="/glossary/youtube-rpm/">RPM</a> down. Don't judge a dub's value purely by view count — check the revenue and <a href="/glossary/youtube-cpm/">CPM</a> it's actually generating by audio track before deciding a language "worked."</li>
</ul>

<h3>Quality caveats</h3>
<p>Auto-dubbed audio is generated, not performed. Expect:</p>
<ul>
<li>A flatter, sometimes robotic-sounding delivery, especially in languages without Expressive Speech support.</li>
<li>Errors with accents, background noise, fast speech, idioms, slang, and proper nouns.</li>
<li>Voice mismatches (an AI voice that doesn't sound like your gender, age, or tone).</li>
<li>No lip movement match — the biggest tell for face-to-camera creators, since viewers notice mismatched mouths before they notice translation quality.</li>
</ul>
<p>Auto-dubbing works best on voiceover-style, tutorial, or informational content with a single clear speaker and minimal background noise. For channels built around a specific on-camera personality or voice, it's a reasonable free starting point — not a permanent substitute for a real localization pass if a language starts driving meaningful <a href="/glossary/monetized-playbacks/">monetized playbacks</a>.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Does auto-dubbing hurt my original video's performance or discovery?</h4>
<p>No — YouTube states that publishing dubs has no negative effect on how your original-language video is recommended. Dubs only add new discovery surface area in other languages; they don't replace or compete with your main upload.</p>
<h4>Can I edit or fix a bad auto-dub?</h4>
<p>No. Automatic dubs can't be edited line-by-line — you can only unpublish or delete a dub, or replace it entirely by uploading your own audio track for that language. Turning on "Publish manually" lets you catch bad dubs before they go live instead of fixing them after.</p>
<h4>Do I need to be in the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a> to get auto-dubbing?</h4>
<p>Not anymore. It launched limited to Partner Program channels in late 2024, but expanded to all channels by early 2026. If you don't see it, check Settings → Content → Automatic dubbing, or the "Advanced settings" early-access option.</p>
