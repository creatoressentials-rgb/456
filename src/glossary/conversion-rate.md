<h2>Conversion Rate</h2>
<p><strong>Conversion rate</strong> is the percentage of eligible interactions, visitors, sessions, users, or leads that complete a defined conversion.</p>
<p>A conversion might be:</p>
<ul>
<li>Purchase</li>
<li>Lead form submission</li>
<li>Email sign-up</li>
<li>App installation</li>
<li>Free-trial start</li>
<li>Paid subscription</li>
<li>Affiliate sale</li>
<li>Product download</li>
<li>Booking</li>
<li>Another business action</li>
</ul>
<p>The formula depends on the question being asked. A creator should always identify both the conversion event and the denominator.</p>
<h3>Conversion rate formula</h3>
<p>A general formula is:</p>
<p><strong>Conversion rate = conversions ÷ eligible opportunities × 100</strong></p>
<p>If a creator receives 40 purchases from 2,000 tracked affiliate-link clicks, the click-to-purchase conversion rate is 2%.</p>
<p>The phrase <strong>eligible opportunities</strong> matters because it might mean clicks, sessions, users, leads, or another unit.</p>
<h3>Common conversion-rate denominators</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>Rate</th>
  <th>Formula</th>
  <th>What it measures</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Click-to-sale rate</td>
  <td>Sales ÷ tracked clicks</td>
  <td>How often a click becomes a purchase</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Session conversion rate</td>
  <td>Converting sessions ÷ sessions</td>
  <td>How often a website visit produces the action</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>User conversion rate</td>
  <td>Converting users ÷ users</td>
  <td>Share of people who convert</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Lead-to-customer rate</td>
  <td>Customers ÷ qualified leads</td>
  <td>Sales effectiveness after lead capture</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>View-to-click rate</td>
  <td>Clicks ÷ video views</td>
  <td>Response from content exposure; often called a click rate rather than conversion rate</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Impression-to-conversion rate</td>
  <td>Conversions ÷ impressions</td>
  <td>End-to-end result from ad exposure</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Trial-to-paid rate</td>
  <td>Paid customers ÷ trial users</td>
  <td>Product or subscription activation</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Two campaigns can report different conversion rates from the same customer journey because they use different denominators.</p>
<h3>Google Ads conversion rate</h3>
<p>Google Ads defines conversion rate as the average number of conversions per ad interaction, expressed as a percentage.</p>
<p>Google's formula uses:</p>
<ul>
<li>Conversions recorded under the selected conversion settings</li>
<li>Ad interactions that can be tracked to a conversion</li>
<li>The applicable attribution and conversion-window rules</li>
</ul>
<p>An interaction can be a click or another qualifying ad interaction, depending on the ad format.</p>
<h3>Can conversion rate exceed 100%?</h3>
<p>Yes.</p>
<p>Google Ads explains that conversion rate can exceed 100% when:</p>
<ul>
<li>More than one conversion action is tracked</li>
<li>The conversion action counts <strong>Every</strong> conversion</li>
<li>One interaction leads to multiple purchases or actions</li>
</ul>
<p>Example:</p>
<ul>
<li>10 eligible ad interactions</li>
<li>14 counted conversions</li>
<li>Reported conversion rate: 140%</li>
</ul>
<p>That result is not automatically a tracking error. It may be correct for a repeatable action such as purchases, while a lead form may be better configured to count one conversion per interaction.</p>
<h3>Conversion rate vs. click-through rate</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>Conversion rate</th>
  <th>Click-through rate</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Measures completed business actions</td>
  <td>Measures clicks after impressions</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Denominator is clicks, sessions, users, or another eligible unit</td>
  <td>Denominator is impressions</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Evaluates post-click or end-to-end effectiveness</td>
  <td>Evaluates whether the message earns a click</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Requires a defined conversion</td>
  <td>Requires a click and impression</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>A video can have a strong click-through rate and weak conversion rate when the landing page, price, product, or checkout fails to persuade visitors.</p>
<h3>Conversion rate vs. engagement rate</h3>
<p>An engagement can include a watch, expansion, interaction, comment, or another platform-defined action.</p>
<p>A conversion represents a business outcome chosen by the advertiser or creator.</p>
<p>A campaign can generate high engagement without producing sales. See <a href="/glossary/cost-per-engagement">Cost per Engagement</a> for the related efficiency metric.</p>
<h3>Conversion rate and conversion tracking</h3>
<p>A conversion rate is only as reliable as the underlying <a href="/glossary/conversion-tracking">conversion tracking</a>.</p>
<p>Tracking problems can include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Conversion tag not firing</li>
<li>Duplicate purchase events</li>
<li>Test orders counted as real</li>
<li>Missing cross-domain tracking</li>
<li>Affiliate redirect failure</li>
<li>Cookie or consent limitations</li>
<li>App-to-web loss</li>
<li>Incorrect transaction IDs</li>
<li>Thank-you page reloads counted repeatedly</li>
<li>Refunds not imported</li>
<li>Offline sales omitted</li>
</ul>
<p>Before optimizing the percentage, confirm that the numerator and denominator are measured correctly.</p>
<h3>Creator affiliate conversion rate</h3>
<p>For an <a href="/glossary/affiliate-program">affiliate program</a>, the creator might calculate:</p>
<p><strong>Approved affiliate sales ÷ tracked affiliate clicks × 100</strong></p>
<p>Use approved rather than initial orders when returns and reversals are significant.</p>
<p>The network may use a different rate based on:</p>
<ul>
<li>Unique clicks</li>
<li>Total clicks</li>
<li>Orders</li>
<li>Approved transactions</li>
<li>New customers</li>
<li>Commissionable sales</li>
</ul>
<p>A creator should label the exact calculation in a <a href="/glossary/creator-media-kit">creator media kit</a> or case study.</p>
<h3>Landing-page conversion rate</h3>
<p>A creator campaign can send qualified traffic to a page that performs poorly because of:</p>
<ul>
<li>Slow load time</li>
<li>Weak mobile layout</li>
<li>Unclear offer</li>
<li>Price mismatch</li>
<li>Missing trust signals</li>
<li>Complicated checkout</li>
<li>Out-of-stock product</li>
<li>Unsupported geography</li>
<li>Broken discount code</li>
<li>Inconsistent message</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator's audience may be well matched even when the advertiser's page suppresses conversion.</p>
<h3>Conversion window and delay</h3>
<p>A customer may convert hours, days, or weeks after the initial interaction.</p>
<p>The reported rate depends on:</p>
<ul>
<li>Conversion window</li>
<li>Attribution model</li>
<li>Reporting date</li>
<li>Conversion delay</li>
<li>Return and approval period</li>
</ul>
<p>A campaign should not be judged immediately when the product has a long consideration cycle.</p>
<h3>Conversion rate and attribution</h3>
<p><a href="/glossary/attribution">Attribution</a> decides which touchpoint receives credit.</p>
<p>Conversion rate describes the proportion of eligible opportunities credited with conversion.</p>
<p>A creator may influence a purchase but receive no tracked conversion when the customer later:</p>
<ul>
<li>Searches the brand</li>
<li>Uses another affiliate link</li>
<li>Purchases on another device</li>
<li>Uses a public coupon</li>
<li>Buys offline</li>
<li>Returns after the window</li>
</ul>
<p>A low tracked rate does not prove the creator had no influence, but it may indicate weak measurable direct response.</p>
<h3>What is a good conversion rate?</h3>
<p>There is no universal good rate.</p>
<p>Benchmarks vary by:</p>
<ul>
<li>Product category</li>
<li>Price</li>
<li>Customer intent</li>
<li>Traffic source</li>
<li>Device</li>
<li>Geography</li>
<li>New vs. returning customer</li>
<li>Landing page</li>
<li>Conversion definition</li>
<li>Attribution window</li>
<li>Sales cycle</li>
<li>Brand familiarity</li>
</ul>
<p>The best comparison is often the advertiser's own historical rate for similar traffic, products, and measurement settings.</p>
<h3>Improving creator campaign conversion rate</h3>
<p>Creators and brands can test:</p>
<ol>
<li>Stronger audience-product fit</li>
<li>Clearer product demonstration</li>
<li>More specific call to action</li>
<li>Better landing-page continuity</li>
<li>Mobile checkout</li>
<li>Accurate pricing and shipping details</li>
<li>Appropriate offer or bonus</li>
<li>Better product availability</li>
<li>Stronger trust and proof</li>
<li>Clear disclosure without interrupting comprehension</li>
<li>Faster page load</li>
<li>Tested links and codes</li>
</ol>
<p>Do not improve rate by hiding material information or using misleading urgency.</p>
<h3>Conversion-rate red flags</h3>
<p>Be cautious when a report:</p>
<ul>
<li>Omits the denominator</li>
<li>Mixes clicks and sessions</li>
<li>Uses initial orders but ignores returns</li>
<li>Combines several conversion actions without explanation</li>
<li>Compares different attribution windows</li>
<li>Claims causation from a tracked rate</li>
<li>Uses a tiny sample as a benchmark</li>
<li>Counts duplicate events</li>
<li>Includes internal tests</li>
<li>Treats all products as equal</li>
<li>Compares awareness content with high-intent search traffic</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>How is conversion rate calculated?</h4>
<p>Divide the number of conversions by the selected eligible denominator and multiply by 100. Define whether that denominator is clicks, sessions, users, leads, or another unit.</p>
<h4>Can conversion rate be over 100%?</h4>
<p>Yes. Multiple counted conversions can result from one ad interaction, especially when the conversion action is configured to count every occurrence.</p>
<h4>Is conversion rate the same as click-through rate?</h4>
<p>No. Click-through rate measures clicks from impressions. Conversion rate measures completed actions from clicks, sessions, users, or another eligible population.</p>
<h4>Why do Google Ads and Google Analytics show different rates?</h4>
<p>They can use different attribution logic, interaction denominators, event definitions, conversion settings, time zones, consent data, and reporting dates.</p>
