<h2>Whitelisting</h2>
<p><strong>Whitelisting</strong> is creator-marketing shorthand for giving an advertiser permission to run paid advertising using a creator's account identity, handle, post, or authorized content.</p>
<p>The term is not one standardized platform feature. Current platforms use different names and controls:</p>
<ul>
<li>Meta uses <strong>partnership ads</strong></li>
<li>TikTok uses <strong>Spark Ads</strong></li>
<li>YouTube uses <strong>brand partner access</strong> and creator partnership promotion</li>
<li>Other platforms may use allowlisting, boosting, creator licensing, or ad authorization</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator should use the platform's official permission system rather than sharing a personal password.</p>
<h3>Why brands request whitelisting</h3>
<p>Ads associated with a creator's identity may:</p>
<ul>
<li>Look more native to the platform</li>
<li>Preserve the creator's handle and social proof</li>
<li>Reach audiences beyond the creator's followers</li>
<li>Allow the brand to test targeting and creative variations</li>
<li>Combine creator trust with paid-media scale</li>
<li>Send traffic to a brand landing page</li>
<li>Generate data for campaign optimization</li>
<li>Extend the useful life of successful creator content</li>
</ul>
<p>This added advertising value is why whitelisting is normally negotiated separately from the fee for producing and publishing sponsored content.</p>
<h3>What whitelisting can mean</h3>
<p>The phrase may refer to several different arrangements.</p>
<h4>Promoting an existing creator post</h4>
<p>The brand receives permission to advertise a post already published by the creator.</p>
<h4>Running a partnership ad</h4>
<p>The advertiser uses Meta's partnership-ad tools to run an eligible ad with the approved creator or partner identity.</p>
<h4>Using a TikTok Spark authorization</h4>
<p>The creator generates a post authorization code or grants another supported permission. TikTok lets the advertiser use the authorized post as Spark Ad creative for the selected authorization period.</p>
<h4>Creating new ads through creator authorization</h4>
<p>A broader permission may let the advertiser upload or create approved advertising under a creator-associated identity rather than promoting only one existing post.</p>
<h4>YouTube creator partnership promotion</h4>
<p>YouTube brand partner access can let a brand view eligible performance metrics and promote the creator's video through supported Google Ads campaigns.</p>
<p>Each setup has different controls. The contract should identify the exact platform feature.</p>

<p>Whitelisting is usually a way to implement paid usage. It is not the complete legal agreement.</p>
<h3>Meta partnership ads</h3>
<p>Meta says partnership ads allow advertisers to run ads with creators, brands, and other partners. The advertiser needs permission from the partner whose handle the ad will run from.</p>
<p>Depending on Meta's current tools, permission may be:</p>
<ul>
<li>Account-level</li>
<li>Content-level</li>
<li>Granted through a partnership ad code</li>
<li>Connected to an existing branded-content post</li>
<li>Used for eligible new ad creative</li>
</ul>
<p>Meta also says partners can revoke permissions. A creator should still check the contract before revoking a permission earlier than the agreed campaign term.</p>
<h3>TikTok Spark Ads</h3>
<p>TikTok Spark Ads let advertisers use:</p>
<ul>
<li>Their own linked TikTok posts</li>
<li>Posts from another authorized creator</li>
<li>Creator content authorized through a video code</li>
<li>Account-level authorized content in supported workflows</li>
</ul>
<p>TikTok documentation allows creators to select an authorization duration for certain post-code workflows, such as 7, 30, 60, or 365 days.</p>
<p>The technical authorization period should match the paid-usage term. An advertiser should not receive a 365-day code for a 30-day contract unless the parties intentionally agree.</p>
<h3>YouTube brand partner access</h3>
<p>YouTube says brand partner access can allow a brand or agency to:</p>
<ul>
<li>View specified organic and paid performance metrics in Google Ads</li>
<li>Promote an eligible creator video in a creator partnerships campaign</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube advises creators to discuss video <a href="/glossary/usage-rights">usage rights</a> and obtain the required agreements independently.</p>
<p>This means accepting a platform request should come after the creator understands the contract term, compensation, and scope.</p>
<h3>What creators should never share</h3>
<p>Whitelisting should not require the creator to provide:</p>
<ul>
<li>Personal account password</li>
<li>Email password</li>
<li>Two-factor authentication code</li>
<li>Backup recovery codes</li>
<li>Full administrator access unrelated to advertising</li>
<li>Access to private messages</li>
<li>Access to unrelated business assets</li>
<li>Permanent ownership of the creator's page or channel</li>
</ul>
<p>Use the narrowest official permission that accomplishes the campaign.</p>
<h3>What the whitelisting agreement should define</h3>
<h4>Platform and feature</h4>
<p>Name Meta partnership ads, TikTok Spark Ads, YouTube brand partner access, or another specific tool.</p>
<h4>Advertiser and ad account</h4>
<p>Identify the brand, agency, and exact ad account receiving permission.</p>
<h4>Content</h4>
<p>State whether permission covers:</p>
<ul>
<li>One existing post</li>
<li>Several named posts</li>
<li>A creator account</li>
<li>New ads uploaded by the brand</li>
<li>Raw footage</li>
<li>Alternate edits</li>
</ul>
<h4>Duration</h4>
<p>Define both:</p>
<ul>
<li>Contractual <a href="/glossary/paid-usage">paid usage</a> period</li>
<li>Technical platform authorization period</li>
</ul>
<h4>Ad creative</h4>
<p>State who controls:</p>
<ul>
<li>Primary text</li>
<li>Headline</li>
<li>Landing page</li>
<li>Call to action</li>
<li>Product claims</li>
<li>Music</li>
<li>Captions</li>
<li>Crops and edits</li>
<li>Localization</li>
<li>New versions</li>
</ul>
<h4>Audience and spend</h4>
<p>The agreement may address:</p>
<ul>
<li>Territory</li>
<li>Target audience</li>
<li>Sensitive targeting</li>
<li>Maximum ad spend</li>
<li>Impression cap</li>
<li>Campaign objective</li>
<li>Retargeting</li>
<li>Use of creator-engaged audiences</li>
<li>Reporting</li>
</ul>
<h4>Approval</h4>
<p>The creator may approve the initial ad and any materially changed versions through the <a href="/glossary/approval-process">approval process</a>.</p>
<h4>Comments and moderation</h4>
<p>Clarify who monitors and hides comments, responds to viewers, and handles complaints.</p>
<h4>Revocation and shutdown</h4>
<p>State when the creator removes permission and how quickly the advertiser must stop active ads.</p>
<h3>Whitelisting compensation</h3>
<p>A whitelisting fee can be:</p>
<ul>
<li>Flat fee for a fixed period</li>
<li>Monthly authorization fee</li>
<li>Percentage of the creator's production fee</li>
<li>Fee based on ad spend</li>
<li>Platform-specific add-on</li>
<li>Minimum fee plus renewal rate</li>
</ul>
<p>There is no universal whitelisting percentage. Value depends on the creator identity, audience, duration, advertising scale, creative control, and platform.</p>
<h3>Whitelisting risks</h3>
<p>Creators should consider:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ads reaching audiences they would not personally target</li>
<li>New copy changing the meaning of the endorsement</li>
<li>Long campaign durations</li>
<li>High ad frequency creating audience fatigue</li>
<li>Comments appearing under creator-associated ads</li>
<li>Sensitive products or claims</li>
<li>Unauthorized edits</li>
<li>Access remaining active after the license expires</li>
<li>The brand using data or audiences beyond the campaign</li>
<li>Ad-account policy violations affecting connected assets</li>
<li>Music or third-party elements not cleared for advertising</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is whitelisting the same as paid usage?</h4>
<p>No. Paid usage is the contractual advertising permission. Whitelisting is a platform-access or identity-based method used to run some paid ads.</p>
<h4>Should a creator give a brand their password?</h4>
<p>No. Use official platform permission tools, authorization codes, or business access. Do not share personal passwords or authentication codes.</p>
<h4>Is whitelisting still the current Meta term?</h4>
<p>Meta's current product name is partnership ads. Whitelisting remains common industry shorthand but can be ambiguous.</p>
<h4>Can a creator stop whitelisted ads?</h4>
<p>Platform permissions can often be revoked, but the creator should follow the agreed contract term. The agreement should also require the advertiser to stop ads when permission or usage rights end.</p>
