<h2>Revision Round</h2>
<p>A <strong>revision round</strong> is one complete feedback cycle in which a client or brand reviews submitted work, provides a consolidated set of requested changes, and the creator returns an updated version. Creator agreements often include a limited number of rounds to keep the review process predictable.</p>
<p>For example, “one revision round included” can mean the brand sends one organized set of notes on the first cut and the creator makes those agreed changes once. It should not automatically mean unlimited emails, comments from new stakeholders, or a full new concept.</p>
<p>The exact meaning is not universal, so it should be defined in the contract or statement of work for the <a href="/glossary/brand-deal/">brand deal</a>.</p>
<h3>How a revision round works</h3>
<p>A typical round follows this sequence:</p>
<ol>
<li>The creator submits the agreed version or milestone.</li>
<li>The brand circulates it to its internal reviewers.</li>
<li>One authorized contact consolidates the feedback.</li>
<li>The brand returns the complete notes by the agreed deadline.</li>
<li>The creator reviews the notes and flags anything outside scope.</li>
<li>The creator produces and submits the revised version.</li>
<li>The parties either approve the work or begin another included or paid round.</li>
</ol>
<p>Calling this a “round” is useful because it groups related changes into one managed cycle rather than counting every small edit individually.</p>
<h3>What counts as one revision round?</h3>
<p>A strong agreement can say that one round consists of:</p>
<ul>
<li>One consolidated feedback document or timestamped review</li>
<li>Submitted by the named brand contact</li>
<li>Delivered within a specified review window</li>
<li>Based on the approved <a href="/glossary/campaign-brief/">campaign brief</a>, <a href="/glossary/creative-brief/">creative brief</a>, and <a href="/glossary/deliverables/">deliverables</a></li>
<li>Followed by one updated version from the creator</li>
</ul>
<p>The contract can also state that comments delivered later, feedback from additional stakeholders, or changes to already approved sections begin another round.</p>

<p>A note such as “make the logo two percent larger” and a note such as “replace the concept with a new sketch filmed in another location” should not be treated as equivalent work.</p>
<h3>What ordinary revisions may include</h3>
<p>Within the approved concept and available source material, a round may cover:</p>
<ul>
<li>Shortening or moving a sponsored segment</li>
<li>Correcting a product fact or required disclosure</li>
<li>Replacing approved wording with another compliant phrase</li>
<li>Adjusting on-screen text, logos, captions, or calls to action</li>
<li>Changing music, graphics, pacing, or a selected shot</li>
<li>Updating a link, code, title, thumbnail, or description</li>
<li>Removing an unsupported claim</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator should confirm that each requested change is technically possible and does not make the endorsement misleading.</p>
<h3>What may be outside the revision scope?</h3>
<p>Common examples include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Changing the campaign objective after the concept was approved</li>
<li>Rewriting the entire script around a new message</li>
<li>Adding a new product or sponsor</li>
<li>Requesting another video, Short, or platform version</li>
<li>Requiring new filming after the approved footage was captured</li>
<li>Moving the shoot to a new location</li>
<li>Changing the talent, language, or format</li>
<li>Expanding <a href="/glossary/usage-rights/">usage rights</a> or exclusivity</li>
<li>Shortening the agreed deadline</li>
<li>Reopening a stage that was already approved</li>
</ul>
<p>These requests can be valid, but they should trigger a discussion about added cost and schedule rather than being hidden inside a normal revision round.</p>
<h3>Why feedback should be consolidated</h3>
<p>Brands may involve marketing, legal, product, agency, and executive reviewers. If each person sends notes separately, the creator can receive conflicting instructions and repeatedly reopen the same edit.</p>
<p>A single brand contact should collect and resolve internal comments before sending them. Consolidated feedback:</p>
<ul>
<li>Makes the round count clear</li>
<li>Reduces contradictory notes</li>
<li>Prevents duplicated work</li>
<li>Protects the publication date</li>
<li>Creates a record of the approved direction</li>
<li>Helps both sides identify true scope changes</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator can provide a review link with timestamped comments, but the brand should still submit one final approved set of notes.</p>
<h3>How to define revision rounds in a contract</h3>
<p>A revision clause can address:</p>
<ul>
<li>Number of included rounds at each stage</li>
<li>Which stages are reviewed: concept, script, rough cut, final cut, thumbnail, or caption</li>
<li>Who may submit feedback</li>
<li>Required feedback format</li>
<li>Brand review deadline</li>
<li>Creator turnaround time</li>
<li>What counts as a creator correction</li>
<li>What requires a reshoot or change order</li>
<li>Fee for additional rounds</li>
<li>Whether unused rounds carry forward</li>
<li>What happens when the brand misses a feedback deadline</li>
<li>When work is considered approved</li>
</ul>
<p>A creator might include one concept round and one rough-cut round rather than two rounds that can be used at any stage. This prevents a brand from approving the concept and later using all remaining feedback to replace it.</p>
<h3>Revisions vs. creator corrections</h3>
<p>A creator should normally fix work that fails to meet an agreed requirement, such as omitting the required disclosure or using the wrong approved product name. The parties can decide that genuine creator errors do not reduce the client’s included revision allowance.</p>
<p>However, “correction” should not become a label for new preferences that were never in the brief. Keeping approved briefs, scripts, and feedback records helps distinguish the two.</p>
<h3>Additional revision rounds</h3>
<p>When the included rounds are exhausted, the creator can quote:</p>
<ul>
<li>A flat fee per additional round</li>
<li>An hourly or day rate</li>
<li>A separate reshoot fee</li>
<li>A new quote for substantial scope changes</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator should obtain written approval before completing extra work. A simple change order can state the additional notes, fee, and revised publication date.</p>
<h3>Revisions and truthful <a href="/glossary/sponsored-content/">sponsored content</a></h3>
<p>A brand may review factual accuracy and legal compliance, but it should not require a creator to make an endorsement that is false or inconsistent with the creator’s real experience. The creator should reject unsupported claims and preserve the ability to communicate honestly.</p>
<p>Disclosure should also remain clear after revisions. A request to hide, shorten, or remove a required <a href="/glossary/sponsorship-disclosure/">sponsorship disclosure</a> can create platform and legal risk.</p>
<h3>Common revision-round mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Using the phrase without defining it:</strong> The parties assume different meanings.</li>
<li><strong>Allowing feedback from everyone:</strong> No one controls the final direction.</li>
<li><strong>Starting edits before notes are consolidated:</strong> More comments arrive after the work begins.</li>
<li><strong>Failing to lock approved stages:</strong> The concept is repeatedly reopened.</li>
<li><strong>Treating a reshoot as a minor edit:</strong> Production time and expenses are ignored.</li>
<li><strong>Offering unlimited revisions:</strong> The project has no natural endpoint.</li>
<li><strong>Skipping response deadlines:</strong> Brand delays compress the creator’s production time.</li>
<li><strong>Making out-of-scope changes without written approval:</strong> The creator loses the ability to charge fairly.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Does one revision round mean one change?</h4>
<p>No. It usually means one consolidated batch of changes followed by one updated submission. The contract should define it because the phrase has no universal legal meaning.</p>
<h4>Does a new email count as another round?</h4>
<p>It can when it contains additional feedback after the brand’s consolidated notes were submitted. The agreed review process should answer this clearly.</p>
<h4>Should creator mistakes count as a revision round?</h4>
<p>The contract can say that corrections needed to meet the approved brief do not count. New preferences or changed instructions are different from creator errors.</p>
<h4>Can a brand request a reshoot during a revision round?</h4>
<p>It can request one, but the reshoot may be additional scope unless it is needed because the creator failed to follow an agreed requirement.</p>
<h4>How many revision rounds are normal?</h4>
<p>There is no universal number. Many creators include a small defined number based on the project’s complexity and price. Clarity matters more than copying an arbitrary industry standard.</p>
