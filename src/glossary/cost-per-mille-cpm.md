<h2>Cost per Mille/CPM</h2>
<p><strong>Cost per mille</strong>, abbreviated <strong>CPM</strong>, is the cost for 1,000 impressions.</p>
<p><em>Mille</em> is a term meaning one thousand.</p>
<p>The standard formula is:</p>
<p><strong>CPM = total cost ÷ impressions × 1,000</strong></p>
<p>If an advertiser spends $2,500 for 500,000 impressions, CPM is $5.</p>
<h3>What is an impression?</h3>
<p>An impression is generally counted when an ad is served or displayed according to the platform's measurement rules.</p>
<p>An impression is not necessarily:</p>
<ul>
<li>A unique person</li>
<li>A video view</li>
<li>A completed view</li>
<li>A click</li>
<li>A conversion</li>
<li>A viewable impression</li>
<li>A monetized playback</li>
</ul>
<p>One person can generate several impressions.</p>
<h3>CPM formula examples</h3>
<h4>Calculate CPM</h4>
<ul>
<li>Cost: $6,000</li>
<li>Impressions: 1,200,000</li>
<li>CPM: $5</li>
</ul>
<h4>Calculate cost from CPM</h4>
<ul>
<li>CPM: $12</li>
<li>Impressions: 250,000</li>
<li>Cost: $3,000</li>
</ul>
<h4>Estimate impressions from cost and CPM</h4>
<ul>
<li>Cost: $5,000</li>
<li>CPM: $10</li>
<li>Estimated impressions: 500,000</li>
</ul>
<p>These calculations assume the same impression definition throughout.</p>
<h3>CPM vs. vCPM</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>CPM</th>
  <th>Viewable CPM or vCPM</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Cost per 1,000 served impressions</td>
  <td>Cost per 1,000 measurable viewable impressions</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Can include impressions that were not actually viewable</td>
  <td>Focuses on impressions meeting viewability criteria</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Broader delivery metric</td>
  <td>Stricter visibility metric</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Often lower than vCPM</td>
  <td>Often higher because denominator is narrower</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Google currently describes a Display ad as viewable when at least 50% of the ad is visible for at least one second, and a video ad when at least 50% plays continuously for at least two seconds, under its Active View rules.</p>
<p>Viewable does not mean the person paid attention or remembered the ad.</p>
<h3>CPM vs. target CPM</h3>
<h4>Average CPM</h4>
<p>The observed average cost per 1,000 impressions.</p>
<h4>Maximum CPM</h4>
<p>A bid ceiling used in older or eligible bidding setups.</p>
<h4>Target CPM</h4>
<p>The average amount the advertiser wants to pay per 1,000 impressions. The system can deliver some impressions above or below that target while attempting to achieve the average.</p>
<h3>YouTube CPM</h3>
<p>YouTube defines CPM as the cost an advertiser pays for 1,000 ad impressions before YouTube's revenue share.</p>
<p>This is an advertiser-side metric surfaced to creators to help explain monetization demand.</p>
<p>It is not the amount the creator keeps.</p>
<h3>Playback-based CPM</h3>
<p>YouTube defines <strong>playback-based CPM</strong> as the cost an advertiser pays for 1,000 video playbacks in which an ad is displayed.</p>
<p>One monetized playback can include more than one ad impression.</p>
<p>This creates an important difference:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>Metric</th>
  <th>Denominator</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>CPM</td>
  <td>Ad impressions</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td><a href="/glossary/playback-based-cpm">Playback-based CPM</a></td>
  <td>Monetized video playbacks</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Video-view CPM</td>
  <td>Video views, if a marketer calculates it that way</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Sponsor CPM</td>
  <td>Often sponsorship fee divided by video views × 1,000</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The terms should not be mixed.</p>
<h3>CPM vs. RPM</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>CPM</th>
  <th><a href="/glossary/youtube-rpm">YouTube RPM</a></th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Advertiser cost</td>
  <td>Creator revenue</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Before YouTube revenue share</td>
  <td>After applicable revenue share</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Per 1,000 ad impressions</td>
  <td>Per 1,000 video views</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Does not equal creator take-home revenue</td>
  <td>Can include ads, memberships, Premium, and other eligible revenue in YouTube's metric</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>A channel can have high CPM but lower RPM when only a portion of views are monetized or when revenue sharing and other factors reduce creator revenue per view.</p>
<h3>CPM vs. monetized playbacks</h3>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/monetized-playbacks">monetized playback</a> occurs when a viewer watches a video and is shown at least one ad impression.</p>
<p>Not every video view is monetized because:</p>
<ul>
<li>No ad is available</li>
<li>Viewer uses YouTube Premium</li>
<li>Content is not advertiser-friendly</li>
<li>Viewer geography has low demand</li>
<li>Ad blocking or delivery conditions apply</li>
<li>Monetization is disabled</li>
</ul>
<p>CPM should therefore not be multiplied by total video views to estimate creator earnings.</p>
<h3>CPM vs. cost per view</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>CPM</th>
  <th>Cost per view</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Cost per 1,000 impressions</td>
  <td>Cost per qualifying video view</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Exposure-based</td>
  <td>View-based</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Useful for reach campaigns</td>
  <td>Useful for video-consumption campaigns</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Does not require a completed view</td>
  <td>Requires the platform's view definition</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>A video ad can generate an impression without a billable or reported view.</p>
<h3>CPM vs. CPA and CPE</h3>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>Metric</th>
  <th>Unit</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>CPM</td>
  <td>Cost per 1,000 impressions</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td><a href="/glossary/cost-per-engagement">Cost per Engagement</a></td>
  <td>Cost per defined interaction</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td><a href="/glossary/cost-per-acquisition">Cost per Acquisition</a></td>
  <td>Cost per conversion or customer acquisition</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>CPM is most directly tied to exposure. It does not reveal whether people engaged or converted.</p>
<h3>Sponsorship CPM</h3>
<p>Brands sometimes evaluate creator sponsorships using an implied CPM:</p>
<p><strong>Sponsorship fee ÷ video views × 1,000</strong></p>
<p>Example:</p>
<ul>
<li>Sponsorship fee: $5,000</li>
<li>Video views: 200,000</li>
<li>Implied creator sponsorship CPM: $25</li>
</ul>
<p>This is not the same as YouTube advertising CPM because:</p>
<ul>
<li>The denominator is video views rather than ad impressions</li>
<li>The creator produces and integrates the endorsement</li>
<li>The brand may receive <a href="/glossary/usage-rights">usage rights</a></li>
<li>Audience trust and content context are involved</li>
<li>The sponsorship may include links, exclusivity, and deliverables</li>
<li>The sponsor message can remain in evergreen content</li>
</ul>
<p>A creator sponsorship should not be priced solely by comparing it with automated ad inventory.</p>
<h3>Factors affecting CPM</h3>
<p>CPM can vary with:</p>
<ul>
<li>Country</li>
<li>Audience demographics</li>
<li>Advertiser demand</li>
<li>Season</li>
<li>Industry</li>
<li>Placement</li>
<li>Device</li>
<li>Format</li>
<li>Viewability</li>
<li>Brand safety</li>
<li>Targeting precision</li>
<li>Frequency</li>
<li>Auction competition</li>
<li>Campaign objective</li>
<li>Inventory supply</li>
</ul>
<p>A high CPM can indicate valuable inventory or expensive competition. It does not automatically indicate better campaign performance.</p>
<h3>CPM and reach</h3>
<p>Impressions count exposures. Reach estimates people.</p>
<p>If a campaign serves 300,000 impressions to 100,000 people, average frequency is three impressions per person.</p>
<p>A low CPM can produce many repeated impressions to the same small group. Brands should review:</p>
<ul>
<li>Unique reach</li>
<li>Frequency</li>
<li>On-target reach</li>
<li>Viewability</li>
<li>Engagement</li>
<li>Lift</li>
</ul>
<h3>Creator revenue and CPM</h3>
<p>Creators should avoid estimating income using CPM alone.</p>
<p>Actual earnings depend on:</p>
<ul>
<li>Monetized playback rate</li>
<li>Number of ad impressions per playback</li>
<li>Revenue share</li>
<li>Viewer geography</li>
<li>Ad format</li>
<li>YouTube Premium</li>
<li>Content eligibility</li>
<li>Seasonality</li>
<li>Revenue adjustments</li>
</ul>
<p>Use YouTube Analytics revenue reports rather than a simple CPM-times-views shortcut.</p>
<h3>CPM red flags</h3>
<p>Be cautious when:</p>
<ul>
<li>Impressions are confused with views</li>
<li>CPM is described as creator take-home pay</li>
<li>Viewable and served impressions are mixed</li>
<li>Sponsor CPM is compared directly with auction ad CPM</li>
<li>Frequency is ignored</li>
<li>Different geographies are compared without context</li>
<li>A high CPM is called profitable without conversion data</li>
<li>Total views are treated as monetized views</li>
<li>Revenue share is ignored</li>
<li>The reporting period or currency is omitted</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>What does CPM stand for?</h4>
<p>Cost per mille, meaning cost per one thousand impressions.</p>
<h4>How is CPM calculated?</h4>
<p>Divide total cost by impressions and multiply by 1,000.</p>
<h4>Is YouTube CPM what the creator earns?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube CPM represents advertiser cost before YouTube's revenue share. Creator revenue per 1,000 views is represented more closely by RPM.</p>
<h4>Is a high CPM always good?</h4>
<p>No. High CPM means impressions are expensive or valuable in the auction. Campaign success still depends on reach quality, engagement, conversion, and business value.</p>
