<h2>Formats Your Viewers Watch</h2>
<p><strong>Formats your viewers watch on YouTube</strong> is an Audience-tab report showing the types of YouTube content your viewers watched during the past 28 days. The current format categories are videos, Shorts, and live streams.</p>
<p>The report helps creators understand whether their audience uses several YouTube formats and which formats are most common in the audience's broader viewing behavior.</p>

<h3>What Does the Report Measure?</h3>
<p>YouTube describes the report as showing what other types of formats people are watching in the last 28 days. The behavior is across YouTube, not limited to the formats viewers consumed on your channel.</p>
<p>The listed formats are:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Videos:</strong> Standard video-on-demand content</li>
<li><strong>Shorts:</strong> Content viewed as YouTube Shorts</li>
<li><strong>Live streams:</strong> Live content watched on YouTube</li>
</ul>
<p>The report does not mean that every viewer watches all three formats or that each format contributes equal viewing time.</p>

<h3>Where Can You Find the Report?</h3>
<ol>
<li>Sign in to YouTube Studio.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Analytics</strong>.</li>
<li>Open the <strong>Audience</strong> tab.</li>
<li>Find <strong>Formats your viewers watch on YouTube</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>The report may be unavailable if the channel lacks enough relevant audience data.</p>

<h3>What Time Period Does It Use?</h3>
<p>The current report covers the last 28 days. It can change as the audience's viewing habits change, especially around live events, viral Shorts, seasonal topics, or a shift in the channel's audience.</p>

<h3>Formats Your Viewers Watch vs. Viewers Across Formats</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Report</th><th>What It Measures</th><th>Where It Applies</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Formats your viewers watch on YouTube</td><td>Which content formats your viewers consume across YouTube</td><td>Audience tab; broader 28-day viewing behavior</td></tr>
<tr><td>Viewers across formats</td><td>The breakdown and overlap of returning viewers consuming formats you publish</td><td>Your own channel's videos, Shorts, and live content</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The first report asks what formats your audience likes on YouTube. The second asks whether returning viewers cross between formats on your channel.</p>

<h3>What Can the Report Tell You?</h3>
<ul>
<li>Whether your audience watches Shorts at all</li>
<li>Whether live content is already familiar to your viewers</li>
<li>Whether standard videos remain an important audience habit</li>
<li>Whether a multi-format experiment may fit the audience</li>
<li>Which format deserves additional research before investing heavily</li>
</ul>
<p>It does not prove that your channel will succeed in a format simply because the audience watches that format elsewhere.</p>

<h3>How to Use the Report for Content Strategy</h3>
<ol>
<li>Identify formats with meaningful audience activity.</li>
<li>Decide whether the format fits your topic, skills, and production capacity.</li>
<li>Create a small controlled test rather than changing the entire channel.</li>
<li>Adapt the idea to the format instead of reposting the same edit unchanged.</li>
<li>Review performance using format-specific analytics.</li>
<li>Check whether existing viewers cross into the new format on your channel.</li>
<li>Continue only when the format serves a clear audience and business purpose.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Should Every Channel Publish Shorts?</h3>
<p>No. Audience interest in Shorts can make testing more reasonable, but a channel should also consider:</p>
<ul>
<li>Whether the topic works in a brief vertical format</li>
<li>Whether Shorts viewers move toward the channel's broader goals</li>
<li>Production time and opportunity cost</li>
<li>Brand and presentation style</li>
<li>Revenue and conversion differences</li>
<li>Whether the creator can provide complete value without misleading compression</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube advises creators not to compare different formats directly because audience behavior and measures of success differ.</p>

<h3>Should You Start Live Streaming?</h3>
<p>A strong live-format signal suggests that your viewers are comfortable watching live content on YouTube. It does not show:</p>
<ul>
<li>Which live topics they prefer</li>
<li>What times they attend</li>
<li>Whether they would watch you live</li>
<li>How long they remain in streams</li>
<li>Whether they prefer events, gaming, Q&amp;A, news, or passive live channels</li>
</ul>
<p>Use <a href="/glossary/when-your-viewers-are-on-youtube/">When your viewers are on YouTube</a>, audience questions, and a small test stream to refine the decision.</p>

<h3>Format Preference vs. Topic Preference</h3>
<p>A person may watch Shorts for entertainment, long videos for education, and live streams for sports or news. The format report does not isolate topic within each format.</p>
<p>Combine it with:</p>
<ul>
<li><a href="/glossary/what-your-audience-watches/">What your audience watches</a></li>
<li>Channels your audience watches</li>
<li>Traffic sources</li>
<li>Top content</li>
<li>Viewer comments and polls</li>
<li>Format-filtered Content analytics</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to Adapt Content to a Format</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Format</th><th>Useful Adaptation</th><th>Common Mistake</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Videos</td><td>Develop complete structure, context, pacing, and viewer payoff</td><td>Stretching a short idea without adding value</td></tr>
<tr><td>Shorts</td><td>Design for vertical viewing, immediate clarity, and concise completion</td><td>Cropping a horizontal video without redesigning text and framing</td></tr>
<tr><td>Live streams</td><td>Plan real-time interaction, moderation, pacing, and replay value</td><td>Treating a live stream like an unedited recorded video</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<h3>What the Report Does Not Tell You</h3>
<ul>
<li>The exact percentage of viewing time spent in each format</li>
<li>Which topics viewers watch within each format</li>
<li>Whether viewers prefer your channel in that format</li>
<li>Whether a new format will increase revenue</li>
<li>Whether the same viewers cross between your own formats</li>
<li>How your content ranks against other creators in the format</li>
</ul>

<h3>Common Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Confusing the report with Viewers across formats.</li>
<li>Assuming it covers only formats watched on your channel.</li>
<li>Treating audience interest as guaranteed channel performance.</li>
<li>Comparing Shorts views directly with long-form views.</li>
<li>Publishing identical edits in every format without adapting them.</li>
<li>Adding live streams without a live-specific audience experience.</li>
<li>Using a 28-day snapshot as a permanent strategy.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Which formats appear in the report?</h4>
<p>YouTube currently identifies videos, Shorts, and live streams.</p>
<h4>Does it show what viewers watch only on my channel?</h4>
<p>No. It describes the formats your viewers watch across YouTube.</p>
<h4>How far back does the report go?</h4>
<p>The current report uses the last 28 days.</p>
<h4>Does a high Shorts signal mean I should stop making long videos?</h4>
<p>No. Viewers can use multiple formats for different needs. Test formats without abandoning a successful strategy based on one audience card.</p>
