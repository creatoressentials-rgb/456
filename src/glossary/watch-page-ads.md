<h2>Watch Page Ads</h2>
<p><strong>Watch Page ads</strong> are advertisements displayed or streamed before, during, after, or around eligible long-form and live YouTube videos on content watch pages.</p>
<p>Creators who meet full YouTube Partner Program eligibility can accept the Watch Page Monetization Module and turn on ads for eligible content. YouTube's published partner earnings overview says creators receive 55% of net revenues from qualifying ads on their public Watch Page videos.</p>

<h3>What Is the YouTube Watch Page?</h3>
<p>YouTube defines the Watch Page as pages within YouTube, YouTube Music, and YouTube Kids dedicated to the description and playback of long-form or live-streaming videos.</p>
<p>The Watch Page model also applies when eligible public videos play inside the YouTube Video Player embedded on other websites or applications. The 55% Watch Page ad revenue share applies to qualifying ads in those embedded players as well.</p>

<h3>Where Can Watch Page Ads Appear?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Before the video, often called pre-roll</li>
  <li>During an eligible video, called mid-roll</li>
  <li>After the video, called post-roll</li>
  <li>Around the video on supported Watch Page layouts</li>
  <li>Within eligible embedded YouTube players</li>
  <li>During eligible live streams and live replays</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube chooses which specific ad formats and ads are appropriate. Turning monetization on does not mean every placement or every playback receives an ad.</p>

<h3>What Must a Creator Do to Earn?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Qualify for the full advertising level of the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a>.</li>
  <li>Accept YouTube's Base Terms and Watch Page Monetization Module.</li>
  <li>Turn on monetization for the eligible video or live stream.</li>
  <li>Have the necessary rights to all audio and visual material.</li>
  <li>Follow channel monetization and advertiser-friendly content policies.</li>
  <li>Complete applicable <a href="/glossary/ad-suitability-checks/">Ad Suitability Checks</a>.</li>
</ol>
<p>Turning on ads is a request to monetize eligible viewing. It is not a guarantee that ads will serve or that the video will earn a specific amount.</p>

<h3>How Much Revenue Does the Creator Receive?</h3>
<p>YouTube's partner earnings overview states that creators who accept the module and turn on Watch Page ads receive <strong>55% of net revenues</strong> from ads displayed or streamed on their public Watch Page videos.</p>
<p>Important distinctions:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The share is based on net ad revenue, not the advertiser's headline budget.</li>
  <li>Transaction taxes are not included as revenue to Google for the partner share calculation.</li>
  <li>Copyright claims or revenue-sharing music can change the amount the uploader receives.</li>
  <li>Estimated earnings can be adjusted for invalid traffic, claims, disputes, and certain campaign types.</li>
  <li>The 55% Watch Page ad share is different from the 45% Shorts Creator Pool share.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Are Ads Shown on Every View?</h3>
<p>No. YouTube explicitly says ads may not display on each video view. Ad delivery can depend on:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Whether an appropriate ad is available</li>
  <li>The viewer's country, age, and account context</li>
  <li>Advertiser targeting and demand</li>
  <li>The content's ad-suitability status</li>
  <li>The viewer's frequency and prior ad exposure</li>
  <li>Whether the viewer uses YouTube Premium</li>
  <li>Ad-blocking or playback environment</li>
  <li>Copyright and monetization restrictions</li>
</ul>
<p>A view without an ad can still contribute watch time and may generate YouTube Premium revenue when the viewer is an eligible Premium member.</p>

<h3>What Are Mid-Roll Ads?</h3>
<p>Mid-roll ads are ads that may appear during a video. YouTube currently allows mid-roll ads on monetized videos that are eight minutes or longer.</p>
<p>Creators can use:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Automatic ad slots:</strong> YouTube identifies potential natural placements.</li>
  <li><strong>Manual ad slots:</strong> The creator identifies places where an ad is permitted to serve.</li>
  <li><strong>Combined automatic and manual slots:</strong> Both approaches can provide eligible opportunities.</li>
</ul>
<p>An ad slot is not a guaranteed ad break. YouTube's systems decide whether to fill an eligible slot based on viewer experience, advertiser value, creator earnings, and other factors.</p>

<h3>Where Should Mid-Roll Slots Be Placed?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Between topics or chapters</li>
  <li>After a complete sentence or thought</li>
  <li>During a natural visual transition</li>
  <li>Between rounds, scenes, demonstrations, or interview questions</li>
  <li>Before a new segment begins</li>
</ul>
<p>Avoid placing slots in the middle of a word, emotional climax, essential instruction, punchline, musical phrase, or continuous action. Poor placements can harm viewer satisfaction even when they create more ad opportunities.</p>

<h3>What Ad Formats Can Viewers See?</h3>
<p>YouTube manages supported ad formats based on device, viewer, campaign, and playback context. Examples can include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Skippable in-stream ads</li>
  <li>Non-skippable in-stream ads</li>
  <li>Bumper ads</li>
  <li>Pre-roll and post-roll placements</li>
  <li>Mid-roll ads on eligible content</li>
  <li>Watch Page display placements where supported</li>
</ul>
<p>Creators no longer manually select every pre-roll and post-roll format. YouTube serves appropriate formats when ads are turned on.</p>

<h3>Watch Page Ads on Embedded Videos</h3>
<p>Eligible embedded videos can show skippable or non-skippable in-stream ads and honor the same monetization settings as the YouTube watch page. Only YouTube and the video owner earn from ordinary YouTube ads in the embed.</p>
<p>Autoplay and child-directed website settings can affect embedded playback and advertising behavior. Creators should not assume every website embed monetizes identically.</p>

<h3>Watch Page Ads on Live Streams</h3>
<p>Eligible live streams can earn through pre-roll, display, and mid-roll ads. Mid-rolls can be inserted automatically or manually through Live Control Room when the channel and stream are eligible.</p>
<p>Live ad delivery remains variable. A creator should not interrupt critical live moments solely because an ad slot exists.</p>

<h3>How Do Copyright Claims Affect Watch Page Revenue?</h3>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a> claimant can monetize, block, or track matched content. If a rights holder monetizes the video, some or all ad revenue can go to the claimant rather than the uploader.</p>
<p>Creator Music revenue-sharing tracks and other licensed arrangements can also divide revenue under their own terms. Owning the video upload does not necessarily mean owning every revenue right.</p>

<h3>How Do Creators Measure Watch Page Ads?</h3>
<p>YouTube Analytics can show:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Estimated revenue</li>
  <li>Watch Page ad revenue</li>
  <li><a href="/glossary/monetized-playbacks/">Estimated monetized playbacks</a></li>
  <li>Ad impressions</li>
  <li><a href="/glossary/playback-based-cpm/">Playback-based CPM</a></li>
  <li>CPM</li>
  <li><a href="/glossary/youtube-rpm/">RPM</a></li>
  <li>Revenue by geography, content, or date when available</li>
</ul>
<p>Watch Page revenue reporting can include AdSense ads, DoubleClick ads, and YouTube Premium revenue for the selected content and period. Check the report label before assuming the total is advertising only.</p>

<h3>Why Can CPM and Creator Earnings Differ?</h3>
<p>CPM represents advertiser spending before YouTube's revenue share and applies to ad impressions. Creator revenue reflects the creator's portion after the platform share and applicable adjustments.</p>
<p>RPM is lower in many cases because it divides total creator revenue by all views, including views that did not show ads. It can also include other sources such as Premium and fan funding.</p>

<h3>Can Ads Appear on Non-Partner Videos?</h3>
<p>Yes. YouTube may place ads on videos even when a channel is not in YPP or has not turned on creator monetization. The uploader does not receive an advertising share unless the channel and content qualify under the applicable monetization terms.</p>
<p>Seeing an ad on a video therefore does not prove that the creator earned money from that playback.</p>

<h3>Common Watch-Page-Ad Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Assuming every monetized view receives an ad.</li>
  <li>Confusing Watch Page ads with Shorts Feed ads.</li>
  <li>Treating a manual mid-roll slot as a guaranteed ad.</li>
  <li>Applying the 55% share to gross advertiser spending or Premium revenue.</li>
  <li>Ignoring embedded-player revenue eligibility.</li>
  <li>Adding disruptive mid-roll placements solely to increase slot count.</li>
  <li>Assuming a green monetization icon guarantees a particular ad or RPM.</li>
  <li>Believing ads on a non-YPP video mean the uploader is paid.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>What percentage of Watch Page ad revenue do creators receive?</h4>
<p>YouTube's published overview states 55% of net qualifying ad revenue under the Watch Page Monetization Module.</p>

<h4>Are Watch Page ads available on Shorts?</h4>
<p>Shorts viewed in the Shorts Feed use the separate Shorts Feed revenue-sharing model. A Short watched in another context should be interpreted according to YouTube's applicable content and monetization rules.</p>

<h4>How long must a video be for mid-roll ads?</h4>
<p>YouTube currently allows mid-rolls on monetized videos that are eight minutes or longer.</p>

<h4>Does placing more mid-roll slots guarantee more revenue?</h4>
<p>No. Slots are opportunities. YouTube decides whether an ad serves, and excessive disruption can reduce viewing and satisfaction.</p>
