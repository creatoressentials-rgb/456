<h2>Talent Manager</h2>
<p>A <strong>talent manager</strong> is a professional who advises, represents, and supports an individual creator, performer, influencer, artist, or other public talent.</p>
<p>For creators, a talent manager may help with:</p>
<ul>
<li>Career strategy</li>
<li>Brand partnerships</li>
<li>Negotiation</li>
<li>Positioning</li>
<li>Team building</li>
<li>Platform relationships</li>
<li>Product opportunities</li>
<li>Scheduling</li>
<li>Public relations</li>
<li>Long-term planning</li>
</ul>
<p>The exact role depends on the contract, jurisdiction, and management business.</p>
<h3>Talent manager vs. creator manager</h3>
<p>The terms often overlap.</p>
<p>A <a href="/glossary/creator-manager">creator manager</a> may emphasize digital platforms, content operations, creator commerce, and brand partnerships.</p>
<p>A talent manager may come from entertainment, advertising, sports, music, or traditional representation.</p>
<p>The practical questions are:</p>
<ul>
<li>What services are provided?</li>
<li>What authority is granted?</li>
<li>Which income is commissioned?</li>
<li>Is the relationship exclusive?</li>
<li>What happens after termination?</li>
</ul>

<p>In some jurisdictions, procuring employment for artists is regulated. A title alone does not determine whether someone is legally acting as an agent.</p>
<h3>Talent manager vs. business manager</h3>
<p>A business manager often handles:</p>
<ul>
<li>Cash flow</li>
<li>Bills</li>
<li>Budgets</li>
<li>Accounting coordination</li>
<li>Financial administration</li>
<li>Insurance</li>
<li>Tax coordination</li>
</ul>
<p>A talent manager focuses more on career and commercial opportunities.</p>
<p>One firm can provide both, but the duties and controls should remain clear.</p>
<h3>Talent manager vs. YouTube Partner Manager</h3>
<p>A <strong>YouTube Partner Manager</strong> is part of an invite-only YouTube support program.</p>
<p>That person is not the creator's private representative and is not paid a management commission.</p>
<p>A private talent manager is selected under a separate agreement and may negotiate commercial opportunities across platforms.</p>

<li>Licensing</li>

<h3>Manager compensation</h3>
<p>Common models include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Percentage commission</li>
<li>Retainer</li>
<li>Salary</li>
<li>Project fee</li>
<li>Hybrid</li>
</ul>
<p>A percentage must identify the base.</p>
<p>Possible revenue categories include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Brand deals</li>
<li>Usage fees</li>
<li>Affiliate income</li>
<li>Platform revenue</li>
<li>Memberships</li>
<li>Products</li>
<li>Appearances</li>
<li>Licensing</li>
<li>Existing relationships</li>
<li>Renewals</li>
</ul>
<p>A narrow commission on manager-negotiated deals is different from a commission on all creator income.</p>
<h3>Authority</h3>
<p>The contract should say whether the manager can:</p>
<ul>
<li>Negotiate</li>
<li>Accept an offer</li>
<li>Sign</li>
<li>Approve content</li>
<li>Hire vendors</li>
<li>Spend money</li>
<li>Receive payment</li>
<li>Submit invoices</li>
<li>Access analytics</li>
<li>Access the channel</li>
<li>Speak publicly</li>
</ul>
<p>Creators should preserve final control unless there is a specific reason to delegate it.</p>
<h3>Exclusivity</h3>
<p>Exclusivity can apply to:</p>
<ul>
<li>All management</li>
<li>Brand deals</li>
<li>One platform</li>
<li>One territory</li>
<li>One industry</li>
<li>Specified opportunities</li>
</ul>
<p>A broad exclusive relationship can block the creator from using specialists.</p>
<h3>Post-term commission</h3>
<p>A manager may request commission after termination for contracts created during the relationship.</p>
<p>Define:</p>
<ul>
<li>Covered agreements</li>
<li>Covered renewals</li>
<li>Duration</li>
<li>Rate</li>
<li>Declining schedule</li>
<li>Options</li>
<li>Existing relationships</li>
<li>New work from old brands</li>
</ul>
<p>An unlimited commission on unrelated future work is a major red flag.</p>
<h3>Permissions and security</h3>
<p>YouTube allows authorized agents and managers to manage eligible creator deals and provides channel permissions.</p>
<p>Use role-based access rather than sharing:</p>
<ul>
<li>Password</li>
<li>Two-factor code</li>
<li>Recovery code</li>
<li>Personal email</li>
<li>Unrelated files</li>
</ul>
<p>Remove access promptly after the relationship ends.</p>
<h3>Conflicts of interest</h3>
<p>A talent manager may have conflicts when they:</p>
<ul>
<li>Represent competing creators</li>
<li>Receive brand-side payments</li>
<li>Own a production company</li>
<li>Recommend owned vendors</li>
<li>Invest in a creator product</li>
<li>Control financial reporting</li>
<li>Prefer high-commission deals over audience fit</li>
</ul>
<p>Conflicts should be disclosed.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>What percentage does a talent manager charge?</h4>
<p>There is no universal percentage. The rate and commission base depend on services, market, leverage, and contract.</p>
<h4>Is a talent manager the same as an agent?</h4>
<p>Not always. Agents often focus on procuring work, while managers commonly provide broader career guidance. Legal distinctions vary.</p>
<h4>Can a manager sign contracts for a creator?</h4>
<p>Only if the creator grants that authority. The agreement should state its limits clearly.</p>
<h4>Is a YouTube Partner Manager a talent manager?</h4>
<p>No. A YouTube Partner Manager is platform support, not private talent representation.</p>
