<h2>When Your Viewers Are on YouTube</h2>
<p><strong>When your viewers are on YouTube</strong> is an Audience-tab report showing when the people who watch your channel are online across YouTube during the last 28 days.</p>
<p>The report is commonly displayed as a day-and-time heatmap. Darker or more intense blocks represent periods when more of the channel's viewers were online on YouTube.</p>

<h3>What Does the Report Measure?</h3>
<p>The report reflects your viewers' online activity across YouTube, not only the minutes when they watched your channel.</p>
<p>It therefore answers:</p>
<ul>
<li>When are people from my audience generally active on YouTube?</li>
<li>Which days and hours may be useful for community interaction?</li>
<li>When might more of my existing audience be available for a Premiere or live stream?</li>
</ul>
<p>It does not show that every viewer will watch your next upload during the darkest block.</p>

<h3>What Time Period Does It Use?</h3>
<p>YouTube currently says the report uses viewer activity from the last 28 days. It is a recent behavioral snapshot rather than a permanent audience schedule.</p>
<p>The pattern can change because of:</p>
<ul>
<li>School and work schedules</li>
<li>Weekends and holidays</li>
<li>Seasonal routines</li>
<li>Audience growth in new time zones</li>
<li>Changes in the channel's topics or formats</li>
<li>Major events and live programming</li>
</ul>

<h3>Where Can You Find It?</h3>
<ol>
<li>Sign in to YouTube Studio.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Analytics</strong>.</li>
<li>Open the <strong>Audience</strong> tab.</li>
<li>Find the <strong>When your viewers are on YouTube</strong> report.</li>
</ol>
<p>The report may be unavailable when YouTube does not have enough relevant audience data. YouTube does not publish the exact threshold and can change data-limiting rules.</p>

<h3>How to Read the Heatmap</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Heatmap Feature</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Darker or more intense block</td><td>More of the channel's viewers were online across YouTube during that time</td></tr>
<tr><td>Lighter block</td><td>Fewer viewers were online during that time</td></tr>
<tr><td>Day column or row</td><td>Viewer activity for a particular day of the week</td></tr>
<tr><td>Hourly block</td><td>A time-of-day interval shown in the report's displayed time zone</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Review the time-zone label shown in YouTube Studio, especially when the creator travels or manages an audience in several regions.</p>

<h3>Best Uses for the Report</h3>
<p>YouTube specifically recommends the report for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Building community</li>
<li>Understanding when to schedule a Premiere</li>
<li>Planning a live stream</li>
</ul>
<p>It can also help with timing community posts, live Q&amp;As, chat moderation, and other activities that benefit from viewers being present at the same time.</p>

<h3>Does the Report Show the Best Time to Upload?</h3>
<p>It can help creators choose a practical publication time, especially when early audience activity matters. However, YouTube says publish time is not known to affect a video's long-term performance.</p>
<p>YouTube's recommendation guidance says publishing while the audience is active may produce more immediate views, but the system aims to deliver relevant content whenever viewers visit. Do not treat the darkest square as a guaranteed ranking advantage.</p>

<h3>Upload Time vs. Premiere and Live Time</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Content Type</th><th>How Timing Matters</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Regular uploaded video</td><td>Timing may affect immediate notifications and early views, but is not known to determine long-term performance</td></tr>
<tr><td>Premiere</td><td>Viewers need to be available at the scheduled time for the shared watch and live chat experience</td></tr>
<tr><td>Live stream</td><td>Concurrent audience availability directly affects live attendance and interaction</td></tr>
<tr><td>Community post</td><td>Posting during active periods may increase the chance of prompt interaction, though distribution is not limited to that moment</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<h3>How Far Before the Peak Should You Publish?</h3>
<p>There is no universal offset. Some creators publish before an active period so notifications, processing, and initial distribution are underway. Others publish during the peak.</p>
<p>Test several consistent time windows and evaluate:</p>
<ul>
<li>First-hour and first-day views</li>
<li>Live attendance</li>
<li>Premiere reminders and chat activity</li>
<li>Audience geography</li>
<li>Whether the time is sustainable for the creator and moderation team</li>
<li>Long-term performance rather than only immediate views</li>
</ul>

<h3>What If Your Audience Spans Multiple Time Zones?</h3>
<ul>
<li>Choose a time that overlaps the largest important audience groups.</li>
<li>Rotate live-stream times when several regions matter.</li>
<li>Use scheduled uploads so production hours do not control publication.</li>
<li>Review top geographies alongside the heatmap.</li>
<li>Offer replays and follow-up posts for viewers who cannot attend live.</li>
<li>Do not assume the creator's local evening is the audience's evening.</li>
</ul>

<h3>What If the Report Is Missing?</h3>
<p>YouTube may limit audience reports when a channel lacks sufficient relevant data, when the audience watches Made for Kids content, or when other privacy or policy conditions apply.</p>
<p>Until more data appears, use:</p>
<ul>
<li>Top geographies</li>
<li>Realtime reports</li>
<li>Past live-stream attendance</li>
<li>Community-post response patterns</li>
<li>A consistent sustainable schedule</li>
<li>Direct audience polls</li>
</ul>

<h3>Common Viewer-Timing Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Reading the report as activity only on the creator's channel.</li>
<li>Treating the darkest block as a guaranteed recommendation boost.</li>
<li>Ignoring the displayed time zone.</li>
<li>Using one 28-day pattern as a permanent schedule.</li>
<li>Optimizing upload time while neglecting topic, thumbnail, title, and viewer satisfaction.</li>
<li>Scheduling live content at a peak time the creator cannot support consistently.</li>
<li>Assuming missing data means the channel has no viewers.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Does the report show when subscribers are online?</h4>
<p>It shows when the channel's viewers are online across YouTube. Viewers and subscribers are not identical groups.</p>
<h4>Is the report based on the last 28 days?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube currently defines it using the last 28 days.</p>
<h4>Will uploading at the darkest time improve long-term views?</h4>
<p>YouTube says publish time is not known to affect long-term performance, though active periods may help immediate viewing.</p>
<h4>Why can't I see the report?</h4>
<p>The channel may not have enough relevant audience data, or the report may be limited by privacy, content, or platform conditions.</p>
