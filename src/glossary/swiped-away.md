<h2>Swiped Away</h2>
<p><strong>Swiped Away</strong> describes a Shorts Feed outcome where the viewer moves past a Short instead of staying to watch beyond the initial seconds.</p>
<p>It appears as the opposite side of YouTube's <strong>How many chose to view</strong> report. The report compares viewers who stayed with viewers who swiped away.</p>

<h3>What Does a Swipe Mean?</h3>
<p>A swipe means the viewer advanced to another item in the Shorts Feed before qualifying as having stayed to watch.</p>
<p>It can reflect:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Low immediate interest</li>
  <li>Unclear opening context</li>
  <li>An audience mismatch</li>
  <li>A familiar or repetitive idea</li>
  <li>Poor audio or visual clarity</li>
  <li>A viewer browsing rapidly without strong intent</li>
</ul>
<p>The metric does not reveal the viewer's exact reason.</p>

<h3>Does Swiped Away Mean Zero Views?</h3>
<p>Not necessarily. Since March 31, 2025, YouTube counts a Shorts view whenever the Short starts to play or replay, without a minimum watch-time requirement.</p>
<p>A fast swipe can therefore contribute to the broader public Shorts Views count while failing to qualify as an <a href="/glossary/engaged-views/">Engaged View</a> or a Stayed to Watch outcome.</p>

<h3>Swiped Away vs. Stayed to Watch</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Outcome</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td><a href="/glossary/stayed-to-watch/">Stayed to Watch</a></td><td>The viewer remained past the initial seconds</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Swiped Away</td><td>The viewer moved on before passing that initial engagement point</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>The percentages are complementary within the same report and should approximately total 100%, subject to display rounding.</p>

<h3>Swiped Away vs. Engaged Views</h3>
<p>Engaged Views count viewers who stayed past the initial seconds and exclude loops. Swiped Away is a percentage outcome from feed presentations.</p>
<p>A creator cannot convert the swipe percentage directly into a precise engaged-view total without compatible exposure and reporting data.</p>

<h3>Swiped Away vs. Retention Dips</h3>
<p><a href="/glossary/retention-dips/">Retention Dips</a> occur after viewers have begun an engaged viewing session and then skip or leave at a particular point.</p>
<p>Swiped Away concerns the initial Shorts Feed decision. It usually happens before the deeper retention graph becomes useful.</p>

<h3>Where Can You See Swiped Away?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Analytics</strong>.</li>
  <li>Open the <strong>Content</strong> tab.</li>
  <li>Select the <strong>Shorts</strong> chip.</li>
  <li>Find <strong>How many chose to view</strong>.</li>
  <li>Review the Viewed and Swiped Away percentages.</li>
</ol>

<h3>What Is a Good Swiped-Away Percentage?</h3>
<p>YouTube does not publish one universal acceptable swipe rate.</p>
<p>A useful comparison depends on:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The creator's normal range</li>
  <li>Short length</li>
  <li>Topic</li>
  <li>Audience familiarity</li>
  <li>Traffic mix</li>
  <li>Geography and language</li>
  <li>How broadly YouTube is showing the Short</li>
  <li>Whether the content is instructional, entertainment, news, or niche</li>
</ul>

<h3>Does a Certain Swipe Rate Kill a Short?</h3>
<p>YouTube does not publish a rule that a Short stops receiving feed distribution at a specific swipe-away percentage.</p>
<p>Claims such as "anything above 40% is dead" or "YouTube stops the video at 50%" are not official platform thresholds.</p>
<p>Initial choice matters, but YouTube also considers personalized relevance, engaged viewing, deeper retention, satisfaction, likes, dislikes, feedback, and other signals.</p>

<h3>Common Reasons for High Swiped Away</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The first frame does not reveal the subject.</li>
  <li>The creator opens with a greeting or logo.</li>
  <li>On-screen text is small or crowded.</li>
  <li>The first sentence is generic.</li>
  <li>The video begins before the audio is understandable.</li>
  <li>The title suggests a different topic.</li>
  <li>The Short resembles low-quality recycled content.</li>
  <li>The content reaches viewers outside its intended niche.</li>
  <li>The same viewer has seen the Short or idea before.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Not Every Swipe Is a Creative Failure</h3>
<p>Shorts viewers make rapid personalized choices. A swipe can occur because:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The viewer does not currently need the information.</li>
  <li>The language is wrong for the viewer.</li>
  <li>The topic is outside the viewer's interests.</li>
  <li>The viewer is looking for entertainment rather than education.</li>
  <li>The viewer has limited time.</li>
  <li>The feed is testing the Short with a broader audience.</li>
</ul>
<p>The goal is not to eliminate all swipes. That is unrealistic.</p>

<h3>High Swiped Away With Strong Retention</h3>
<p>This can indicate a narrow but highly satisfied audience. The opening may need clearer targeting rather than broader clickbait.</p>
<p>Try:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Naming the intended viewer immediately.</li>
  <li>Showing the specific problem.</li>
  <li>Using clearer text in the first frame.</li>
  <li>Keeping the niche promise intact.</li>
  <li>Comparing performance across similar niche Shorts.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Low Swiped Away With Weak Completion</h3>
<p>A strong opening can earn the initial stay while the rest of the Short underdelivers.</p>
<p>Possible problems include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>A delayed payoff</li>
  <li>Repetition</li>
  <li>An unclear middle</li>
  <li>A misleading opening</li>
  <li>A weak conclusion</li>
  <li>Unnecessary length</li>
</ul>
<p>Review average view duration, percentage viewed, and retention—not only the swipe rate.</p>

<h3>How to Reduce Avoidable Swipes</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Start with the subject, result, or problem.</li>
  <li>Remove blank frames and slow fades.</li>
  <li>Use clear mobile-sized captions.</li>
  <li>Make the first spoken line specific.</li>
  <li>Use visual motion that supports the topic.</li>
  <li>Match the title and opening.</li>
  <li>Avoid long channel branding.</li>
  <li>Test opening structures across separate uploads.</li>
  <li>Review several Shorts before drawing conclusions.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Should You Re-upload a Short With High Swipes?</h3>
<p>Repeatedly deleting and re-uploading the same content is not a reliable strategy. It discards the existing video's data and can create duplicate-content or audience-fatigue problems.</p>
<p>A better approach is to apply the lesson to a meaningfully improved version or a future Short with:</p>
<ul>
  <li>A clearer first frame</li>
  <li>A stronger opening sentence</li>
  <li>Faster context</li>
  <li>Better audio</li>
  <li>A different structure or angle</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to Compare Swipe Performance</h3>
<p>Compare Shorts that share:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Similar topics</li>
  <li>Similar lengths</li>
  <li>Similar audience size</li>
  <li>Similar publication periods</li>
  <li>Similar traffic-source mixes</li>
</ul>
<p>Comparing a 12-second comedy clip with a 90-second technical tutorial can produce misleading conclusions.</p>

<h3>Swipes and Viewer Satisfaction</h3>
<p>A lower swipe rate is helpful only if the Short delivers. Misleading hooks can reduce swipes initially while harming <a href="/glossary/viewer-satisfaction/">Viewer Satisfaction</a>, comments, dislikes, and long-term trust.</p>
<p>The opening should accurately earn attention rather than trap the viewer.</p>

<h3>Common Swiped-Away Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Assuming a swipe means no public view.</li>
  <li>Treating a swipe as proof the video is bad.</li>
  <li>Using an unofficial universal cutoff.</li>
  <li>Ignoring audience mismatch.</li>
  <li>Optimizing the opening while neglecting the payoff.</li>
  <li>Comparing different formats and lengths.</li>
  <li>Repeatedly deleting and re-uploading.</li>
  <li>Using misleading hooks to lower the rate.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>What does Swiped Away mean?</h4>
<p>It means a Shorts Feed viewer moved past the Short before staying beyond the initial seconds.</p>

<h4>Can a swipe still count as a view?</h4>
<p>Yes. Since March 31, 2025, Shorts Views count starts and replays without a minimum watch-time requirement.</p>

<h4>What is a bad swipe rate?</h4>
<p>YouTube does not publish a universal bad rate. Compare similar Shorts on your own channel.</p>

<h4>Does high Swiped Away permanently stop distribution?</h4>
<p>YouTube does not publish an exact cutoff or permanent-stop rule.</p>
