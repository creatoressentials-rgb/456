<h2>YouTube Premieres</h2>
<p>A <strong>YouTube Premiere</strong> turns an already-finished, already-uploaded video into a scheduled live event. Instead of publishing quietly, the video gets a public countdown page where fans can set reminders, chat, and comment before it "airs," then everyone watches it together in real time — complete with live chat and, if enabled, <a href="/glossary/super-chat/">Super Chat</a> and <a href="/glossary/super-stickers/">Super Stickers</a>. Once the Premiere ends, it becomes a normal video on the channel.</p>
<p>Premieres are popular for music video drops, big announcements, product reveals, and any upload where a creator wants the hype and community energy of a live stream without the risk of actually going live on camera.</p>

<h3>How a YouTube Premiere Works</h3>

<h4>Before the Premiere</h4>
<p>As soon as it's scheduled, YouTube creates a public watch page for the video — it shows up in search, on the homepage, and in <a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">suggested videos</a> just like a normal upload. Viewers who land on the page before the start time can:</p>
<ul>
<li>Set a reminder (they're notified ~30 minutes before and again when it starts)</li>
<li>Leave comments and chat</li>
<li>Watch an optional trailer, if the creator uploaded one</li>
<li>Send Super Chats or Super Stickers if the channel has them turned on</li>
</ul>

<h4>During the Premiere</h4>
<p>A countdown plays for a period the creator chooses when setting it up (commonly a couple of minutes), then the video begins playing for everyone at once. Viewers can rewind but can't skip ahead of what's already played — it behaves like a live broadcast of a finished file. Live chat and comments stay open the entire time, and the creator can drop into chat too, though nothing requires them to be present; the video plays automatically either way.</p>

<h4>After the Premiere</h4>
<p>The countdown theme disappears, and the video settles onto the channel as a normal upload. Its view count from the Premiere carries over to the video, and Chat Replay lets people scroll back through the live chat as if they'd been there. From here it behaves like <a href="/glossary/monetized-playbacks/">monetized playbacks</a> and <a href="/glossary/engaged-views/">engaged views</a> on any other video — the Premiere event doesn't create a separate, permanent category of viewing.</p>

<h3>How to Schedule a YouTube Premiere</h3>
<ol>
<li><strong>Upload the video.</strong> In <a href="/glossary/youtube-studio/">YouTube Studio</a>, click Create → Upload videos and select your file. Fill in title, description, thumbnail, and other details as usual. (Note: Shorts, 360°/VR180 video, and resolutions above 1080p aren't supported for Premieres.)</li>
<li><strong>Choose visibility.</strong> On the Visibility step, pick Schedule, set a date and time, then check Set as Premiere. To release it as soon as processing finishes instead, choose Public → Set as instant Premiere.</li>
<li><strong>Set up the Premiere.</strong> Click Set up Premiere to choose a countdown theme and length. Channels with 1,000+ subscribers and no active Community Guidelines strikes can also add a 15-second to 3-minute trailer to play on the watch page before the countdown.</li>
<li><strong>Turn on monetization options.</strong> If your channel runs ads, confirm ads are enabled (only pre-roll ads play during the Premiere itself). If you're eligible, make sure <a href="/glossary/youtube-supers/">Super Chat and Super Stickers</a> and <a href="/glossary/channel-memberships/">channel memberships</a> perks are switched on so fans can support you live.</li>
<li><strong>Publish and share.</strong> Click Done or Schedule, then share the watch page URL so fans can set reminders, comment, and build anticipation before the countdown starts.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Monetization During a Premiere</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Ad revenue</strong>: Only pre-roll ads can serve during the Premiere countdown and playback; mid-roll and post-roll ads are unavailable until the Premiere ends, after which the channel's normal upload ad defaults take over. This affects <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">ad revenue</a> and <a href="/glossary/youtube-rpm/">YouTube RPM</a> calculations for the event window specifically.</li>
<li><strong>Super Chat &amp; Super Stickers</strong>: These work the same way they do on a live stream — available automatically if the channel is <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a>-eligible for Supers and has live chat turned on (the default). Creators can also explicitly toggle them on before or during the Premiere.</li>
<li><strong>Channel memberships</strong>: Members-only perks like a members chat, custom emoji, and loyalty badges carry over into the Premiere chat if the channel offers memberships.</li>
<li><strong>Views and watch time</strong>: Viewing during a Premiere counts the same way a regular public upload's viewing does — the view count transfers onto the permanent video once the Premiere is over, and the video contributes to standard <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">traffic sources</a> reporting in YouTube Analytics like any other upload.</li>
</ul>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Can I chat during my own Premiere?</h4>
<p>Yes. Nothing requires the creator to be present — the video plays automatically at the scheduled time either way — but you can join the live chat during your own Premiere to talk with viewers in real time, the same way you would during a live stream.</p>
<h4>Do Super Chat and Super Stickers work during a Premiere?</h4>
<p>Yes, as long as your channel meets Super Chat/Super Stickers eligibility requirements and the feature is turned on. Because live chat is on by default, Supers are generally available automatically during a Premiere if you already have them enabled for your channel; you can also confirm the toggle is on before or during the event.</p>
<h4>Are ads shown during a Premiere the same as on a normal video?</h4>
<p>Not exactly. Only pre-roll ads can run during the Premiere itself — mid-roll and post-roll ads are unavailable until the Premiere ends, at which point the video reverts to your channel's regular upload ad defaults.</p>
<h4>How is a Premiere different from just scheduling a regular upload?</h4>
<p>A scheduled upload simply goes public at a chosen time with no countdown or event page — viewers just see the finished video appear. A Premiere adds a public pre-release watch page, a countdown, live chat, comments, and optional Super Chat/Super Stickers and trailer, so it plays out like a live event even though the underlying video is fully pre-recorded.</p>
