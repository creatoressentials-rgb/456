<h2>Device Type</h2>
<p><strong>Device type</strong> is a YouTube Analytics report showing what percentage of a channel's watch time comes from each supported device category.</p>
<p>YouTube's current Audience report lists four device types: mobile, computer, TV, and tablet. The categories are ordered from the highest to the lowest share of watch time.</p>

<h3>What Does Device Type Measure?</h3>
<p>The report measures the percentage of <strong>watch time</strong> generated on each device type. It does not directly show:</p>
<ul>
<li>The percentage of unique viewers using each device</li>
<li>The percentage of views from each device</li>
<li>The exact phone, television, browser, or computer model</li>
<li>The number of people watching together on one screen</li>
<li>Whether one person uses several devices</li>
</ul>
<p>A device can produce a high watch-time share because viewers watch longer on it, even if it generates fewer individual starts.</p>

<h3>Supported Device Categories</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Device Type</th><th>Typical Viewing Context</th><th>Creator Consideration</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Mobile</td><td>Phones and mobile YouTube apps</td><td>Small screens, vertical use, headphones, and on-the-go viewing</td></tr>
<tr><td>Computer</td><td>Desktop and laptop viewing</td><td>Large browser windows, multitasking, search, and detailed interfaces</td></tr>
<tr><td>TV</td><td>YouTube on television screens and TV viewing environments</td><td>Distance viewing, shared screens, large text, and remote navigation</td></tr>
<tr><td>Tablet</td><td>Tablet apps and tablet-sized displays</td><td>Intermediate screen size and mixed mobile or home use</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>These are broad Analytics categories. Do not infer exact hardware or household behavior from them.</p>

<h3>Where Can You Find the Report?</h3>
<ol>
<li>Sign in to YouTube Studio.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Analytics</strong>.</li>
<li>Open the <strong>Audience</strong> tab.</li>
<li>Find the <strong>Device type</strong> card.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube also says creators can view device analytics by content type, helping distinguish how audiences watch videos, Shorts, or other supported content formats.</p>

<h3>Device Type vs. Operating System</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Report</th><th>What It Groups</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Device type</td><td>Broad viewing devices such as mobile, computer, TV, and tablet</td></tr>
<tr><td>Operating system</td><td>Software platforms used to access YouTube, when available in expanded reports</td></tr>
<tr><td>Geography</td><td>Viewer location by country or region</td></tr>
<tr><td>Playback location</td><td>Where the video was watched, such as YouTube watch pages or embedded players</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>A mobile device can use different operating systems, and one operating system can appear across more than one device category.</p>

<h3>Why Device Type Matters</h3>
<ul>
<li>Screen size affects text readability.</li>
<li>TV viewing can make detailed interfaces and small captions difficult to see.</li>
<li>Mobile viewers may use portrait orientation and headphones.</li>
<li>Computer viewers may follow software tutorials while working in another window.</li>
<li>Shared TV viewing complicates assumptions about one viewer per device.</li>
<li>Device mix can differ across Shorts, live streams, and standard videos.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to Design for Mobile Viewers</h3>
<ul>
<li>Use readable text and avoid tiny interface captures.</li>
<li>Keep important subjects clear at phone size.</li>
<li>Use close-ups when demonstrating small controls.</li>
<li>Test captions around YouTube interface elements.</li>
<li>Make audio understandable through small speakers and headphones.</li>
<li>Do not assume every mobile viewer watches vertically.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to Design for TV Viewers</h3>
<ul>
<li>Use larger on-screen text.</li>
<li>Keep diagrams and charts simple enough to read from a distance.</li>
<li>Use clean audio suitable for speakers rather than headphones alone.</li>
<li>Avoid placing essential details near the extreme edges.</li>
<li>Allow enough time for viewers to read titles and labels.</li>
<li>Consider that several people may share the screen.</li>
<li>Make navigation and calls to action understandable without tiny visual cues.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Device Type and Shorts</h3>
<p>Shorts are strongly associated with mobile viewing, but YouTube content can be watched on several device categories. Use the content-type filter rather than assuming the channel-wide device mix applies equally to every format.</p>
<p>A creator publishing both long-form videos and Shorts may find that the formats produce substantially different device behavior.</p>

<h3>Device Type and Screen Recordings</h3>
<p>Software tutorials may attract computer viewers while still being watched on phones and televisions. A screen recording that looks clear on the editing monitor may become unreadable elsewhere.</p>
<p>Use <a href="/glossary/screen-recording/">screen-recording</a> crops, zooms, cursor emphasis, and narration so the video remains understandable without relying solely on small text.</p>

<h3>Can Device Type Explain Retention?</h3>
<p>It can provide context but does not prove causation. TV viewers may watch longer because they select long content, or long content may attract TV viewers. Mobile viewers may leave a detailed tutorial because the text is small, or because they only needed one answer.</p>
<p>Compare device information with content type, video length, traffic source, and retention before drawing conclusions.</p>

<h3>How to Use Device Data Strategically</h3>
<ol>
<li>Review the channel-wide device mix.</li>
<li>Filter by content type where available.</li>
<li>Compare important videos with different audiences or formats.</li>
<li>Test text, graphics, captions, and audio on the dominant devices.</li>
<li>Improve accessibility for smaller and distance-viewing screens.</li>
<li>Track whether device mix changes after a format shift.</li>
<li>Avoid redesigning the entire channel from one short reporting period.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Common Device-Type Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Reading the report as the percentage of viewers on each device.</li>
<li>Assuming one TV screen equals one person.</li>
<li>Using channel-wide device data for every format without filtering.</li>
<li>Ignoring small text because most editing occurs on a computer monitor.</li>
<li>Assuming mobile always means vertical viewing.</li>
<li>Inferring specific brands or models from broad categories.</li>
<li>Claiming device type directly caused retention or revenue changes.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Which devices does YouTube list?</h4>
<p>The current Audience report lists mobile, computer, TV, and tablet.</p>
<h4>Does Device type show viewer percentage?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube defines it as the percentage of watch time from each device.</p>
<h4>Can I view device data by content type?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube says device analytics can also be viewed by content type.</p>
<h4>Why can TV watch time be high with fewer views?</h4>
<p>TV sessions may be longer. Watch-time share and view share are different measurements.</p>
