<h2>YouTube Partner Program</h2>
<p>The <strong>YouTube Partner Program (YPP)</strong> is YouTube's official monetization program — the gate every creator has to pass through before they can earn <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">ad revenue</a>, accept <a href="/glossary/fan-funding/">fan funding</a>, sell through <a href="/glossary/youtube-shopping/">YouTube Shopping</a>, or access most other ways to make money on the platform. If you've read about <a href="/glossary/super-chat/">Super Chat</a>, <a href="/glossary/channel-memberships/">Channel Memberships</a>, or <a href="/glossary/super-thanks/">Super Thanks</a> in this glossary, YPP is the program that makes all of them possible — you can't turn on any revenue feature until your channel is accepted.</p>
<p>YPP is free to join, but it isn't automatic. YouTube sets minimum subscriber, watch-hour, and Shorts-view thresholds, then puts every applicant through a policy review before granting access. As of 2026, YouTube runs two eligibility tiers: an Early Monetization tier that unlocks fan-funding and Shopping features at a lower bar, and a standard tier that unlocks full ad revenue sharing.</p>

<h3>What is the YouTube Partner Program?</h3>
<p>YPP gives eligible creators:</p>
<ul>
<li>A share of the revenue from ads shown on their videos and Shorts</li>
<li>A share of YouTube Premium subscription revenue from members who watch their content</li>
<li>Access to fan-funding features like memberships, Super Chat, <a href="/glossary/super-stickers/">Super Stickers</a>, and Super Thanks</li>
<li>Access to <a href="/glossary/youtube-shopping/">YouTube Shopping</a> and other commerce features</li>
<li>Access to YouTube's Creator Support team and, at higher subscriber tiers, a dedicated partner manager</li>
<li>Eligibility for <a href="/glossary/youtube-creator-partnerships/">YouTube Creator Partnerships</a> and brand-facing programs that require an active YPP channel</li>
</ul>
<p>Joining YPP doesn't guarantee income — it only unlocks the ability to earn. What you actually make still depends on factors covered elsewhere in this glossary, like your <a href="/glossary/youtube-rpm/">YouTube RPM</a>, <a href="/glossary/playback-based-cpm/">playback-based CPM</a>, and how many of your views are <a href="/glossary/monetized-playbacks/">monetized playbacks</a>.</p>

<h3>YouTube Partner Program requirements (2026)</h3>
<p>YouTube evaluates channels against two sets of thresholds. You only need to clear one of the two paths (watch hours or Shorts views) within each tier — they aren't additive.</p>

<h4>Eligibility tier comparison</h4>
<table>
<thead><tr><th></th><th>Early Monetization (Expanded YPP)</th><th>Standard YPP (Full Monetization)</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Subscribers</td><td>500</td><td>1,000</td></tr>
<tr><td>Recent uploads</td><td>3 valid public uploads in the last 90 days</td><td>Not separately required</td></tr>
<tr><td>Watch hours or Shorts views</td><td>3,000 valid public watch hours (last 12 months) or 3,000,000 valid public Shorts views (last 90 days)</td><td>4,000 valid public watch hours (last 12 months) or 10,000,000 valid public Shorts views (last 90 days)</td></tr>
<tr><td>What it unlocks</td><td>Channel Memberships, Super Chat &amp; Super Stickers, Super Thanks, Jewels and gifts during live streams, Shopping</td><td>Everything in Early Monetization, plus ad revenue sharing (watch page and Shorts Feed ads) and YouTube Premium <a href="/glossary/revenue-share/">revenue share</a></td></tr>
<tr><td>Country/region availability</td><td>Available in a defined, expanding list of countries/regions</td><td>Available more broadly</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p><strong>Important nuance most guides skip:</strong> watch hours generated by Shorts views inside the Shorts Feed do not count toward the 4,000-hour (or 3,000-hour) long-form watch-hour threshold. If you're tracking progress in Studio, make sure you're reading the right metric — a channel can rack up millions of Shorts views without moving its long-form watch-hour counter at all.</p>
<p>Shorts and long-form are two separate, parallel qualification paths. A channel with 1,000 subscribers and 10 million Shorts views in 90 days qualifies for full YPP without a single long-form upload; a channel with 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours qualifies without any Shorts. You never need to hit both.</p>

<h4>Additional requirements (apply to both tiers)</h4>
<p>Meeting the subscriber/watch-hour/view numbers only gets you an application, not approval. YouTube also requires:</p>
<ul>
<li>Compliance with the <a href="/glossary/youtube-channel/">YouTube channel</a> monetization policies — this includes rules on <a href="/glossary/reused-content/">reused content</a> and repetitious uploads, which is one of the most common rejection reasons</li>
<li>No active <a href="/glossary/copyright-strike/">Community Guidelines strikes</a> on the channel</li>
<li>2-Step Verification turned on for the linked Google Account</li>
<li>"Advanced features" access on the channel — see <a href="/glossary/youtube-advanced-mode/">YouTube Advanced Mode</a> for what this unlocks and how to verify your phone number to get it</li>
<li>One active AdSense for YouTube account linked to the channel (or readiness to create one during the application)</li>
<li>Your channel based in a country/region where YPP (or the expanded tier) is available</li>
<li>Age 18+ to hold the linked AdSense account and receive payouts. Creators 13–17 can still be in YPP, but a parent or guardian must own and manage the AdSense account that receives the money</li>
</ul>
<p>A channel that is entirely or mostly <a href="/glossary/made-for-kids/">Made for Kids</a> can still join YPP, but several monetization features — including personalized ads, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks, and Shopping — are automatically disabled on that content, which caps how much of YPP's earning potential it can actually use.</p>

<h3>How to apply to the YouTube Partner Program</h3>
<ol>
<li>Confirm you've cleared one of the eligibility paths above in <a href="/glossary/youtube-studio/">YouTube Studio</a>'s Earn tab.</li>
<li>Turn on 2-Step Verification for your Google Account, if it isn't already.</li>
<li>Go to YouTube Studio → Earn → Apply now.</li>
<li>Review and accept the YPP Base terms.</li>
<li>Set up a new AdSense for YouTube account, or link an existing active one (only create a new one inside YouTube Studio — a self-created AdSense account outside that flow can cause linking problems).</li>
<li>Submit. Your channel enters an automated + human review queue; status shows as "In Progress" under "Get Reviewed."</li>
</ol>

<h3>Review timelines and reapplying</h3>
<p>YouTube states that review decisions typically take about one month, though volume spikes, system issues, or disagreement between reviewers can extend that. If your counts (subscribers, watch hours, Shorts views) dip below the threshold while you're waiting for review, it doesn't matter — you're still evaluated as long as you met the threshold at the time you applied.</p>
<p>If your application is rejected:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>First rejection:</strong> you can appeal within 21 days, or simply keep uploading original content and reapply after 30 days.</li>
<li><strong>Second (or later) rejection:</strong> the waiting period before you can reapply extends to 90 days.</li>
</ul>
<p>Most rejections trace back to policy issues rather than the numbers — heavy use of <a href="/glossary/reused-content/">reused content</a>, unresolved <a href="/glossary/copyright-claim/">copyright claims</a> or a <a href="/glossary/copyright-strike/">copyright strike</a>, or content that doesn't meet advertiser-friendly guidelines. Fixing the underlying content issue matters more than reapplying quickly.</p>

<h3>What you can earn once you're in</h3>

<tr><td>Super Thanks</td><td>70% creator / 30% YouTube</td><td>Early Monetization+</td></tr>

<p>Long-form ad revenue depends on demand for ads against your content, which is reflected in metrics like <a href="/glossary/youtube-rpm/">YouTube RPM</a> and <a href="/glossary/playback-based-cpm/">playback-based CPM</a> — two numbers every partner should understand once they're monetizing, since they explain why similar view counts can produce very different payouts.</p>
<p>Shorts ad revenue works differently from long-form: YouTube pools all Shorts Feed ad revenue each month, deducts a share to cover music licensing based on how much licensed music appears across monetized Shorts, and distributes the remainder (the "Creator Pool") to partners based on their share of total <a href="/glossary/engaged-views/">engaged views</a> from monetizing creators. Creators keep 45% of their allocated share regardless of whether they used music.</p>

<h3>Growing toward the thresholds</h3>
<p>Since watch hours and Shorts views are the two levers that decide when you can apply, the fastest path to YPP is usually the same set of habits that grow any channel:</p>
<ul>
<li>Build a base of <a href="/glossary/regular-viewers/">regular viewers</a> and <a href="/glossary/returning-viewers/">returning viewers</a> rather than relying only on one-off spikes — repeat viewership compounds watch hours faster than constant new-viewer acquisition alone.</li>
<li>Pay attention to which <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">traffic sources</a> are actually driving views — channels that get meaningful volume from <a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">suggested videos</a> and <a href="/glossary/browse-features/">browse features</a> tend to accumulate watch hours more efficiently than those relying only on external traffic.</li>
<li>Track how often viewers <a href="/glossary/stayed-to-watch/">stayed to watch</a> versus clicking away immediately — <a href="/glossary/retention/">retention</a> has a direct effect on how much of your <a href="/glossary/unique-viewers/">unique viewers</a> pool converts into real watch hours.</li>
<li>If your audience spans multiple languages, features like <a href="/glossary/multi-language-audio/">multi-language audio</a> and <a href="/glossary/auto-dubbing/">auto-dubbing</a> can expand your reach into new regions and add watch hours without producing entirely new videos.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Staying in YPP after you're approved</h3>
<p>Acceptance isn't permanent. YouTube can pause or remove monetization if:</p>
<ul>
<li>Your channel has no uploads or Community posts for 6 months or more</li>
<li>You violate the YouTube channel monetization policies, regardless of your current subscriber count or watch hours</li>
<li>You accumulate <a href="/glossary/copyright-strike/">Community Guidelines strikes</a>, or a pattern of <a href="/glossary/copyright-claim/">copyright claims</a> through <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a> signals a compliance problem</li>
<li>Your AdSense account is suspended or unlinked</li>
</ul>
<p>If monetization is removed for policy reasons, you can generally reapply after 30 days once the underlying issue is fixed, following the same review process as a first-time applicant.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Do I need 1,000 subscribers to join the YouTube Partner Program?</h4>
<p>No — not anymore. YouTube's Early Monetization tier lets you join at 500 subscribers (plus 3 uploads in 90 days and either 3,000 watch hours or 3 million Shorts views), which unlocks memberships, Super Chat, Super Thanks, and Shopping. You still need 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours (or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days) to unlock ad revenue sharing specifically.</p>
<h4>Can I qualify for YPP with Shorts only, no long-form videos?</h4>
<p>Yes. Shorts views and long-form watch hours are two separate, parallel paths — you only need to clear one. 1,000 subscribers plus 10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days qualifies a channel for full YPP, including ad revenue, without a single long-form video.</p>
<h4>How long does the YouTube Partner Program review take?</h4>
<p>YouTube's own guidance is "typically about one month." Some channels hear back sooner, but a month is the realistic expectation to plan around rather than the few-days-to-two-weeks figure that circulates in some unofficial guides.</p>
<h4>What happens if my YPP application gets rejected?</h4>
<p>After a first rejection, you can appeal within 21 days or simply reapply after 30 days once you've addressed the issue. After a second rejection, the wait before you can try again extends to 90 days. Most rejections come down to policy problems — like reused content or unresolved copyright issues — not the subscriber or watch-hour numbers.</p>
