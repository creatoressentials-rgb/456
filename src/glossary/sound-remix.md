<h2>Sound Remix</h2>
<p><strong>Sound Remix</strong> is a YouTube Shorts feature that lets you create new visuals using audio from an eligible Short, long-form video, music video, or shared sound page. The remixed audio becomes the soundtrack or spoken-audio layer of your Short, while YouTube connects the new upload to the sound and its source.</p>
<p>Creators use Sound Remix for music trends, voice clips, dialogue, jokes, narration, challenges, and repeatable formats built around a recognizable audio cue.</p>

<h3>How Sound Remix works</h3>
<p>Sound Remix separates the audio from the source's visuals. You record or upload your own video while the selected sound plays in the Shorts editor.</p>
<p>YouTube groups Shorts that use the same sound on a shared audio page. Viewers can tap the sound to see other Shorts made with it and, when eligible, create their own version.</p>
<p>The source may be:</p>
<ul>
<li>Original audio recorded in another Short.</li>
<li>Audio from an eligible long-form YouTube video.</li>
<li>An eligible song or music video.</li>
<li>An eligible AI-generated Dream Track soundtrack.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to use Sound Remix</h3>
<h4>From another Short</h4>
<ol>
<li>Open the Short containing the sound.</li>
<li>Tap the <strong>Sound</strong> chip in the lower-right corner.</li>
<li>Review the sound page and other Shorts using the audio.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Use this sound</strong>.</li>
<li>Record or add your own visuals.</li>
<li>Edit and publish the Short.</li>
</ol>
<h4>From a long-form video</h4>
<ol>
<li>Open the eligible video in the YouTube mobile app.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Share</strong>.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Remix</strong>.</li>
<li>Choose <strong>Use this sound</strong>.</li>
<li>Record and edit your Short.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube's current creator update places Remix for regular videos inside the Share menu. Older guides may show the control directly beneath the player.</p>

<h3>How much audio can Sound Remix use?</h3>
<p>The available duration depends on the source and its licensing.</p>
<p>For eligible music videos, YouTube's current Help Center says creators can sample <strong>up to 90 seconds</strong> of audio. Some videos are limited to <strong>30 seconds</strong> because of partner agreements. The editor should display the amount available for the selected sound.</p>
<p>Older articles that state a universal 60-second maximum are no longer accurate for every eligible music-video remix. However, the 90-second figure should not be treated as a promise for every sound. Original audio, individual songs, territories, and partner rights can produce different limits.</p>

<h3>Sound Remix vs. YouTube's audio libraries</h3>
<p>YouTube uses several audio-related terms that are easy to confuse.</p>

  <th>Purpose</th>

<p>Sound Remix is source-linked. The downloadable YouTube Audio Library is a separate collection intended for broader video editing workflows.</p>

<h3>Why is a sound unavailable to remix?</h3>
<p>A sound may be unavailable because:</p>
<ul>
<li>The original creator restricted audio remixing.</li>
<li>The source was deleted, made private, or edited.</li>
<li>The rights holder did not make the audio available in your territory.</li>
<li>Music ownership or partner agreements limit the duration or use.</li>
<li>The video has copyright or policy restrictions.</li>
<li>The app, account, or device does not show the feature.</li>
</ul>
<p>A source can sometimes permit audio remixing while blocking visual remix tools. YouTube notes that certain third-party claims may prevent Cut, Collab, or Green Screen even when the underlying music remains available as audio.</p>

<h3>Attribution and sound pages</h3>
<p>YouTube attributes a Sound Remix through the sound chip, audio page, and source metadata. Viewers can use the sound page to discover the original audio and other Shorts using the same sound.</p>
<p>For creators analyzing discovery, traffic from a shared audio results page can appear under <strong>Sound pages</strong> in <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">YouTube traffic sources</a>. This is different from the <strong>Remixed video</strong> source used for visual remixes.</p>

<h3>What happens if the source creator removes the sound?</h3>
<p>Audio remixes can be affected after publication. If the original creator deletes the source, restricts remixing, or makes certain edits, YouTube says the dependent audio remix may be:</p>
<ul>
<li>Muted.</li>
<li>Set to unlisted.</li>
<li>Scheduled for deletion in 30 days.</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube emails the remixing creator about the change. The creator may be able to download the video without the remixed audio from YouTube Studio and upload it again with a different soundtrack.</p>
<p>This is an important difference from visual remixes, which may be deleted when the source video becomes unavailable.</p>

<h3>Copyright and off-platform use</h3>
<p>Using <strong>Use this sound</strong> inside YouTube does not grant unlimited ownership of the audio. It provides access within the Shorts creation workflow under the permissions and licenses YouTube has for that source.</p>
<p>Downloading the audio and editing it into a video outside YouTube's tools may create a different rights situation. YouTube warns that externally added copyrighted material can receive a <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a> match, a <a href="/glossary/copyright-claim/">copyright claim</a>, or a removal request that may lead to a <a href="/glossary/copyright-strike/">copyright strike</a>.</p>
<p>For Shorts longer than one minute, YouTube says an active Content ID claim can cause the Short to be blocked globally. Creators should use the duration shown in the editor and pay attention to upload checks and rights notifications.</p>

<h3>Can Sound Remixes be monetized?</h3>
<p>A Sound Remix is not automatically eligible for monetization. The creator must qualify for the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a>, and the Short must follow YouTube's Shorts and channel monetization policies.</p>
<p>Music licensing is considered within YouTube's Shorts revenue-sharing system, but the creator still needs to contribute original visuals or performance. Repeatedly publishing low-effort copies built around other people's audio can also create <a href="/glossary/reused-content/">reused content</a> or inauthentic-content concerns depending on the channel as a whole.</p>

<h3>Effective ways to use Sound Remix</h3>
<p>Sound Remix works well for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Participating in a recognizable music or dialogue trend.</li>
<li>Creating a visual punchline timed to a voice clip.</li>
<li>Demonstrating a process to a beat or spoken instruction.</li>
<li>Adding a new interpretation to a song or soundtrack.</li>
<li>Building a recurring series around the same audio cue.</li>
<li>Showing several visual examples under one narration or sound.</li>
</ul>
<p>The sound may attract the first view, but the visuals and idea should give viewers a reason to watch your version rather than another Short using the same audio.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is Sound Remix limited to 60 seconds?</h4>
<p>Not universally. YouTube currently says eligible music-video audio can be used for up to 90 seconds, while some tracks are limited to 30 seconds. Other sounds may have different limits.</p>
<h4>Is Sound Remix copyright-free?</h4>
<p>No. It is licensed or permitted for eligible use within YouTube's Shorts tools. That does not make the audio public domain or grant unrestricted off-platform rights.</p>
<h4>Can the original creator remove a remixed sound later?</h4>
<p>The creator or rights holder can restrict or remove the source. YouTube may then mute the remix, set it to unlisted, and schedule it for deletion after 30 days.</p>
<h4>Is Sound Remix the same as the YouTube Audio Library?</h4>
<p>No. Sound Remix reuses audio tied to an existing YouTube source. The YouTube Audio Library is a separate downloadable catalog of music and sound effects in YouTube Studio.</p>
