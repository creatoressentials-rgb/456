<h2>Public Video</h2>
<p>A <strong>Public video</strong> is a YouTube upload that anyone on YouTube can watch, subject to separate age, country, copyright, account, or policy restrictions.</p>
<p>Public videos are posted on the creator's channel and can appear in search results, related-video lists, recommendations, subscriber feeds, playlists, embeds, and external sharing.</p>

<h3>Where Can a Public Video Appear?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The creator's channel</li>
  <li>YouTube Search</li>
  <li><a href="/glossary/browse-features/">Browse features</a>, including Home</li>
  <li><a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">Suggested videos</a> and related-video lists</li>
  <li>The Subscriptions feed</li>
  <li>Notifications for eligible subscribers</li>
  <li>Public playlists</li>
  <li>External websites and apps through links or embeds</li>
  <li>Search engines outside YouTube</li>
  <li>Social media and messaging platforms</li>
</ul>
<p>"Can appear" does not mean the video will appear prominently or at all in each location.</p>

<h3>Public Does Not Guarantee Reach</h3>
<p>Public is an access and eligibility setting, not a distribution promise. A Public video may receive few impressions when:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The topic has low demand.</li>
  <li>The audience is poorly matched.</li>
  <li>The title or thumbnail is unclear.</li>
  <li>The video faces strong competition.</li>
  <li>Viewer response is weak.</li>
  <li>The upload is restricted by copyright, age, geography, or policy.</li>
  <li>The channel is new or has limited audience history.</li>
</ul>
<p>Creators should analyze <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">traffic sources</a>, impressions, click-through rate, retention, watch time, and audience response rather than treating visibility as an algorithm diagnosis.</p>

<h3>Public vs. Published</h3>
<p>On YouTube, Public is the visibility state that gives general access. "Published" is often used more broadly to describe making the upload available through Public, Unlisted, or another release workflow.</p>
<p>A scheduled video is uploaded and stored before it is publicly published. An Unlisted video is accessible by link but is not a normally listed Public video.</p>

<h3>Public Videos and Subscriber Feeds</h3>
<p>YouTube's visibility table says Public videos can appear in subscriber feeds. Creators can choose not to publish a particular upload to the Subscriptions feed by disabling <strong>Publish to subscriptions feed and notify subscribers</strong>.</p>
<p>When that option is disabled, subscribers do not receive upload notifications and do not see the upload in their Subscription feed through that release action.</p>

<h3>Do All Subscribers Get a Notification?</h3>
<p>No. Notification delivery depends on viewer bell settings, device and account settings, channel notification limits, platform systems, and the creator's upload setting.</p>
<p>YouTube limits viewers to a maximum of three upload and live-stream notifications from one channel in a 24-hour period. A Public upload can therefore be fully visible without notifying every subscriber.</p>

<h3>Can Public Videos Have Comments?</h3>
<p>Yes, when comments are enabled and permitted for the video. The creator can:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Allow comments</li>
  <li>Hold potentially inappropriate comments</li>
  <li>Hold all comments for review</li>
  <li>Limit commenting to certain groups where supported</li>
  <li>Disable comments</li>
</ul>
<p>Public visibility does not force comments to remain open.</p>

<h3>Can Anyone Share or Embed a Public Video?</h3>
<p>Anyone can copy and share the Public URL. Embedding can work when the creator and video settings allow it.</p>
<p>Public availability also means:</p>
<ul>
  <li>People can quote or discuss the video.</li>
  <li>Third-party websites can link to it.</li>
  <li>Viewers can create Clips when eligible.</li>
  <li>Viewers may remix eligible content under the creator's remix settings.</li>
  <li>People can capture screenshots or recordings, subject to law and platform rules.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Can a Public Video Still Be Restricted?</h3>
<p>Yes. Public visibility does not remove other restrictions. A Public video can be:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Age-restricted</li>
  <li>Blocked in certain countries</li>
  <li>Blocked worldwide by a rights holder</li>
  <li>Limited to signed-in viewers</li>
  <li>Unavailable on some embedded players</li>
  <li>Limited or ineligible for ads</li>
  <li>Removed under Community Guidelines or copyright law</li>
</ul>
<p>Check the Notices or Restrictions areas in YouTube Studio rather than assuming Public means unrestricted.</p>

<h3>Public Videos and YouTube Checks</h3>
<p>Before publication, creators can upload as Private or Unlisted and wait for:</p>
<ul>
  <li><a href="/glossary/copyright-checks/">Copyright Checks</a></li>
  <li><a href="/glossary/ad-suitability-checks/">Ad Suitability Checks</a></li>
  <li>Processing</li>
  <li>Caption generation</li>
  <li>Thumbnail and title review</li>
  <li>Manual chapters</li>
  <li>Cards and end screens</li>
</ul>
<p>Publishing while checks are running is allowed, but claims or monetization decisions can appear later.</p>

<h3>Changing a Public Video to Unlisted</h3>
<p>Changing to Unlisted removes ordinary public listing and discovery but keeps the same URL accessible to anyone who has it. Existing embeds and shared links can continue working.</p>
<p>The link may already have spread while the video was Public, so changing to Unlisted does not make the old audience forget or lose the URL.</p>

<h3>Changing a Public Video to Private</h3>
<p>Changing to Private removes access for ordinary viewers, disables comments, and breaks general link and embed access. The creator can invite approved accounts privately.</p>
<p>The underlying views, comments, URL, and Analytics can remain associated with the upload even though they are no longer publicly available.</p>

<h3>Making an Existing Video Public for the First Time</h3>
<p>When a Private or Unlisted upload becomes Public for the first time, it can be posted to the channel and made eligible for search, recommendations, subscriber feeds, and notifications.</p>
<p>Before changing it:</p>
<ol>
  <li>Review old Unlisted comments.</li>
  <li>Confirm the title and <a href="/glossary/video-packaging/">video packaging</a>.</li>
  <li>Check processing and policy status.</li>
  <li>Confirm the publish-to-subscriber-feed setting.</li>
  <li>Review timestamps, chapters, captions, cards, and end screens.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Public Watch-Page Dates</h3>
<p>YouTube says the date shown on the watch page is based on Pacific Standard Time. Creators in other time zones may see a different public date than the date displayed in Studio or expected from local publication time.</p>
<p>This matters most for videos published or scheduled close to midnight Pacific Time.</p>

<h3>Should Every Finished Video Be Public?</h3>
<p>No. Public is appropriate when general access and potential discovery support the creator's goal.</p>
<p>Keep a video Unlisted or Private when:</p>
<ul>
  <li>It is a client preview.</li>
  <li>Rights or releases are incomplete.</li>
  <li>The content is outdated or inaccurate.</li>
  <li>The upload is awaiting checks or collaborator approval.</li>
  <li>The audience is intentionally limited.</li>
  <li>The video is an internal draft or archive.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Common Public-Video Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Assuming Public guarantees views.</li>
  <li>Publishing before processing and checks finish.</li>
  <li>Expecting every subscriber to receive a notification.</li>
  <li>Confusing Public with unrestricted or policy-approved.</li>
  <li>Publishing sensitive information because the intended audience is small.</li>
  <li>Ignoring the subscriber-feed notification checkbox.</li>
  <li>Changing to Unlisted and assuming previous viewers no longer have the link.</li>
  <li>Deleting a video when a visibility change would preserve its history.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Can anyone watch a Public YouTube video?</h4>
<p>Generally yes, although separate age, country, account, copyright, or policy restrictions may limit access.</p>

<h4>Will a Public video appear in search?</h4>
<p>It can appear, but Public visibility does not guarantee indexing, ranking, or impressions.</p>

<h4>Does Public automatically notify subscribers?</h4>
<p>It may notify eligible subscribers, but delivery is limited by creator settings, viewer settings, and YouTube's notification rules.</p>

<h4>Can I make a Public video Private later?</h4>
<p>Yes. The existing upload remains in Studio, but ordinary public access and comments are removed.</p>
