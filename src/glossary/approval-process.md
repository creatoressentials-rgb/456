<h2>Approval Process</h2>
<p>An <strong>approval process</strong> is the agreed workflow used to review and authorize creator work before publication, delivery, payment, or brand use. It identifies what the creator must submit, who reviews it, which issues the reviewer may request changes to, how long each review takes, and when the work becomes final.</p>
<p>A clear approval process prevents a <a href="/glossary/brand-deal/">brand deal</a> from becoming an open-ended series of comments from multiple stakeholders. It also helps the creator plan production and protects the campaign launch date.</p>
<h3>Common stages in a creator approval process</h3>
<p>The exact workflow depends on the campaign, but it may include:</p>
<h4>Creator selection and contract approval</h4>
<p>The brand confirms the creator, and both sides approve the compensation, <a href="/glossary/deliverables/">deliverables</a>, rights, timing, disclosure, and other contract terms.</p>
<h4>Concept approval</h4>
<p>The creator submits a short treatment, hook, outline, or concept before investing in full production. The brand verifies that the idea fits the campaign without taking over every creative decision.</p>
<h4>Script or talking-point review</h4>
<p>The brand checks product names, factual claims, required messages, prohibited statements, links, codes, and the planned <a href="/glossary/sponsorship-disclosure/">sponsorship disclosure</a>.</p>
<p>A script review should not automatically authorize the brand to rewrite the creator's honest opinion or require unsupported claims.</p>
<h4>Rough-cut review</h4>
<p>The creator submits a draft video or other near-complete asset. The brand reviews it against the agreed <a href="/glossary/creative-brief/">creative brief</a>, factual requirements, and deliverables.</p>
<h4>Compliance or legal review</h4>
<p>A legal, regulatory, product, or brand-safety reviewer may verify disclosures, claims, trademarks, regulated language, or other high-risk details. The contract should identify whether this is a separate review stage and whether it can create additional revisions.</p>
<h4>Final approval</h4>
<p>The named approver confirms that the asset can be published or delivered. Final approval should be documented in writing.</p>
<h4>Publication and post-publication verification</h4>
<p>After posting, the creator may send the live URL, screenshots, disclosure proof, or other required evidence. The brand may confirm that links, tags, codes, captions, and the live version match the approved asset.</p>
<h3>What the contract should define</h3>
<p>A workable approval clause should state:</p>
<ul>
<li>Which assets or stages require approval</li>
<li>The file format and submission method</li>
<li>The person or role with final approval authority</li>
<li>Whether multiple departments review simultaneously or in sequence</li>
<li>The review deadline for each submission</li>
<li>What happens when the brand misses its deadline</li>
<li>How feedback must be consolidated</li>
<li>The number of included <a href="/glossary/revision-round/">revision rounds</a></li>
<li>Which changes count as ordinary revisions</li>
<li>When a reshoot, new concept, or added asset becomes extra scope</li>
<li>Whether factual, legal, or compliance corrections are treated differently</li>
<li>The latest date the brand may change products, links, offers, or talking points</li>
<li>How launch delays affect the creator's schedule and payment</li>
<li>Whether final approval is required in writing</li>
<li>What happens if the platform rejects, removes, or restricts the content</li>
</ul>
<p>A creator should not assume that silence means approval unless the contract expressly creates a deemed-approval rule.</p>

<p>Approval of the creative does not automatically transfer ownership or grant usage rights. Those permissions must be defined separately.</p>
<h3>Who should approve creator content?</h3>
<p>The contract should identify one person or a clearly defined role that can give binding final approval. That person may collect internal comments from:</p>
<ul>
<li>Brand marketing</li>
<li>Product or technical teams</li>
<li>Legal or compliance</li>
<li>Advertising agencies</li>
<li>Retail or distribution partners</li>
<li>The campaign's end client</li>
</ul>
<p>The creator should receive one consolidated response. Conflicting comments from several people can turn one <a href="/glossary/revision-round/">revision round</a> into multiple rounds and create avoidable delays.</p>
<h3>Protecting creator authenticity</h3>
<p>Approval should let the brand check accuracy and compliance without making the content misleading or unnatural. Useful boundaries include:</p>
<ul>
<li>The brand can correct objective product facts.</li>
<li>The brand can require agreed disclosures and mandatory claims that are properly supported.</li>
<li>The creator retains control of personal opinions, delivery, and audience-appropriate wording unless the contract says otherwise.</li>
<li>The creator can reject statements that are false, unsupported, or inconsistent with actual experience.</li>
<li>Major changes after concept approval may require a revised fee or schedule.</li>
</ul>
<p>A highly scripted endorsement can create both performance and compliance problems if the creator no longer sounds truthful.</p>
<h3>A practical approval timeline</h3>
<p>A campaign schedule can include:</p>
<ol>
<li>Concept due Monday</li>
<li>Brand feedback due Wednesday</li>
<li>Draft due the following Monday</li>
<li>Consolidated feedback due within two business days</li>
<li>Revised draft due within two business days</li>
<li>Final approval due within one business day</li>
<li>Publication on the agreed date</li>
</ol>
<p>The important point is not the exact number of days. It is that each dependency has an owner and deadline.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Does every sponsored video need brand approval?</h4>
<p>Not necessarily. Approval rights depend on the agreement. Some brands approve only claims and disclosure, while others require concept, script, rough-cut, and final approval.</p>
<h4>How many approval rounds are normal?</h4>
<p>There is no universal number. The contract should state how many revision rounds are included and require the brand to consolidate feedback. Additional rounds or reshoots can be separately priced.</p>
<h4>What if the brand does not respond by the deadline?</h4>
<p>Follow the contract. Possible outcomes include schedule extension, deemed approval, creator rescheduling, or additional fees, but none should be assumed unless written into the agreement.</p>
<h4>Can a brand request changes after final approval?</h4>
<p>The creator can agree, but substantial post-approval changes may be new scope. The contract should address emergency legal corrections, changed offers, product recalls, and optional creative changes separately.</p>
