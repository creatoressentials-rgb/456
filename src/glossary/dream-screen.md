<h2>Dream Screen</h2>
<p><strong>Dream Screen</strong> is a generative-AI feature built into the <a href="/glossary/youtube-shorts/">YouTube Shorts</a> camera that lets creators type a text prompt to generate an AI image or video — used either as a green-screen background behind them or as a standalone clip inserted into a Short. It's powered by Google DeepMind's Imagen and Veo models, and it's one of several AI creation tools YouTube has rolled into the Shorts "AI Playground" since 2023.</p>
<p>Dream Screen is about <strong>generating new visuals from a prompt</strong> — it does not select, summarize, or repurpose a creator's existing footage. That makes it a fundamentally different tool from <a href="/glossary/auto-generated-clips/">Auto-Generated Clips</a> and <a href="/glossary/auto-generated-shorts/">Auto-Generated Shorts</a>, which use AI to identify and re-cut highlight moments from long-form videos a creator already uploaded. See the disambiguation section below for the full breakdown.</p>

<h3>How Dream Screen Works</h3>
<ol>
<li>A creator opens the Shorts camera and selects <strong><a href="/glossary/green-screen/">Green screen</a></strong>, then <strong>Dream Screen</strong> (accessed via the sparkle/AI icon).</li>
<li>They type a text prompt describing the background they want — for example, "a candy-colored landscape" or "a magical forest with a stream" — and optionally pick a visual style (such as claymation or vintage film).</li>
<li>The system generates four still images (originally powered by Imagen 3) for the creator to choose from.</li>
<li>The creator either uses the chosen image as a static background or taps <strong>Create video</strong> to animate it into a short AI video clip (powered by Veo), which then plays behind them as they record.</li>
<li>Since 2025, creators can also skip the green-screen step entirely and generate a <strong>standalone AI video clip</strong> — with up to three reference images as of the January 2026 "Ingredients to Video" update — to drop anywhere into a Short, such as a establishing shot or transition.</li>
</ol>
<p>All Dream Screen output is automatically watermarked with Google's invisible <strong>SynthID</strong> technology and labeled as AI-generated content — creators don't need to take any separate disclosure step.</p>

<h3>Step-by-Step: How to Use Dream Screen</h3>
<p><strong>To generate a video background:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Open the YouTube app and tap <strong>Create (+)</strong>.</li>
<li>Tap the sparkle/AI icon, then select <strong>Backgrounds</strong> (or, inside the Shorts camera, tap <strong>Green screen → Dream Screen</strong>).</li>
<li>Describe the background you want and choose a style.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Create</strong>, then choose your favorite of the generated images.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Use image</strong> or <strong>Create video</strong> to animate it.</li>
<li>Record yourself in front of the background, then finish editing and add your Short's details before publishing.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>To generate a standalone AI video clip:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Open the Shorts camera and tap <strong>Add</strong> to open the media picker.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Create video</strong> at the top.</li>
<li>Describe the clip you want (optionally attaching up to three reference images).</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Create</strong>, preview the result, and choose your desired clip length.</li>
<li>Insert the clip into your Short's <a href="/glossary/timeline/">timeline</a> and continue editing as normal.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Availability, Cost, and Eligibility</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cost:</strong> Free. No subscription or YouTube Premium requirement.</li>
<li><strong>Devices:</strong> Mobile only, via the YouTube app — works the same on iOS and Android. There is no desktop version.</li>
<li><strong>Language:</strong> In most countries, your YouTube app's device language needs to be set to <strong>English</strong> to see Dream Screen at all.</li>
<li><strong>Country availability:</strong> Rolled out unevenly and expanded gradually — starting in the US, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand, later adding India (via the <a href="/glossary/youtube-create/">YouTube Create</a> app) and select markets in the Middle East and North Africa. <strong>The EU/European Economic Area, UK, and Switzerland are excluded from some or all Dream Screen sub-features</strong> (green-screen backgrounds, standalone clips, Photo to Video, and Make Me Move each have slightly different exclusion lists). Because YouTube updates this rollout frequently without a single canonical list, check directly in your Shorts camera to confirm current access rather than relying on any article's country list — including this one.</li>
<li><strong>Subscriber requirement:</strong> None. Dream Screen isn't gated by subscriber count, watch hours, or <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a> status.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Content Policy and Labeling</h3>
<p>All Dream Screen output must comply with YouTube's Community Guidelines, and YouTube states it has built in safeguards that block prompts violating policy or touching sensitive topics. Notably, the tool is designed to avoid generating photorealistic images of identifiable real people, specifically to reduce the risk of deepfake misuse. Creators are still responsible for reviewing generated content before publishing.</p>
<p>Every Dream Screen creation carries two layers of AI disclosure:</p>
<ul>
<li>An <strong>invisible SynthID digital watermark</strong> embedded in the file itself.</li>
<li>A <strong>visible "AI-generated" label</strong> YouTube automatically attaches to the Short — no manual disclosure step required from the creator.</li>
</ul>
<p>If you're weighing whether heavy use of AI-generated visuals affects monetization eligibility, note that Dream Screen is a creative tool layered onto a Short you still record and edit yourself — it's not the same as uploading wholesale <a href="/glossary/reused-content/">reused content</a>. Review YouTube's monetization and policies guidance if you're building a channel style that leans heavily on AI-generated backgrounds.</p>
<p>Because Dream Screen output is AI-generated video, be mindful of prompts that reference copyrighted characters, brands, or media properties — publishing a Short built around a prompt like "generate [a copyrighted character] in space" can still expose a video to a <a href="/glossary/copyright-claim/">copyright claim</a> or <a href="/glossary/content-id/">Content ID</a> match if the output is close enough to protected material, even though the pixels were AI-generated.</p>

<h3>Dream Screen vs. Other YouTube AI Tools (Don't Confuse These)</h3>
<p>Dream Screen shares a "Made on YouTube" AI lineage with several other tools that get mixed up in search results and casual conversation:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Tool</th>
<th>What it actually does</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><strong>Dream Screen</strong></td>
<td>Generates a new AI image/video background or standalone clip from a text prompt</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><a href="/glossary/auto-generated-clips/">Auto-Generated Clips</a></td>
<td>Uses AI to find and extract highlight moments from a creator's <em>existing</em> long-form videos</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><a href="/glossary/auto-generated-shorts/">Auto-Generated Shorts</a></td>
<td>Uses AI to automatically repurpose segments of existing long-form uploads into Shorts</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Dream Track</strong></td>
<td>Generates an AI-created <strong>music/soundtrack</strong> clip for a Short from a text prompt — audio, not visuals</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong><a href="/glossary/ask-studio/">Ask Studio</a></strong></td>
<td>An AI assistant inside <a href="/glossary/youtube-studio/">YouTube Studio</a> for brainstorming ideas, writing titles/descriptions, and answering channel questions — not a video/image generator</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>If a Short's background or scenery looks AI-generated, it almost certainly used Dream Screen. If a Short is a repurposed clip from a longer video, that's Auto-Generated Clips or Auto-Generated Shorts at work — a completely different mechanism.</p>

<h3>Why It Matters for Growth</h3>
<p>Dream Screen lowers the production bar for Shorts that need a specific look — a fantasy setting, an establishing shot, a stylized backdrop — without filming or licensing footage. Used well, distinctive visuals can help a Short stand out while scrolling through Shorts <a href="/glossary/browse-features/">browse features</a> and <a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">suggested videos</a>. Used poorly (generic, overused AI aesthetics), it risks blending into the flood of AI-generated Shorts content flagged by multiple creator-economy outlets as a saturation concern. Treat it as a production shortcut for a specific creative need, not a substitute for a strong hook or story.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Is Dream Screen free to use?</h4>
<p>Yes. It's built into the standard YouTube app at no cost, with no subscription or YouTube Premium requirement.</p>
<h4>Is Dream Screen available on both iPhone and Android?</h4>
<p>Yes, it works the same way on iOS and Android. It's mobile-only — there's no desktop version.</p>
<h4>Does Dream Screen content get labeled as AI-generated?</h4>
<p>Yes, automatically. Every Dream Screen creation carries an invisible SynthID watermark and a visible AI-generated label that YouTube adds without any extra step from the creator.</p>
<h4>Is Dream Screen the same as Auto-Generated Clips or Auto-Generated Shorts?</h4>
<p>No. Dream Screen generates brand-new AI visuals from a text prompt. <a href="/glossary/auto-generated-clips/">Auto-Generated Clips</a> and <a href="/glossary/auto-generated-shorts/">Auto-Generated Shorts</a> instead use AI to select and repurpose moments from footage a creator already uploaded — no new visuals are generated.</p>
