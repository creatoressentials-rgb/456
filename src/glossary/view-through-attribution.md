<h2>View-Through Attribution</h2>
<p><strong>View-through attribution</strong> assigns conversion credit after a person sees an eligible advertisement, does not click or otherwise complete a stronger qualifying interaction, and later converts within the applicable view-through window.</p>
<p>The reported outcome is commonly called a <strong>view-through conversion</strong>.</p>
<p>A simplified journey is:</p>
<ol>
<li>A person sees a creator-based advertisement.</li>
<li>The person does not click.</li>
<li>The person later visits the brand through another route.</li>
<li>The person completes a purchase or lead.</li>
<li>The advertising system connects the conversion with the earlier impression.</li>
<li>The conversion is reported as view-through under the platform's rules.</li>
</ol>
<h3>View-through attribution vs. click attribution</h3>

<tr>

</tr>

<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Begins with an eligible ad impression</td>
  <td>Begins with a click</td>
</tr>
<tr>

</tr>
<tr>

</tr>

</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>A view-through conversion should not be described as a click conversion.</p>
<h3>View-through attribution vs. engaged-view attribution</h3>
<p>Google also reports <strong>engaged-view conversions</strong> for eligible video campaigns.</p>
<p>An engaged-view conversion can occur when the viewer watches enough of an eligible video ad or completes another qualifying engagement without clicking, then converts within the selected window.</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
  <th>Interaction type</th>
  <th>Viewer behavior</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
  <td>Click-through</td>
  <td>Clicks the ad or eligible link</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>Engaged-view</td>
  <td>Watches or engages enough to satisfy the platform's rule</td>
</tr>
<tr>
  <td>View-through</td>
  <td>Sees the ad without an eligible click or engagement</td>
</tr>

  <td>Organic video view</td>
  <td>Watches ordinary creator content outside the paid-ad measurement rule</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>The exact thresholds depend on campaign and ad format.</p>
<h3>View-through attribution vs. organic creator views</h3>
<p>A viewer watching a creator's normal sponsored video is not automatically a Google Ads view-through impression.</p>
<p>View-through attribution normally refers to eligible <strong>paid advertising impressions</strong> measured by the ad platform.</p>
<p>Creator organic influence can instead be measured through:</p>
<ul>
<li>Affiliate links</li>
<li>Promo codes</li>
<li>Surveys</li>
<li>Brand Lift</li>
<li>Search interest</li>
<li>Direct traffic</li>
<li>Creator partnership reporting</li>
<li>Experimental lift studies</li>
</ul>
<h3>What is a view-through conversion window?</h3>
<p>The <strong>view-through conversion window</strong> is the period after an eligible ad impression during which a later conversion can receive view-through credit.</p>
<p>Google Ads allows eligible advertisers to select view-through windows and notes that these windows are typically shorter than interaction-based windows.</p>
<p>Examples can include:</p>
<ul>
<li>One day</li>
<li>Three days</li>
<li>Another eligible campaign setting</li>
</ul>
<p>A longer window increases the number of conversions that can be associated with earlier impressions.</p>
<h3>Conversion hierarchy</h3>
<p>Some ad systems prioritize stronger observed interactions.</p>
<p>Google's current documentation for certain conversion-optimized bidding describes a hierarchy of:</p>
<ol>
<li>Clicks</li>
<li>Engagements</li>
<li>Views</li>
</ol>
<p>This helps avoid crediting a conversion to an impression when the same person also completed a qualifying click or engagement within the relevant rules.</p>
<p>Creators and brands should still verify how each report deduplicates conversions across campaigns and platforms.</p>
<h3>Why brands use view-through attribution</h3>
<p>View-through measurement can be useful for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Video advertising</li>
<li>Display campaigns</li>
<li>Awareness campaigns</li>
<li>Connected television</li>
<li>Creator-based ads</li>
<li>Retargeting</li>
<li>Products with delayed response</li>
<li>Campaigns where viewers remember the brand and search later</li>
</ul>
<p>Many people do not click video ads immediately. A view-through report attempts to capture some of that later response.</p>
<h3>Why view-through attribution can overstate impact</h3>
<p>A person may see many ads before purchasing.</p>
<p>The credited impression may be:</p>
<ul>
<li>Incidental</li>
<li>Barely visible</li>
<li>One of many exposures</li>
<li>Unrelated to the final decision</li>
<li>Shown to an existing customer</li>
<li>Delivered after the person already intended to buy</li>
</ul>
<p>View-through attribution records exposure followed by conversion. It does not prove the exposure caused the conversion.</p>
<h3>Viewability and exposure</h3>
<p>An ad impression is not necessarily:</p>
<ul>
<li>Fully visible</li>
<li>Watched</li>
<li>Remembered</li>
<li>Heard</li>
<li>Understood</li>
<li>Associated with the creator</li>
</ul>
<p>Platforms define impression and viewability rules differently.</p>
<p>A campaign report should distinguish:</p>
<ul>
<li>Served impression</li>
<li>Viewable impression</li>
<li>Video view</li>
<li>Engaged view</li>
<li>Completed view</li>
<li>View-through conversion</li>
</ul>
<h3>View-through attribution and creator ads</h3>
<p>A brand may turn creator content into:</p>
<ul>
<li>YouTube creator partnership ads</li>
<li>Meta partnership ads</li>
<li>TikTok Spark Ads</li>
<li>Brand-account video ads</li>
<li>Display or streaming ads</li>
</ul>
<p>The platform can then report view-through outcomes under its advertising rules.</p>
<p>The contract should define:</p>
<ul>
<li><a href="/glossary/paid-usage">Paid usage</a></li>
<li>Ad identity</li>
<li>Platforms</li>
<li>Term</li>
<li>Territory</li>
<li>Attribution source</li>
<li>View-through window</li>
<li>Conversion definition</li>
<li>Reporting</li>
<li>Whether view-through conversions count toward bonuses</li>
</ul>
<h3>Comparing view-through results across platforms</h3>
<p>Do not compare totals without checking:</p>
<ul>
<li>Impression definition</li>
<li>Viewability</li>
<li>Window</li>
<li>Click and engagement hierarchy</li>
<li>Cross-device matching</li>
<li>Modeled conversions</li>
<li>Logged-in data</li>
<li>Conversion event</li>
<li>New-customer rules</li>
<li>Time zone</li>
<li>Deduplication</li>
<li>Refund treatment</li>
<li>Reporting delay</li>
</ul>
<p>Two platforms can both claim credit for the same customer.</p>
<h3>View-through attribution vs. incrementality</h3>
<p>View-through attribution is especially vulnerable to confusing correlation with causation.</p>
<p>A stronger measurement plan can include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Randomized holdout</li>
<li>Conversion Lift</li>
<li>Geographic test</li>
<li>Audience split</li>
<li>Brand Lift</li>
<li>Marketing mix modeling</li>
<li>Pre-registered success criteria</li>
</ul>
<p>Incrementality estimates additional conversions caused by the campaign rather than merely associated with impressions.</p>
<h3>When view-through attribution is useful</h3>
<p>It can be useful when:</p>
<ul>
<li>Clicks are not the main campaign behavior</li>
<li>Video is expected to create memory</li>
<li>The buying cycle continues after exposure</li>
<li>The platform has reliable conversion data</li>
<li>The window is reasonable</li>
<li>Click and engaged conversions are separated</li>
<li>Results are interpreted alongside lift or business data</li>
</ul>
<h3>View-through attribution red flags</h3>
<p>Be cautious when a report:</p>
<ul>
<li>Adds click and view-through conversions without explaining overlap</li>
<li>Uses a long window for a low-consideration product</li>
<li>Calls every organic video viewer a view-through conversion</li>
<li>Treats impressions as watched views</li>
<li>Gives no conversion definition</li>
<li>Includes existing customers without labeling them</li>
<li>Claims causation</li>
<li>Compares platforms with different windows</li>
<li>Uses view-through revenue as verified incremental profit</li>
<li>Hides modeled or estimated data</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Does a view-through conversion require a click?</h4>
<p>No. It applies when the viewer sees an eligible ad, does not complete the qualifying interaction, and later converts under the platform's rules.</p>
<h4>Is an organic YouTube view a view-through conversion?</h4>
<p>No. View-through conversion is an advertising attribution metric, not a label for every viewer of creator content.</p>
<h4>Does view-through attribution prove the ad caused the sale?</h4>
<p>No. It reports an eligible impression followed by a conversion. Incrementality testing is needed for stronger causal evidence.</p>
<h4>Why is the view-through window shorter than the click window?</h4>
<p>A shorter window reduces the chance of crediting conversions that are only loosely connected to a passive impression.</p>
