<h2>Traffic Sources</h2>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Traffic sources are the categories <a href="/glossary/youtube-studio/">YouTube Studio</a> uses to tell you <em>how</em> people ended up watching each of your videos — Browse Features, Suggested Videos, Search, External, Direct/Notifications, Playlists, the Shorts feed, and a handful of smaller sources. Your traffic source mix is the clearest fingerprint of how your channel actually grows, and it's the first thing worth checking before you try to diagnose any views, growth, or "why did this flop" problem.</p>
<p>You'll find the full breakdown in <strong>YouTube Studio → Analytics → Reach tab → Traffic source types</strong>. Click any individual source there (or switch to Advanced Mode) to see exactly which videos, <a href="/glossary/keywords/">keywords</a>, or pages sent you those views.</p>

<h3>The traffic source categories, briefly</h3>
<p>Each of these is its own line item in your Reach tab. The first four drive the vast majority of views for most channels — the rest matter, but usually in smaller amounts.</p>
<p><strong>Browse Features</strong> — Views from your subscribers' home feed and subscriptions feed: YouTube actively choosing to push your video to people. This is the source that turns a good video into a big one. See the full <a href="/glossary/browse-features/">Browse Features</a> entry for how it actually gets triggered.</p>
<p><strong>Suggested Videos</strong> — Views from the "up next" sidebar (desktop) or the autoplay queue (mobile), shown alongside or after another video. Driven by topic match and co-viewing patterns rather than your subscriber base. Full entry: <a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">Suggested Videos</a>.</p>
<p><strong>YouTube Search</strong> — Someone typed a query into the search bar and clicked your video. The most controllable source: title, thumbnail, and on-topic content move this number directly. Click into this source in Studio to see the exact keywords you're ranking for.</p>
<p><strong>Shorts Feed</strong> — Views from the vertical, swipe-driven Shorts shelf on mobile. For Shorts-first and hybrid channels this is now routinely the single largest traffic source, and it behaves differently from the sources above — expect huge swings from video to video.</p>
<p><strong>External</strong> — Views from outside YouTube entirely: blogs, embeds, search engines like Google, social media links, newsletters, Discord, wherever. This is the one source you can generate without waiting on YouTube's algorithm at all.</p>
<p><strong>Direct or Unknown / Notifications</strong> — Two related buckets: "Direct or unknown" is typed URLs, bookmarks, or unrecognized apps; "Notifications" covers views from bell alerts on new uploads. Both tend to skew toward your most loyal, already-subscribed audience.</p>
<p><strong>Playlists</strong> — Views that happened while a viewer was working through a playlist (yours or someone else's), including autoplay from one video to the next. A strong lever for session watch time, which the algorithm rewards.</p>
<p><strong>Everything else</strong> — Channel pages, <a href="/glossary/end-screens/">end screens</a>, video cards, hashtag pages, and paid "YouTube advertising" views. Individually small, but worth a glance if one of them spikes unexpectedly.</p>
<p><strong>A note on outdated info:</strong> if you see an article listing "Trending" as its own traffic source or as part of Browse Features, it's stale — YouTube retired the standalone Trending page in July 2025, and it no longer appears anywhere in Studio's Reach tab.</p>

<h3>How to read your own traffic mix</h3>
<p>Don't just look at the numbers — look at what they're telling you:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Search-heavy, everything else thin?</strong> Your content answers specific queries well, but YouTube's own algorithm isn't picking you up yet. Normal for newer channels; worth watching if it never shifts as you grow.</li>
<li><strong>Browse/Suggested-heavy, Search thin?</strong> The algorithm trusts you, but your titles and descriptions aren't matching what people actually type in. Easy fix, high upside.</li>
<li><strong>Direct/Notifications dominant?</strong> You're mostly being watched by people who already know you — a sign of a loyal core audience, but also a signal you're not reaching new viewers yet.</li>
<li><strong>A huge, one-video spike in any source?</strong> Check whether it's one <a href="/glossary/viral/">viral</a> video or a real trend before you change strategy. One outlier doesn't rewrite your whole channel's playbook.</li>
<li><strong>Shorts feed dominant, everything else near zero?</strong> Common and expected for Shorts-first channels — just don't assume that traffic behaves like long-form traffic when forecasting growth or planning a long-form upload.</li>
</ul>

<h3>What a healthy mix looks like</h3>
<p>There's no single "correct" split — it depends on your content and channel stage:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>New channels (under ~1,000 subscribers):</strong> Search and External often dominate, since Browse and Suggested need watch-history signal YouTube doesn't have yet.</li>
<li><strong>Established evergreen/tutorial channels:</strong> Search usually stays strong (often 30%+) because "how to" content keeps getting found for years.</li>
<li><strong>Established entertainment/commentary channels:</strong> Browse Features and Suggested Videos typically make up the largest share, since this content leans on algorithmic recommendation more than search intent.</li>
<li><strong>Shorts-first or hybrid channels:</strong> Shorts feed can easily account for the majority of total views, with long-form uploads pulling a very different mix.</li>
</ul>
<p>Treat these as rough shapes to sanity-check against, not targets to hit.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>What's the difference between Browse Features and Suggested Videos?</h4>
<p>Browse Features is YouTube placing your video on a viewer's home feed or subscriptions feed — a standalone recommendation. Suggested Videos is YouTube placing your video alongside or after a <em>different</em> video someone is already watching. Both are algorithmic, but they're triggered by different signals and shown in different places.</p>
<h4>Why is a big chunk of my traffic "Direct or Unknown"?</h4>
<p>YouTube genuinely can't trace where that click came from — typed URLs, bookmarks, or unrecognized third-party apps all land here. It's not a tracking failure on your end, and it usually reflects viewers who already know your channel well.</p>
<h4>Should I worry if one traffic source is much bigger than the others?</h4>
<p>Not on its own. A lopsided mix is often just a reflection of your content type (tutorials skew Search, entertainment skews Browse/Suggested, Shorts skew Shorts feed). Only treat it as a problem if it's stuck somewhere you don't want it — e.g., you're relying entirely on Direct traffic and never reaching new viewers.</p>
