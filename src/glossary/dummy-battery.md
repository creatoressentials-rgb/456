<h2>Dummy Battery</h2>
<p>A <strong>dummy battery</strong> is a battery-shaped power coupler that fits inside a camera's battery compartment and supplies power from an external source. Camera manufacturers commonly call this part a <strong>DC coupler</strong>.</p>
<p>It is not a rechargeable battery and does not store meaningful power by itself. The coupler replaces the removable battery as the electrical connection between the camera and a compatible AC adapter, USB Power Delivery source, battery plate, or regulated power system.</p>

<h3>How Does a Dummy Battery Work?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>The camera's normal battery is removed.</li>
  <li>The correctly matched DC coupler is inserted into the battery compartment.</li>
  <li>The power cable exits through the camera's designated cord opening.</li>
  <li>The coupler connects to a compatible external power adapter.</li>
  <li>The adapter converts or regulates the source to the voltage and current the camera expects.</li>
</ol>
<p>Some camera bodies will not power on unless the battery door is fully closed because the door activates a safety switch. A compatible setup uses the camera's built-in cable flap rather than pinching the wire or defeating the door mechanism.</p>

<h3>What Must Be Compatible?</h3>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Camera model:</strong> Match the coupler to the exact battery family and supported camera list.</li>
  <li><strong>Voltage:</strong> The regulated output must match the coupler and camera requirements.</li>
  <li><strong>Current or wattage:</strong> The power system must supply enough energy during demanding recording modes.</li>
  <li><strong>Connector polarity and pinout:</strong> Similar plugs can use different electrical arrangements.</li>
  <li><strong>USB PD profile:</strong> USB-powered couplers may require a specific negotiated voltage and wattage.</li>
  <li><strong>Cable capability:</strong> A USB cable must support the required current and Power Delivery mode.</li>
  <li><strong>Battery door:</strong> The cable must route through the intended opening while the door closes normally.</li>
</ul>
<p>For example, Sony's official DC-C1 coupler requires a USB PD source rated at 65 watts or more and a compatible 5-amp USB PD cable. That requirement belongs to that specific product and should not be generalized to every camera or coupler.</p>

<h3>Why Use a Dummy Battery?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Long YouTube livestreams</li>
  <li>Remote interviews and video calls</li>
  <li>Studio talking-head recording</li>
  <li>Product and overhead demonstrations</li>
  <li>Time-lapse photography</li>
  <li>Stop-motion projects</li>
  <li>Events where interrupting the feed to change batteries would be disruptive</li>
  <li>Camera feeds sent through <a href="/glossary/clean-hdmi/">clean HDMI</a> and a <a href="/glossary/capture-card/">capture card</a></li>
</ul>

<h3>Dummy Battery vs. Direct USB Power</h3>
<p>Some cameras can operate directly from USB power while a normal battery remains installed. This can be simpler than a dummy battery, but the camera may still draw from the battery during high-load operation or may require a particular USB PD adapter.</p>
<p>A DC coupler feeds power through the battery compartment and is often used by cameras that do not support sufficient direct USB power. Check the camera manual because the two methods can have different recording-mode, charging, and battery requirements.</p>

<h3>Does a Dummy Battery Prevent Overheating?</h3>
<p>No. Removing a discharging lithium-ion battery may reduce one source of heat in some setups, but the sensor, processor, display, memory card, and internal electronics still generate heat. Continuous external power may allow the camera to run long enough to reach a thermal limit.</p>
<p>A dummy battery also does not remove firmware recording limits, memory-card limits, file-size limits, sleep timers, or livestream-software problems.</p>

<h3>How to Set Up a Dummy Battery Safely</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Confirm the exact camera and battery compatibility in official documentation.</li>
  <li>Use the specified AC adapter, USB PD source, or reputable regulated equivalent.</li>
  <li>Inspect the coupler, cable, and power supply for damage.</li>
  <li>Turn the camera off before installing or removing the coupler unless the manufacturer states otherwise.</li>
  <li>Route the cord through the dedicated opening and close the battery door normally.</li>
  <li>Secure the cable so a pull cannot damage the camera or interrupt the feed.</li>
  <li>Run a full-duration test using the intended resolution, frame rate, autofocus, and accessories.</li>
  <li>Keep a charged real battery available in case external power fails.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Common Dummy-Battery Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Calling it an unlimited rechargeable battery rather than a powered coupler.</li>
  <li>Matching only the battery shape and ignoring voltage, polarity, and camera support.</li>
  <li>Assuming every USB charger or power bank supports the required PD profile.</li>
  <li>Using a charge-only or low-current USB cable.</li>
  <li>Pinching the cable by forcing the battery door closed.</li>
  <li>Defeating the battery-door switch instead of using the correct coupler.</li>
  <li>Assuming continuous power guarantees uninterrupted recording.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Does a dummy battery charge inside the camera?</h4>
<p>No. A DC coupler does not store a charge. It conducts regulated power from an external source to the camera.</p>

<h4>Can a dummy battery damage a camera?</h4>
<p>An incompatible, poorly regulated, damaged, or incorrectly wired system can damage equipment. Use an approved setup or a reputable product verified for the exact camera and required power source.</p>

<h4>Can a power bank run a dummy battery?</h4>
<p>Some couplers are designed for a compatible USB or USB PD power bank. The bank and cable must support the required voltage, current, wattage, and negotiation profile.</p>

<h4>Is a dummy battery better than USB power?</h4>
<p>Neither is universally better. Direct USB power is convenient on supported cameras, while a DC coupler may provide a more appropriate continuous-power path for other models.</p>
