<h2>Search Query</h2>
<p>A <strong>search query</strong> is the word, phrase, question, or request a viewer enters into YouTube Search.</p>
<p>Examples include "how to light a YouTube video," "best microphone for echo," or "why did my Shorts views drop." The query expresses what the viewer is trying to find at that moment.</p>

<h3>Search Query vs. Keyword</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Term</th><th>Meaning</th><th>Example</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Search query</td><td>The actual wording entered by a viewer</td><td>best camera for YouTube under 500</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Keyword</td><td>A creator or marketer's target concept or phrase</td><td>YouTube camera</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Topic</td><td>The broader subject being covered</td><td>Camera equipment</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Search intent</td><td>The outcome the viewer wants</td><td>Choose an affordable camera</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>Many different queries can express the same underlying intent.</p>

<h3>How YouTube Evaluates a Search Query</h3>
<p>YouTube says Search prioritizes three broad factors:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Relevance:</strong> How well the title, tags, description, and video content match the query</li>
  <li><strong>Engagement:</strong> Aggregate viewer behavior, including watch time for a particular video and query</li>
  <li><strong>Quality:</strong> Signals related to expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness on the topic</li>
</ul>
<p>Freshness or popularity can matter more in areas such as music and entertainment. YouTube places stronger emphasis on authoritative sources for topics such as news, politics, medicine, and science.</p>

<h3>Query Matching Is More Than Exact Words</h3>
<p>YouTube evaluates the video content as well as metadata. A video does not need to repeat the exact query unnaturally in every field.</p>
<p>Useful matching can include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Synonyms</li>
  <li>Related phrases</li>
  <li>Spoken explanation</li>
  <li>On-screen text</li>
  <li>Captions</li>
  <li>Examples that answer the need</li>
  <li>Viewer engagement showing that the result was relevant</li>
</ul>

<h3>Search Query vs. YouTube Search Terms</h3>
<p>A Search Query is any request entered into YouTube. YouTube Search Terms is the Analytics report showing popular queries viewers used when they actually found the creator's content.</p>
<p>The report is therefore a filtered subset of queries connected to your traffic—not a list of every possible search related to the topic.</p>

<h3>Search Query vs. Searched on YouTube</h3>
<p><a href="/glossary/searches-across-youtube/">Searched on YouTube</a> is a Trends insight based on the creator's audience and saved searches over the last 28 days.</p>
<p>A query in Trends may not have generated traffic to the channel. A query in the Search Terms report did generate traffic during the selected period.</p>

<h3>How to Research a Query</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Enter the phrase in YouTube Search.</li>
  <li>Review autocomplete suggestions.</li>
  <li>Inspect the top results.</li>
  <li>Identify the dominant viewer intent.</li>
  <li>Check publication dates and freshness.</li>
  <li>Read comments for missing questions.</li>
  <li>Review Trends and Content Gap insights.</li>
  <li>Check your own YouTube Search Terms report.</li>
  <li>Decide whether your channel can provide a better answer.</li>
</ol>

<h3>How to Match a Query Without Keyword Stuffing</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Use clear natural wording in the title.</li>
  <li>Explain the result or topic in the description.</li>
  <li>Answer the query directly in the video.</li>
  <li>Use accurate chapters when the video covers several related needs.</li>
  <li>Include relevant variations naturally.</li>
  <li>Do not repeat the phrase excessively.</li>
  <li>Avoid unrelated tags intended only to capture traffic.</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube says tags can help with common spelling mistakes but are not essential for discovery.</p>

<h3>Query Intent and Packaging</h3>
<p>The title and thumbnail should communicate that the video answers the viewer's need.</p>
<ul>
  <li>A tutorial query benefits from a clear outcome.</li>
  <li>A comparison query benefits from showing both choices.</li>
  <li>A troubleshooting query benefits from identifying the problem and solution.</li>
  <li>A review query benefits from showing the actual product and decision criteria.</li>
</ul>
<p>Vague packaging can weaken Search performance even when the description includes the exact phrase.</p>

<h3>Query-Specific Engagement</h3>
<p>YouTube says it can examine watch time for a particular video for a particular query when estimating relevance.</p>
<p>This means a video may perform well for one query and poorly for another. The people arriving from each query may have different expectations.</p>

<h3>Personalized Search Results</h3>
<p>Search results can differ based on factors such as:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Search and watch history</li>
  <li>Location</li>
  <li>Language</li>
  <li>Current trends</li>
  <li>Content freshness</li>
  <li>Viewer age or account settings</li>
  <li>Quality and authority signals</li>
</ul>
<p>Ranking first on your own account does not prove that every viewer sees the same order.</p>

<h3>Do Tags Determine Query Ranking?</h3>
<p>No. YouTube considers title, tags, description, video content, engagement, and quality. Its current creator guidance says tags are mainly useful for common spelling mistakes and are not essential for discovery.</p>
<p>Creators should prioritize accurate content, clear packaging, and satisfying the query.</p>

<h3>Query Freshness</h3>
<p>Some queries require current information, such as:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Software instructions</li>
  <li>Platform policies</li>
  <li>Product prices</li>
  <li>Sports and entertainment news</li>
  <li>Tax and legal changes</li>
  <li>New camera or microphone releases</li>
</ul>
<p>An older video can lose relevance even if it once ranked well.</p>

<h3>Common Search-Query Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Using keyword and query as though they always mean the same thing.</li>
  <li>Targeting a phrase without understanding intent.</li>
  <li>Assuming exact-match wording guarantees ranking.</li>
  <li>Stuffing the phrase into tags and descriptions.</li>
  <li>Ignoring query-specific watch behavior.</li>
  <li>Using a broad query for a narrow video.</li>
  <li>Checking rankings only while signed in to your own account.</li>
  <li>Ignoring freshness and authority for high-stakes topics.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>What is a YouTube search query?</h4>
<p>It is the wording a viewer enters into YouTube Search.</p>

<h4>Is a query the same as a keyword?</h4>
<p>Not exactly. A query is the viewer's actual wording; a keyword is a target concept selected for research or optimization.</p>

<h4>Does exact-match wording guarantee ranking?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube also considers engagement, quality, personalization, freshness, and authority.</p>

<h4>Can one video rank for several queries?</h4>
<p>Yes, when the content is relevant and satisfying for several related searches.</p>
