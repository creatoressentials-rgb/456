<h2>YouTube Collaborations</h2>
<p><strong>YouTube Collaborations</strong> refers to two related things: the platform's built-in tools for linking creators together on a piece of content, and the broader creator-growth practice of teaming up with other YouTubers to reach new audiences. On YouTube specifically, "Collaborations" can mean either the <strong>Collab</strong> option inside the Shorts Remix menu, or the newer <strong>Collaborations</strong> feature that lets an uploader officially tag other channels as collaborators on a video. This entry covers both platform features first, then the general growth strategy.</p>
<p>Not sure this is the term you're looking for? YouTube Collaborations are not the same as <a href="/glossary/auto-generated-shorts/">Auto-Generated Shorts</a> or <a href="/glossary/auto-generated-clips/">Auto-Generated Clips</a>, which are AI-repurposed content with no other creator involved, and they're not the same as <a href="/glossary/channel-permissions/">Channel Permissions</a>, which controls who can log in and manage your channel's back end rather than who appears on your content.</p>

<h3>The Collab feature in YouTube Shorts</h3>
<p>Collab is one of several <strong>Remix</strong> tools available on Shorts. It lets you record your own video side-by-side with — or as a direct response to — an existing eligible Short or long-form video, similar in spirit to a duet or stitch on other short-form platforms.</p>
<p><strong>How to use it:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>From the Shorts player: tap <strong>Remix</strong>, then <strong>Collab</strong>.</li>
<li>From a long-form video's watch page: tap <strong>Share</strong>, then <strong>Remix</strong>, then <strong>Collab</strong>.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Eligibility and rules:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Shorts and videos are opted in to remixing by default. Creators can turn this off for individual videos, or in bulk, from YouTube Studio ("Shorts remixing" setting).</li>
<li>If the original creator later restricts remixing or deletes the source video, any Collabs or remixes built from it get taken down (audio-only remixes are muted and set to unlisted first, then deleted after 30 days).</li>
<li>Collab videos are attributed back to the original creator, with a link to the source content — a way for the original creator to gain exposure too, not just the remixer.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Duration correction:</strong> Several roundup articles still describe Shorts (and Collabs) as capped at 60 seconds. That's outdated — YouTube expanded Shorts to support videos up to <strong>3 minutes</strong> long, and Collabs follow the current Shorts length limits, not the old 60-second cap.</p>

<h3>The Collaborations feature (creator tagging)</h3>
<p>Separately, YouTube has been testing a feature — announced by YouTube staff in August 2025 and still rolling out gradually — that lets an uploader formally tag other channels as <strong>collaborators</strong> on any video, not just Shorts.</p>
<p><strong>How it works:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>While uploading, the primary creator opens <strong>Details → Audience → Show more → Collaboration → Invite a collaborator</strong> in YouTube Studio (or <strong>Add details → Collaborations, Comments &amp; more → Invite a collaborator</strong> in the Studio mobile app).</li>
<li>Each invited channel must <strong>accept</strong> the invitation before appearing as a collaborator — tagging isn't one-sided.</li>
<li>Once accepted, collaborator icons appear next to the video title. Clicking through opens a box listing every collaborating channel with its own subscribe button.</li>
<li>YouTube's recommendation system then treats the video as relevant to <strong>every tagged collaborator's audience</strong>, not just the uploader's — this is the core reason the feature matters for <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">traffic sources</a> and <a href="/glossary/suggested-videos/">suggested videos</a> exposure.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>What it doesn't do (yet):</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>The video is not posted or mirrored to collaborators' own channels — it lives only on the uploader's channel, unlike similar features on some other platforms.</li>
<li>Ad revenue is <strong>not split automatically</strong>. The uploading channel keeps 100% of <a href="/glossary/ad-revenue/">ad revenue</a>; any revenue-sharing arrangement between collaborators has to be handled privately, outside YouTube. This matters most for creators already in the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a>, since only the uploading channel's monetization status applies.</li>
<li>How many collaborators you can tag is reported inconsistently: some sources say up to five, others say up to four. Since the feature is still in limited testing, treat any specific number as unconfirmed until YouTube publishes official documentation — it may also vary by rollout stage.</li>
<li>Whether views, watch time, and other analytics are shared with collaborators (versus attributed only to the uploader) is still unclear and not addressed in official YouTube documentation.</li>
</ul>
<p>This is a different thing from <a href="/glossary/youtube-creator-partnerships/">YouTube Creator Partnerships</a>, which covers brand-and-business partnerships (advertisers working with creators) rather than creator-to-creator collaboration. If you're looking for information on brand deals or sponsorships, that's the term to check instead.</p>

<h3>YouTube Collaborations as a growth strategy</h3>
<p>Beyond the platform features above, "collaborating" is one of the oldest and most consistently recommended growth tactics on YouTube — appearing on someone else's channel, co-producing a video, or cross-promoting each other's content to convert their <a href="/glossary/regular-viewers/">regular viewers</a> and <a href="/glossary/returning-viewers/">returning viewers</a> into your own.</p>
<p><strong>What tends to work, based on consistent guidance across creator-economy sources:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Match audience size roughly</strong>, so the exposure trade feels balanced to both parties — a 1,000-subscriber channel pitching a 500,000-subscriber creator is a much harder sell than pitching channels in a similar range.</li>
<li><strong>Pick niche-adjacent partners, not identical ones.</strong> The goal is audience overlap in interest, not direct competition — think a personal-finance channel collaborating with a minimalism channel, not with another personal-finance channel chasing the same <a href="/glossary/unique-viewers/">unique viewers</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Agree on format and publish timing up front</strong> — whether it's a joint video, a two-part release across both channels, or a channel takeover, and whether both videos go live at the same time so viewers can cross-navigate.</li>
<li><strong>Promote beyond the video itself</strong>, using the Community tab, end screens, and off-platform channels (social, email) to widen reach further.</li>
<li><strong>Measure beyond raw views.</strong> Track whether watch time, subscriber growth, and comment engagement improve versus your solo baseline — a collab that spikes views without holding attention isn't necessarily a win.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Common mistakes:</strong> partnering purely on subscriber count rather than actual audience fit, skipping a plan for who hosts/edits/promotes, and treating a single collab as a guaranteed algorithmic boost — none of the platform's collaboration tools automatically improve recommendations; they simply widen the pool of viewers a video can be shown to.</p>

<h3>FAQ</h3>
<h4>Does YouTube split ad revenue between collaborators?</h4>
<p>No. Whether you use the Shorts Collab tool or the newer Collaborations (tagging) feature, all ad revenue goes to the channel that uploaded the video. Any revenue split has to be arranged privately between creators — YouTube doesn't handle it automatically.</p>
<h4>Is the Shorts "Collab" option the same as the new "Collaborations" feature?</h4>
<p>No, and this is the most common source of confusion. Collab is a Remix tool for Shorts that lets you record side-by-side with another Short or video. Collaborations is a separate, newer feature (still in limited testing) that lets an uploader tag other channels as official collaborators on any video, triggering recommendations to each collaborator's audience.</p>
<h4>How long can a Shorts Collab be?</h4>
<p>Collabs follow YouTube's current Shorts length limits, which now extend up to 3 minutes — not the older 60-second cap that some outdated guides still reference.</p>
<h4>Do I need to be a YouTube partner to collaborate with another creator?</h4>
<p>No — you don't need to be in the YouTube Partner Program to appear in someone else's video or be tagged as a collaborator. You only need Partner Program status if you want to earn ad revenue directly, and even then, only the uploading channel's monetization applies to that specific video.</p>
