<h2>Members-Only Live Stream</h2>
<p>A <strong>members-only live stream</strong> is a YouTube live broadcast that only active channel members at the selected membership level can watch.</p>
<p>Creators can start a stream as members-only or, under YouTube's newer workflow, transition an existing public stream into a separate members-only segment.</p>

<h3>Who Can Watch?</h3>
<p>The creator can allow:</p>
<ul>
  <li>All paid channel members</li>
  <li>A selected membership level and every higher level</li>
</ul>
<p>Non-members and members below the required level may see the thumbnail, title, description, and Join or upgrade prompt, but they cannot watch the exclusive broadcast.</p>

<h3>How to Start a Members-Only Stream</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio or the supported YouTube mobile live workflow.</li>
  <li>Create or schedule a live stream.</li>
  <li>Open Visibility.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Members only</strong>.</li>
  <li>Select all paid members or the minimum membership level.</li>
  <li>Schedule the stream or go live.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube says members receive notifications about members-only streams in a manner similar to public broadcasts, subject to ordinary notification eligibility and settings.</p>

<h3>Live-Streaming Requirements Still Apply</h3>
<p>To use live streaming, the channel must meet YouTube's live eligibility requirements. Current members-only live guidance includes:</p>
<ul>
  <li>A verified channel</li>
  <li>No live-streaming restrictions in the previous 90 days</li>
  <li>An active channel-membership program</li>
  <li>Compliance with live, membership, copyright, and Community Guidelines</li>
</ul>

<h3>Members-Only Stream vs. Members-Only Chat</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Who Can Watch?</th><th>Who Can Chat?</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Members-only live stream</td><td>Qualifying members</td><td>Eligible viewers in the exclusive stream, subject to chat settings</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Public stream with members-only chat</td><td>Everyone</td><td>Channel members only</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>Restricting chat does not automatically restrict the video.</p>

<h3>Public-to-Members-Only Transition</h3>
<p>YouTube introduced a workflow that lets a creator begin with a public stream and schedule a transition to members-only while already live.</p>
<p>The creator chooses:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The membership level that can continue</li>
  <li>A countdown of 1, 5, 10, 15, 30, or 60 minutes</li>
</ul>
<p>When the countdown ends, qualifying members continue into the exclusive segment. Non-members are prompted to join or upgrade.</p>

<h3>Does the Transition Change One Stream's Visibility?</h3>
<p>No. YouTube says the transition creates two separate streams that share the same stream key:</p>
<ul>
  <li>A public stream segment</li>
  <li>A members-only stream segment</li>
</ul>
<p>The viewing experience is designed to feel continuous, but the records, archives, and Analytics are separate.</p>

<h3>Can the Transition Be Scheduled Before Going Live?</h3>
<p>A standalone members-only stream can be scheduled in advance. The public-to-members-only <em>transition</em> can only be scheduled while the creator is already live.</p>
<p>This distinction resolves a common misunderstanding in YouTube's current FAQ wording.</p>

<h3>Can a Stream Transition More Than Once?</h3>
<p>Yes. YouTube says creators can chain transitions—for example:</p>
<ol>
  <li>Begin publicly.</li>
  <li>Transition to all paid members.</li>
  <li>Later transition to a higher membership level.</li>
</ol>
<p>Each added restriction should be explained clearly so viewers understand what access they purchased.</p>

<h3>Is the Transition Available for Vertical Live?</h3>
<p>No. YouTube's current guidance says the public-to-members-only transition is unavailable for vertical live streams.</p>
<p>A creator can still use supported direct members-only live workflows, but should not assume the transition control will appear in the vertical live experience.</p>

<h3>What Do Viewers See Before the Transition?</h3>
<p>All viewers receive a notice and countdown. At the transition:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Qualifying members automatically continue.</li>
  <li>Lower-tier members are prompted to upgrade.</li>
  <li>Non-members are prompted to join.</li>
  <li>Delayed viewers encounter the transition when they reach that point in the recording.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How DVR Affects Delayed Viewers</h3>
<p>With DVR enabled, a viewer watching behind live can finish the complete public segment before being prompted to move into the members-only portion, even when the live broadcast has already transitioned.</p>
<p>The transition follows the viewer's playback position rather than instantly cutting off every delayed session at the same clock time.</p>

<h3>How the Streams Are Archived</h3>
<p>When DVR is active, YouTube archives the public and members-only portions as separate videos.</p>
<p>The members-only archive:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Is accessible only to qualifying members</li>
  <li>Initially inherits the title and description from the public stream</li>
  <li>Can have its metadata edited after archival</li>
  <li>Has its own Analytics record</li>
</ul>

<h3>Analytics for Members-Only Live Streams</h3>
<p>YouTube reports the public and exclusive streams as distinct videos. Creators can review:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Concurrent viewers</li>
  <li>Views</li>
  <li>Watch time</li>
  <li>Chat activity</li>
  <li>Membership activity</li>
  <li>Revenue where applicable</li>
  <li>Audience retention for the archived stream</li>
</ul>
<p>Do not combine the two records without accounting for overlap in the viewer journey.</p>

<h3>Good Uses for Members-Only Live Streams</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Member Q&amp;A sessions</li>
  <li>Behind-the-scenes production</li>
  <li>Office hours</li>
  <li>After-shows</li>
  <li>Extended tutorials</li>
  <li>Small-group game nights</li>
  <li>Project updates</li>
  <li>Early previews</li>
</ul>

<h3>Using a Public Teaser</h3>
<p>A public portion can demonstrate membership value before the exclusive segment. The transition should be transparent rather than deceptive.</p>
<p>Tell viewers:</p>
<ul>
  <li>That the stream will become members-only</li>
  <li>When the transition will occur</li>
  <li>Which level is required</li>
  <li>What additional content members will receive</li>
</ul>

<h3>Testing the Transition</h3>
<p>YouTube recommends testing the workflow with an Unlisted stream before using it in a public broadcast.</p>
<p>Test:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The countdown</li>
  <li>Membership-level selection</li>
  <li>Stream-key continuity</li>
  <li>Chat behavior</li>
  <li>DVR behavior</li>
  <li>The separate archives</li>
</ul>

<h3>Copyright and Policy Rules</h3>
<p>Members-only live streams remain subject to:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Live-streaming policies</li>
  <li>Community Guidelines</li>
  <li>Copyright and music rights</li>
  <li>Channel membership perk policies</li>
  <li>Monetization policies</li>
</ul>
<p>An exclusive audience does not make unlicensed or prohibited content permissible.</p>

<h3>Membership Revenue vs. Stream Revenue</h3>
<p>Membership payments, advertising, <a href="/glossary/youtube-premium-revenue/">YouTube Premium Revenue</a>, and fan-funding transactions are separate earning sources.</p>
<p>A members-only stream can strengthen membership retention, but YouTube does not guarantee a particular number of joins, renewals, ads, or Super transactions.</p>

<h3>Common Members-Only-Live Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Confusing members-only viewing with members-only chat.</li>
  <li>Assuming a transition edits one stream instead of creating two records.</li>
  <li>Trying to schedule the transition before going live.</li>
  <li>Using the transition in a vertical live stream.</li>
  <li>Forgetting that higher levels inherit access.</li>
  <li>Failing to explain the countdown and required level.</li>
  <li>Ignoring DVR behavior for delayed viewers.</li>
  <li>Comparing exclusive concurrency directly with a public stream.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Can I schedule a members-only live stream?</h4>
<p>Yes. A direct members-only stream can be scheduled. A transition from public to members-only must be scheduled while already live.</p>

<h4>Can non-members see the stream page?</h4>
<p>They can see the thumbnail and metadata, but cannot watch the exclusive broadcast.</p>

<h4>Does transitioning create a separate video?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube creates separate public and members-only stream records that share the same stream key.</p>

<h4>Can I transition a vertical live stream?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube currently says the transition feature is unavailable for vertical live streams.</p>
