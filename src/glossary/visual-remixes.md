<h2>Visual Remixes</h2>
<p><strong>Visual remixes</strong> are Shorts that use eligible video imagery from another Short or YouTube video through tools such as Cut, Green Screen, Collab, or supported AI transformations.</p>
<p>They are different from audio-only remixes. YouTube identifies traffic generated from visual remixes through the <strong>Remixed Video</strong> traffic source.</p>

<h3>Main Types of Visual Remixes</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Type</th><th>How the Source Appears</th><th>Common Use</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Cut</td><td>A short segment of the source video is sampled</td><td>Responding to, quoting, or continuing a moment</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Green Screen</td><td>The source video becomes the background</td><td>Commentary, explanation, reaction, or location-style storytelling</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Collab</td><td>The source and creator appear together in a layout</td><td>Reaction, comparison, duet-style participation, or response</td></tr>
    <tr><td>AI remix</td><td>An eligible scene is transformed from prompts or suggested actions</td><td>Reimagining a scene with a new concept or visual twist</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>Cut</h3>
<p>Cut lets a creator sample an eligible segment of a Short or long-form video. For eligible music videos, YouTube currently describes Cut as a one-to-five-second sample that includes both audio and video.</p>
<p>Available durations and layouts depend on the source and rights.</p>

<h3>Green Screen</h3>
<p>Green Screen places the source content behind the remix creator. It can use:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Visual content only</li>
  <li>Audio and visual content where permitted</li>
  <li>Different layout choices available in the app</li>
</ul>
<p>This format is commonly used for reactions, tutorials, commentary, and contextual explanation.</p>

<h3>Collab</h3>
<p>Collab displays the source and the new creator's contribution together. The available layout can vary by app and source format.</p>
<p>Collab can be useful for:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Responding to an argument</li>
  <li>Trying a technique</li>
  <li>Comparing results</li>
  <li>Participating in a trend</li>
  <li>Adding commentary alongside the original moment</li>
</ul>

<h3>AI Visual Remixing</h3>
<p>YouTube's current upgraded Remix tools can let eligible users transform a scene using a prompt or suggested action, such as adding themselves into a scene or changing its concept.</p>
<p>Current Help notes include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>English-language prompts</li>
  <li>Gradual rollout to most users</li>
  <li>Optional use of up to three reference photos</li>
  <li>Generated audio based on the prompt and scene context</li>
  <li>Additional editing before upload</li>
</ul>

<h3>Visual Remix vs. Audio Remix</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Visual Remix</th><th>Audio Remix</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Source material</td><td>Video imagery, sometimes with audio</td><td>Original audio or music</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Examples</td><td>Cut, Green Screen, Collab, AI transformation</td><td>Use this sound</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Traffic source to original</td><td>Remixed Video</td><td>Sound Pages and related audio discovery can contribute</td></tr>
    <tr><td>If source is restricted</td><td>Existing video remixes can be deleted</td><td>Audio remixes can be muted, made Unlisted, and scheduled for deletion</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>What Is Remixed Video Traffic?</h3>
<p>YouTube defines Remixed Video as traffic from visual remixes of your content.</p>
<p>A viewer can discover the original source through attribution or remix-related metadata and then watch it. Those attributed views can appear under Remixed Video in the source video's Reach reporting.</p>

<h3>Where Can You See Visual Remix Traffic?</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Open YouTube Studio.</li>
  <li>Select the source video or channel Analytics.</li>
  <li>Open the Reach or Content report.</li>
  <li>Review traffic sources.</li>
  <li>Find <strong>Remixed Video</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>This measures traffic to the original content from visual remixes—not the total number of views the remix creators received.</p>

<h3>What Is the Top Remixed Report?</h3>
<p>YouTube's Top Remixed report can show:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Remix views</li>
  <li>Total remixes</li>
  <li>Your most-remixed source content</li>
  <li>Filters for Sound, Cut, or Green Screen</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube describes highly remixed content as having potential to spark creativity or a trend beyond the creator's existing community.</p>

<h3>Remix Views vs. Remixed Video Traffic</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Metric</th><th>What It Measures</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Remix views</td><td>Views generated by Shorts created from the source content</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Total remixes</td><td>How many remixed Shorts were created</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Remixed Video traffic</td><td>Views that returned to the original source through visual remix paths</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>A remix can receive many views without sending an equal number of viewers back to the source.</p>

<h3>Can Visual Remixes Use Long-Form Videos?</h3>
<p>Yes. Eligible long-form videos can offer Cut, Green Screen, and Collab through the Share and Remix workflow.</p>
<p>Availability can be limited by privacy, creator settings, copyright claims, music relationships, and feature rollout.</p>

<h3>Why Is Visual Remixing Unavailable?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>The source creator opted out.</li>
  <li>The source is Private.</li>
  <li>A third-party rights holder did not permit visual remixing.</li>
  <li>The video contains a third-party claim that disables visual features.</li>
  <li>The feature is unavailable in the viewer's app, account, or country.</li>
  <li>The content format is unsupported.</li>
  <li>The source was deleted, restricted, trimmed, or blurred.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Third-Party Claims</h3>
<p>YouTube says visual remix features are unavailable for Shorts with third-party claims in certain music-partner contexts. Audio may still be available when the claimed song is eligible in Shorts.</p>
<p>This is why a source can offer Use This Sound but not Cut, Green Screen, or Collab.</p>

<h3>What Happens If the Source Creator Opts Out?</h3>
<p>Existing visual remixes are deleted when the source creator restricts the source's video remixing. New visual remixes can no longer be created.</p>
<p>YouTube emails affected remix creators. Views already accrued remain in their lifetime view totals.</p>

<h3>What Happens If the Source Is Edited?</h3>
<p>Trimming or blurring the source in YouTube Studio can cause existing video remixes to be deleted.</p>
<p>This matters because Studio Editor changes are not reversible through the former Revert to Original function. Source creators should inspect remix analytics before saving changes.</p>

<h3>Visual Remixes and Copyright</h3>
<p>The in-product tools make eligible material available under YouTube's current permissions. They do not give creators a general license to download, recreate, or upload the same source outside those tools.</p>
<p>Outside use can trigger a <a href="/glossary/copyright-claim/">Copyright Claim</a> or removal request.</p>

<h3>Visual Remixes and Original Value</h3>
<p>A visual remix should add a meaningful contribution, such as:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Commentary</li>
  <li>Reaction</li>
  <li>Education</li>
  <li>Comparison</li>
  <li>Demonstration</li>
  <li>New storytelling</li>
  <li>Participation in a format with original performance</li>
</ul>
<p>Merely placing a face beside another video may not create sufficient originality for viewers or monetization review.</p>

<h3>Visual Remixes and Reused Content</h3>
<p>Using YouTube's tools does not automatically guarantee compliance with <a href="/glossary/reused-content/">Reused Content</a> policies. YPP reviewers evaluate whether the channel adds significant original value across its content.</p>

<h3>Using Visual Remixes Strategically</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Respond to a specific idea rather than copying the whole format.</li>
  <li>Make the original contribution understandable immediately.</li>
  <li>Choose source material that your audience genuinely cares about.</li>
  <li>Respect the source creator and avoid misleading endorsement claims.</li>
  <li>Preserve your original recordings in case the remix is later removed.</li>
  <li>Use analytics to identify which source moments inspire participation.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Should Source Creators Allow Visual Remixes?</h3>
<p>Benefits can include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Audience participation</li>
  <li>Trend creation</li>
  <li>Additional attribution</li>
  <li>Remixed Video traffic</li>
  <li>Discovery beyond the original community</li>
</ul>
<p>Risks can include loss of message context, unwanted reactions, brand concerns, and later disruption if the source must be edited or removed.</p>

<h3>Common Visual-Remix Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Calling audio-only use a visual remix.</li>
  <li>Confusing remix views with traffic returned to the original.</li>
  <li>Assuming Collab automatically creates meaningful transformation.</li>
  <li>Ignoring third-party claim restrictions.</li>
  <li>Assuming existing remixes remain after opting out.</li>
  <li>Downloading the source instead of using the in-product tools.</li>
  <li>Ignoring reused-content review.</li>
  <li>Editing a source without checking remix consequences.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>What is a visual remix?</h4>
<p>It is a Short using eligible source video imagery through tools such as Cut, Green Screen, Collab, or supported AI transformations.</p>

<h4>What is Remixed Video traffic?</h4>
<p>It is traffic to original content from visual remixes made by other creators.</p>

<h4>Can a visual remix be deleted if the source changes?</h4>
<p>Yes. Deleting, restricting, trimming, or blurring the source can cause video remixes to be deleted.</p>

<h4>Does a visual remix automatically qualify for monetization?</h4>
<p>No. It must still satisfy originality, rights, advertiser-friendly, and YPP requirements.</p>
