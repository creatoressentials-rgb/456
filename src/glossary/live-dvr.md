<h2>Live DVR</h2>
<p><strong>Live DVR</strong> is a <a href="/glossary/youtube-live/">YouTube Live</a> setting that lets viewers pause, rewind, and continue watching while a stream is still in progress. It gives the live player on-demand-style controls without ending the broadcast.</p>
<p>When a viewer resumes after pausing, playback continues from the point where the viewer stopped. The viewer can seek forward toward the live moment when the available player controls allow it.</p>

<h3>What DVR means in a live stream</h3>
<p>DVR stands for <strong>digital video recording</strong>. In a live-streaming context, it refers to a temporary playable history of the active broadcast. YouTube retains enough of the stream for viewers to move backward in the <a href="/glossary/timeline/">timeline</a> while the event is still live.</p>
<p>YouTube Live DVR is not the same as YouTube TV's cloud DVR. YouTube TV DVR records television programs to a subscriber's library. Live DVR is a creator-controlled playback setting for an individual YouTube live stream.</p>

<h3>What viewers can do with Live DVR</h3>
<ul>
<li>Pause an active live stream.</li>
<li>Rewind to an earlier part of the event.</li>
<li>Resume from the point where playback was paused.</li>
<li>Seek forward toward the live edge when supported by the player.</li>
<li>Replay a moment that happened earlier in the same broadcast.</li>
</ul>
<p>Viewers cannot rewind to before the creator started the broadcast.</p>

<h3>How creators enable Live DVR</h3>
<p>For supported live-stream workflows:</p>
<ol>
<li>Open <a href="/glossary/youtube-studio/">YouTube Studio</a>.</li>
<li>Click <strong>Create</strong> and then <strong>Go live</strong>.</li>
<li>Create a stream or select one from the Stream or Manage area.</li>
<li>Open <strong>Stream settings</strong>.</li>
<li>Under additional settings, toggle <strong>Enable DVR</strong> on or off.</li>
</ol>
<p>The exact interface can change, but the setting is managed through <a href="/glossary/live-control-room/">Live Control Room</a>.</p>

<h3>When DVR cannot be disabled</h3>
<p>YouTube says disabling DVR is not supported for webcam and mobile streaming. In those workflows, viewers may retain rewind and pause capabilities without the creator receiving the same off switch available for supported encoder or mobile-management configurations.</p>
<p>Because YouTube changes live features over time, creators should verify the available settings in their own Live Control Room before promising that an event cannot be rewound.</p>

<h3>Live DVR limits</h3>
<p>YouTube does not promise unlimited rewind for every live stream and device. Current Help documentation states that:</p>
<ul>
<li>DVR may be limited or unavailable when a stream becomes longer than 12 hours.</li>
<li>Apple TV, AirPlay, and older app versions may have lower rewind limits.</li>
<li>Pause and resume are subject to the same DVR window.</li>
<li>A viewer cannot seek to a point before the broadcast started.</li>
</ul>
<p>This is more accurate than assuming every YouTube stream always has a fixed 12-hour rewind window. The feature can become limited on very long broadcasts and may vary by playback environment.</p>

<h3>Can DVR be changed after the stream starts?</h3>
<p>For streams where the toggle is supported, YouTube allows creators to enable or disable DVR while live. However, the change applies only to viewers who begin playing the stream after the setting is changed.</p>
<p>Someone who already has the stream open may continue with the playback behavior established when that viewing session began.</p>

<h3>What happens when Live DVR is turned off?</h3>
<p>During the active event, viewers cannot seek backward through the live timeline. They watch from the point where they joined or from the current live position available to them.</p>
<p>Turning DVR off is not the same as disabling the archive. YouTube says viewers may still be able to watch a recording of the full stream after the replay becomes available.</p>

<h3>Live DVR vs. the post-stream replay</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>When it is used</th><th>Purpose</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Live DVR</td><td>While the broadcast is active</td><td>Pause and revisit earlier moments during the live event</td></tr>
<tr><td>Live-stream archive</td><td>After the broadcast ends and processing completes</td><td>Watch the event as an on-demand video</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>YouTube generally archives streams under 12 hours automatically, but archive availability can still depend on processing, settings, rights, policy, and technical conditions.</p>

<h3>Live DVR and <a href="/glossary/stream-latency/">stream latency</a></h3>
<p>Live DVR and latency are related playback concepts but are not the same setting.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>DVR</strong> controls whether viewers can move backward through the active stream.</li>
<li><strong>Latency</strong> controls the delay between the creator's capture and the viewer's playback.</li>
</ul>
<p>A viewer who rewinds is intentionally farther behind the live edge. Even on a low-latency stream, that viewer will not be seeing the newest moment until seeking forward or using a jump-to-live control.</p>

<h3>Advantages of enabling Live DVR</h3>
<ul>
<li>Late arrivals can return to the beginning or an important earlier segment.</li>
<li>Viewers can replay a demonstration, announcement, goal, performance, or answer.</li>
<li>Audiences can pause without immediately missing the next part.</li>
<li>Long educational, sports, conference, and event streams become easier to navigate.</li>
</ul>
<p>DVR can improve viewer convenience, especially when the content remains understandable outside the exact live moment.</p>

<h3>Reasons a creator might disable Live DVR</h3>
<ul>
<li>The event depends on everyone experiencing a reveal at approximately the same time.</li>
<li>The creator wants live chat and video to remain closely synchronized for new viewers.</li>
<li>The production is highly time-sensitive and older information may quickly become misleading.</li>
<li>A rights holder or event agreement requires different playback controls.</li>
</ul>
<p>Disabling DVR does not prevent viewers from joining late, recording their own screens, or watching a later archive when one is available. It is a playback control, not a complete content-protection system.</p>

<h3>Live chat while watching behind live</h3>
<p>A viewer who rewinds the video may be watching an earlier moment while live chat continues to discuss the current moment. This can create spoilers or confusion.</p>
<p>Creators can reduce confusion by announcing major segments clearly, adding on-screen context, and reminding viewers when a discussion refers to the live edge rather than an earlier DVR position.</p>

<h3>How DVR affects live-stream analysis</h3>
<p>DVR gives viewers more control over where they watch, but <a href="/glossary/concurrent-viewers/">Concurrent Viewers</a> still measures people watching simultaneously, including viewers who may be at different positions in the live timeline.</p>
<p>When reviewing performance, creators should consider total watch time, <a href="/glossary/average-view-duration/">average view duration</a>, chat participation, replay traffic, and <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">Traffic Sources</a> rather than assuming rewind behavior explains every audience change.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Can viewers rewind every YouTube live stream?</h4>
<p>No. DVR availability depends on the creator's settings, streaming method, stream length, device, app version, and YouTube's current support.</p>
<h4>Does turning off DVR delete the replay?</h4>
<p>No. Disabling DVR prevents seeking backward while the stream is live, but a full archive may still become available after the broadcast.</p>
<h4>How far back can viewers rewind?</h4>
<p>The available window varies. YouTube warns that DVR may be limited or unavailable for streams longer than 12 hours and that some devices have lower limits.</p>
<h4>Does resume jump back to the live moment?</h4>
<p>Not automatically. YouTube says playback continues from the point where the viewer paused. The viewer can then seek forward or use a live control when available.</p>
