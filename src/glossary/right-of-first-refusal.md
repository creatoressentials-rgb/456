<h2>Right of First Refusal</h2>
<p>A <strong>right of first refusal</strong>, commonly abbreviated <strong>ROFR</strong>, is a contract provision giving one party the opportunity to accept or match a future deal before the other party completes that deal with someone else.</p>
<p>In a creator agreement, an existing sponsor may receive a ROFR covering a renewal, another campaign, a named content series, or future work in a defined product category. The right usually becomes relevant after the creator receives a genuine third-party offer or decides to accept specified terms from another brand.</p>
<p>A ROFR does not automatically renew the sponsorship. It gives the holder a contractual opportunity to take the covered deal under the matching rules written into the agreement.</p>
<h3>How a creator ROFR can work</h3>
<p>A simplified process might look like this:</p>
<ol>
<li>A creator's existing software sponsorship ends.</li>
<li>A competing software company offers the creator a new campaign.</li>
<li>The offer falls within the subject, dates, and category covered by the ROFR.</li>
<li>The creator sends the existing sponsor the contractually required notice and deal terms.</li>
<li>The existing sponsor has a defined period to match or accept those terms.</li>
<li>If the sponsor exercises the right correctly, the creator contracts with that sponsor.</li>
<li>If the sponsor declines or misses the deadline, the creator may accept the third-party deal, normally only on terms no more favorable than those disclosed.</li>
</ol>
<p>Every step depends on the wording. A clause may instead require the creator to offer a renewal package before seeking outside bids, which functions more like a right of first offer or first negotiation.</p>

<p>These labels are not always used consistently. Creators should read the actual procedure instead of relying on the heading.</p>
<h3>What opportunity does the ROFR cover?</h3>
<p>The clause should define whether the right applies to:</p>
<ul>
<li>The next sponsorship with the creator</li>
<li>Renewal of one named campaign</li>
<li>A recurring video series</li>
<li>A specific product category</li>
<li>Named competitors</li>
<li>All YouTube integrations</li>
<li>Dedicated <a href="/glossary/sponsored-video">sponsored videos</a></li>
<li>Shorts, livestreams, podcasts, newsletters, or other platforms</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/paid-usage">Paid usage</a> or licensing renewals</li>
<li>An ambassador relationship</li>
<li>A specific territory or audience</li>
</ul>
<p>A ROFR covering “all future brand opportunities” is far broader than one covering a single renewal in the sponsor's category.</p>
<h3>What triggers the right?</h3>
<p>Possible triggers include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Receipt of a bona fide written third-party offer</li>
<li>The creator's intention to accept an offer</li>
<li>Agreement on material terms with a third party</li>
<li>The creator making a covered opportunity available</li>
<li>The creator deciding not to renew on existing terms</li>
<li>A third party offering better economic terms</li>
<li>Expiration of the original campaign</li>
</ul>
<p>The phrase <strong>bona fide offer</strong> should be considered carefully. The contract may require a signed offer, detailed term sheet, proposed agreement, or other credible evidence rather than an informal inquiry.</p>
<h3>What does matching mean?</h3>
<p>Matching is not always as simple as offering the same dollar amount. A brand deal can include:</p>
<ul>
<li>Guaranteed cash fee</li>
<li>Affiliate commission</li>
<li>Performance bonuses</li>
<li>Free products, travel, or services</li>
<li>Number and format of <a href="/glossary/deliverables">deliverables</a></li>
<li>Production requirements</li>
<li><a href="/glossary/usage-rights">Usage rights</a></li>
<li><a href="/glossary/category-exclusivity">Category exclusivity</a></li>
<li>Publication dates</li>
<li>Approval and revision terms</li>
<li>Payment schedule</li>
<li>Cancellation rights</li>
<li>Creative control</li>
<li>Term length</li>
<li>Option or renewal rights</li>
</ul>
<p>The clause should state whether the sponsor must match every material term, only the economic value, or a defined combination of terms.</p>
<p>A third-party offer may also include benefits the existing sponsor cannot reproduce. For example, one brand might offer event access, a speaking opportunity, unique products, or a long-term strategic relationship. The agreement needs a way to evaluate nonidentical consideration.</p>
<h3>Information the creator must disclose</h3>
<p>A ROFR may require the creator to provide enough information for the sponsor to evaluate the match. That can include:</p>
<ul>
<li>The third-party brand or category</li>
<li>Proposed compensation</li>
<li>Deliverables</li>
<li>Usage and exclusivity</li>
<li>Campaign dates</li>
<li>Payment terms</li>
<li>Material conditions</li>
<li>A copy or summary of the offer</li>
</ul>
<p>This creates confidentiality issues. The third-party offer may contain nondisclosure language that prevents the creator from sharing the brand's identity or complete contract.</p>
<p>A creator should avoid promising to disclose information that another agreement prohibits. The ROFR can permit a redacted offer, counsel-certified summary, or disclosure limited to material terms.</p>
<h3>Important ROFR deadlines</h3>
<p>Define:</p>
<ul>
<li>How quickly the creator must provide notice</li>
<li>How long the sponsor has to respond</li>
<li>Whether the response period uses calendar or business days</li>
<li>What counts as a valid exercise</li>
<li>How quickly the replacement agreement must be signed</li>
<li>What happens if negotiations continue after an exercise</li>
<li>How long the creator has to close the third-party deal after a refusal</li>
<li>Whether a materially changed third-party offer must be presented again</li>
</ul>
<p>A short response window protects the creator from losing the new opportunity. An indefinite process can effectively block future sponsorships.</p>
<h3>Creator risks from a broad ROFR</h3>
<p>A broad right can:</p>
<ul>
<li>Delay negotiations</li>
<li>Discourage new brands from making offers</li>
<li>Require disclosure of confidential terms</li>
<li>Give the incumbent sponsor free market intelligence</li>
<li>Create repeated matching cycles</li>
<li>Cover opportunities the sponsor did not help create</li>
<li>Limit the creator's leverage in renewal negotiations</li>
<li>Conflict with another <a href="/glossary/exclusivity-clause">exclusivity clause</a></li>
<li>Prevent the creator from accepting a strategically better but financially different deal</li>
<li>Continue after the sponsor has stopped actively working with the creator</li>
</ul>
<p>The sponsor may reasonably want renewal protection, but the right should be limited enough that the creator can still run a functioning business.</p>
<h3>Terms creators can negotiate</h3>
<p>Consider limiting the ROFR by:</p>
<ul>
<li>Named brand or product category</li>
<li>One renewal opportunity</li>
<li>One platform or format</li>
<li>A fixed expiration date</li>
<li>A short matching window</li>
<li>A minimum campaign value</li>
<li>A maximum number of exercises</li>
<li>A requirement for a bona fide written offer</li>
<li>Exclusions for inbound opportunities already in progress</li>
<li>Exclusions for existing relationships and affiliate programs</li>
<li>No disclosure of confidential third-party identity</li>
<li>A rule preventing repeated rematching after minor changes</li>
<li>Release if the sponsor has an unpaid invoice or material breach</li>
<li>Automatic expiration after the sponsor declines</li>
<li>Compensation for an extended hold or negotiation period</li>
</ul>
<h3>Is a ROFR the same as category exclusivity?</h3>
<p>No. Category exclusivity prevents defined competing activity during a restricted period. A ROFR allows the creator to pursue a future opportunity but gives the holder a chance to take or match it before completion.</p>
<p>The two can appear together. For example:</p>
<ul>
<li>During the campaign: the creator cannot promote competing products.</li>
<li>After the campaign: the sponsor gets a 15-day ROFR on one future category deal.</li>
</ul>
<p>That combination can create a much longer practical restriction than either clause alone.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Does a right of first refusal guarantee the sponsor a renewal?</h4>
<p>No. The sponsor must normally exercise the right under the agreed procedure and match or accept the required terms.</p>
<h4>Is a ROFR the same as a right of first negotiation?</h4>
<p>No. A right of first negotiation generally creates an exclusive negotiation period before outside negotiations. A ROFR usually becomes relevant after a third-party offer is available to match.</p>
<h4>Must a creator disclose the competing brand's identity?</h4>
<p>Only if the agreement requires it and the creator is legally permitted to disclose it. A creator can negotiate for redacted or summarized terms when confidentiality applies.</p>
<h4>Can a ROFR last forever?</h4>
<p>The parties can write a long clause, but creators should negotiate a clear expiration. Enforceability and interpretation depend on the agreement and governing law.</p>
