<h2>Shorts Feed</h2>
<p>The <strong>YouTube Shorts Feed</strong> is YouTube's continuous vertical viewing experience for discovering and watching short-form videos.</p>
<p>In the Shorts player, viewers can swipe or scroll from one item to the next, pause, like, comment, share, subscribe, remix, open sound pages, and provide recommendation feedback.</p>

<h3>What Does the Shorts Feed Look Like?</h3>
<p>The feed presents one video prominently at a time in a vertical player. Viewers move forward or backward through the stream by swiping, scrolling, or using device controls.</p>
<p>YouTube describes it as a never-ending stream of videos. Each Short is currently three minutes or less.</p>

<h3>How Do Viewers Enter the Shorts Feed?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Tap the Shorts tab in the YouTube mobile app.</li>
  <li>Select Shorts from YouTube's desktop navigation.</li>
  <li>Open a Short from a Home shelf.</li>
  <li>Select a Short from YouTube Search.</li>
  <li>Open a Short from a channel page.</li>
  <li>Follow a shared Shorts link.</li>
  <li>Open a sound or remix-related experience.</li>
</ul>
<p>After a Short opens in the player, the viewer can continue to other Shorts without returning to the original discovery surface.</p>

<h3>Shorts Feed vs. Shorts Shelf</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Feature</th><th>Purpose</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Shorts shelf</td><td>A row or collection of Short thumbnails on Home, Search, or another YouTube surface</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Shorts Feed</td><td>The full-screen vertical player where viewers continuously watch and swipe</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>A shelf can be the entry point. The feed is the viewing experience after the viewer selects a Short.</p>

<h3>Shorts Feed vs. Shorts Tab</h3>
<p>The Shorts tab is a navigation button that opens the Shorts experience. The feed is the stream of content displayed within that experience.</p>
<p>Creators often use the terms casually as synonyms, but the distinction is useful when discussing navigation and Analytics.</p>

<h3>Shorts Feed vs. Watch Page</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Experience</th><th>Typical Viewer Action</th><th>Monetization Model</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Shorts Feed</td><td>Swipe through a continuous vertical stream</td><td>Pooled ads shown between Shorts and eligible Premium viewing</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Watch Page</td><td>Select and watch a specific long-form or live video</td><td>Ads before, during, after, or around eligible content and Premium allocation</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>The associated agreements and revenue-sharing calculations are different.</p>

<h3>What Content Appears in the Feed?</h3>
<p>The feed primarily contains eligible Shorts. YouTube also says viewers may encounter eligible live streams while browsing Shorts in supported app experiences.</p>
<p>Shorts can include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Original recorded clips</li>
  <li>Edited vertical or square uploads</li>
  <li>Remixes</li>
  <li>Templates</li>
  <li>Green-screen creations</li>
  <li>Music-based videos</li>
  <li>Short educational, entertainment, news, and commentary content</li>
</ul>

<h3>What Qualifies as a Short?</h3>
<p>YouTube's current creation tools support Shorts up to three minutes long. Shorts are designed as short-form vertical content, and current upload classification depends on factors including duration, aspect ratio, upload date, and format.</p>
<p>Creators should follow the latest YouTube eligibility guidance rather than relying on the older rule that every Short must be 60 seconds or less.</p>

<h3>How Are Shorts Recommended?</h3>
<p>YouTube's recommendation systems personalize content for each viewer. Signals can include:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Watch history</li>
  <li>Search history</li>
  <li>Subscriptions</li>
  <li>Likes and dislikes</li>
  <li>Not interested feedback</li>
  <li>Don't recommend channel feedback</li>
  <li>Satisfaction surveys</li>
  <li>How similar viewers respond</li>
</ul>
<p>YouTube does not publish one fixed Shorts Feed algorithm or an exact formula creators can reproduce.</p>

<h3>What Happens When a Short Starts?</h3>
<p>Shorts normally begin playing within the vertical player. Since March 31, 2025, a legitimate Shorts view can count when playback starts or replays, with no minimum watch-time requirement.</p>
<p>The viewer can then:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Stay and continue watching</li>
  <li>Swipe away</li>
  <li>Pause</li>
  <li>Replay or let the Short loop</li>
  <li>Like, comment, share, or subscribe</li>
  <li>Open the audio page</li>
  <li>Use available remix tools</li>
</ul>

<h3>Shorts Feed vs. Vertical Live Feed</h3>
<p>Vertical live streams can appear in a swipeable mobile live experience and may be encountered while browsing Shorts. However, a live stream is not automatically a Short.</p>
<p>The two formats differ in:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Live versus prerecorded playback</li>
  <li>Concurrent-viewer reporting</li>
  <li>Chat and live fan-funding features</li>
  <li>Replay and archive behavior</li>
  <li>Eligibility and distribution rules</li>
</ul>

<h3>Where Can Shorts Appear Outside the Feed?</h3>
<ul>
  <li>YouTube Search</li>
  <li>Channel pages</li>
  <li>Home and Browse surfaces</li>
  <li>Subscriptions</li>
  <li>Playlists</li>
  <li>Suggested video contexts</li>
  <li>External websites and apps</li>
  <li>Messages and direct links</li>
</ul>
<p>The Shorts Feed is a major discovery surface, but it is not the only place viewers can find a Short.</p>

<h3>Feed Exposure Metrics</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Metric</th><th>Meaning</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Shown in feed</td><td>How many times the Short appeared in the Shorts Feed</td></tr>
    <tr><td><a href="/glossary/stayed-to-watch/">Stayed to watch</a></td><td>The percentage of times viewers remained past the initial seconds</td></tr>
    <tr><td><a href="/glossary/engaged-views/">Engaged views</a></td><td>How many viewers stayed past the initial seconds, excluding loops</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Shorts Views</td><td>Legitimate starts and replays under the current view-count system</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

<h3>Shorts Feed and Impressions</h3>
<p>The feed is not primarily measured through the standard thumbnail impressions funnel. Use Shown in feed and the viewer-choice report to evaluate feed presentation.</p>
<p>A Short can also receive registered thumbnail impressions on eligible surfaces such as Search, Home, channel pages, or playlists outside the full-screen vertical experience.</p>

<h3>Shorts Feed Ads</h3>
<p><a href="/glossary/shorts-feed-ads/">Shorts Feed Ads</a> appear between organic Shorts. Revenue is pooled monthly, adjusted for music licensing, allocated by eligible engaged-view share within each country, and then paid at the applicable creator share.</p>
<p>The ad is not attached exclusively to the Short shown immediately before or after it.</p>

<h3>Viewer Controls and Feedback</h3>
<p>Viewers can shape recommendations by:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Liking or disliking</li>
  <li>Selecting Not interested</li>
  <li>Selecting Don't recommend channel</li>
  <li>Subscribing</li>
  <li>Searching and watching related content</li>
  <li>Managing watch and search history</li>
</ul>
<p>These controls help personalize future feed content.</p>

<h3>Can Creators Force a Short Into the Feed?</h3>
<p>No title, hashtag, upload time, or setting guarantees feed distribution.</p>
<p>Creators should avoid claims that:</p>
<ul>
  <li>#Shorts guarantees inclusion.</li>
  <li>A specific upload time forces a test.</li>
  <li>Deleting and re-uploading resets distribution.</li>
  <li>Every Short receives a fixed initial audience.</li>
  <li>One retention percentage guarantees virality.</li>
</ul>

<h3>How to Create for the Feed</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Make the first frame understandable.</li>
  <li>Use mobile-readable text.</li>
  <li>Deliver clear audio immediately.</li>
  <li>Match the opening to the actual payoff.</li>
  <li>Frame important content away from interface buttons and captions.</li>
  <li>Use a length appropriate to the idea.</li>
  <li>Design loops only when they improve the experience.</li>
  <li>Review the video on a phone before publishing.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Common Shorts-Feed Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Calling a Shorts shelf the feed.</li>
  <li>Assuming all Shorts views come from the feed.</li>
  <li>Using the old 60-second definition universally.</li>
  <li>Treating live vertical streams as ordinary Shorts.</li>
  <li>Assuming #Shorts forces distribution.</li>
  <li>Using standard thumbnail CTR as the only feed metric.</li>
  <li>Believing every viewer sees the same sequence.</li>
  <li>Confusing ads between Shorts with ads assigned to one Short.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>What is the Shorts Feed?</h4>
<p>It is YouTube's continuous vertical player for discovering and watching short-form videos.</p>

<h4>How long can a Short be?</h4>
<p>YouTube's current Shorts experience supports Shorts up to three minutes long.</p>

<h4>Are all Shorts watched in the feed?</h4>
<p>No. Shorts can also be found through Search, Channel Pages, Browse, playlists, External sources, and direct links.</p>

<h4>Can a creator guarantee feed distribution?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube does not provide a setting or published formula that guarantees placement.</p>
