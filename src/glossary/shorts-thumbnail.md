<h2>Shorts Thumbnail</h2>
<p>A <strong>Shorts thumbnail</strong> is the preview image used to represent a YouTube Short on eligible surfaces such as Search, channel pages, hashtag pages, audio pivot pages, and other thumbnail-based discovery areas.</p>
<p>Creators cannot upload a separate custom image for a Short in the same way they can for a long-form video. Instead, YouTube lets creators select a frame from the Short using the mobile YouTube app.</p>

<h3>Can You Upload a Custom Shorts Thumbnail?</h3>
<p>No. YouTube's current thumbnail documentation says creators cannot upload a separate custom thumbnail image for Shorts.</p>
<p>You can:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Select a frame from the Short</li>
  <li>Add text through the mobile thumbnail editor</li>
  <li>Apply available filters</li>
</ul>
<p>The frame must come from the uploaded Short itself.</p>

<h3>How to Select a Thumbnail Before Uploading</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Create or upload the Short in the YouTube mobile app.</li>
  <li>Continue to the final upload-details screen.</li>
  <li>Tap <strong>Edit</strong> on the displayed thumbnail.</li>
  <li>Move through the Short and select a frame.</li>
  <li>Add text or filters when useful.</li>
  <li>Tap <strong>Done</strong>.</li>
  <li>Complete the upload.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Can You Edit a Shorts Thumbnail After Upload?</h3>
<p>YouTube's current Android and iPhone Shorts creation Help pages say creators can select a new frame after upload through the YouTube mobile app:</p>
<ol>
  <li>Open the YouTube app.</li>
  <li>Go to your channel and open <strong>Your videos</strong>.</li>
  <li>Tap More on the Short.</li>
  <li>Select <strong>Edit</strong>.</li>
  <li>Choose a frame.</li>
  <li>Add available text or filters.</li>
  <li>Tap <strong>Done</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>The feature is managed in the YouTube app, not YouTube Studio.</p>

<h3>Why Do Some Help Pages Say It Cannot Be Changed?</h3>
<p>YouTube's general custom-thumbnail Help page still contains older wording saying a selected Shorts frame cannot be changed after upload, while the current dedicated mobile Shorts creation instructions provide after-upload editing steps.</p>
<p>The detailed mobile workflow is the more current operational instruction. Availability can still vary during rollouts, so creators should update the app and check the Short's mobile Edit menu.</p>

<h3>Where Does the Selected Thumbnail Appear?</h3>
<p>YouTube specifically identifies surfaces including:</p>
<ul>
  <li>YouTube Search results</li>
  <li>Your channel page</li>
  <li>Hashtag pages</li>
  <li>Audio and sound pivot pages</li>
</ul>
<p>Other YouTube surfaces can display automatically generated crops or previews depending on device, layout, and product experience.</p>

<h3>Does the Thumbnail Control the Shorts Feed?</h3>
<p>Not in the same way a long-form thumbnail controls a standard click. The Shorts Feed is a full-screen auto-playing experience.</p>
<p>The opening frame and first seconds usually matter more inside the feed. The selected thumbnail matters most on surfaces where the viewer chooses from a collection before opening the Short.</p>

<h3>Shorts Thumbnail vs. First Frame</h3>
<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Element</th><th>Where It Matters</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Selected thumbnail frame</td><td>Search, channel pages, hashtag pages, audio pages, and other thumbnail surfaces</td></tr>
    <tr><td>First playback frame</td><td>The opening visual when the Short begins in the feed or player</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>The selected thumbnail does not necessarily replace the actual first playback frame.</p>

<h3>Shorts Thumbnail vs. Custom Thumbnail</h3>
<p>A long-form <a href="/glossary/custom-thumbnail/">Custom Thumbnail</a> can be a separately designed image uploaded from a device. A Shorts thumbnail is selected from the video's frames.</p>
<p>This limits the creator's ability to build a completely separate composition, but creators can plan a thumbnail-friendly frame during production.</p>

<h3>How to Create a Thumbnail-Friendly Frame</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Hold a clear pose or product shot for part of the Short.</li>
  <li>Keep the subject large enough for mobile display.</li>
  <li>Use clean contrast.</li>
  <li>Leave space for optional text.</li>
  <li>Avoid motion blur.</li>
  <li>Do not cover the subject with interface elements.</li>
  <li>Make the frame accurately represent the content.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Adding Text and Filters</h3>
<p>YouTube's current mobile workflow allows creators to add text and apply filters to the selected thumbnail frame.</p>
<p>The Help instructions warn that after completing an edit, creators cannot return to earlier edit states to make changes within that editing sequence. Preview carefully before tapping Done.</p>

<h3>Thumbnail Aspect Ratio</h3>
<p>Shorts are commonly vertical or square. YouTube may crop the selected frame differently across surfaces.</p>
<p>Keep important elements:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Near the visual center</li>
  <li>Away from extreme top and bottom edges</li>
  <li>Large enough to remain readable after cropping</li>
  <li>Separated from interface overlays</li>
</ul>

<h3>Do Vertical Custom Thumbnails Appear Everywhere?</h3>
<p>YouTube's broader thumbnail guidance notes that vertical videos with 16:9 custom thumbnails can receive an automatically generated 4:5 thumbnail on Home, Explore, and Subscription pages.</p>
<p>For true Shorts, creators should rely on the selected frame and understand that YouTube can adapt presentation by surface.</p>

<h3>Shorts Thumbnails and Search</h3>
<p>Search viewers actively choose among results, so a clear selected frame can help communicate:</p>
<ul>
  <li>The subject</li>
  <li>The result</li>
  <li>The product or person featured</li>
  <li>The problem being solved</li>
  <li>The tone of the Short</li>
</ul>
<p>Use the title and thumbnail together rather than repeating the same words unnecessarily.</p>

<h3>Shorts Thumbnails and Channel Pages</h3>
<p>On a channel page, thumbnails help visitors understand the creator's content library. Consistent framing can make a series recognizable, but identical frames can make Shorts difficult to distinguish.</p>

<h3>Does Changing the Thumbnail Reset Performance?</h3>
<p>YouTube does not state that selecting another frame resets the Short's views, Analytics history, feed distribution, or URL.</p>
<p>Changing the frame can affect how the Short is presented on eligible thumbnail surfaces. It should not be treated as a feed-algorithm reset.</p>

<h3>Can Shorts Use A/B Testing?</h3>
<p>YouTube's native <a href="/glossary/youtube-a-b-testing/">YouTube A/B Testing</a> feature does not support Shorts. Creators cannot use native Test &amp; Compare to test multiple Shorts thumbnail frames.</p>
<p>Review Search and Channel Pages performance over time rather than claiming a controlled thumbnail experiment.</p>

<h3>Thumbnail Policies</h3>
<p>Shorts thumbnails must follow YouTube's Community Guidelines and thumbnail policies. Prohibited or restricted imagery can result in removal, strikes, loss of thumbnail privileges, or account action.</p>
<p>A selected frame is not exempt merely because it comes from the video.</p>

<h3>How to Evaluate a Shorts Thumbnail</h3>
<ol>
  <li>Check how it appears on the channel page.</li>
  <li>Search for the Short from another account or signed-out context.</li>
  <li>Review whether the subject remains clear at small size.</li>
  <li>Check crops on multiple devices.</li>
  <li>Compare Search and Channel Pages traffic.</li>
  <li>Separate thumbnail-surface performance from Shorts Feed performance.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Common Shorts-Thumbnail Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Trying to upload a separate custom image.</li>
  <li>Assuming the thumbnail is the feed's first frame.</li>
  <li>Designing only for a 16:9 crop.</li>
  <li>Using tiny text.</li>
  <li>Choosing a misleading frame.</li>
  <li>Expecting a thumbnail change to reset feed distribution.</li>
  <li>Looking for the editor in Studio instead of the mobile app.</li>
  <li>Assuming native A/B testing supports Shorts.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>Can I upload a custom thumbnail image for a Short?</h4>
<p>No. YouTube currently requires creators to select a frame from the Short.</p>

<h4>Can I change the selected frame after upload?</h4>
<p>Current mobile Shorts creation Help provides after-upload editing steps through the YouTube app.</p>

<h4>Where does the thumbnail appear?</h4>
<p>It can appear in Search, hashtag and audio pages, and on the channel page.</p>

<h4>Does it determine whether viewers swipe in the Shorts Feed?</h4>
<p>Not directly. Feed viewers encounter the playing Short, so the actual opening frame and first seconds are more important there.</p>
