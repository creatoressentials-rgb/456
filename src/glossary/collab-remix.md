<h2>Collab Remix</h2>
<p><strong>Collab Remix</strong> is a <a href="/glossary/youtube-shorts/">YouTube Shorts</a> creation tool that lets you record a new Short beside an eligible YouTube video or Short. The source content and your recording appear together in a split-screen layout, making the format useful for reactions, comparisons, choreography, demonstrations, and point-by-point responses.</p>
<p>YouTube automatically connects the remix to the source content. That <a href="/glossary/attribution/">attribution</a> helps viewers identify the original video and gives the source creator a potential path to new traffic.</p>

<h3>How Collab Remix works</h3>
<p>Collab combines two visual elements in one Short:</p>
<ol>
<li>An eligible source video or Short already published on YouTube.</li>
<li>New video that you record in the Shorts camera.</li>
</ol>
<p>You can choose from the layout options available in the editor before recording. Depending on the layout, the videos may appear side by side, stacked, or in another split-screen arrangement.</p>
<p>Collab is designed for simultaneous visual context. Viewers can see the source while they watch your reaction, performance, explanation, or alternative version.</p>

<h3>How to create a Collab Remix</h3>
<p>Collab Remix is created in the YouTube mobile app.</p>
<h4>From another Short</h4>
<ol>
<li>Open the Short you want to use.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Remix</strong>.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Collab</strong>.</li>
<li>Choose a layout.</li>
<li>Record and edit your Short.</li>
<li>Add the title, audience setting, and other upload details, then publish.</li>
</ol>
<h4>From a long-form video</h4>
<ol>
<li>Open the eligible video in the YouTube mobile app.</li>
<li>Tap <strong>Share</strong>.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Remix</strong>, then <strong>Collab</strong>.</li>
<li>Choose a layout and record your portion.</li>
</ol>
<p>YouTube has been moving the Remix controls for regular videos into the Share menu. An older tutorial may still tell you to look for a separate Remix button under the player.</p>

<p>A Collab Remix does <strong>not</strong> make the source creator your publishing partner. It does not automatically share ownership, revenue, channel access, or editing control with that creator. It is a remix format with source attribution.</p>

<p>Choose Collab when the source needs to remain visible throughout your response. Choose Cut when you only need a brief excerpt, <a href="/glossary/green-screen/">Green Screen</a> when you want to stand in front of the source, or Sound when the audio is the only element you need.</p>

<h3>What videos are eligible for Collab Remix?</h3>
<p>Collab is available only when YouTube and the source creator allow visual remixing. The option may be missing when:</p>
<ul>
<li>The source creator has restricted Shorts remixing.</li>
<li>The source is private or otherwise unavailable.</li>
<li>Copyright ownership or licensing limits visual remixing.</li>
<li>A third-party claim prevents visual remix features from being offered.</li>
<li>The feature is not available for that account, device, region, or app version.</li>
</ul>
<p>A missing Collab option does not necessarily mean something is wrong with your channel. It may reflect the source video's permissions or rights status.</p>

<h3>Attribution, analytics, and discovery</h3>
<p>Shorts made with YouTube's remix tools are attributed back to the original work. The source creator may receive notifications about remixes and can review remix activity in <a href="/glossary/youtube-studio/">YouTube Studio</a>.</p>
<p>Traffic generated by Collab, Cut, and Green Screen remixes can appear under the <strong>Remixed video</strong> source in <a href="/glossary/traffic-sources/">YouTube traffic sources</a>. Creators can use <a href="/glossary/youtube-advanced-mode/">YouTube Advanced Mode</a> and other Analytics reports to compare how remix-driven viewers behave.</p>
<p>Attribution is not the same as an endorsement. A Collab Remix can be linked to the original even when the source creator did not personally approve or participate in the new Short beyond leaving remixing enabled.</p>

<h3>What happens if the source video changes?</h3>
<p>A Collab Remix depends on continued access to the source video. If the original creator deletes the video, restricts video remixing, or makes certain edits in YouTube Studio, the visual remix may be removed.</p>
<p>This is different from an audio-only remix, which may first be muted and set to unlisted before scheduled deletion. Visual remix formats such as Collab can be deleted when the source is no longer available for remixing.</p>

<h3>Can a Collab Remix be monetized?</h3>
<p>Using Collab does not automatically make a Short eligible for revenue. A channel must qualify for the <a href="/glossary/youtube-partner-program/">YouTube Partner Program</a>, and the Short must comply with YouTube's monetization and copyright rules.</p>
<p>A creator should contribute meaningful original value rather than relying on the source video to carry the entire Short. Commentary, analysis, performance, education, humor, or a clearly original response can help distinguish the new work. Channels that publish minimally changed third-party material may run into <a href="/glossary/reused-content/">reused content</a> concerns even when the platform technically allowed the remix.</p>

<h3>Best uses for Collab Remix</h3>
<p>Collab works especially well for:</p>
<ul>
<li>Reacting while the original moment stays visible.</li>
<li>Performing choreography beside an artist or another creator.</li>
<li>Comparing a technique, product, result, or before-and-after example.</li>
<li>Correcting or expanding on a claim in real time.</li>
<li>Showing your attempt beside a trend, challenge, or tutorial.</li>
<li>Creating a visual conversation without downloading the source file.</li>
</ul>
<p>The strongest Collabs make your contribution understandable even to a viewer who has never seen the original video.</p>

<h3>Frequently asked questions</h3>
<h4>Is Collab Remix the same as a duet?</h4>
<p>It is YouTube's closest built-in equivalent to a split-screen duet format. YouTube calls the tool Collab, and it places your recording beside eligible source content.</p>
<h4>Does Collab Remix invite the original creator to my video?</h4>
<p>No. The source creator is attributed, but Collab Remix does not add that channel as an invited collaborator or co-publisher.</p>
<h4>Can I Collab with a regular YouTube video?</h4>
<p>Yes, when the video is eligible for visual remixing. In the current mobile interface, open the video, tap Share, and choose Remix &gt; Collab.</p>
<h4>Can the original creator remove my Collab Short?</h4>
<p>The creator can restrict future remixing or remove the source. When a visual source becomes unavailable for remixing, YouTube may delete Shorts that depend on it.</p>
