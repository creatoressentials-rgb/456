<h2>Channels Your Audience Watches</h2>
<p><strong>Channels your audience watches</strong> is a YouTube Analytics report showing other channels that your viewers consistently watched outside your channel during the past 28 days.</p>
<p>The report helps creators understand the broader viewing habits of their audience and identify potential collaborators, adjacent interests, familiar formats, and channels competing for the same viewers' attention.</p>

<h3>What Does the Report Measure?</h3>
<p>YouTube describes the report as showing other channels your viewers <strong>consistently watched</strong> outside your channel over the past 28 days.</p>
<p>That wording matters. The report is not simply a list of:</p>
<ul>
<li>Every channel one viewer opened once</li>
<li>Channels that link to your videos</li>
<li>Your subscribers' subscriptions</li>
<li>Channels YouTube considers direct competitors</li>
<li>Creators who share your exact topic</li>
</ul>
<p>It represents recurring outside-channel viewing behavior among people who watch your content, subject to YouTube's available data and privacy thresholds.</p>

<h3>Where Can You Find the Report?</h3>
<ol>
<li>Sign in to YouTube Studio.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Analytics</strong>.</li>
<li>Open the <strong>Audience</strong> tab.</li>
<li>Find the <strong>Channels your audience watches</strong> card.</li>
</ol>
<p>The report may be unavailable when YouTube does not have enough relevant audience data. YouTube does not publish the exact threshold required for the card to appear.</p>

<h3>What Time Period Does It Use?</h3>
<p>The current report covers the past 28 days. It is a recent audience-interest snapshot rather than a permanent map of every channel your viewers have ever watched.</p>
<p>The list can change when:</p>
<ul>
<li>A news event or trend attracts attention</li>
<li>Your channel reaches a new audience</li>
<li>Seasonal viewing habits change</li>
<li>You publish a different topic or format</li>
<li>A related creator releases a popular series</li>
<li>Your audience shifts between videos, Shorts, and live content</li>
</ul>

<h3>Channels Your Audience Watches vs. What Your Audience Watches</h3>
<table>
<thead><tr><th>Report</th><th>Focus</th><th>Current Window</th><th>Best Use</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Channels your audience watches</td><td>Other channels watched consistently</td><td>Past 28 days</td><td>Broader audience overlap and collaboration research</td></tr>
<tr><td><a href="/glossary/what-your-audience-watches/">What your audience watches</a></td><td>Specific videos, Shorts, live streams, and podcasts watched outside your channel</td><td>Past seven days</td><td>Recent topics, titles, thumbnails, and content ideas</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>A single popular outside video may reflect a temporary event. Repeated channel overlap may indicate a more durable audience interest.</p>

<h3>Does the Report Show Competitors?</h3>
<p>Sometimes, but "competitor" is too narrow for the entire list. Your viewers may consistently watch:</p>
<ul>
<li>Direct competitors covering the same topic</li>
<li>Complementary educators or service providers</li>
<li>Entertainment channels with a similar audience</li>
<li>News and commentary channels</li>
<li>Product manufacturers and official brands</li>
<li>Creators in an adjacent niche</li>
<li>Large mainstream channels unrelated to your business model</li>
</ul>
<p>Judge each channel by why the audience watches it rather than assuming every result competes with your channel.</p>

<h3>How to Research a Listed Channel</h3>
<ol>
<li>Review its recurring topics and series.</li>
<li>Identify whether it serves the same problem, a neighboring problem, or a different entertainment need.</li>
<li>Study the channel's video formats, hosts, pacing, and publishing cadence.</li>
<li>Review recent titles and thumbnails for structural patterns.</li>
<li>Look at audience comments for repeated questions and unmet needs.</li>
<li>Identify what your channel can contribute without copying.</li>
<li>Record whether the channel is a competitor, collaborator, source, advertiser, or unrelated overlap.</li>
</ol>

<h3>How to Find Collaboration Opportunities</h3>
<p>YouTube specifically says the report can help creators find collaboration opportunities. A strong candidate usually has:</p>
<ul>
<li>Meaningful audience overlap</li>
<li>A complementary rather than identical value proposition</li>
<li>A format that can feature both creators naturally</li>
<li>Comparable audience expectations and brand safety</li>
<li>A clear benefit for both audiences</li>
<li>A realistic way to contact the creator or team</li>
</ul>
<p>A large channel appearing in the report is not automatically the best partner. A smaller creator with a closely aligned audience and clear joint idea may be more practical.</p>

<h3>Content Ideas From Channel Overlap</h3>
<ul>
<li>Cover a related topic from your specialized perspective.</li>
<li>Create a beginner or advanced companion to a popular format.</li>
<li>Answer questions that the other channel's audience still asks.</li>
<li>Compare two approaches without attacking the other creator.</li>
<li>Invite an expert from a complementary channel.</li>
<li>Create a reaction or analysis only when you add substantial original value and follow copyright rules.</li>
<li>Build a series around a recurring audience interest rather than copying one viral video.</li>
</ul>

<h3>What the Report Does Not Tell You</h3>
<ul>
<li>How many individual viewers watched each listed channel</li>
<li>Whether those viewers subscribed to the other channel</li>
<li>Why they watched it</li>
<li>Whether they prefer that channel over yours</li>
<li>Whether a collaboration request will succeed</li>
<li>Whether copying the channel's topic will perform well</li>
<li>The exact audience-overlap percentage</li>
</ul>
<p>Use the card as a research lead, not as a complete competitive-intelligence system.</p>

<h3>How to Use the Report Responsibly</h3>
<ul>
<li>Study patterns rather than copying scripts, thumbnails, or branding.</li>
<li>Confirm that a topic fits your own channel promise.</li>
<li>Use several reports, including <a href="/glossary/what-your-audience-watches/">What your audience watches</a> and <a href="/glossary/monthly-audience/">Monthly audience</a>.</li>
<li>Review the list over multiple 28-day periods.</li>
<li>Separate durable overlap from temporary news or trend behavior.</li>
<li>Respect copyright, trademarks, and creator identity.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Why Might the Report Be Missing?</h3>
<p>YouTube may limit audience reports when there is not enough relevant data, when privacy thresholds are not met, or when content and account conditions restrict reporting. A missing card does not mean your viewers watch no other channels.</p>
<p>Smaller or newer channels can instead use:</p>
<ul>
<li>Viewer comments and polls</li>
<li>Search and traffic-source reports</li>
<li>Public audience research</li>
<li>Collaboration referrals</li>
<li>The Trends tab</li>
<li>Repeated questions from viewers and clients</li>
</ul>

<h3>Common Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
<li>Calling every listed channel a direct competitor.</li>
<li>Assuming the list contains channels your subscribers follow.</li>
<li>Confusing the 28-day channel report with the seven-day content report.</li>
<li>Copying a channel's successful packaging instead of studying the audience need.</li>
<li>Contacting every listed creator without a specific collaboration idea.</li>
<li>Treating one temporary report as a permanent audience profile.</li>
<li>Assuming the list ranks channels by exact overlap percentage.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>How far back does Channels your audience watches go?</h4>
<p>YouTube currently says the report covers the past 28 days.</p>
<h4>Does it show channels my subscribers watch?</h4>
<p>It reflects viewers of your channel, which is not the same group as subscribers. Subscribers who do not watch and viewers who are not subscribed affect the distinction.</p>
<h4>Can I use the report to find collaborators?</h4>
<p>Yes. YouTube specifically identifies collaboration research as one use of the report.</p>
<h4>Why is a seemingly unrelated channel listed?</h4>
<p>People have multiple interests. The overlap may be demographic, entertainment-based, temporary, or related to a topic that is not obvious from your channel alone.</p>
