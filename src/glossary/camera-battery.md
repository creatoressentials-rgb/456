<h2>Camera Battery</h2>
<p>A <strong>camera battery</strong> is a removable or built-in power source that supplies electricity to a camera and its electronic systems. Most modern mirrorless cameras, DSLRs, action cameras, and camcorders use rechargeable lithium-ion battery packs designed for a particular camera family.</p>
<p>Camera batteries power more than the image sensor. They may also run the processor, display, electronic viewfinder, autofocus motors, image stabilization, wireless connections, microphones, powered accessories, and recording media.</p>

<h3>What Do Camera Battery Specifications Mean?</h3>
<table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Specification</th><th>What It Describes</th><th>Why It Matters</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Voltage (V)</td><td>The electrical potential the battery is designed to provide</td><td>The battery and camera power system must be compatible</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Capacity (mAh)</td><td>Charge capacity in milliamp-hours</td><td>Useful when comparing compatible batteries operating at the same voltage</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Energy (Wh)</td><td>Watt-hours, calculated from voltage and amp-hours</td><td>Better for comparing total stored energy across batteries with different voltages</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Battery model</td><td>The manufacturer's physical and electrical battery family</td><td>A similar shape does not guarantee compatibility</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Cycle or health information</td><td>An estimate of battery condition or charging history on supported systems</td><td>Can help identify an aging pack</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>A larger mAh number does not automatically mean longer runtime when two batteries operate at different voltages or use different electronics. Watt-hours provide a more complete energy comparison, but actual camera runtime still depends on power consumption and camera behavior.</p>

<h3>Why Does Camera Battery Life Vary?</h3>
<p>Published shot counts or recording times are standardized estimates under stated test conditions, not universal promises. Runtime changes with:</p>
<ul>
  <li>Video resolution, frame rate, codec, and recording format</li>
  <li>Display and electronic-viewfinder brightness</li>
  <li>Continuous autofocus and subject tracking</li>
  <li>In-body or lens stabilization</li>
  <li>Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, GPS, and smartphone connections</li>
  <li>Powered microphones, lenses, monitors, or accessories</li>
  <li>Ambient temperature and battery temperature</li>
  <li>Battery age, condition, and charge level</li>
  <li>How often the camera sleeps, wakes, reviews clips, or changes settings</li>
</ul>
<p>A camera's still-photo rating should not be treated as its video runtime. Some official camera specifications publish separate still, movie, and playback estimates because the workloads differ substantially.</p>

<h3>OEM vs. Third-Party Camera Batteries</h3>
<table>
  <thead>
    <tr><th>Factor</th><th>Manufacturer Battery</th><th>Third-Party Battery</th></tr>
  </thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Camera compatibility</td><td>Designed and tested for listed models</td><td>Must be verified by exact battery and camera model</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Communication</td><td>More likely to support battery percentage, health, and warnings fully</td><td>Some packs provide limited or inconsistent camera communication</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Firmware behavior</td><td>Generally receives direct manufacturer support</td><td>Camera updates can sometimes produce warnings or incompatibility</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Price</td><td>Usually higher</td><td>Often lower</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Quality</td><td>Controlled by the camera manufacturer</td><td>Varies considerably by brand and product</td></tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
<p>Third-party does not automatically mean unsafe, and manufacturer-branded does not mean a battery cannot age or fail. The practical issue is whether the exact pack is reputable, correctly specified, supported, undamaged, and compatible with the camera and charger.</p>

<h3>USB Charging vs. USB Power Supply</h3>
<p>Charging a battery and powering a camera are separate functions. Some cameras charge only while turned off. Others can receive USB power while operating, but the internal battery may still discharge slowly if the power source cannot meet the camera's demand.</p>
<p>USB-C also does not guarantee USB Power Delivery support. Check the camera manual for the required adapter output, cable, battery model, and operating mode. Even within one manufacturer's battery family, only certain revisions may support in-camera USB charging.</p>

<h3>How to Extend Camera Battery Runtime</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Carry fully charged spare batteries.</li>
  <li>Reduce display brightness when practical.</li>
  <li>Disable wireless features that are not being used.</li>
  <li>Use airplane mode when connectivity is unnecessary and the camera supports it.</li>
  <li>Allow the camera to sleep between setups rather than leaving every system active.</li>
  <li>Use an approved USB power supply or DC-coupler system for long studio sessions.</li>
  <li>Keep spare batteries reasonably warm in cold conditions without overheating them.</li>
  <li>Replace packs that show swelling, damage, leakage, excessive heat, or a major loss of runtime.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Camera Battery Safety and Storage</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Use a charger approved for the exact battery type.</li>
  <li>Do not use swollen, punctured, wet, crushed, or unusually hot batteries.</li>
  <li>Protect loose terminals from metal objects that could cause a short circuit.</li>
  <li>Store batteries in a cool, dry place within the manufacturer's stated conditions.</li>
  <li>Do not leave batteries in a hot vehicle or direct sunlight.</li>
  <li>Follow manufacturer guidance for long-term storage and periodic charging.</li>
  <li>Recycle lithium-ion batteries through an appropriate battery-recycling program rather than household trash.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Can Camera Batteries Go in Checked Luggage?</h3>
<p>In the United States, FAA guidance requires spare, uninstalled lithium batteries to remain in carry-on baggage with terminals protected from short circuit. Battery size limits and airline approval rules can also apply, especially above 100 watt-hours.</p>
<p>Installed batteries and international travel may be subject to additional airline or national rules. Check the current airline and aviation-authority guidance before traveling rather than relying on an old packing list.</p>

<h3>Common Camera Battery Mistakes</h3>
<ul>
  <li>Buying by physical appearance instead of the exact supported model number.</li>
  <li>Comparing mAh values across different battery voltages as if they were directly equivalent.</li>
  <li>Assuming a CIPA still-photo rating predicts continuous video runtime.</li>
  <li>Assuming any USB-C charger can power the camera while it records.</li>
  <li>Leaving a damaged or swollen battery in service because it still turns on.</li>
  <li>Packing loose spare lithium batteries in checked luggage.</li>
  <li>Running an important shoot without a tested spare-power plan.</li>
</ul>

<h3>Frequently Asked Questions</h3>
<h4>How long does a camera battery last?</h4>
<p>There is no universal runtime. It depends on the battery, camera, recording mode, accessories, temperature, settings, and battery condition. Use the manufacturer's model-specific estimates as a starting point and run your own full-session test.</p>

<h4>Can a higher-capacity battery damage a camera?</h4>
<p>A battery designed and approved for that camera can provide more energy without forcing the camera to consume it. However, a pack with the wrong voltage, contacts, communication, dimensions, or charger compatibility can create serious problems.</p>

<h4>Can a camera run while charging?</h4>
<p>Some cameras can operate from USB power, while others charge only when off or require a battery to remain installed. The adapter and cable must meet the camera's requirements.</p>

<h4>When should a camera battery be replaced?</h4>
<p>Replace it when the manufacturer recommends it, when runtime has dropped substantially, or immediately if it swells, leaks, overheats abnormally, is physically damaged, or produces repeated errors.</p>
